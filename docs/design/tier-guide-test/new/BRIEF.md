# Designer Brief: Ogres

Your task is to design a complete new Hearthstone Battlegrounds minion type (tribe) based on the **Ogres** of Warcraft. This brief has two parts: the race's lore, and how Battlegrounds plays as of November 2026 (Season 14, patch 36.6.x).

---

## Part A: Ogre lore

**Origins.** Ogres come from Draenor, descended from titan-made giants through the gronn and ogron, who long kept them as slaves. **Gog the Gronnslayer**, who learned arcane magic from the arakkoa, slew gronn and founded the **Gorian Empire**, ruled by sorcerer-kings titled **Imperator** from **Goria** and later **Highmaul** in Nagrand. Imperator Molok lost Goria in a war with the orcs; Hok'lon's attack on the draenei city of Shattrath failed; Kelgrok's Bladespire sorcerers bred the **Mok'nathal**, orc-ogre half-breeds; **Mar'gok** was the last. In *Warlords of Draenor*, Mar'gok rules Highmaul and its gladiator arena, where enslaved orcs such as Kargath Bladefist fought; its raid bosses include **The Butcher**, the twin ogron **Phemos and Pol**, **Ko'ragh** and Mar'gok himself. The gronn **Gruul the Dragonkiller** ruled ogres in Gorgrond and Blade's Edge, where some still worship him; **Gruul's Lair** pairs him with **High King Maulgar** and his council. Ogre lords keep gronn traits: huge size and bony growths.

**Ogre magi and two heads.** Two-headed births were rare until Gorian arcanists learned to copy them. In the Second War **Gul'dan** had **Cho'gall** run rituals at the **Altars of Storms**, built from high elven runestones, creating **ogre magi** given cunning by the spirits of dead Shadow Council warlocks. Warcraft II made the bickering heads famous: each blames the other. Cho'gall later led the **Twilight's Hammer**, a doomsday cult of the Old Gods, served Deathwing in the Cataclysm, and was fought in the **Bastion of Twilight**; he burned Mar'gok alive. The cult recruited ogre clans across Azeroth, and in *Midnight* ogres march with the Twilight's Blade.

**Culture.** Independent clans under chieftains; strength is the main virtue and beating a rival is how you rise. Among the Gordunni of **Dire Maul**, anyone may challenge for the throne and the winner becomes **King Gordok** (the players' "tribute run": kill the king and his court, Captain Kromcrush and Cho'Rush the Observer, crowns you). Disputes are settled by slave gladiator fights. Ogres hire out as mercenaries and bodyguards, especially to goblin cartels, and are capable sailors who helped build juggernauts. They live in **ogre mounds**, eat mostly fresh meat and drink heavily (**Gordok Green Grog**). The popular image is slow, hungry, greedy brutes saying "Me smash!"; magi and imperators show a cunning, arcane side.

**More notable figures.** **Dentarg**, Ner'zhul's ogre enforcer; **Rexxar**, Mok'nathal beastmaster with his bear Misha, who brought the **Stonemaul** into the Horde after killing the tyrant **Kor'gall**; **Mog'dorg the Wizened**, sage of **Ogri'la**, ogres seeking peace after escaping the gronn; **Leoroxx**, leader of the Mok'nathal revolt; Orgrim Doomhammer's elite ogre guard.

**Clans and places.** Gordunni (Feralas), Stonemaul (Dustwallow Marsh), Boulderfist (Arathi; under Krol the first clan to join the Old Horde), Crushridge (Alterac), Mosh'Ogg (Stranglethorn), Dunemaul (Tanaris), Dreadmaul (Blasted Lands), Glopgut (Twilight Highlands), Bloodmaul and Bladespire (Blade's Edge), Warmaul (Nagrand), Highmaul, Goria, Gorgrond, Blackrock Spire.

**Objects and icons.** Spiked clubs and mauls, crude crowns, tribute chests, grog barrels, chains and shackles, arena cages, runestone altars, mage staves, cauldrons of meat, bone totems, Highmaul's arcane sigils.

**In Hearthstone so far.** Single cards, not a type: Ogre Brute and Mogor the Ogre ("50% chance to attack the wrong enemy"), Ogre Magi, Ogre Ninja, Ogre Warmaul, Cho'gall.

**Themes.** Might makes right, challenges for the crown, appetite and greed, tribute, mercenaries, slaves and gladiators, brute force versus sorcery, two heads that disagree, a fallen empire, service to darker masters (Gul'dan, Cho'gall, the Old Gods, the Iron Horde).

## Part B: Battlegrounds as of November 2026

**Format.** Eight players (Solo) or four teams of two sharing one life total (Duos, where teammates can pass cards). Each player picks a hero (30 Health, often some Armor) with a Hero Power. Players fight a different opponent each round until one remains. With an odd number of players, the unpaired player fights a ghost of a defeated opponent.

**Minion types.** Each lobby uses **five** of the active minion types. Active types: Beast, Demon, Dragon, Elemental, Mech, Murloc, Pirate, Quilboar, Undead, Aberration. **Naga** were rotated out on 22 September 2026, when Aberrations arrived. Neutral minions appear in every lobby. Some minions are dual-type or "All" types.

**Economy.** You start with 3 Gold and gain 1 more maximum Gold per turn, up to 10. Unspent Gold does not carry over. Buying a minion costs 3, selling gives 1, and a Refresh costs 1. Freeze, which keeps the current offer for next turn, is free. The board holds 7 minions; the hand holds 10 cards.

**Tavern tiers.** Tiers run 1–6, and some effects reach Tier 7. Upgrading costs 5 / 7 / 8 / 11 / 11 for Tiers 2–6, and each upgrade gets 1 cheaper every turn you wait. The Tavern offers 3 minions at Tier 1, plus one more at Tiers 2, 4 and 6. Minions come from a shared pool, with fewer copies of each minion at higher tiers.

**Triples.** Holding three copies of a minion makes a **golden** version with double stats and, often, a doubled effect. Playing it gives a Triple Reward: **Discover** a minion from one tier above your current tier.

**Combat.** Combat is automatic. The player with more minions attacks first (random if tied). Minions attack from left to right, alternating sides, and pick random targets, but must attack Taunt minions first. The surviving player deals damage equal to their Tavern Tier plus the tiers of their surviving minions. Permanent buffs gained in the Recruit Phase persist; changes during combat do not.

**Keywords in use.** Battlecry, Deathrattle, Divine Shield, Reborn, Taunt, Windfury, Venomous (now "destroy the first minion this damages *each combat*"), Magnetic, Avenge (X), Start of Combat, **Rally** (triggers whenever the minion attacks), **Activate** (spend Gold in the Recruit Phase to use a minion's ability once per turn), Discover, Choose One, Spellcraft (Naga, now out), and Bonus Keywords.

**Tavern Spells.** One-use spells sold in the Tavern beside minions, at various Gold costs (buffs, Gold, Discovers, tribe-specific effects).

**Trinkets.** Discovered at turn 6 (a Lesser Trinket) and turn 9 (a Greater Trinket), each with a Gold cost. They give a lasting passive effect, and each offer includes at least one trinket for your most common minion type.

**Dark Gifts (Season 14).** From turn 3, a Dark Gift button costs 3 Gold. It Discovers one of three minions, each paired with a different Dark Gift (a lasting enhancement). You can use it once per turn and three times per game, and waiting improves the offers.

**Retired for now.** Anomalies, the Timewarped Tavern, Quests and Buddies are not in the current game.

**Existing tribes' core mechanics.**
- **Beast:** Deathrattles that summon tokens; buffs from summoning; Fishbait (Beasts attacking during the Recruit Phase).
- **Demon:** Eating Tavern minions for their stats and spending hero Health; Fodder minions in the Tavern for Demons to eat.
- **Dragon:** Stacking stat buffs on Dragons, Battlecry/Start of Combat/Rally; Chromadrakes (made by specific cards) that improve Tavern Spells.
- **Elemental:** Buffing Elementals that are still in the Tavern; rewards for playing Elementals.
- **Mech:** Magnetic upgrades (including Volumizer tokens), Divine Shield, and Deathrattles.
- **Murloc:** Battlecries and their repeats, Venomous, buffing Murlocs in hand and on board.
- **Pirate:** Gold: making, spending and counting it; Lockbox.
- **Quilboar:** Blood Gems.
- **Undead:** Avenge, Reborn, and permanent Attack buffs to all Undead.
- **Aberration:** Discard synergies feeding a **Deity** (C'Thun or Y'Shaarj, one per game) that joins combat after 4 friendly Aberration deaths.

## Part C: tier guide

A short guide to what each tier is for and roughly how much a unit there gives. It was built from 310 Battlegrounds units valued by role and by stats added per turn. It's a guide, not a limit: a unit can break it if that makes the tribe more fun and you say why. Its starting shape is written for a 19-unit tribe; you have 24, so scale it up.

### What each tier is for

| Tier | Job | Roles it usually holds (Battlegrounds today) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A body that wins the early fights, or a small seed a later engine grows from | tempo, combat engine; a few buffers, scalers and economy |
| 2 | The first buffers and economy; a direction starts | buffer (about 40%), economy, enabler, combat engine |
| 3 | Enablers that name your composition: they make what the tribe feeds on | enabler, economy, combat engine, buffer |
| 4 | "A strong player in a composition that sets up future things, enables, gives you some stats" | buffer, economy, enabler, plus the first multipliers |
| 5 | "Strategic direction or a lot of tempo": the card that says what this game's build is, or holds the line while you find it | buffer (about 40%), combat engine, economy, multipliers |
| 6 | "A capstone … a pivotal engine piece or a big scaling payoff": the comp is built to reach it | capstone, payoff, enabler, multiplier |

**A 19-unit tribe (2 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 3 / 3), as a starting shape:**
- **Tier 1:** one tempo body and one seed.
- **Tier 2:** two buffers or enablers and one economy piece.
- **Tier 3:** two enablers, one buffer, and one combat engine or economy piece.
- **Tier 4:** one or two buffers, one enabler, and one multiplier or economy piece.
- **Tier 5:** one direction card (a payoff or a strong buffer), one combat engine, and one tempo piece or multiplier.
- **Tier 6:** one or two capstones and one payoff.

Change the shape when the tribe's idea needs it, and say why.

**Three rules of thumb:**
1. **The tier is paid for in text, not body.** Printed stats rise only about ×1.26 a tier (median 4.5 at Tier 1, 12.5 at Tier 6).
2. **Output roughly doubles each tier from Tier 3.**
3. **The same job one tier up gives about three times as much,** through more triggers or more recipients rather than a bigger number per trigger. Examples:
   - Gatekeeper Amalgam (T6) gives the same +4/+4 packet as Lightfang (T5), four times a turn instead of once: 160 against 40.
   - Brann (T5) repeats every Battlecry, where Kelp Keeper (T4) repeats one: +72 against +24.
   - Nadina (T6) gives three shields, where Glowscale (T5) gives one: 120 against 40 a fight.

### The curve, as ratios

**The unit of measure is a body:** the median printed stats (Attack + Health) of a vanilla unit at the same tier. Every target below is in bodies, so it moves with the scale.

The targets are for a normal (non-golden) copy in a good setup, meaning the tribe's composition is mostly assembled, around two-thirds of the way through a game. **Permanent** stats stay on the board. **In fight** is stat value inside one fight, starting again each fight. **Once** is a one-off permanent buff. A unit usually gives one of the three.

| Tier | Body grows (vs T1) | Permanent / turn | Or in fight | Or once | Ceiling with goldens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0 | ~0.4 body | ~0.6 | ~1 | ×2 |
| 2 | 1.3 | 0.3–1 body | ~1 | ~1.3 | ×2 |
| 3 | 1.6 | ~0.8 body | 0.7–1.4 | ~1–1.4 | ×2 |
| 4 | 2.0 | ~1.3 bodies | ~2 | ~2 | ×2–4 |
| 5 | 2.2 | ~2 bodies (top ~5) | ~4 | ~2.5 | ×2–4 |
| 6 | 2.8 | ~4 bodies (top ~10) | ~6 | ~6 | ×2–4 |

**The curve in one line:** bodies grow about ×1.2 a tier, while what a unit adds grows about ×2 a tier from Tier 3, so by Tier 6 a unit adds about four of its own bodies a turn.

- **Goldens.** One golden key piece usually doubles its effect. Two golden key pieces, or a golden multiplier (a golden Brann triggers three times), double it again. The owner (12 Nov): "With a single golden unit, you can start doubling things, and you get multiple copies of goldens, you can start doubling things." The Battlegrounds values were measured on normal copies, so their top end is understated. Cost the normal copy against the table, and check that the golden ceiling is a high roll worth chasing, not a runaway.
- **Economy.** Price gold against what it buys at that stage: one gold is worth the share of a unit purchase it pays for (in Battlegrounds, a third of a unit).
- **Well outside** means about twice the tier's target, or half of it, with no reason written down. Flag those and propose a change that keeps the card's core idea.
- **Battlegrounds' own numbers.** The bodies are 4.5 / 6 / 7 / 9 / 10 / 12.5 stats at Tiers 1–6. Median permanent growth a turn is 2 / 2 / 5.5 / 12 / 20 / 53, and in fight it is 2.5 / 6 / 4.5 / 20 / 42 / 80.
