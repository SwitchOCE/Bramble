# Ogres: the Crown

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: the Crown.** Among the Gordunni, anyone may challenge for the throne and the winner becomes king. So the tribe works through one shared crown that moves around your board.

- **The Crown.** At most one friendly minion is **Crowned**. It has the **Crown's bonus** on top of its own stats, shown as a separate gold chip. The Crown appears with +1/+1 the first time an Ogre effect crowns a minion or pays Tribute. If you have no Crowned minion at that moment, your highest-Attack minion gets it.
- **Succession.** When the Crowned minion dies, is sold or leaves the board, the Crown **passes** to your highest-Attack minion (ties go to the leftmost), and the bonus goes with it. Each move by any means counts as "the Crown passes".
- **Tribute +X/+Y:** give the Crown's bonus +X/+Y. Tribute in the Recruit Phase is permanent. Tribute during combat lasts for that combat only.
- **Challenge:** when you play this minion, it takes the Crown.
- **Usurp:** triggers whenever this minion takes the Crown, in the Recruit Phase or in combat.
- **Crown a minion:** move the Crown to it.

**How it plays.** You choose one minion to be king and pour stats into a bonus that belongs to the throne, not to any one body. When the king falls in combat, the whole bonus jumps to the next in line, so stats stored in the Crown get used several times in one fight. Ogre cards do five jobs:
- **Make or move the Crown:** Challenge, crowning, succession rules.
- **Grow it:** Tribute.
- **Convert it:** into gold, out of a minion, or back into a minion.
- **Pay off on it:** while Crowned, and whenever the Crown passes.
- **Multiply it:** a second Crown, double triggers, Tribute shared out.

The Crown can sit on any minion, so the best king is often from another tribe: a Venomous Murloc, a Reborn Undead, a Divine Shield Mech. Each pass is also a trigger, so a Johnny player can treat a dying king, a sale or a cheap Activate as fuel, not loss.

Rules notes:
- Combat runs on a copy of your board, so after combat the Crown is back where you left it in the Recruit Phase. Dentarg uses this: one king for the shop, another for the fight.
- "Tribute its Attack and Health" uses the minion's own stats, not the Crown's bonus.
- Effects that read "the new Crowned minion" also see Cho'gall's second Crown.
- Gruul is a gronn, typed as an Ogre because the clans of Blade's Edge worship him.

**Extras (kept to two):**
- **Tavern Spell: Challenge for the Throne** (Tier 2, 1 Gold). Crown a friendly minion. Tribute +1/+1. It is the cheap way to make a pass in the Recruit Phase.
- **Lesser Trinket: Tribute Chest** (cost 3). At the start of your turn, gain 1 Gold for every 8 Attack on the Crown's bonus, up to 2.

## 3. Builds

**1. One tall king (Spike's line).** Pile on Tribute with Tithe-Taker, Fence, Envoy and Kor'gall, add Food-Taster and Mar'gok, and keep the king alive with Divine Shield (Mechs), Taunt or Reborn. It stores stats in one place, then uses them again when the king dies. This is the plain, strong line, and it holds the floor for Spike.

**2. Churn the throne (Johnny's main line).** In this build every pass is the resource:
- Fuel: Squabbler, Pit Boss, Butcher and Gruul.
- Passes in the Recruit Phase: Usurper, Challenge for the Throne, selling the king (that also triggers Fence), and Pretender killing the king.
- Multiply: Phemos and Pol doubles every pass.
- Lawgiver aims succession down the line, so a left-side king that attacks first and dies hands the Crown to the next attacker.
- In combat, Beast tokens, Reborn Undead and cheap kings who die early give extra passes.

**3. The puppet king.** Crown a non-Ogre carry: a Windfury Dragon with Rally, a Venomous Murloc, Brann, a Reborn Undead. Mog'dorg swaps a fat Demon or Elemental's stats into the Crown, so they survive the body. Cartel Bodyguard wears the Crown in the shop for gold, and Dentarg hands it to the fighter at Start of Combat.

**4. Two heads (Cho'gall).** One Crown goes to your strongest minion and one to your weakest, because the heads disagree. It pairs with:
- Taunt walls: a small Taunt minion carrying the full bonus.
- Squabbler and Pit Boss: twice the passes.
- Lawgiver: it applies only to the first Crown, so you steer one head and the other goes its own way.

**Off-label uses Johnny can find:**
- Pretender destroys a Deathrattle king in the Recruit Phase. Beast summons, Mech drops and Shackled Brawler's Tribute then fire and stay.
- Brann with Pretender destroys two kings in a row.
- Mog'dorg swapped onto a 1/1 dumps the whole Crown onto one body before a pivot.
- Treasurer turns surplus Tribute into Pirate gold.
- Fence plus Demon fodder or Beast tokens turns selling into growth.
- Envoy pays you to keep one minion of every type.
- Food-Taster plus Mar'gok makes every Tribute a board buff.

**In a five-tribe lobby.** Ogres are a frame other tribes plug into.
- Mechs and Undead protect or revive the king.
- Beasts and Aberrations make deaths that become passes.
- Demons, Elementals and Quilboar build big bodies to swap or Pretend into the Crown. Blood Gems stay on the body, so a gemmed king keeps its gems when the Crown leaves.
- Pirates spend Treasurer and Bodyguard gold.
- Murlocs double Kingmaker and Pretender.

A pure-Ogre board works (Kor'gall, Butcher and Gruul are real bodies). The deepest builds still mix in two other types.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Pricing the carry.** Crown stats get used about 1.5–2 times a fight, because each heir gets the full bonus fresh. All Tribute numbers here are set at about half a normal buff on that assumption. If playtests show kings rarely die, Tribute is underpriced the other way. Fallback: the bonus loses half its Health on each pass in combat.
- **Rule load.** Crown, Tribute, Challenge and Usurp are four new terms. The UI must show the Crown as a separate gold chip and animate every pass, or opponents can't read the fight.
- **Clashing succession rules.** Lawgiver, Cho'gall's second Crown and Dentarg all move the Crown. They need a fixed priority: Cho'gall's Crown follows its own rule, Lawgiver beats highest-Attack, and Dentarg acts once at Start of Combat.
- **Pass runaway.** Recruit passes cost gold or a card, and Activate is once a turn, but golden Gruul with golden Phemos and Pol and Cho'gall reaches about 200 stats a turn. That is at the ×4 ceiling, and the combination needs watching.
- **Splash, never core.** Because the best king is often a non-Ogre, Ogre bodies may end up as support you sell off. Kor'gall, Butcher and Ringmaster exist to keep Ogres on the final board.
- **Solved combo.** Cartel Bodyguard with Dentarg may become an automatic pick. If so, make the Bodyguard pay only on turns its Crown has at least +4 Attack.
- **Weak early Crown.** With only Challenger and Squabbler, Tier 1 Ogres are thin bodies. Their early wins come from the +1/+1 Crown plus Usurp, which is narrow.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** one tempo body (Challenger, effectively 4/4 on play) and two seeds. Tithe-Taker gives about 0.6 bodies a turn after the carry, above the 0.4 target on purpose, because an early Crown with no bonus does nothing. I kept the body low (1/3) to pay for it.
- **Tier 2:**
  - Two buffers (Kingmaker at about 1 body once, Fence at about 0.5–1 a turn), one economy piece (Bodyguard, 1 Gold) and one enabler (Brawler, about 1 body in a fight). This fits the guide.
- **Tier 3:**
  - Treasurer is economy.
  - Usurper and Lawgiver are the enablers that name the comp.
  - Pit Boss is a combat engine.
  - Brewmaster is a buffer at about 6 permanent stats, near the target of 5.5.
  - Usurper's own growth is low (about 2 a turn) on purpose: its value is the pass it makes.
- **Tier 4:**
  - Envoy and Food-Taster are buffers at about 9–12 a turn.
  - Ringmaster is a combat engine at about 20 in a fight.
  - Pretender is a converter.
  - Dentarg is an enabler.
  - Food-Taster is the first multiplier, as the guide asks.
- **Tier 5:**
  - Butcher is a combat engine at about 30–40 in a fight.
  - Phemos and Pol is a multiplier.
  - Mog'dorg is the direction card: it decides what feeds the Crown.
  - Kor'gall is tempo. Its once-buff (+5/+5 into the Crown, about 15) is under the 25 target, but the body is above it and Usurp fires again when it inherits.
  - Strong buffers are thinner here than the guide's 40%. I broke that so Tier 5 could hold the tribe's conversions.
- **Tier 6:**
  - Mar'gok is the scaling capstone at about 60 a turn.
  - Cho'gall is the multiplier capstone: it doubles the Crown in a fight.
  - Gruul is the payoff at about 40 a turn, rising with the pass engine.
  - Bodies are 14–16, a little above the 12.5 median, because their text pays off only with the Crown built up.

## 6. Design rationale

1. **One moving stat pool as the core.** No current tribe stores stats on a role instead of a body, and moving it creates triggers that Johnny can chain.
2. **Succession goes to the highest Attack.** Might makes right, and it is deterministic, so Spike can plan around it and Johnny can manipulate it.
3. **Five categories: make or move, grow, convert, pay off, multiply.** Each one is a separate lever, so cards combine across categories instead of stacking one number.
4. **Shape 3/4/5/5/4/3.** This is the guide's shape scaled up to 24, with the extra slots in the middle tiers where the enablers and converters live.
5. **"The Crown passes" is the key trigger.** Deaths, sales, Activates, spells and battlecries all make passes, so cards meant for one use work for others.
6. **Tribute priced at about half.** The carry reuses the stats, and doing it this way keeps Spike's balance honest.
7. **Converters both ways (gold, swap, regicide, Deathrattle).** Being able to empty, fill and cash out the Crown is where off-label play comes from.
8. **Recruit-phase passes cost gold or a card.** That caps the churn engine without a hard limit.
9. **Shop king and fight king can differ (Dentarg, Bodyguard).** It turns the "combat runs on a copy" rule into a decision.
10. **Rule-changers at Tiers 3 and 6 (Lawgiver, Cho'gall).** Changing who inherits is the most Johnny thing the system can do.
11. **The Crown fits any minion.** That makes Ogres a frame for a five-tribe lobby, with Envoy rewarding the mix.
12. **Lore last.** Gordok's tribute run became Pretender, Cho'gall's two heads became two Crowns that disagree, Phemos and Pol became double triggers, and Highmaul's arena became the combat engines.
