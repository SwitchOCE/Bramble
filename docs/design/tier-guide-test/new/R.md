# Ogres

## 1. Identity

**New keyword: Challenge.** *Start of Combat: this attacks the enemy minion with the highest Attack, ignoring Taunt.* A Challenge is a real attack: it triggers Rally, and the challenger takes damage back as normal. Challenges resolve left to right, after hero-power and other Start of Combat effects. A minion **wins its Challenge** if its target dies from that attack. It doesn't matter whether the challenger survives, because permanent buffs to a minion that dies in combat still stay.

**New rules term: Chieftain.** Your Chieftain is your minion with the highest Attack, of any type, and the game marks it with a crown. On a tie, the current Chieftain keeps the crown. A new minion becomes Chieftain only by having strictly more Attack. This applies in the Recruit Phase and in combat. When the Chieftain dies in combat, the crown passes to the next minion in line.

**Card frame: Two-Headed.** A Two-Headed minion has two texts, Left and Right. Only one head is awake each turn, and the awake head swaps at the start of your turn. Left wakes first.

**How the tribe plays.** Ogres play around the biggest minion on each board. Their Challenges pick fights with the enemy's biggest minion, and their buffs flow up to your own biggest. Most Ogre payoffs fire when a Challenge wins, when the Chieftain grows, or when the crown changes hands, and none of those needs the minion involved to be an Ogre. A Venomous Murloc with Challenge is an assassin. A Divine Shield Mech with Challenge takes the hit for free. A Deathrattle Beast or Aberration with Challenge and low Attack dies on purpose at Start of Combat, which fires its Deathrattle first and counts toward a Deity or an Avenge. The Chieftain can be your Dragon or your Undead carry, with Ogres as its hired muscle. So the tribe's choices are about which minion to crown, whom to send into the arena, and when to pass the crown on. That makes the tribe as much a toolkit for other tribes as a tribe of its own.

- **Settle It** (Tavern spell, 1 Gold, offered only when Ogres are in the lobby): swap the awake head of a Two-Headed minion.

## 3. Builds

**The Arena (Challenge and wins).** The cards are Brute, Enforcer, Bookmaker, Clubsmith, Prizefighter, Crowd-Pleaser, Ogre Magi (Left), The Butcher, Phemos and Pol, and Mar'gok. You send several challengers at the enemy's biggest minion. The first ones soften it and a later one wins, so challenger order is a real Recruit-Phase decision. Clubsmith makes each Challenge hit harder, and Phemos and Pol go on to the next-biggest minion. Mar'gok turns every win into team-wide permanent stats. With other tribes:
- **Murlocs:** Venomous challengers win once per combat against anything.
- **Mechs:** Divine Shield challengers take the return hit for free.
- **Dragons and other Rally units:** Challenge triggers Rally one extra time.

**The Crown (one Chieftain, fed by the clan).** The cards are Mound Cook, Tax-Taker, Tribute-Bearer, Altar-Stone Magus, Grog-Brewer, Maulgar, Kromcrush and Gruul. Ogres feed every end-of-turn buff to the Chieftain, and that can be a Dragon, an Undead or an Elemental carry. Cartel Bodyguard pays 2 Gold a turn for guarding a non-Ogre king, while Grog-Brewer wants more Ogres on the board, so the build pulls two ways. Once Kromcrush or Gruul gives the Chieftain Challenge, it hunts the enemy's biggest minion. Gruul then takes half of what it kills: the Dragonkiller eats the other player's carry.

**Usurpers (passing the crown on purpose).** The cards are King Gordok, Kingmaker, Tribute Chest, Cho'Rush, and selling your own Chieftain. Gordok pays +5/+5 each time the crown changes hands, so you leapfrog two or three minions. You buff the runner-up past the king, sell the old king so the heir is crowned, or use Kingmaker to swap Attack from a glass cannon onto a Divine Shield Taunt or a Windfury minion. In combat, Cho'Rush and Gordok both fire each time a Chieftain dies, so every death crowns a stronger heir.

**The Twilight Council (Two-Headed and end-of-turn effects).** The cards are Tax-Taker, Altar-Stone Magus, Ogre Magi and Cho'gall, plus the Settle It spell. A trick outside the intended use: on the Right-head turn, Tax-Taker pays its Gold at the start of the turn. Settle It then wakes Left before the end of the turn, so you get the Gold and the buff on the same turn. Cho'gall wakes both heads and repeats every end-of-turn effect on the board, including other tribes' effects.

**In a five-tribe lobby.** Ogres don't need many Ogres. Enforcer, Kromcrush, Ogre Magi (Right) and Gruul hand Challenge to other tribes' minions, and every Crown card pays off a non-Ogre carry. Ogres also change how everyone plays. Challenges ignore Taunt and go for the biggest minion, so the counterplay is a high-Attack decoy: a Reborn or Divine Shield minion, or a Deathrattle body you are happy to lose. That gives non-Ogre players a positioning decision they don't have today. With an Aberration Deity in the lobby, low-Attack challengers count up its four deaths at Start of Combat.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Feels-bad removal.** A Challenge can delete a carry before it swings. The decoy counterplay above softens it, but losing your carry at Start of Combat for the first time will feel unfair until players learn it. Watch for Venomous plus Challenge becoming the default answer to every scaling comp.
- **The Arena build fades late.** Late-game carries with 100+ Health are hard to kill even with chained Challenges, so win-based payoffs (Bookmaker, Prizefighter, Mar'gok) depend on what the opponent fields. Ghost fights and decoy-aware players make that worse. Clubsmith and the chaining are the patch. If late wins stay rare, Mar'gok should pay on any Challenge, smaller.
- **Multiplier stacking.** Cho'gall (end-of-turn effects again) with Maulgar (second recipient) and a few Crown feeders is about ×4 on Chieftain buffs. Golden Cho'gall makes it about ×6. This is the runaway risk and should be the first cap tested, for example by limiting Gall's repeat to Ogres' effects.
- **Kingmaker anti-synergy.** Brann repeats the Battlecry and swaps the Attack straight back. It's funny, but it's a trap card to note in tooltips. Kingmaker adds no stats, which keeps Spike safe, but moving 80 Attack onto a Windfury or Cleave body needs a check.
- **Readability.** Players have to read a live crown during combat, plus the tie rule. Challenge order also has to be shown at Start of Combat, or wins will look random.
- **Three sub-themes in 24 cards.** Two-Headed is only four cards plus a spell, so it can be hard to find. It lives off Crown and Arena text so that it is never dead.
- **Gladiator economy.** 1 Gold almost every turn from a Tier 1 card may be strong in the early game. A fallback is to pay only if the Gladiator wins or dies in its own Challenge.
- **Taunt loses value** in Ogre lobbies. That's by design, but Mechs and Undead lean on Taunt.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1 (3):** One tempo body (Brute, 5 stats plus a keyword), one seed (Mound Cook, +2 stats a turn, about 0.45 body) and one economy piece (Gladiator, a small body paid for in text). This matches the guide.
- **Tier 2 (4):** Two buffers (Bookmaker, 2–4 a turn; Tribute-Bearer, about 6 once plus Gold back), one enabler (Enforcer) and one economy piece (Tax-Taker, alternating 4 stats and 1 Gold). This matches the guide, with the bridge card set this early on purpose.
- **Tier 3 (5):** Two enablers (Kromcrush, Clubsmith), one buffer (Altar-Stone, about 6 a turn), one combat engine (Cho'Rush, 6–12 in fight) and one economy piece (Prizefighter). This matches the guide, scaled up to five cards.
- **Tier 4 (5):** Two buffers (Crowd-Pleaser 8–12, Grog-Brewer about 12), two enablers (Kingmaker, Ogre Magi at about 12) and one economy piece (Bodyguard). Two enablers is a break on purpose, because crown-passing needs its own tool and it adds no stats.
- **Tier 5 (4):** Direction (Gordok, 10–30 a turn), combat engine (Butcher, about 40 in fight), tempo (Phemos and Pol) and multiplier (Maulgar, about ×2). This matches the guide.
- **Tier 6 (3):** Three capstones, one per loop: Gruul (about 30–60 a turn), Mar'gok (about 42 a turn) and Cho'gall (a multiplier). Gruul's top end is the enemy's carry, so it's watched under Risks.

## 6. Design rationale

1. **Gap:** Combat targeting is random, and the only answer to one giant carry is Venomous. No tribe picks its fights or interacts with the opponent's board on purpose.
2. **Gap:** "Make one minion huge" payoffs exist only inside a tribe. Nothing rewards your biggest minion whatever its type, and no one plays with *which* minion is biggest.
3. **Gap:** Modal cards and sequencing inside a turn are thin, since Choose One is rare. That gap got a small sub-theme only.
4. **Challenge fills gap 1.** It's a targeting tool that gives other tribes' keywords new uses (Venomous, Divine Shield, Deathrattle, Rally, Aberration deaths) and gives opponents a decoy decision.
5. **Chieftain fills gap 2.** It's a rules term, not a keyword, so any tribe's carry can wear the crown and Ogres act as mercenaries.
6. **"Wins" means the target dies,** whether or not the challenger survives. Permanent buffs still stay, so the rule reads simply and makes chained challengers (soften, then finish) a real decision.
7. **"Ties keep the crown"** was set before any Usurp card. It stops crown-change loops with Maulgar and Gordok.
8. **Kingmaker swaps Attack instead of adding it,** so crown-passing is pure Johnny play with no stats gained.
9. **Each loop has one multiplier:** Maulgar for the Crown, Phemos and Pol plus Clubsmith for the Arena, and Cho'gall for end-of-turn effects. So goldens double a loop instead of everything.
10. **Economy follows the loops:** Gold when a challenger dies (Gladiator), Gold when one wins (Prizefighter), Gold for guarding a non-Ogre king (Bodyguard, a deliberate pull against Grog-Brewer), and Gold from Two-Headed alternation.
11. **The shape is 3/4/5/5/4/3.** It's the 19-unit shape scaled up, with the extra cards at Tiers 1–4, where the bridge and enabler roles are.
12. **Names came last,** fitted to the slots. The Gordok court (Kromcrush, Cho'Rush, the King) carries the crown, the Highmaul arena carries the Challenge, and the Altars of Storms carry the two heads.
