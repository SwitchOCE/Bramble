# Ogres

## 1. Identity

**The fantasy.** Among ogres, might makes right. The biggest brute wears the crown, everyone else pays him tribute, and every ogre is watching for the moment to knock him down and take it. Ogres also hire out as muscle to anyone strong enough to pay. And now and then an ogre is born with two heads that never agree.

**Core mechanic: the Chieftain.**
- **Chieftain:** your minion with the highest Attack, of any type, shown with a crown. It is checked again after every effect and every death. On a tie, the current Chieftain keeps the crown. If none of the tied minions holds it, the leftmost takes it.
- **Heir:** the minion that would be Chieftain if the Chieftain were gone.
- **Tribute +X/+Y:** give your Chieftain +X/+Y. Tribute in the Recruit Phase is permanent. Tribute in combat lasts for that combat only, unless the card says otherwise.
- **Usurp (new keyword):** triggers when the crown passes to this minion from another minion, whether that minion died, lost Attack or was overtaken. It does not trigger at the start of combat or when this is your only minion. Each minion's Usurp triggers at most once per Recruit turn and once per combat.
- **Two-Headed (new keyword):** the card has a **Brute** head and a **Cunning** head. They take turns each time the effect triggers, starting with Brute. A golden Two-Headed minion triggers both heads every time. If an effect is repeated (a doubled Deathrattle, Reborn, and so on), the second trigger uses the other head.

**How the tribe plays.** Ogres don't need to be the strongest minion on your board. They feed whoever is. Most Ogres send stats to the Chieftain, and that can be your Dragon, your Demon or your Deity. The rest of the tribe is about who holds the crown. You can steer it with Grog, sell the king to pass his strength on, set your own minions fighting each other, and line up heirs whose Usurp effects fire when the king falls mid-combat. One Ogre player builds one huge king with Taunt-proof succession behind him. Another builds a fragile king on purpose, so that his death sets off a chain of Usurps. A third uses the two-headed ogres, whose alternating heads pay off with any effect that triggers twice.

**Token (Tavern spell)**
- **Gordok Green Grog** (0 Gold): Give a minion +4 Attack until the end of your turn. End-of-turn effects still see the Attack, and it wears off after them. Only Ogre cards make it, and you can pass it to your teammate in Duos.

**Trinkets**
- **Gordok Tribute Chest** (Lesser, 4 Gold): At the end of your turn, Tribute +1/+1 for each Ogre you control.
- **Highmaul Sigil** (Greater, 6 Gold): Your minions' Usurp can trigger twice each combat.

## 3. Builds

**A. One King (Tribute stacking).** Pick a carry from any tribe and feed it. Gronn-Worshipper, Shakedown, Initiate and Glutton scale it every turn. Kromcrush, King Gordok and Maulgar answer Venomous and destroy effects, so that losing the king doesn't lose the fight. The Butcher turns one giant attacker into a sweeper. Gronn-Worshipper pays more when the king *isn't* an Ogre, which pushes you to crown a Dragon or a Demon. This is the steady, Spike-friendly line.

**B. Succession (Usurp chain).** Build a king that is meant to fall: a high-Attack, low-Health minion, or one with Reborn or a Deathrattle worth triggering. Behind him, line up heirs in Attack order: Gog, Ko'ragh, Challenger and Upstart. Each death passes the crown and fires the next Usurp. Kromcrush shields every new king, and King Gordok hands each one half of the last king's stats. Imperator Mar'gok fires every Usurp at Start of Combat as well, so Gog kills the enemy's biggest minion before anyone attacks. Gordunni Challenger is the fuse. It attacks your own king at Start of Combat, which can kill him, pop his Divine Shield or trigger his Deathrattle on your timing.

**C. Grog games (crown steering in the Recruit Phase).** Grog is the Johnny tool. One Grog does several jobs:
- It crowns a minion for the turn, so it Usurps, and so do Brewmaster (more Grog) and Upstart.
- It sends that turn's end-of-turn Tribute to the minion you choose.
- Kromcrush gives each minion it crowns a lasting Divine Shield.
- When it wears off, the crown goes back to the old king, which can trigger *his* Usurp too.
- Sold to Glopgut Glutton, the grogged minion pays its inflated Attack as Tribute.
- It raises your Chieftain's Attack just before Dire Maul Champion's challenge.

**D. Two Heads.** Bruiser, Initiate, Shakedown, Phemos and Pol and Cho'gall, with Cho'Rush paying off every head that triggers. Anything that repeats an effect gives you both heads: Beast and Mech Deathrattle doublers on Bruiser, Reborn, and golden copies. Shakedown on alternate turns works like a Pirate gold engine. Cho'gall makes the whole package fire both heads at once.

**Mixing in a five-tribe lobby.** Ogres are mercenaries, so most of their power goes to other tribes' carries.
- **Dragon, Demon:** natural kings. Dragons stack stats, and Demons grow by eating, so they stay crowned.
- **Elemental:** Elementals buff the Tavern, and Dire Maul Champion then eats the buffed minion whole.
- **Undead:** a Reborn king makes Challenger and the Succession build work twice. Challenger's death also counts toward Avenge.
- **Mech:** Kromcrush gives Divine Shields, and Challenger pops your own shields to trigger Mech shield payoffs.
- **Beast:** Mok'nathal Outrider turns token swarms into Tribute, and Deathrattle doublers trigger both of Bruiser's heads.
- **Aberration:** Devotee and Challenger deaths count toward the Deity, and a Deity that arrives mid-combat becomes the Chieftain and Usurps the crown for the Old Gods.
- **Murloc:** Battlecry repeats double the Thug, Sellsword and Champion. A Venomous Murloc king with The Butcher may hit three minions at once (see Risks).
- **Pirate:** Shakedown's gold feeds Pirate gold counting.
- **Quilboar:** Blood Gems on the king stack with Tribute.

Dunemaul Sellsword rewards boards that mix the most types.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Tall-board fragility.** Build A loses to Venomous, destroy effects and Taunt walls. The fixes (Kromcrush, Gordok, Maulgar) sit at Tiers 4–6, so the build may be weak in the midgame.
- **The tribe may be support for other tribes rather than a tribe of its own.** Tribute buffs any king, so the best result may be three Ogres splashed into a Dragon board. Only Ko'ragh, Cho'gall and the Lesser trinket reward a board full of Ogres. If that proves too weak, more "your Ogres" payoffs are needed.
- **Dire Maul Champion + Elementals** could take +30/+30 in one Battlecry. It may need a cap, for example only the Tavern minion's base stats.
- **Grog + Glutton** turns each 0-Gold Grog into +4 permanent Attack, at the cost of selling a minion. Watch Brewmaster Usurp loops that supply many Grogs.
- **Gog + Mar'gok (golden)** destroys the enemy's two biggest minions twice at Start of Combat. That may be oppressive at Tier 6.
- **The Butcher + Venomous:** does a cleave count as the "first minion damaged"? The rule has to be explicit (proposed: only the main target).
- **Tracking.** The crown moves constantly in combat. The game needs a clear crown icon and a visible Heir marker, and players need a predictable rule for when ties keep the crown. Usurp's per-turn limit is extra rules text that Spike must learn.
- **Two-Headed is thin.** Five cards plus one payoff, so Cho'Rush is often dead in the Tavern. The head each card is on must be shown on it, or the alternation is guesswork.
- **Gronn-Worshipper** pays more for a non-Ogre king, which rewards *fewer* Ogres. That is on purpose for mixing, but it works against the tribe as a tribe.
- **Stonemaul Bodyguard** and **Twilight's Hammer Devotee** are plain filler and may be weak.
- **Untested points:** how Sellsword counts "All"-type minions, and whether the start-of-turn gold from Shakedown is too strong at Tier 3.

## 5. Design rationale

1. Started from "might makes right": the strongest minion wears the crown, which became the Chieftain, chosen by Attack because Attack is how ogres measure strength.
2. The Chieftain can be any type, because ogres are mercenaries serving stronger masters, which makes the tribe mix by nature.
3. Tribute gives stats to the Chieftain, from the lore of tribute and greed. It is the tribe's basic engine.
4. Usurp came from challenges for the crown. The crown changing hands, not holding it, is the event that the Johnny combos turn on.
5. Ties keep the current Chieftain, so that reordering the board can't trigger Usurp for free.
6. Usurp is limited to once per turn and once per combat, so that crown flip-flop loops stay bounded for balance.
7. The Heir and the succession cards (Kromcrush, Gordok, Maulgar) come from Dire Maul's "kill the king, become the king". They also answer the tall build's weakness to Venomous.
8. Gordok Green Grog and the Glutton came from grog and appetite. They turned into the Recruit Phase tools for steering the crown, each with several uses beyond its obvious one.
9. Gordunni Challenger, from the gladiator arena: an ogre that fights its own king, so that triggering your own Deathrattles, shields and deaths is a deliberate tool.
10. Two heads that disagree became the Brute and Cunning heads that take turns, so any repeated effect pays both, and the golden version is simply "both heads".
11. Named figures went where the lore puts them: Gordok's court at Tiers 4–5, Gog's gronn-slaying as a Usurp kill, and Mar'gok and Cho'gall as the masters of the Usurp and Two-Headed engines at Tier 6.
12. Dual types (Mok'nathal as Beast, Twilight's Hammer as Aberration) and the two trinkets were kept few and taken straight from the lore links.
