# Ogres

## 1. Identity

**New keyword: Devour.** *Destroy a friendly minion with less Attack than this. This permanently gains its Attack and Health.*

Rules that matter:
- **Only smaller prey.** The target must have strictly less Attack than the Devourer. The check uses current Attack, including temporary Attack.
- **It is a real death.** A Devour in the Recruit Phase fires the eaten minion's Deathrattle and Reborn. Anything they summon or give **stays**, because no combat ends to undo it.
- **No Gold.** You never get the 1 Gold you would have got for selling. The slot is freed either way.
- Most Devourers carry **Activate (0): Devour.** Activate is the existing keyword, so it is free and once per turn.

**How the tribe plays.** Every Battlegrounds player reaches the moment where a minion is obsolete and gets sold for 1 Gold. Ogres offer a better rate that doesn't help you right now: put that minion's stats onto an Ogre, and turn its combat-only Deathrattle into a permanent one, at the cost of a body and the Gold. Each turn becomes an ordering puzzle. Which Ogre eats, in what order, so that its Attack climbs past the next meal? Is a Deathrattle minion worth more fighting every combat, or eaten once for keeps? Do you want seven bodies, or four huge ones? Ogres treat every other tribe's minions as ingredients. A Beast whose Deathrattle summons tokens, an Undead with Reborn, or an Elemental fattened in the Tavern can each be eaten for something its own tribe never planned.

## 3. Builds

**A. Deathrattle harvest (wide).** Core: Ritualist, Fattened Glopgut, Mog'dorg, any Devourer. Eat a Glopgut in the Recruit Phase and every Ogre gets +2/+2 for good, twice with the Ritualist. Mog'dorg hands back a 1/1 Glopgut to eat again next turn, so a one-shot meal becomes +4/+4 to the team every turn. The Pitfighter lets non-Ogre boards use the Ritualist too. It kills your own Deathrattle minions without needing an Ogre big enough to eat them. **Mixes with:** Beasts (eaten token-summoners leave permanent tokens, which are then food), Mechs (Deathrattle Mechs and Magnetic stats on cheap bodies), Undead (Reborn minions get eaten twice: once whole, once as the 1-Health return).

**B. One big Ogre.** Core: Trophy-Taker, Gruul, Gorgrond Ogron, Overlord, Goader. Pile everything onto one or two Ogres: Divine Shield from a Mech, Venomous from a Murloc, Windfury from anything. Then Gruul eats every smaller enemy it targets, and Divine Shield doesn't save them. Run four or five minions and let the Ogron's empty-space bonus make up for the missing bodies. **Mixes with:** Murlocs (Venomous, and hand buffs you then eat) and Quilboar (dump Blood Gems on a cheap minion, then eat it for concentrated stats). This build is weak to the enemy's once-per-combat Venomous and to "destroy" effects.

**C. Buffet economy.** Core: King Gordok, Tribute-Taker, Gnashers, Meat-Picker. Each Ogre eats once a turn straight from the Tavern, so refreshing finds you food, not purchases. The Tribute-Taker turns this turn's meals into next turn's Gold. **Mixes with:** Elementals. Their Tavern-buffing grows the Tavern minions Gordok then eats, so the Elemental player's main engine feeds your Ogres. Pirates help too, because extra Gold means more refreshes. This build competes with Demons for the same Tavern if both tribes are in the lobby.

**D. Stolen minds.** Core: Altar-Born Magus, Phemos and Pol, Bickering Magus. The Magus eats a minion of another tribe and keeps its text, which makes that text an Ogre's. Copy a non-Ogre Activate minion and Phemos and Pol now let you use it twice. Copy a Dragon's Rally onto a Windfury Ogre built by the Trophy-Taker. The golden Magus keeps two texts: one head for each.

**Threshold tools used across all builds.** The rule is "smaller prey only", so Attack-only effects are puzzle pieces. Green Grog lifts an Ogre past a meal for this turn. The Goader makes the next Ogre hungrier. The Bickering Magus turns a 3/14 Ogre into a 14/3 that can eat your biggest non-Ogre. It can also turn a 12/2 food minion into a 2/12, which is now edible and gives the same total stats. The Meat-Picker eats Attack only, so it climbs fastest. Its Health goes into a Leftovers you can drop on food that another Ogre will eat.

**In a five-tribe lobby.** Ogres are a glue tribe: they want other tribes' minions as ingredients more than they want an all-Ogre board. They are best with at least one Deathrattle tribe (Beast, Mech or Undead) or with Elementals. They are worst beside Demons (shared Tavern-eating, and Demons' eating already scales well) and in lobbies with heavy Venomous or Cleave. In Duos you can pass food to your teammate, and passing Leftovers lets one player's spare Health reach the other's carry.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Foreign Deathrattles turned permanent.** This is the tribe's biggest balance exposure. Every Deathrattle in the pool written as "combat value" needs an audit. Strong summon-Deathrattles may need a "Combat only" tag. Because the eaten card is used up, most are roughly fair: one card for its Deathrattle once.
- **Loops.** Mog'dorg plus a Deathrattle minion is the one repeatable engine (one meal per turn, so at most one copy). Mog'dorg plus Ritualist plus golden Glopgut needs testing. Gnasher golden, Trophy-Taker golden, Overlord and King Gordok golden all multiply each other's doubling. The doublings should probably add rather than multiply.
- **King Gordok overlaps Demons' identity.** If it feels too close, change it to "Devour from your hand".
- **Gruul ignores Divine Shield.** That might be too strong against Mech boards. Fallback: Divine Shield blocks the Devour.
- **Mar'gok and "All" types.** One All-type meal reaches the cap, about +18/+18 per Ogre (+36/+36 golden). That is a fair T6 goal, but it may come too reliably.
- **Avenge and Aberration Deity.** The design assumes Devours don't advance combat counters. If the Deity counter persists between combats, Devours would feed it, which needs a ruling.
- **Turn timer and clicks.** Five Activates a turn, plus target checks, is a lot of input. The UI must grey out targets that are too big to eat.
- **Weak early tempo.** Eating early costs bodies. Runt, Herder and Herdmaster are there to make Tiers 1–2 playable, but Ogres may simply be a scaling tribe that loses early health.
- **Pitfighter rules.** Recruit-phase damage needs a clear "heals at end of turn" rule. Popping your own Divine Shield in a fight should probably not be permanent.
- **Leftovers flexibility.** Leftovers can target any minion, so a non-Ogre player who takes the Stewpot trinket gets a strong neutral effect. It may need a cap.

## 5. Design rationale

1. I started from the sell decision: an obsolete minion is worth 1 Gold. I wanted a better-rate conversion (stats) that isn't the Gold you need now, so I made Devour.
2. Free full-stat eating made every choice obvious. "Only less Attack" turned it into an ordering puzzle.
3. That threshold made Attack-only and swap effects (Grog, Goader, Meat-Picker, Bickering Magus) into puzzle tools rather than plain buffs.
4. Asking "what happens to the eaten minion's Deathrattle?" gave the best Johnny hook: Recruit-phase deaths make combat Deathrattles permanent.
5. That became the shared mechanic: Ogres kill their own minions in the Recruit Phase. The Pitfighter and the Ritualist's "Recruit Phase death" wording widen it beyond Devour.
6. Remainders (from the Meat-Picker, the Butcher and the Stewpot) become a Leftovers spell, so unused stats are a resource you can spend elsewhere, not a loss.
7. I used Activate (0) rather than a second keyword. It reuses existing UI and lets Phemos and Pol and the Magus touch other tribes' Activates.
8. Food cards (Runt, Herder, Herdmaster, Glopgut) are good both alive and eaten, so keeping or eating them is a real choice.
9. Concentration costs bodies and damage, so the Ogron, Gruul and the Cauldron-Lord give the tribe a combat side that rewards being narrow.
10. Each capstone breaks one rule: Gordok changes where you eat, the Challenger and Gronn-Tooth the Attack limit, Mar'gok what eating counts for, and Gruul whom you eat.
11. The Tribute-Taker pays Gold next turn, which keeps the "better rate, but not now" tension inside the economy.
12. Names came last and were fitted to roles: the bickering two-headed magus swaps, the Butcher portions the meat, King Gordok takes tribute from the Tavern.
