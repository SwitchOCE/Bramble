# Mechanics ideas: creatures first

**Status: ideas for the owner, 2 November 2026. Nothing here is adopted.** Written in answer to the owner's ask for "explicit attempts to create different effects than just what exists in Hearthstone Battlegrounds" ([2026-11-02f note](../owner-notes/2026-11-02f-new-effects-and-goldfish-designers.md)), and the point that Battlegrounds keywords "are illustrations" ([2026-11-02e note](../sources/2026-11-02e-mechanics-are-examples.md)).

**The angle.** Start from what a woodland animal, fairy or ooze really does (or does in folklore), and find the mechanic that behaviour suggests. 18 mechanics, 17 creatures.

**Numbers are by feel.** The writer of this list did not see the power budget, on purpose. Every tier and stat line below is a guess for flavour and shape, to be costed by someone else. Tribe homes are suggestions against the longlist in [archetypes.md](../archetypes.md) §4.

**Close variants.** Three of the 18 are close to an existing keyword, and say so: Ride (Magnetic plus a death summon), Jink (Divine Shield) and Nocturnal (Galaxy's Sneak). The other 15 have no direct equivalent in Battlegrounds or Galaxy that this writer found.

Rule numbers are from [rulebook.md](../rulebook.md).

## 1. Curl (hedgehog)

**Rules.** *Curl: This doesn't attack, and it takes half damage (rounded down). Whenever anything damages it, the unit responsible takes 1 damage; when it is the last unit on its side, it uncurls and fights normally.*

**Bends.** 9.4.5: a unit with Attack chooses to sit out the attack order. 9.6.1 step 3: the damage exchange stops being equal.

**One finding about thorns.** In Battlegrounds every defender already hits back for its full Attack (9.6.1 step 3), so "damage back when attacked" is already the default. What is new is thorns against damage that *isn't* an attack: pings, splash, Start of Combat shots, random effects. Curl's prick fires on any damage, from any source. If the source isn't a unit (a hero power), a random enemy takes it.

**Creature.** A hedgehog doesn't fight or run; it rolls into a ball of spines and lets the predator hurt itself. The Folio's hedge-apothecaries "carry cures on their spines".

**Archetype.** STICKY: a wall that wins by making the enemy run out of attacks. It also punishes BURST and WIDE: half of 1 rounds to 0, so 1-Attack tokens can't scratch it and take a prick each.

**Example.** *Pincushion Hedgehog*, tier 2, 2/6. Curl. (Golden: pricks for 2.)

**Fun against.** It turns the enemy's best tools against them: the ping board that kills itself on spines, the token swarm that bounces off. The answer is clear and fair: one big hitter, since halving a 10 still lets 5 through. The uncurl clause stops two Curl boards from staring at each other until the fight ties (9.9.1).

## 2. Bury (red squirrel, jay)

**Rules.** *Bury: At the end of your turn, this buries a Nut in a random empty slot on your board (at most 3 Nuts on a board). The first unit to enter a Nut's slot eats it for +1/+1 permanently; a Nut nobody eats for 3 of your turns sprouts into a 1/2 Oakling in that slot.*

**Bends.** 8.1 and 8.3.1: a slot can hold an object, not only a unit, so a gap becomes a resource rather than a missing body. 9.1.1: a Nut eaten in combat is eaten only on the combat copy, so it is still there afterwards (proposal: that is intended, and makes a Nut gap a standing +1/+1 for whatever summon lands there each fight).

**Creature.** Red squirrels and jays scatter-hoard: they bury nuts in hundreds of places and forget many, and the forgotten ones grow into trees. The Folio's Acorn Exchange "forgets where a third of them went".

**Archetype.** WIDE (free bodies, food for summons) and ECO (a delayed free unit). It differs from the Hedgerow Acorns in archetypes.md §4.1: Acorns are cards in hand; Nuts live in slots on the board.

**Example.** *Hoarder Squirrel*, tier 1, 1/2. Bury.

**Fun against.** The decision belongs to the squirrel player: eat the Nut now for a small buff, or leave the gap open, weaker today, and get a free unit in three turns. Opponents see a board with holes in it that will fill on a known turn, so there is a window to punish it.

## 3. Burrow (mole)

**Rules.** *Burrow: Start of Combat: this goes underground. It leaves its slot and can't attack, be attacked or be hit by any effect; after the first friendly death, it surfaces in that unit's slot (or the nearest empty one) and attacks immediately.*

**Bends.** 9.1.2: every unit starts combat in its slot, and this one is in combat but on no slot. That is a new zone. 9.4.12: it attacks out of turn when it surfaces. Units are counted for who attacks first (9.2.1) before it digs, so the count isn't affected.

**Creature.** Moles live in tunnels and come up where you don't expect them; the molehill appears overnight. The Folio's "velvet conspirators" had a molehill topple a king.

**Archetype.** WIDE and STICKY: a refill that is safe from Start of Combat damage and arrives in the gap the first death leaves. Pairs with Deathrattle-style units: if the dead unit's own summon takes the slot (8.4.1), the mole takes the nearest one.

**Example.** *Velvet Sapper*, tier 3, 4/3. Burrow. When this surfaces, it has +3 Attack for that attack.

**Fun against.** You know the mole is coming (its slot is empty and a molehill shows), so the first kill is a choice: kill the small unit in front first and the mole meets your wounded attacker; let a ping kill one at Start of Combat and it surfaces before your units have hit anything.

## 4. Shiny (magpie)

**Rules.** *Shiny: This attacks enemy golden units if there are any, in either row. Whenever it damages a golden unit, it steals +1/+1 from it permanently.*

**Bends.** 9.5.1: back-row protection, but only for golden targets. 7.3: golden status becomes something the enemy can aim at, not only a reward.

**Creature.** Magpies in folklore can't resist shiny things, and the Folio's Shiny Syndicate "can't resist anything shiny, including evidence".

**Archetype.** SCAM, and an answer to golden chasing (archetypes.md §1.3 says no tribe owns triples; magpies could own both the hoarding and the stealing). With no golden enemy it attacks normally.

**Example.** *Fence Magpie*, tier 3, 3/3. Shiny. (Golden: steals +2/+2, and treats other golden units as ordinary targets: thieves don't rob each other.)

**Fun against.** Your triple becomes a target as well as a prize. It isn't a removal spell: the magpie still has to survive the exchange with a golden unit, which is usually bigger. It is a tax on the player who is far ahead, which feels fair.

## 5. Swarm (honeybee, bumblebee)

**Rules.** *Swarm: When a Swarm unit attacks, every other Swarm unit in its row joins in and hits the same target at the same time. The target hits back only the unit that started the attack, and all of them count as having attacked this cycle.*

**Bends.** 9.4.1 and 9.4.5: one attack per side per turn, from one unit. Here one turn is several units' attack. The pointer is set by the leader and every joiner is flagged. 9.6.1: the exchange is no longer one against one.

**Creature.** Honeybees mob an intruder together; a hive defends itself as one body, not as single bees.

**Archetype.** WIDE, built to beat TALL: five 1/1 bees deal 5 to the big unit and lose one bee, where Battlegrounds-style trades lose five. Cleave, splash and Curl are the natural answers (every bee that damages a hedgehog gets pricked).

**Example.** *Worker Bee*, tier 1, 1/1. Swarm. *Drone Captain*, tier 4, 3/3: Swarm. Your other Swarm units have +1 Attack while they join this one's attacks.

**Fun against.** It is easy to read (a cloud of bees goes for your big unit) and has clear counters that other tribes carry. It also changes the attack-count maths, which makes "wide against tall" a choice of form, not only of numbers.

## 6. Nocturnal (owl) *(close variant of Galaxy's Sneak)*

**Rules.** *Nocturnal: This sits out the first attack cycle of each combat. From the second cycle on, it attacks the enemy with the lowest Health, in either row.*

**Bends.** 9.4.5: it opts out of cycle 1. 9.5.1 and 9.5.5: it ignores back-row protection and doesn't choose at random. *Close to* Galaxy's Sneak and the longlist's Swoop (archetypes.md §4.1, Birds). The difference is when and whom: Sneak goes in early for the back row; Nocturnal waits for dark and picks off whatever is weakest.

**Creature.** Owls hunt at night, silently, and take the weak or the stragglers. The Folio's owls sit as "the court that sits after dark".

**Archetype.** SCAM (a finisher for wounded units) that lives in the back row, where it is safe through the first cycle while it waits.

**Example.** *Tawny Magistrate*, tier 3, 5/2. Nocturnal.

**Fun against.** Fragile back-row support units are in danger once night falls, so Health on support units matters, and so does finishing the fight fast. "The owl comes for your weakest" is a rule a player can see and plan around.

## 7. Bud (ooze)

**Rules.** *Bud: Whenever this survives damage, it splits: it loses half its current Attack and Health (rounded down), and a copy with that half and the same text appears in the nearest empty slot. Split copies are combat-only.*

**Bends.** 9.6.3: damage normally only lowers Health. Here it also makes bodies. 8.4.2: where the bud lands. No stats are created: an 8/12 that takes 2 becomes two units of 4/5. The board's 7 slots are the natural limit; a full board means no split.

**Creature.** Oozes are magic slime: they are unstable, they divide. Real slime moulds split and rejoin.

**Archetype.** TALL in the shop (one big blob to pump), WIDE in the fight once it is hit. This is one answer to archetypes.md question 14 (merge or split?): grow by merging, fight by splitting.

**Example.** *Pudding Ooze*, tier 4, 6/10. Bud.

**Fun against.** It is a puzzle with a visible answer: chip damage makes it worse, and one big hit that kills it outright, or Cleave to clean up the splits, solves it. The pleasure is watching a player who knew that.

## 8. Changeling (fairy)

**Rules.** *Changeling: Start of Combat: this swaps places with the enemy unit across from it, and each fights for its new side this combat. If the slot across is empty, nothing happens.*

**Bends.** 9.1.1: each side fights with its own copied board. Here a unit fights for the other side. 8.2.6: across from. 10.1.1: the hero damage formula counts surviving units; proposal: a swapped unit counts for the side it ended the fight on. This is the most rule-breaking idea on the list.

**Creature.** The changeling of folklore: fairies take a child and leave one of their own in its place. It differs from the longlist's Changeling (archetypes.md §4.1, "become a copy of the unit behind this"), which is a copy; this one is the swap.

**Archetype.** SCAM. The changeling is a weak body (that's what the enemy gets) and the enemy's unit fights for you.

**Example.** *Changeling Babe*, tier 4, 1/1. Changeling.

**Fun against.** Your own unit hitting your board is the most memorable story a fight can tell. The defence is position: an enemy only takes the unit in the slot across, so a cheap unit in the mirrored slot guards against it. Async play makes across-from hard to aim (archetypes.md §3, rule 3), so it plays as a random theft unless the player sees the snapshot (question 6 there).

## 9. Bargain (fairy)

**Rules.** *Bargain: When you play this, you get its gift at once, and its price comes due at the start of your turn a set number of turns later. Selling the fairy before then pays the price at once, doubled.*

**Bends.** 3.2.1: a new step at the start of the Recruit Phase where debts are paid. 4.1: gold borrowed against later turns. 4.3: selling has a cost as well as a value.

**Creature.** Fairy bargains always carry a catch, and the debt always comes due. It pairs with the longlist's fairy gold that vanishes (archetypes.md §4.2).

**Archetype.** ECO (borrowed tempo) and TALL (a big gift now on one unit, paid for later).

**Example.** *Hob of the Crossroads*, tier 2, 2/2. Bargain: give a unit +4/+4 permanently now. Price, in 2 turns: your shop has one fewer unit that turn. *Thrice-Kissed Sprite*, tier 4, 3/3. Bargain: gain 4 gold now. Price, in 3 turns: lose 6 gold; if you can't, your highest-Attack unit loses 4 Attack permanently.

**Fun against.** The debt day shows on the fairy, so a player can see when an opponent will be weak. In async play this is mostly a choice for the fairy player; the price should land on the board sometimes, not only on gold, so their fights show it.

## 10. Ride (wren) *(close variant of Magnetic plus a death summon)*

**Rules.** *Ride: Play this onto a friendly unit with more Attack; it rides that unit and uses no slot (one rider per mount). When the mount dies, the rider leaps into its slot and attacks immediately.*

**Bends.** 8.1.1 and 7.2.2: at most 7 units, one per slot. A rider is an eighth unit. 9.4.12: it attacks out of turn when it leaps. *Close to* Magnetic (11.4.1), which also uses no slot, and to a death summon. The difference is that the rider stays itself: its own stats, its own text, its own entry.

**Creature.** The fable of the wren that hid on the eagle's back, flew higher than it and became king of the birds (Folio hook "Rider"). The Folio's mice "ride larger allies into battle" too.

**Archetype.** STICKY (the slot takes two kills) and WIDE (more bodies than slots).

**Example.** *Wren King*, tier 2, 2/1. Ride. When this leaps, it has +2 Attack.

**Fun against.** Killing the bear isn't the end: a wren pops out and pecks the wounded attacker. Proposal: Cleave and splash damage hit the rider too, so tribes with Cleave have an answer.

## 11. Hungry (shrew)

**Rules.** *Hungry: This attacks first in each cycle, ahead of the reading order. At the start of each of your turns it eats 1 gold; if you have none, it loses 1/1 permanently.*

**Bends.** 9.4.2 and 9.4.5: attack order is reading order, from slot F1. Hungry jumps it, which Battlegrounds never does (it has no speed). 4.1: an upkeep that takes gold every turn.

**Creature.** Shrews must eat every few hours or die, and they are the fastest things in the hedge (Folio hook: "Pay upkeep each round or lose HP; very fast attacks").

**Archetype.** Tempo for early WIDE boards. It may also be the counter to ECO that archetypes.md §2.2 says is missing: it hits hard on turns 1–4 while ECO boards are weak.

**Example.** *Pygmy Shrew*, tier 1, 3/1. Hungry.

**Fun against.** It hurts early and fades. With uncapped gold carry-over (4.1.2–4.1.3), 1 gold a turn is a real cost on turn 2 and almost nothing on turn 12, so the upkeep may need to scale (for example, gold equal to its tier). A Hungry shrew in the back row attacks first and still can't be attacked: whether that is fine is a question for testing.

## 12. Hibernate (dormouse)

**Rules.** *Hibernate: While this is in your hand, it gains +1/+1 at the end of each of your turns. Once played, it stops growing.*

**Bends.** 3.4.2 and 7.1: units in hand do nothing. Here the hand becomes a den: a slow place to keep power, beside the gold that already carries over.

**Creature.** Dormice sleep through more than half the year (the Folio: "late-game scaler"; the Marmot Cantons "sleep through half the year").

**Archetype.** TALL. A long-term saving that is readable and can't loop, since the growth is one step per turn.

**Example.** *Hazel Dormouse*, tier 2, 1/1. Hibernate. *Old Sleeper*, tier 5, 4/4. Hibernate. When you play this, give its stats gained in hand to the units adjacent to it as well.

**Fun against.** The dormouse player runs a thinner board while their hand fattens, so there is a window to punish them. The cost is a hand slot and a unit not on the board, both visible and simple.

## 13. Whistle (marmot)

**Rules.** *Whistle: This doesn't attack. When its turn in the attack order comes, it whistles instead, and the next enemy attack this cycle deals half damage (rounded up).*

**Bends.** 9.4.1 and 9.4.5: sides take turns to attack. Whistle spends its side's turn on something that isn't an attack. The pointer moves past it as if it attacked. 9.6.1: the next attack's damage is halved for the attacker's whole damage step, splash included.

**Creature.** Alpine marmots post sentries on rocks that whistle an alarm, and the colony dives for cover. The Folio: "whistle-sentries who raise the alarm at everything, including clouds".

**Archetype.** STICKY: the side gives up tempo to make every enemy hit count less.

**Example.** *Whistle-Watch Marmot*, tier 2, 1/5. Whistle. Whenever an enemy attacks one of your other units, this gains +1 Attack this combat. (This is the owner's "rewarded when something else is attacked instead".) It never attacks, so the Attack only matters when it is hit back.

**Fun against.** Each whistle is a trade the player can see: one attack given up, half of one enemy hit saved. Many small attackers or a Swarm beat it, since only one attack is halved. A big single hitter is what it is built to stop.

## 14. Play Dead (fox)

**Rules.** *Play Dead: The first time this would die each combat, it lies limp at 1 Health instead: it can't be attacked and skips its attacks, and the enemy's "after this kills" effects fire as if it had died. After the next enemy attack on a unit adjacent to it, it springs up and attacks that attacker immediately; if it is the last unit on its side, it springs up at once.*

**Bends.** 9.6.3: a unit at 0 Health dies. Here it doesn't. 9.7.2: kill triggers fire with no death. 9.5: it leaves the candidate list. 9.4.12: it attacks out of turn. Random effects (9.5.6) can still hit it while limp, which is the counter.

**Creature.** Reynard the fox, in the old fables, plays dead by the road to catch the crows that come to peck him. The Folio's foxes are a "travelling theatre" and "Reynard's lawyers".

**Archetype.** SCAM and STICKY. It is a second life that pays off with an ambush rather than a return.

**Example.** *Reynard's Clerk*, tier 3, 3/3. Play Dead. When this springs up, it has +4 Attack for that attack.

**Fun against.** The enemy gets a false reward: its on-kill unit fires and celebrates, then the fox gets up. It is a good moment to watch, and random damage and splash are clear answers.

## 15. Jink (hare) *(close variant of Divine Shield)*

**Rules.** *Jink: The first time each combat this is attacked, if a slot next to it in its row is empty, it darts into that slot and the attack misses (no damage either way).*

**Bends.** 9.6.1: an attack whose defender is gone ends without damage. Here the defender leaves on purpose. 8.3.5: units change slots in combat. 9.4.9: if it darts to a lower-numbered slot, it is flagged as having attacked. *Close to* Divine Shield (11.2.2), which also blocks one hit. The difference is that Jink needs a gap beside it, and moving changes who is adjacent and in front.

**Creature.** A hare doesn't fight; it jinks, turning sharply sideways at full speed.

**Archetype.** STICKY that wants gaps, so it pulls the other way from wide boards.

**Example.** *Jack Hare*, tier 1, 2/2. Jink.

**Fun against.** It is visible from the board: a hare with an empty slot beside it will dodge once. Random effects and splash can't be dodged. The move also breaks the hare player's own adjacency auras, a cost they chose.

## 16. War Dance (stoat)

**Rules.** *War Dance: Instead of its first attack, this dances: the highest-Attack enemy is entranced and can't attack until this attacks or dies. This unit's next attack goes at the entranced unit, in either row, for double damage.*

**Bends.** 9.4.5: an enemy unit is taken out of its own side's attack order by an effect. 9.5.1: the strike ignores back-row protection. It spends a turn, as the owner's "something that skips its attack" asked.

**Creature.** Stoats really do this: a wild, leaping "war dance" that seems to hold a rabbit still before the strike. The Folio's stoats include a "war-dance troupe".

**Archetype.** SCAM: it shuts down the enemy's carry for a cycle, then hits it hard. It targets by text, as archetypes.md §3 rule 3 asks, not by across-from.

**Example.** *Ermine Dancer*, tier 4, 3/4. War Dance.

**Fun against.** Your carry stands still, watching. The answer is to kill the stoat before its second turn: it is entranced only while the dancer lives, so pings and fast front units free it.

## 17. Tongue (frog, toad)

**Rules.** *Tongue: Start of Combat: this pulls the lowest-Health enemy back-row unit into an empty slot in their front row. If their front row has no empty slot, nothing happens.*

**Bends.** 8.3.5: units change slots only by their owner's moves or effects that say so. Here an enemy effect moves your unit. 9.5.1: the unit is exposed by moving it, not by attacking over the front row. 8.2.4: the pulled unit's "in front of" and "behind" auras now point elsewhere.

**Creature.** Frogs and toads snatch prey from a distance with a sticky tongue.

**Archetype.** SCAM, and a third way into the back row beside Swoop and Trample (archetypes.md §3, rule 4) that changes position rather than reach.

**Example.** *Bullfrog Tenor*, tier 3, 3/4. Tongue.

**Fun against.** It creates a real board-building tension: gaps in your front row are good for squirrels, oozes and summons, but a frog can pull your fragile back-row support into one. Filling the front row is the defence.

## 18. Flood (beaver)

**Rules.** *Flood: Start of Combat: raise the water by 1 on both boards for this combat (to a maximum of 3). Each water level gives front-row units -1 Attack, except units with Swim, which get +1 Attack instead.*

**Bends.** 9.3.1: game-wide combat modifiers come from the modifier layer. Here a unit sets terrain for both boards. It adds a combat state that other text can read ("while the water is up").

**Creature.** Beavers dam streams and flood the land around them, reshaping the place everything else lives. The Folio's Dyke Board: "the water level decides every battle". Swim belongs on otters, frogs, newts and water voles. The back row stands on higher ground and stays dry.

**Archetype.** MEDIUM for water creatures, and a counter to front-row TALL carries. Beavers have no home in the longlist yet; they would fit Hedgerow folk or a water tribe.

**Example.** *Dam-Warden Beaver*, tier 3, 2/5. Flood. *Ditch Otter*, tier 2, 3/2. Swim.

**Fun against.** It is symmetric, so it is a fight about terrain both players can see: lean on the back row, or field swimmers. It also hurts the beaver player's own dry front row, which keeps it honest.

## Cut, but worth a look

- **Churn (wild boar).** When this attacks, the target's slot is churned up: no summon can enter it this combat. An anti-WIDE tool that uses fixed slots (bends 8.4.1). Cut because Bud, Burrow and Bury already lean on slot refills; this is their answer if they turn out too strong.
- **Echo (bat).** While this is on your board, you see the snapshot you will fight before you end your turn. An information economy (Folio seed "The Echo Cartographers"). Cut because it depends on archetypes.md question 6.
- **Brood (cuckoo).** Lay an Egg in an empty enemy slot; it hatches mid-fight and pushes out its neighbours. Cut because it needs the same "unit on the other side" rule as Changeling, and one such idea is enough until the owner says yes to it.
- **Pouch (pelican).** Carry the unit behind this, safe from harm, and release it when this dies. Cut as too close to Ride.

## Rule questions these raise

1. **A non-attack action in the attack order** (Whistle, War Dance, Curl, Nocturnal). Does spending a side's turn on something that isn't an attack fit 9.4, and does the attack limit (9.10.1) count these turns?
2. **Zones in combat besides the slots:** underground (Burrow), riding (Ride), limp (Play Dead). Should the engine have one "off-slot" state with shared rules for targeting and for when combat ends?
3. **Objects in slots** (Bury's Nuts, Flood's water, Churn). Should slots hold objects that aren't units? Galaxy's slot buffs are a precedent.
4. **Units fighting for the other side** (Changeling, Brood). Yes, no, or not in v0?
5. **Rounding.** Curl rounds half down; Whistle rounds half up. Which way a halving rounds changes who wins it (the owner: "either it rounds up or down, I don't know"). These two pick opposite ways on purpose: rounding down makes Curl immune to 1-damage hits, and rounding up stops Whistle zeroing them. This needs one rule or a clear per-keyword choice.
