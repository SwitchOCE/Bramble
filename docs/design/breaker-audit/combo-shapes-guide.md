# Combo-shapes field guide (as given to the breakers in the guide round)

Written knowing the test's answers, so the guide round below is not a fair held-out test.

## A field guide to how runaway combos are built

Runaway engines come in a few shapes. Each has a question that finds it. The examples come from other games and other cards; none is one of the 9 you are judging.

| Shape | How it breaks | The question to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-reference | The gain is measured from a number the gain itself raises | What number does this read? Can anything it gives, or anything in the pool, raise that number? | A card that triples your two smallest units every turn: the smallest keeps rising, so it keeps tripling |
| Cross-feed | Two pieces each feed the other | What if there were two of this side by side, or this plus a card that does the reverse? | Two units that each gain a share of the other when it returns |
| Phase shift | An effect that normally resets (in combat, temporary, until next turn) is made to happen where it sticks | Where does this normally trigger? What makes it trigger in the shop, or makes the result permanent? | A Dragon that doubles its own Attack when it attacks, next to a card that keeps combat gains permanently |
| Trigger stack | Several "twice / extra time" effects touch the same chain | List every repeater that touches each link of the chain, then multiply them, not add them | Battlecries twice, on a battlecry that is itself repeated each turn |
| Copy supply | The engine needs several copies; something turns rare into reliable | If you could have as many copies as you wanted, what happens? Then find everything that gives copies: Discover, hero powers, triples, generators, shop copying | A unit that comes back home multiplied every few turns, as many times as you like |
| Reset | A "once" limit is attached to something that can be reset | What does "once" attach to: the card, the unit, the turn? What puts it back to the start? | A card's "when played" text firing again because it returns to hand |
| Duplication | A step creates stats instead of moving them; chain it, then consolidate | Does any step give the same stats to two places? Can the copies be merged back into one? | A shop bonus measured from your biggest unit, which that unit then eats |
| Free fuel | A cycle that pays for itself removes the cost brake | Can the loop pay its own cost in gold, board slots or cards? | A card whose repeated effect refunds more gold than the card costs, so every repeat is free |

How to use it, per card:
1. Name the card's role or roles in an engine: reader, raiser, multiplier, converter (phase shift), copier, resetter, fuel.
2. An engine needs a reader, something that raises what it reads, and a way to repeat. For each missing role, ask that shape's question and search the snapshot for it.
3. If you find a loop, write its growth per turn as a ratio. Any number that grows by a share of itself turn after turn, with no stated cap, is BROKEN, however modest the numbers look at turn 18. Don't downgrade a loop because its early numbers are small or it takes several pieces; say how rare it is separately.

Method, work backwards for each card:
1. Ask: what would have to be true for this card to break the game? Name the pieces that would make it loop or run away (copies of itself, ways to get more copies such as Discover or generating cards, retriggers, permanence, stat transfers, gold, Golden versions, hero powers, buddies, trinkets, anomalies, spells).
2. Search the card's snapshot for each of those pieces by mechanic, not just by name (grep for words like Discover, copy, sell, Golden, twice, permanently). Record which exist and how a player gets them.
3. Use the field guide above for both steps, then give the verdict (rule 3 of the guide applies).

Under 1,500 words. Also say which shapes, if any, led you to each finding. Final message: a table card | verdict | strongest line | growth | pieces searched for and found, then one line per card on your confidence.
