# The Dyke Board

## 1. Identity

**The tribe's law: one water level for everyone.** All your Dyke Board units (**Board units**) have the same Health, your **Level**.

- **Pool.** Health a Board unit would gain goes into your **Pool**. Your Level is the Pool shared evenly among your Board units, rounded down. The remainder stays in the Pool. The Level is never below 1, and with no Board units the Pool just waits.
- **Joining.** A Board unit you play pours its own Health into the Pool, then sits at the Level like the rest.
- **Leaving.** A Board unit that leaves while recruiting (sold, tripled, anything) leaves its share in the Pool. Water only goes out when a card takes it: "take X from your Pool", or an Ooze's Eat, which takes the eaten unit's share.
- **Fights.** Each Board unit starts the fight at the Level and takes damage on its own. Health gained in a fight stays on that unit.
- **Attack is never shared.** Every unit keeps its own Attack.
- **Pour X:** add X to your Pool. This is the tribe's only way of making Health from nothing.
- **Under:** a unit on either board that isn't one of your Board units and has less Health than your Level. This includes your own units from other tribes, and enemy Board units.

**How it plays.** Every Health buff you get goes to the whole Board, so you can never build one big tank. What you decide is how many seats the Board has. Say your Pool is 120. With 6 Board units at Level 20 you have more attackers and the count cards pay off. With 3 at Level 40 each unit is a wall and more enemies are Under, so they drown. A new 4/1 coot drops everyone's Level, and selling one raises it, because sold units leave their water behind. Beavers pour, coots fight, and Attack is where your units stand apart. Units from other tribes are both a resource and a risk. They can feed the Pool, take a seat on the Board, or be left outside, where a high Level drowns them along with the enemy.

## 3. Builds

**High Water (tall).** Keep 2 or 3 Board units: Levy-Master, the Peilmeester or Hendrika, and a Rearing Coot or Kees. Fill the other 4 slots with another tribe. Levy-Master pours 20 a turn from those 4, doubled by the Miller and repeated by Kobus. All of it is shared among 3 units, so the Level climbs fast. The catch is that your own outsiders drown under the Peilmeester if their Health is below the Level. So you want outsiders that are fat (Bog Mire tanks, Oozes that have eaten), or a Heemraad to seat the weak one, though a seated unit no longer counts for Levy-Master. Every slot is a choice between "pays the levy" and "on the Board".

**Full Benches (wide).** Play 6 or 7 Board units: Bench Coot, Chicks, Mud Hauler, Winter Raft, Dike-Walker, Aaltje. The Level stays modest (15 to 25), but the count cards scale and Aaltje turns water into Attack across the whole bench. The capstone is the Spaarndam Pup: every hit is shared, so no unit is picked off and the Board breaks all at once or not at all. Old Bram is the other way to finish. Chicks die early, and each death pours half a Level into whoever is left.

**Seats for Hire (cross-tribe).** Heemraad seats a unit from another tribe, and its Health becomes everyone's:
- A seated Ooze's Eat and Digest gains pour into the Pool.
- A seated Bird can Fly South. Its share stays behind, Ledger Coot still pays 1 gold, and it comes home with doubled stats to pour in again.
- A seated Bog unit with Mire draws attacks, and under the Pup the damage is spread over the whole Board.
- Kobus repeats seated units' own End of Turn effects too.

**Sluice and Ledger (water as money).** The Miller and Biesbosch Kits fill the Pool. Eel-Trap Coot sells water for 2 gold a turn, and Aaltje sells it for Attack. The Level stays low on purpose. Sell Chicks into Ledger Coot. This build wins on gold and Attack, not Health, and it is the build that least wants the Peilmeester.

**In a lobby.**
- **Bog folk, Oozes:** the best partners. They supply big-Health outsiders that stay above the Level, or that are worth seating.
- **Birds, Fairies:** feed Seats for Hire. A Ring on a Board unit's slot gives the whole Board its Health.
- **Hedgerow folk:** Pass X into a rightmost Board unit spreads across the Board.
- **Hunters:** Quarry on the enemy across pairs with Kees's Under targeting.
- **As an opponent:** the Board is worst for wide boards of small units, which the Peilmeester drowns, and harmless to a few huge units.
- **Mirror match:** two water boards compare Levels, and the lower one's Board units are Under.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Readability.** The Level is a moving average. The board needs a gauge staff that shows the Pool and the Level, and a preview of the new Level while you drag a unit in or out. Without one, buying a unit just looks like everyone losing Health.
- **Early feel-bad.** Dilution hurts on turns 1 to 4. A 4/2 Rearing Coot dropping your Kit from 3 to 2 Health feels wrong until you see the water stay.
- **The Peilmeester is swingy.** It kills wide boards outright and does nothing to big ones. Lever if needed: half the difference.
- **Stacked multipliers.** The Miller (×2 amount) and Kobus (×2 triggers) make ×4 on End of Turn Pours. Levy-Master with 4 outsiders then pours 80 a turn at Tier 5, against a top of about 60. With goldens it goes to ×9. It grows linearly, not by compounding, but it is the high roll to watch. Lever: the Miller doesn't stack with another Miller or doubles only Tier 1 to 4 Pours.
- **Seated Bird loop.** Fly South doubles the Level-sized Health, so a seated Bird adds twice the Level every two turns. Lever: a Board unit flying south doubles its printed Health only.
- **The Spaarndam Pup changes hero damage.** All your Board units tend to survive or die together. That needs a playtest, and it adds a split to every hit.
- **Rules for other tribes.** Seats change a unit's tribe permanently. Shy, "biggest", Eat and triples need clean edge rules. Eat taking a share and leaving water behind are both written into the law.
- **Holes.** No Shield, Swoop or back-row reach. Few Start of Combat answers at Tiers 1 to 2. High Water hangs on two cards at Tiers 5 to 6 (Levy-Master, Peilmeester). Nothing uses spells, beyond Pouring them.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1.** Both are 4-stat bodies. Bench Coot is tempo, about +1 to +3 Attack in a fight. Biesbosch Kit is the seed, at +2 a turn (about 0.5 body). Its 1 Health dilutes on purpose.
- **Tier 2.** Mud Hauler gives about 8 once at 4 units (1.3 bodies). Rearing Coot gives about 4 to 6 in a fight. Ledger Coot is economy at 1 gold a turn. Its 5-stat body is light on purpose.
- **Tier 3.** Winter Raft gives about 5 a turn at 5 units. Kees gives about 5 to 8 extra in a fight. Heemraad's value is whatever it seats. Nest-Platform Coot is under the guide in raw stats, but it adds a body a turn at the Level and feeds the count and sell cards.
- **Tier 4.** Dike-Walker gives about 10 a turn at 5 units, on the guide. The Miller is the first multiplier. Aaltje makes only about 5 net, but she moves 10 Health into 15 Attack. Eel-Trap Coot is 2 gold a turn, above Battlegrounds' Tier 4 economy, paid for in water.
- **Tier 5.** Levy-Master pours about 20 a turn with 4 outsiders, the direction card. Old Bram gives about 40 in a fight. Kobus is a Brann-style trigger multiplier. With the Miller it breaks the top on purpose, as the tribe's chase.
- **Tier 6.** Hendrika gives about half the Pool as Health in a fight, plus a stronger Level, about on the target. The Spaarndam Pup's value is hard to price: no extra stats, but it ends picking units off. The Peilmeester breaks the target against wide boards on purpose, as the drowning the seed promises. See Risks for the lever.

## 6. Design rationale

1. **The law came first:** Board units share one Health. "One water level for everyone" taken literally gives a real choice: more bodies or higher water.
2. **Health is shared, Attack isn't,** so units keep an identity and Attack buffs are how they differ.
3. **Sold units leave their water,** so going tall is a choice rather than a loss, and "who will not dike must leave" becomes a play.
4. **Under compares the Level with every non-Board unit, on both boards.** Swim or drown hits your own outsiders too, which makes seats matter.
5. **The tribe splits along the law's axis.** Count cards (wide) against Level cards (tall), each with a Tier 6.
6. **Outsider cards (Heemraad, Levy-Master)** make other tribes the water source, for Johnny's cross-tribe uses.
7. **Take-from-Pool cards (Aaltje, Eel-Trap)** make lowering the water a vote too, and turn the Level into a currency.
8. **No Pour scales with the Pool or the Level,** so growth stays linear and passes the runaway check.
9. **There are two multipliers on different axes** (the Miller on amount, Kobus on triggers), so combinations and goldens have a ceiling to chase.
10. **Shared damage in fights is held back to one Tier 6,** so the law reaches combat only if you build for it.
11. **Hendrika's raise follows reading order,** so placement against the Peilmeester and the Rearing Coots is a decision.
12. **Names came last.** Beavers pour, coots fight, and the figures from the seed went to the cards that fit them.
