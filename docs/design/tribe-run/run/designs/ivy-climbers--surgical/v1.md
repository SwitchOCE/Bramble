# The Ivy and Mistletoe

## 1. Identity

**Climb (new keyword).** This unit's **host** is the biggest friendly unit in front of it (the left one on a tie). Only back-row units have anyone in front of them, so a climber in the front row has no host. The host is checked again whenever the board changes, fights included. A climber with no host just stands.

**Rise (part of Climb).** At the very end of End of Turn, after every other effect, each climber gains half of its host's **biggest permanent gain this round**, permanently. "Biggest" means most Attack plus Health in one gain. "This round" means since your last End of Turn, so lasting gains won in the last fight count. A gain from rising never makes another unit rise. A golden climber rises with its host's two biggest gains.

**Host** in card text means any friendly unit that has at least one unit climbing it. Hosts can be of any tribe, or neutral.

**How it plays.** The Ivy and Mistletoe don't win by themselves. They make whatever you're already building stronger. Your front row holds the real bodies, often another tribe's carry. Your three back slots hold climbers, which grow with whichever front unit is biggest in front of them, and each card does one more thing with that link. Honeysuckles feed their host and get half of it back. Mistletoe drains the host's Attack into the safe back row. Dodder hangs on the enemy instead. The decisions are where to put each unit, which three climbers get the back row, and which carry to hang them on. Each staggered back slot sits in front of two front slots, so B1, B2 and B3 each choose between two hosts. F2 and F3 can carry two climbers each, and you control which front unit is biggest.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Patron (one carry).** You pick one big unit from any tribe (an Ooze that has eaten, a Fairy on two Rings, a Hunter after a Take) and put it in F2 or F3, so that two back slots climb it. The pieces are Woodbine and Night-Scented Honeysuckle to feed it, Barley-Sugar Twiner to repeat its biggest gain, and the Honourable Viscum Highbough, who copies every gain in full. Viscum is snobbish enough to limit himself: if he ever becomes your biggest unit, he loses his host. To keep him rising, you keep the carry bigger than him. A typical board: Missel, the carry and two tanks in front; Viscum, Twiner and Woodbine behind.

**B. The Season (a mixed board).** You spread climbers over hosts of three or four tribes. Hop Bine pays you for buying whatever its host is. Well-Connected Ivy Discovers from the host's tribe, which helps you find new tribes. Each turn you move Lady Hedera behind a host of a tribe she hasn't signed yet; at five signatures every host gains +10/+10 a turn. Woodbine takes the same shuffle well, giving +3/+3 to each new patron, while Ivy Bush rewards climbers that stay put. Blackcap grafts Climb onto other tribes' back-row units: a climbing Bird banks its echoes and then Flies South to double them.

**C. Parasites.** Dodder Seedling and Cousin Dodder take from the enemy. Apple Mistletoe moves the host's Attack into the back row and makes the host tankier, which pairs well with a Bog Mire or a Hunkering host. Mistletoe's attacks ignore Shield, the oath it never swore. Old Man's Beard inherits the host when it falls. Traveller's Joy makes fight gains rise too: the Health that Mistletoe gives the host comes half back to every climber on it. Cousin Dodder's takes are permanent, so a climber on Cousin Dodder rises with them. If Cousin Dodder is your biggest unit, Viscum rises with every take.

**D. The Trellis (late, wide).** From Tier 4, Ivy-clad Ruin lets front-row climbers climb beside them, so one board can hold five climbers on two hosts. The finish is Ivy of the Grand Oak: at Start of Combat each host takes in its climbers' combined stats, so the stats stored in the back row join the front line.

**In a lobby.** With Hedgerow folk, Pass X targets your rightmost unit, so a host in that seat echoes every Pass. Oozes give the biggest single gains, so build A loves them. Fairies' Ring stats are standing stats, not gains, so they don't rise; a Shy Fairy is a poor host. Birds' Swoop is the main threat to climbers, and Missel answers it. With Bog folk, a Mired, Bitter host plus Mistletoe means the front holds while the back hits. With Hunters, each Take landing next turn is one big gain to echo.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **The echo multiplies other tribes' carries.** A golden Ooze that eats a 40/40 is a huge single gain. The lever is ready: half becomes a third, or "biggest gain" stays capped at one a round. Watch Viscum on an Ooze.
- **The back row is a bottleneck.** 13 of 19 units climb and only three slots climb before Ivy-clad Ruin. Shops in the middle game will often offer climbers you can't place. That is the intended choice, but it may read as dead draws. If it does, move one climber (Hop Bine) to also work from the front row.
- **Mono-tribe is weak by design.** The tribe's own hosts are Ivy Bush, Ivy-clad Ruin, Missel and the Dodders. Paired with a weak tribe in the lobby, it may have nothing worth climbing.
- **Readability.** Host links shift as units die. The screen must draw the vine from climber to host and animate the rise at End of Turn, or nobody sees the tribe working.
- **Contested pool.** One or two climbers help any board, so other players may take them as splashes and drain copies.
- **Early-game tempo is thin.** Little rises while buffs are +1/+1 (half rounds down), so the first turns lean on Dodder Seedling and Ivy Bush.
- **Viscum can feel bad** when he loses his host by growing. That is intended, but it needs clear feedback.
- **Open:** whether "biggest unit in front" handles rows correctly in every edge case once back-row access rules are settled, and whether Ruin's sideways climbing should also reach the back row.

## 5. Tier check

Targets come from Part C, with a body of 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14 stats.

- **T1:** Dodder Seedling has a body of 4 and swings about 2 in a fight (target 2.4). Young Ivy has a small body. Late on, Adult Ivy rises 4–10 a turn, above 0.4 body. **I broke this on purpose:** Young Ivy is the seed, all of its value is borrowed and it uses one of three back slots.
- **T2:** Ivy Bush gives +2/+2 a turn with two climbers, plus their echo. Woodbine gives 2–6. Hop Bine gives about 1 gold a turn, worth a third of a unit. All within 0.3–1 body.
- **T3:** Kissing Bough gives +6 a turn for four turns, then 3 gold (target about 5.6). Blackcap and Well-Connected Ivy are one-off pieces worth about one body. Apple Mistletoe nets about half its host's Attack in a fight: on target at a 10-Attack host, high late. **On purpose:** it scales with a host from another tribe, not with itself.
- **T4:** Night-Scented Honeysuckle gives 8–12 a turn (target 11). Missel's two Shields are worth about 16–20 in a fight (target 17). Ivy-clad Ruin has a body of 10, above 8.5 on purpose because it is a host and tank whose text adds capacity, not stats. I changed the shape: two enablers (Traveller's Joy, Missel) instead of a second buffer, because climbers need hosts that survive and fights that rise.
- **T5:** Viscum gives about 20–30 a turn (target 24, top 60 on a Dodder or Ooze carry). Old Man's Beard inherits about 40–60 in a fight (target 48). Barley-Sugar Twiner repeats about 24 a turn.
- **T6:** Lady Hedera gives 36–60 a turn plus echoes at three to five signatures (target 56). Cousin Dodder keeps 50–75 a fight permanently. Ivy of the Grand Oak moves about 100 into the front in a fight (target 84). Golden ceilings stay at about ×2. Runaway check: Cousin Dodder takes from the enemy, Viscum loses his host once he is the biggest, and rising never chains, so nothing feeds its own source.

## 6. Design rationale

1. **Gaps.** Every tribe's payoff feeds its own units, yet every lobby mixes five tribes, so no card makes another tribe's carry better and nothing rewards a mixed board.
2. **Gaps.** The back row is only a waiting room (only Swoop cares about it), and there is no shared buff multiplier.
3. **Filled with one keyword:** climbers live in the back row and rise with the front unit in front of them, of any tribe. That makes the tribe a bridge, a multiplier and the back row's purpose at once.
4. **The host is "the biggest unit in front of it"** rather than a chosen link. It uses Bramble's existing words and board, it is readable on a recorded board, and choosing whom to climb becomes arranging the board.
5. **Rising is half of the biggest gain this round, at End of Turn.** It doesn't depend on play order (no trap with Start of Turn gains), a stream of small buffs can't multiply, and it rewards Johnny for concentrating buffs.
6. **Rising never chains**, which removes loops and runaway with one sentence.
7. **Only three back slots is the scarcity on purpose**, and Ivy-clad Ruin at Tier 4 releases it as the tribe's first multiplier.
8. **Roles first, in three directions:** friend (feed the host), parasite (take from the host or the enemy), patron (one carry). Each direction got a Tier 1–3 entry, a Tier 4–5 engine and a Tier 6 finish.
9. **The economy is a bridge** (Hop Bine, Well-Connected Ivy) because this tribe's real need is finding its host's tribe.
10. **Missel answers Swoop**, the existing keyword that threatens climbers. Traveller's Joy brings fight gains in for the parasite build.
11. **Capstones reward a behaviour, not a number:** rotate patrons (Hedera), grow from the enemy (Dodder), empty the back row into the front (Grand Oak). Viscum limits himself by snobbery.
12. **Names and flavor came last**, fitted to the slots: the snob on the biggest host, the gatecrasher on the enemy, the poet flattering new patrons.
