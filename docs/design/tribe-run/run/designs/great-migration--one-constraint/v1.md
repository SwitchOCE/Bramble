# The Great Migration (v1)

## 1. Identity

**The herd rule: Follow the Front.** Your **Front** is your first Lemming in reading order (F1–F4, then B1–B3). **A Lemming's text works only while it is your Front.** Every other Lemming is a plain body until the one ahead of it falls or moves. "When played" works only if the Lemming is your Front once it lands. Non-Lemmings are never your Front and never block it.

**Take the Lead** (trigger): when this becomes your Front in a fight. The Front you start with takes the lead right after all Start of Combat effects. After that, a Lemming takes the lead whenever the one before it dies or moves. Each Lemming takes the lead at most once a fight. Swapping your Front while recruiting triggers nothing.

**Banner** (tribe counter, starts at 0): your Front has +X/+X, where X is your Banner. Raises are permanent unless a card says "for this fight". The Banner moves with the Front, so when a Front dies the next one picks it up.

**How it plays.** One Lemming speaks for the herd at a time. While you recruit, you decide who stands in front for each moment of the turn: one Front while you play units, another while you sell, and a last one for End of Turn. That last one also starts the fight, because nothing moves between End of Turn and combat. In the fight, your reading order is a queue of leaders. Each death hands the Banner and the lead to the next Lemming, and its Take the Lead fires. The herd's power comes from that ordering: which leader goes first, who comes next, where the blank 1/1 Kits sit to delay or speed up the queue, and which few cards let more than one Lemming speak at once. A Lemming that's bad at the back can be the best card on the board when it stands in front at the right moment.

**Rules notes.**
- Effects that a Front starts "for the rest of this fight" keep going after it dies. Only *starting* them needs the Front.
- The Front Lemming's doubling doesn't stack with a second copy.
- Herald copies whatever its next Lemming says, even another Herald, so a line of Heralds chains, up to the board's seven slots.
- With Herding Council, both Fronts carry the Banner, and either one can take the lead.

**Token.**
- **Kit**, 1/1 Lemming, no text. Created, so it sells for 0. As your Front, a Kit is a blank leader: it carries the Banner, but nothing else speaks until it falls.

No tribe spells.

## 3. Builds

**The Cliff Cult (cycle the leaders).** Gerd leads first. From then on, enemies hit whoever is in front, so your leaders die in the order you set. Cantor goes second, so every death after it raises the Banner by 2. Then come Pilgrims (+2 more each) and Kits from Ole and the Digger as cheap deaths, and Brother Onward goes near the back of the queue. When Onward takes the lead, the Banner might be 10 or more, and every Lemming still alive gets it. Put Kits *in front of* Onward on purpose, so his turn comes late. The Kits aren't padding: they're a timer. The weak point is Gerd's Shield and the first death: if the enemy cleaves through two leaders at once, the order slips.

**The Matriarch (protect the leader).** Old Mother Lemen is the Front, and she sits in **B1**, behind a front row made entirely of other tribes: Bog folk with Mire, Hunters, neutral Shield units. While she lives, every Kit is a copy of her body plus the Banner. Thaw Nester and Ole fill the board, and Banner-Bearer and Banner-Stitcher raise the Banner, which every Kit copies. Kit-Minder looks like it belongs here and doesn't: her stats overwrite the Kits' own. Keep it only for the moment she falls, when the Kits drop back to their own stats with the damage they've taken. That's the crash. Here Gerd is a trap, because he points every enemy at her. Herding Council fixes the conflict: Gerd in F1 and Lemen in F2 are both Fronts, so the enemies' attacks split between them.

**The Relay (take the lead again and again).** The Front Lemming leads, starts the double triggers, and steps back. Trailbreakers then pass the lead forward, each one buffing the next leader as it goes. Squeaker and Brother Onward each fire twice. Herald sits in front of whichever card you most want twice, and with The Front Lemming's doubling that card fires four times. This build is hard to read and full of tricks, and it's the Johnny one.

**Banner and gold (the early economy).** On turns 1–5, put Thaw Nester in front at End of Turn. Next turn, put Tally in front and sell the Kits for 1 gold each. Banner-Bearer takes over the End of Turn spot once you have a Lemming worth carrying the Banner. You rarely want more than one of these at a time, and that choice is the point.

**In a lobby.** Non-Lemmings fill the front row so the Front survives, as in the Matriarch. Otherwise they hold the line while Kits and Pilgrims cycle in the back.
- Ooze Bellies are a lead queue in storage. A Lemming released into an earlier slot becomes the Front and takes the lead mid-fight.
- A Fairy Ring on the Front's slot stacks with the Banner, and the stack carries over to whoever takes that slot.
- Harvest Supper's tribe bonus reaches every Kit made later.
- Effects that hit the **biggest** unit often find the Front, because of the Banner.
- Other players who rely on Cleave and Swoop shred the cycle: their extra hits break the order the Cliff Cult depends on.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Readability.** In a fight, players need to see who the Front is, the Banner's number and the next two leaders. Without a clear Front marker and a lead-queue preview, the tribe is noise.
- **A dead-looking board.** Six silent bodies and one active text may feel flat in the shop. Herald, Council and the Relay are the answer, and they come at Tiers 4–6. Tiers 1–3 could feel dull.
- **Fiddly recruiting.** Swapping the Front between plays, sells and End of Turn means extra moves. An "it's your Front" slot highlight, or snapping into F1, may be needed.
- **Accidental blank Fronts.** Ole fills empty slots *before* your Front too, so a careless player gets a Kit in F1 and a silent opener. That's a Johnny tool, but it'll be a beginner's trap.
- **Runaway in a fight.** Cantor, then The Front Lemming, then Onward as golden: the Banner climbs 4 per death, and then twice the Banner goes to everyone, twice. That's a ×4 high roll, inside the ceiling only if the Banner stays under about 15. Check this one first.
- **The Matriarch crash.** Old Mother Lemen against Cleave or "random enemy" damage that reaches B1 is all or nothing.
- **The fox.** Tally carries the Lemming tag. It's a flavour stretch, and the timing rule for a sold Kit needs settling.
- **Timing questions.** A double trigger of a Take the Lead that swaps places: does the second copy still resolve after the unit has moved? Proposal: yes, from its new slot.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** bodies 4 each. Thaw Nester adds about 2 stats a turn as Kits (~0.5 body, on target). Squeaker gives 2–6 Attack in a fight. It's on target early and about 2× late, kept on purpose because it's a T1 tempo body that late boards rarely hold.
- **Tier 2:** bodies 6–7. Herd Caller gives +8–10 once (~1.4 bodies). Banner-Bearer's +1 Banner is worth about 5 a turn, counting 2–3 Fronts that actually fight (~0.8 body). Tally gives about 1–2 gold a turn. All on target. Tally is an economy piece pulled down from Tier 3 so it can pair with Thaw Nester early.
- **Tier 3:** bodies 7. Trailbreaker gives +6 in a fight plus an extra lead change, Pilgrim about 12 (2 Banner across ~3 later Fronts, a little high because it must die as Front), the Digger two Kits, and Kit-Minder about 6 a turn with 3 Kits. On target.
- **Tier 4:** bodies 8.5–9. Herding Elder gives about 12 a turn on 6 others, and Banner-Stitcher about 10 a turn (2 Banner). On target. Herald is the first multiplier: it doubles one text. Ole is an enabler with about 6 stats of bodies, low alone and priced for what Kits feed.
- **Tier 5:** bodies 11–12, with The Front Lemming 12 and Cantor 11. Cantor gives about 40–60 in a fight across 5 deaths (~4 bodies). The Front Lemming doubles every later Take the Lead, the "Brann" slot. Gerd is a combat engine that shapes the fight instead of adding stats.
- **Tier 6:** bodies 14. Onward gives Banner × Lemmings: with Banner 6 on 7 Lemmings, 84 (~6 bodies). Lemen gives about 70+ across 5 Kits. Both are on target. Council is a capstone that doubles everything, so it isn't priced in stats.
- **Shape.** As in the guide, except that the economy piece moved from Tier 3 to Tier 2, and Tier 5 has a multiplier and a combat engine but no tempo body. Gerd's Shield is the closest thing.

## 6. Design rationale

1. I picked "only your Front's text works" because it turns positioning into the whole game, both while recruiting and in the fight, and it matches "whoever is in front leads".
2. Take the Lead is the reward for followers, so the bodies at the back each get a moment, and death becomes a handoff instead of a loss.
3. Once a fight per Lemming stops swap loops. Fronts set while recruiting trigger nothing, to avoid busywork.
4. The Banner gives the herd one stat pool that walks down the queue, the tribe's way of being "the many over the one".
5. Kits are blank on purpose. The rule makes them leaders that say nothing, so they're bodies, timers and spacers all at once.
6. End of Turn and the fight's opening leader are forced to be the same unit, which is the core choice each turn.
7. I made two opposed directions, cycling (Cantor, Pilgrim, Gerd) and protecting (Lemen behind other tribes), and Gerd works for one and against the other.
8. The multipliers bend the rule without breaking it: Herald (one more text), The Front Lemming (double triggers), Council (two Fronts).
9. Lasting "for the rest of this fight" effects let a Front's text outlive the Front, so the order leaders die in matters.
10. The economy (Tally selling Kits) needs a Front swap in the middle of the turn, which teaches the tribe's rhythm by Tier 2.
11. Lemen's set-stats override makes Kit-Minder a deliberate anti-synergy, for the crash and for decisions.
12. Names last: the seed's figures are mapped onto the roles that fit them (Onward the payoff, Lemen the brood, Gerd the shouter who draws fire, Ole raining Kits).
