# The Rot Guild

## 1. Identity

**Three moments this tribe is built to produce:**
- **Shop:** "I sold three units in one turn. Each slot grew a Sprout bigger than the last, the shop's units came in pre-buffed, and my Guild all grew. I ended the turn with seven full slots, every unit bigger, and more gold than I started with."
- **Fight:** "Their Cleave killed my whole front row in one swing. Four Sprouts came up in the same slots, each bigger than the last, then my puffball burst and buffed the lot. Losing the front row won me the fight."
- **Across the game:** "Every unit I lost or sold went into the Register. By turn 12 Grandmother Bootlace was reading it out, and every Sprout came up at 15/15. My board was bigger dead than alive."

What the three share is that losing a unit, in a fight or in the shop, is the same act, and the slot it leaves fills with something sized by how many have gone. So the mechanic is:

**Laid to rest:** a friendly unit is laid to rest when it dies or is sold, in a fight or while recruiting, by any cause and from any tribe. Death and sale are one event, so sell triggers fire in fights and death triggers fire in the shop.

**Fruit** (new keyword): *When this is laid to rest, summon a Sprout in its slot.*

**Sprout** (token): a Guild unit, X/X, with no text. X is the number of friendly units laid to rest **this fight**, or **this turn while recruiting**, counting the one it came from. Sprouts summoned while recruiting stay. Sprouts summoned in a fight last that fight.

**The Register** (tribe counter, like the Larder or the Toll): the number of friendly units laid to rest this game, Sprouts included. It never goes down. The hero frame shows it.

**How it plays.** The Guild doesn't fear losing units. It plans the order of its funerals. Fruit units in the front row turn every enemy kill into a slot that refills bigger. Fruit units in the back die last, when the count is highest. In the shop, selling stops being a loss: a sold Fruit unit leaves a Sprout in its slot, and sell triggers make the shop, the board and the gold grow. "Any tribe" in *laid to rest* is the Johnny door. An Ooze eating in the shop, a Bird flying South, a Bog tank dying under its Mire: all of them feed the Guild, and Guild cards that hand out Fruit turn other tribes' units into mourners.

**Token:** Sprout (above). No spells or items needed.

**Which moment each card builds towards:**
- **Shop:** Apprentice, Mute, Steward, Inkcap, Sexton, Bellows, Old Fingers.
- **Fight:** Apprentice, Crape Spinner, Undertaker, Stinkhorn, Lamplighter, Cordwainer, Pallbearer, Raindrop, Amanita, Candlesnuff.
- **Across the game:** Mute, Inkcap, Ludwig, Bellows, Bootlace, Old Fingers.

**Interactions worth spelling out (the Johnny layer):**
- **Inkcap is a battery.** He dies of his own writing after about eight entries, and that death is itself laid to rest, so it triggers the rest of the board. Anything that adds Health recharges him: Hedgerow Pass, a Fairy Ring, Harvest Supper. Give him Fruit and he leaves a Sprout when he goes.
- **Pallbearer + Cordwainer:** each of the two Sprouts gets half the dead unit's stats, so a sold 20/20 Fruit unit comes back as two 10/10+X bodies. Selling no longer loses the stats.
- **Old Fingers wants empty slots,** and selling makes them. Sell your best non-Fruit unit for gold, leave its slot empty, and its copy fights there anyway. A Bird that Flew South is both away and digging back in. A Fruit sale fills its own slot with a Sprout, so you pick which units to sell clean.
- **Undertaker** kills the unit on his left at Start of Combat. That unit could be a Fruit unit (an early Sprout plus every laid-to-rest trigger), an Ooze (its Belly opens before the first attack) or a weak unit you'd rather turn into Attack. Because Start of Combat resolves in reading order, a later Old Fingers digs up the Undertaker's victim.
- **Sprouts take the dead unit's slot,** so slot-based bonuses (Fairy Rings, Hedgerow turns-in-slot runs) carry on in that slot.

## 3. Builds

1. **The Procession (fight).** Fruit units across the front (Apprentice, Bier Bearer, Pallbearer, and anything beside Stinkhorn), with Crape Spinner, Lamplighter and Cordwainer behind. Each enemy kill makes a larger slot, often two. Capstones: Candlesnuff Abbess, which chains Sprouts six times a fight, and Grandmother Bootlace, which scales every Sprout with the game's dead. Raindrop Puffball is the late burst.
2. **The Wake (shop).** Sell on purpose every turn. Mute, Steward and Sexton from the early tiers; Bellows Puffwell as the direction card; Ludwig grows off the count; Inkcap spends it. The loop is buy a Fruit unit, sell it, sell its Sprout. That's two laid-to-rest events for 2 net gold (three with Cordwainer): a buffed shop, two or three Bellows triggers and next turn's Ludwig growth. Old Fingers keeps the best sold units fighting.
3. **The Undertaker's Bargain (mixed).** The Guild as a host for other tribes' death effects. Bier Bearer and Stinkhorn give Fruit to non-Guild units, Undertaker opens an Ooze's Belly at Start of Combat, and Amanita punishes every kill the enemy makes. Played with 3–4 Guild units and a second tribe's core.

**With the other tribes in the lobby:**
- **Oozes:** Eat in the shop is a death, so it is laid to rest. An Ooze eating a Fruit unit gains the stats and leaves a Sprout. Undertaker opens Bellies early.
- **Birds:** Fly South is a sale, so it is laid to rest and triggers Bellows, Sexton and Steward. Old Fingers fights a copy of the Bird while it's away. Watch for the Nest Egg filling the slot Old Fingers needs.
- **Bog folk:** Mire tanks in front die on purpose, and Fruit refills the slot. Note the refill is not Mired.
- **Fairies:** a Ring under a Fruit slot buffs every Sprout that grows there.
- **Hedgerow:** Sprouts can become the "rightmost unit" that Pass feeds, and Pass and Supper keep Inkcap alive.
- **Hunters:** the weakest pairing. Pack beside Sprouts works, but Hunters want survivors.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Sell loops.** Laid to rest folds sales into deaths, which opens shop loops. I closed the obvious ones: Candlesnuff works in fights only, Sprouts still sell for 0, and Bellows, Sexton, Ludwig and Inkcap have caps. Even so, Cordwainer + Pallbearer + Bellows + Sexton is the combo to simulate first.
- **Hero damage.** Sprouts didn't start the fight, so they add no hero damage when the Guild wins. A Procession board can win fights and deal little damage. That may be fine, or the tribe may need a payoff for it.
- **Board as HP.** Undertaker's self-kill early in a fight gives a Sprout with X=1, which is a real cost. He could be a trap card for new players.
- **Rules needed:** is Eat a "death" (I assume yes)? Is Fly South a "sale" (I assume yes)? Does a copy summoned at Start of Combat get its own Start of Combat (I assume no)? Inkcap's Health drain during a fight: is the loss "permanent" while the fight's Health is separate?
- **Old Fingers can whiff** on a full board. That is intended tension, but it needs clear UI ("no empty slot").
- **Readability.** In a long fight, a slot can refill many times with growing numbers. Animation has to show the Sprout grow out of the dead unit, or the chain reads as noise.
- **Spike tracking load:** three counts (this fight, this turn, the Register). The hero frame should show them.
- **Hunters lobby** gives the Guild little to feed on outside its own units.
- **Ludwig can't be sold.** The drawback is deliberate, but it blocks sell triggers on him and makes it awkward to clear his slot.

## 5. Tier check

Body targets: 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14.

- **Tier 1:** Apprentice (tempo, 4 stats plus a Sprout) and Mute (seed, +2 permanent a turn, target ~1.6). On guide.
- **Tier 2:** Crape Spinner (buffer, ~6 in fight with 2–3 Sprouts), Bier Bearer (enabler, a lasting Fruit), Steward (economy, ~1 gold a turn, a third of a unit). On guide.
- **Tier 3:** Stinkhorn and Undertaker (enablers), Inkcap (buffer, ~6 a turn against ~5.6, then dies by design), Lamplighter (combat engine, 5–10 in fight). Inkcap's 1/8 body is above the body target on purpose, because its Health is fuel.
- **Tier 4:** Pallbearer and Ludwig (buffers; Ludwig ~10–12 a turn against ~11), Cordwainer (multiplier), Sexton (economy, ~8–12 stats of shop buffs bought a turn). On guide. Cordwainer is the first multiplier, as the guide expects.
- **Tier 5:** Bellows (direction, ~36 permanent a turn at the cap against ~24 target and ~60 top), Raindrop (combat engine, ~48 in fight against ~48), Amanita (tempo, ~30 retaliation damage plus a 12 body). Bellows is high on purpose because it is the sell build's whole reason.
- **Tier 6:** Bootlace and Candlesnuff (capstones, ~70–90 in fight each against ~84), Old Fingers (payoff: two copies of buffed units, around 40 stats plus their text). Together with Cordwainer, the capstones go well past ×4. That is the golden-ceiling high roll, and it is bounded by "six times a fight" and by fights ending.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Wrote the three moments first. All three are "losing a unit refills something bigger", so that became the core.
2. Folded sale into death ("laid to rest"), so the shop moment and the fight moment run on the same triggers. This is the main Johnny lever.
3. Sprouts take the dead unit's slot, because the board is HP and gaps never close. The slot refills rather than the board shrinking.
4. Sprout size is the count laid to rest so far, so death order becomes a positioning puzzle (front dies early and small, back dies late and big).
5. "Any tribe" in laid to rest, so Ooze Eat, Bird Fly South and Bog tanks plug in without Guild-specific text.
6. Fruit is grantable (Bier Bearer, Stinkhorn), so other tribes' units can be the mourners.
7. Added the Register as one lasting count for the across-the-game moment. Only Bootlace reads it, to keep the counters few.
8. Capped every shop-side trigger (twice, three times, at most +6) after tracing the buy-sell-Sprout loop. Candlesnuff works in fights only for the same reason.
9. Kept Sprouts selling for 0. Paying for them made each Fruit unit gold-neutral to cycle.
10. Gave Inkcap a built-in death, so a buffer is also a fuse and Health buffs become fuel. This is the "outside its intended purpose" use.
11. Made Old Fingers need empty slots, so selling and digging up compete with Fruit refills. That is a choice, not a bonus.
12. Names and lore came last, matched to the brief's figures by job: Bootlace scales from below, Inkcap writes himself away, Bellows hosts the feast.
