# The Shiny Syndicate (v1)

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: the Ledger.** Debts are bark slips pinned beside your hero, one per debt. The ravens hold them, and every coin you pay goes to the family.

- **IOU N:** a slip on your Ledger for N gold. The Ledger holds 5. A sixth IOU adds its N to your smallest IOU instead.
- **Borrow N:** gain N gold now and write an IOU N.
- **Interest:** Start of Turn, after income, you pay 1 gold per IOU. Interest doesn't shrink the IOU (the debt is never quite paid off). Gold you're short of is added to your oldest IOU.
- **Pay off:** while recruiting, put any amount of gold onto an IOU. At 0 it is **cleared**. *Fresh ink:* you can't pay an IOU on the turn it was written.
- **Collect: X** (new keyword): X happens once for each gold paid onto your Ledger, whether you paid it (interest, pay-offs) or someone **paid it for you** (cards below). During a fight, Collect gains last for that fight only.
- **Skim** (card text, not a keyword): the loser pays. Clear your oldest IOU; it counts as paid in full.

**How it plays.** The Syndicate takes power now, as gold or stats, and pays later. Debt is a cost, but it's also fuel: every coin of interest feeds the ravens who Collect. So the tribe has two halves that pull against each other. Borrowers (magpies, Thought, the pawnshop) write IOUs. Collectors (the ravens) turn payments into stats. And the family makes other people pay for you: beaten opponents, the enemies who killed one of yours, the other-tribe units paying protection on your board. The big decision every turn is whether to **hold** debt (interest fuels Collect, and Corbie's Eldest scales with open IOUs) or **clear** it (Joy and Grandmother Corbie pay out on each clear). Johnny gets a resource with five ways in and five ways out. Spike gets a loan to manage, with real interest.

## 3. Builds

**A. Running a tab (hold the debt).** Thought, Mr Magpie and the Father of the Bride write IOUs. Oast Clerk and the Wassail Warden Collect the interest. Corbie's Eldest turns four or five open IOUs into a big in-fight buff. Memory doubles every Collect, and Seven makes the whole tab free while still counting as paid. On a full Ledger, Seven's 5 free interest is 10 Collects with Memory, and Eldest adds +5/+5 to everyone in the fight.

**B. The pawnshop (do it again).** Uncle Jack pawns a When-played unit for 3 gold. When its IOU is cleared it comes home, and you play it again. Father of the Bride gives +4/+4 a second time, and so does any other tribe's When-played unit. The Bagman's Skim clears the oldest IOU after a win, so a pawned unit can come home paid for by the loser. Silas Glint turns the 3 gold from each pawn into +3/+3. Joy and Corbie pay out on each clear. The Magpie in the Mirror can copy Uncle Jack for two pawns a turn. Pawned copies still count toward triples, so you can bank a copy for gold and still triple it. *(Resembles Battlegrounds' sell-and-rebuy Battlecry lines, but here the gold comes first and you pay for the replay later.)*

**C. The family business (mixed board).** Keep two or three strong units from another tribe and give them a Syndicate frame. The Gallows Lane Enforcer has them pay your interest and gives them Shield. Silas Glint feeds on other tribes' gold: Hedgerow Acorns, a Shy Fairy's 2 gold, neutral gold units. Sorrow fences copies of the units that beat you. The Mirror copies your best foreign unit, and that copy counts toward its triple. Arne turns spare gold into stats. Here the Ledger becomes a gold-and-Shield service for someone else's carry.

**D. Win and skim (tempo).** Mr Magpie, the Bagman, the Scolding Jackdaw and Joy beside Sorrow. Borrow early to tier up fast, win fights, and let the losers clear your slips. Black feathers pay off debt mid-fight, and those payments fire Collect in the same fight. Sorrow softens losing streaks.

**In a lobby.** The Syndicate cares about gold and about who pays, not about a tribe tag. So it mixes well with any tribe that makes gold (Hedgerow, Fairies' Shy) and with any carry that wants Shield (Hunters' Pack units, Bog folk's Mire tanks under the Enforcer). The Silver-Spoon Magpie answers Shield and Bristle boards: place it across their key unit, since you can see your next opponent's last board. Interactions to settle: pawning a Bird replaces its sale, so it doesn't Fly South. A pawned unit isn't on the board, so an Ooze can't Eat it.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Collect stacking.** Memory, Warden, Clerk and Corbie with Seven's free interest could run away. Cheapest fix: cap Memory to "Collect effects", leaving clear effects out.
- **Pawn plus Skim.** If the only IOU you have is a pawn, every win brings the unit home for free: +3 gold and a replay each win. If sims show it's too strong, rule that pawn IOUs can't be Skimmed.
- **Sorrow's reach.** After a loss at Tier 3 it can offer a Tier 6 copy for 1–2 gold. That's a strong comeback, and maybe a swingy one. Possible cap: up to your tier +1.
- **Losing spiral.** Interest is paid while you're losing too. The 5-slip cap and "short gold goes onto the oldest IOU" stop it from ever freezing your turn, but a losing debt player still drains about 3–5 gold a turn. It needs a sim.
- **Ledger readability.** Interest, fresh ink, part payments and "counts as paid" are four rules. The slips must show their numbers and animate coins flying to the Oast.
- **Seven's Hidden** hides information in async scouting. The slot should look covered rather than empty, so an opponent who misreads the board doesn't feel cheated.
- **Silver-Spoon is swingy.** Against boards with no keywords it only steals Attack. That's fine as a counter-pick, weak as a default.
- **The Mirror** copies anything, including a foreign Tier 6 that came from Sorrow. Watch it for triple abuse.

## 5. Tier check

The body is 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14. "Gold paid" assumes about 3–4 gold a turn mid-game and 6–8 late.

- **Tier 1:** Mr Magpie is a 6-stat tempo body, 1.5× body, paid for with an IOU 2 plus interest: above the guide on purpose, because it's the tribe's entry into debt. Oast Clerk is the seed, at up to 4 stats a turn (about 1 body). That's above the 0.4 target, but it needs about 2 gold a turn paid. **Golden Mr Magpie keeps IOU 2 on purpose:** doubling a drawback would make the golden worse for players who don't run Collect.
- **Tier 2:** the Bagman is the economy piece, about 1–1.5 gold a turn at a 50% win rate. Silas Glint gives about 4–8 stats a turn off pawns, sells and borrowing: at or a bit over the 0.3–1 body range. The Father of the Bride is a once-off buffer: 8 stats (target ~8), with a debt attached.
- **Tier 3:** Thought (enabler) gives 2 gold a turn and writes slips. The Scolding Jackdaw (combat engine) focuses fire and pays up to 2 gold a fight. Sorrow is the economy piece and Joy the buffer: Joy gives 4–8 stats per clear, and paired with Sorrow up to about 12. **Joy with Sorrow runs up to about 2× on purpose.** The rhyme pair has to be worth placing side by side.
- **Tier 4:** the Warden gives about 8–10 a turn (target 11). The Enforcer gives 2 gold of debt relief plus 2 Shields, roughly 1.5× the in-fight target. That's deliberate: it's the entry fee for the mixed build. Uncle Jack is the enabler and economy piece. The Mirror is the first multiplier.
- **Tier 5:** Eldest gives 4 IOUs × 7 units × 2 = 56 in a fight (target ~48). Memory is the multiplier, and a two-card high roll with Collect units reaches the top of about 60 a turn. Silver-Spoon swings about 12 Attack plus whatever keywords it steals.
- **Tier 6:** Corbie gives 42 stats per 3-gold clear and 1–2 clears a turn (target 56; top 140). Seven is worth up to 5 gold a turn plus its Collect growth. Arne gives 84 stats for 6 gold (target 56), because she eats the gold that would otherwise have bought two units.
- **Goldens:** Golden Memory (×3) with golden Corbie is the runaway risk. It's a real high roll, but it needs two goldens and a full Ledger. Run it through the two-pass runaway check before playtest.

## 6. Design rationale

1. I started with the IOUs scratched on bark: debt as an object you can see and count, beside the hero, so debt doesn't take up hand space.
2. Interest is 1 per slip, and it never shrinks the debt. "The debt never quite paid off" gives debt a steady cost without forcing a repayment schedule.
3. The ravens hold everyone's debts, so I added Collect. Paid gold flows to the family as stats, which turns debt from a pure cost into fuel: the off-label use Johnny wants.
4. The lone magpie you greet became Mr Magpie: stats on credit, so the tribe's first card is already a way into debt.
5. The pawnshop became Uncle Jack. Pawning is borrowing against a unit, and getting it back replays its When-played text, which gives the tribe a retrigger engine.
6. "Skim from whoever you beat" became the Bagman: the loser clears your slip, so winning fights feeds the Ledger.
7. The jackdaws who learn a face became the black feather. It pays debt in the middle of a fight, so Collect matters in combat as well as while recruiting.
8. Sorrow and Joy are never seen apart, so they get a beside bonus. One for sorrow pays out on a loss (the fence) and two for joy on a clear.
9. Fresh ink and the 5-slip cap came after the first cards showed two holes: borrowing and paying back in the same turn for free Collects, and a debt spiral nobody could get out of.
10. I wanted a tension Spike would feel, so Corbie's Eldest pays you to stay in debt and Grandmother Corbie pays you to clear it.
11. Protection money and the finder's fee reach into other tribes on purpose, so the Syndicate can serve another tribe's carry.
12. Seven and Arne went to Tier 6 because theirs are the founding stories: the secret account and the island sold for gold. Each is a payoff for a different half of the Ledger.
