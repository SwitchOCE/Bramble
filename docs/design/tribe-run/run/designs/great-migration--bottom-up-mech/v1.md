# The Great Migration (v1)

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: the Herd and its Burrow.** The tribe makes **Kits**: identical little Lemming tokens. Kits that don't fit on the board wait in the **Burrow**, an off-board reserve shown on your hero. **Onward** turns that reserve into fight HP. Every card in the tribe either grows the Herd, makes Kits bigger, or spends Kits on something else.

- **Kit (token):** a Lemming whose stats are your **Kit size**. Kit size starts at 1/1, and cards raise it permanently for every Kit you have, now and later, in the Burrow too. Kits are Created, sell for 0, aren't in the pool and never make triples. Harvest Supper's Migration bonus reaches them as well.
- **Burrow:** a Kit made with no empty slot goes into your Burrow. While recruiting, you can move Kits between the board and the Burrow for free. A Kit that goes into the Burrow loses its own buffs and keeps only Kit size. Your **Herd** is all your Kits, on the board and in the Burrow.
- **Onward (new keyword):** *When this dies, a Kit from your Burrow takes its slot.* Each Burrow Kit can come out once a fight. Fights never cost you Kits: the Burrow is full again afterwards.

**How it plays.** Early on, Kits are free bodies that let you attack first and fill gaps. By the midgame you're running a stockpile that has three jobs at once. On the board it fills slots, which earns gold and moves Kits out of reach of the cards that spend from the Burrow. In the Burrow it's the Onward chain's fuel and ammunition for Ole. Spent, it becomes permanent stats on your real units. The tribe's numbers puzzles all pull on that one pile. You grow **count** (Kit makers) or **size** (Kit size), and they multiply. Spending at a good rate leaves you short of fight fuel, and some cards trigger at Herd thresholds, so a Kit spent below the next threshold is free. The tribe owns no big bodies of its own, so its best boards borrow a few strong units from other tribes and wrap them in Kits.

**Rules notes.**
- A Kit that comes out through Onward counts as the dying unit's own summon, so it takes that slot. If the slot is taken (by an Ooze's Belly, say), it goes to the nearest empty slot.
- A unit with Onward from two sources still brings out only one Kit.
- Ole's Kits *die*, so Cantor sees them.

## 3. Builds

**The Endless Tide (Burrow as fight HP).** Nester, Matron, Litter Keeper and Herald build a Burrow of 12–20 Kits. Front Lemming, then Old Mother Lemen, pour it out through Onward. Gerd makes every Kit an enemy hits cost it 3 Health, so a long chain of Kits wears down a board of giants. Cantor turns each Kit death into stats on your one big unit, which is often another tribe's unit with Cleave or Shield. The weakness: Kits that come out of the Burrow didn't start the fight, so a win deals little hero damage. The Tide wins rounds and hits lightly.

**The Crossing (Burrow as currency).** Keep only three or four real units, ideally strong ones from another tribe, and feed them Kits. Raft-builder cashes Kits at a great early rate, and Herding Council takes 2 a turn. Ferrywife drains half the Burrow every turn into permanent stats. The puzzle is the order of operations. Herald counts the Burrow at Start of Turn. Matron counts the Herd at End of Turn, so seat her before the Council and the Ferrywife in reading order. Any Kit you want to keep, move onto the board before End of Turn, where the spenders can't reach it. The Crossing and the Tide fight over the same pile, and most games settle on a ratio between them.

**The Banner (whoever is in front leads).** Brother Onward, plus Front Lemming or Snow Hall Guard, at F1. Front Kits die quickly and pass a Banner that grows each time: after five deaths, the sixth front unit carries +24/+24. Put a Kit at F1 so the Banner grows early, then a sturdy unit behind it to catch it. A Bog Mire unit or a Hunker unit is ideal, because it has to be attacked and doesn't die.

**Kits for anyone (splash).** Squeaker plus Identical Cousin is a two-card package for any comp. Cousin turns a spare Kit into a copy of your key unit, and Created copies count for triples, so two copies of a Fairy or an Ooze plus a Kit make a golden and a Discover.

**Mixing in a lobby.**
- *Oozes:* Eat a Kit for a free permanent snack at Kit size, and a Belly can hold Kits. Nester is an endless pantry.
- *Fairies:* a Ring slot with an Onward unit on it gives every Kit that steps in the Ring bonus. A ringed F1 under Front Lemming is a Kit factory with +4/+4 per Ring.
- *Hedgerow:* tuck Kits into the Burrow so Pass reaches a real rightmost unit.
- *Birds:* a Nest is an empty slot, so Tally fails, but a Bird away South frees a slot for Kits.
- *Bog:* a Mire unit at the front is the best Banner holder.

## 4. Risks and holes

1. **Fight length and readability.** An Onward chain of 15 Kits and a passing Banner could make fights long and hard to follow. It needs a quick Kit-emerges animation and a Burrow count that ticks down on the hero.
2. **Quadratic growth.** Kit count times Kit size grows on two axes. Herald plus Litter Keeper is the worst pair, at up to 10 Kits a turn. Litter Keeper's cap and Herald's cap keep both linear, but the Tide with a golden Mother Lemen and a golden Cantor needs a simulation check.
3. **Stalling.** Plain 1/1 Kits with Onward and Bristle could drag fights out against boards with low Attack. Ties deal nothing, so a Tide that can't kill gives a lot of ties.
4. **Identical Cousin as a generic card.** It works for any comp that owns a Kit. The guard is that Kits come only from Migration cards, but Squeaker is a Tier 1, so splashing is cheap. If Cousin turns up in every comp, require a *Lemming* next to it, or make the copy lose its text.
5. **Tally outside the tribe.** Late in a game every board is full, so Tally is a generic gold-a-turn card. That may be fine (it costs a slot), or it may need "if 3 or more are Kits".
6. **Hero damage.** The Tide's surfaced Kits add nothing to hero damage. That's a deliberate check, but the owner might feel it undercuts the fantasy.
7. **Hidden information.** The Burrow is off the board, so its count should show on the opponent's hero.
8. **Rules gaps.** End of Turn order (assumed reading order) and Onward against Belly summons need a ruling.

## 5. Tier check

Kit value is taken at Kit size 1/1 early and 2/2–3/3 in a good setup. Bodies are 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14.

- **Tier 1:** Squeaker gives 3 stats plus a Kit, 5 once (1.25 bodies), a tempo Alleycat. Nester gives 2 stats a turn (0.5 bodies against 0.4), a little high on purpose, because Kits pay off only once the slot and Kit-size cards arrive.
- **Tier 2:** Tally's 1 gold a turn is about a third of a unit, which fits the economy slot. Weaver is about 6 stats now plus every future Kit, about 1.3 bodies. Digger's two Onwards bring out about 4–6 stats in a fight, about 1 body. The bodies are slightly under 6 to pay for the text.
- **Tier 3:** Front Lemming brings out 3 Kits, 6–12 stats in a fight, 0.9–1.7 bodies. That's at or slightly over the 0.7–1.4 range, on purpose: it's the card that names the comp. Matron makes up to 3 Kits, 6 stats a turn (0.86 bodies). Raft-builder gives 12 stats once for 3 Kits, net about 1 body. Ole deals 3–9 damage in a fight (0.4–1.3 bodies).
- **Tier 4:** Council gives about +1/+1 to five units, 10 a turn (1.2 bodies), and costs 2 Kits. Litter Keeper adds up to 4 Kits, 8–16 a turn, on target to slightly high. Gerd reflects 3 per Kit hit, about 15 a fight (1.8 bodies against 2). Cousin's value is a body of 8–14 plus text (1–1.6 bodies) plus triple progress. The triple progress is the real price, and the multiplier slot here is spent on copying rather than stats.
- **Tier 5:** Herald makes up to 6 Kits, 24–36 a turn (2–3 bodies against 2, top 5), capped so it can't run away. Cantor gives 8 Kit deaths at 4–6 stats, 32–48 in a fight (2.7–4 bodies against 4). Snow Hall Guard gives +4 to about 8 Kits plus a 14-stat body, about 3.8 bodies in a fight. It's the tempo piece that holds the line.
- **Tier 6:** Mother Lemen pours out a Burrow of 10 at 3/3 to 4/4, plus her aura, about 70–90 in a fight (5–6.4 bodies against 6). Brother Onward's Banner over 5 front deaths totals about 80 in a fight (5.7 bodies). Ferrywife at a steady Burrow of 8–12 gives 4–6 Kits a turn to six units, 48–72 a turn (3.4–5 bodies against 4). It eats the Tide's fuel, which is the trade.
- **Shape changes:** Tier 4 has a combat engine (Gerd) in place of a second buffer. This tribe's in-fight power comes through many small bodies, so an engine that pays per Kit hit belongs where the Burrow first gets big. Tier 6 has two capstones that compete (Tide against Crossing) plus the Banner payoff.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Started with the slot puzzle: a full board pays (Tally), but you need an empty slot to play. Free filler bodies make that a decision every turn.
2. Filler that can't be sold at value needs somewhere to go, so overflow became a reserve: the Burrow.
3. A reserve is dull unless it's spent three ways at different rates. Raft-builder, Council and Ferrywife convert it to stats, Ole to damage, Onward to fight HP. That's the leftover-rate puzzle.
4. Onward emerged as what the death and reserve cards had in common, so it became the keyword. Fights never cost Kits, so the reserve stays a long-term number.
5. Kit size as a single standing stat ("more recipients, not bigger numbers") gave a second axis, count against size, that multiply.
6. Making the Burrow the only place spenders reach gave a positional trick: move a Kit onto the board to protect it.
7. Matron's thresholds (every 4) make some spending free, a deliberate small leftover puzzle.
8. Ole's Kits die and can't come out again: the same Kit is either a missile or a body, and it feeds Cantor.
9. Reading order already decides who attacks first, so "the front" became a role. Front Lemming at Tier 3, the growing Banner at Tier 6.
10. Created copies count for triples, so a Kit became raw material for a copy (Cousin). That's the main cross-tribe, use-it-for-something-else card.
11. Caps on every self-feeding Kit maker (Matron, Litter Keeper, Herald) keep count growth linear. Fight-only payoffs (Cantor, Banner) carry the big numbers.
12. Names and lore came last: lemmings making Kits, a fox census-taker, a learned lemming who proves they fall from the sky, the Edge cult, and the crossing of the Wide Water.
