# The Changelings — v1

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: Changelings wear other units' text.** Each one has small stats and a small text of its own, plus one borrowed **Guise**. A Guise can come from the shop, from your own board, from a unit you sell, or from the enemy across. A second verb, **Exchange**, swaps one of your units with a unit in the shop, so the tribe trades with the shop as well as buying from it.

**Guise (new keyword).** A Changeling can wear one other unit's Guise. That means the unit's own text (as it stands, golden if that unit is golden) and its tribe, on top of the Changeling's own. Stats are never copied unless a card says so.
- A new Guise replaces the old one.
- **One of each:** no two of your Changelings wear the same unit's Guise. If one takes a Guise another already wears, the other loses it.
- A Guise taken while recruiting stays on the card wherever it goes: hand, shop, or flying south. If the Changeling wears it when played, the Guise's "When played" fires too. A Guise taken in a fight ends with the fight, and the earlier Guise comes back.
- A Guise copies a unit's own text, never that unit's Guise. It never copies a Tier 6 unit.

**Exchange (verb).** Put a friendly unit into the shop in place of a shop unit you choose, and take that shop unit into your hand (you need a free hand space). The swap is fair: the new unit counts as Bought if you gave a Bought unit, and as Created otherwise. Until the shop refreshes you can buy back the unit you gave, with its buffs and Guise, at the normal price. After a refresh it goes back to the pool, and a token is removed.

**How it plays.** A Changeling player is a guest in someone else's tribe. Early on you pick up a Kit and a Hob and borrow the best text in the shop or on your board. In the middle game you decide whether to keep your Guises, which pays over time, or keep swapping them, which pays each time. You also decide whether to work the shop with Exchanges. Because a Guise brings its tribe with it, Changelings count as Birds, Oozes or Hedgerow folk for those tribes' own cards. The best Changeling board is two tribes at once. Late on, the Lady Under the Hill wears all of your Guises at once, the Fetch copies your carry for each fight, and Bettie Underhill pays out for every Exchange you made this game.

## 3. Builds

**The Masquerade (Guise bridge).** Cradle Kit, Pip, Counter Stand-In, Tam Twice and Grandfer Acorn, building to the Lady and the Keeper of the Lost Hour. You pick a second tribe from the lobby and wear its best texts. A Kit wearing a Bird's Guise is a Bird, so selling it sends it south and it comes home with doubled stats, still in its Guise. With Hedgerow folk, Pip mirrors "Pass X: your rightmost other unit" into a leftmost pass, so you get a second pass lane. A Changeling wearing an Ooze's Belly holds units like an Ooze. The Matron grows with each tribe your Guises bring. The Lady then holds all those texts in one body: Belly, Mire and Pass on one 7/7.

**The Board of Exchanges (shop swap).** Clerk of Exchanges, Midnight Swap, Wicker Moll, Placement Officer and Coat-Peg Steward, building to Bettie Underhill. Exchange a spent or small unit for the shop's best each turn. Moll leaves a Stock in its slot that keeps the old unit's text, and the Steward moves the old unit's buffs onto the new one. Off-label uses:
- Exchange a buffed unit away and **freeze**: the shop holds it as an eighth slot until you buy it back.
- Buy it back to replay its "When played".
- Exchange a 0-value Stock to get a free shop unit.

Bettie pays once for each Exchange in the game, so starting early matters.

**The Long Swap (keepers).** Weeks-Old Hob, Veil-Stitcher, Long-Stay Hollin and the Matron. Put high-tier Guises on early and never change them. A Kit played at Tier 5 copies a Tier 5 shop unit, and Hollin then pays +5/+5 a turn for it. This pulls against Grandfer and the Stand-In, which pay for changing Guises. Choosing between keeping and swapping is the tribe's main decision in the middle game.

**Impostors (fight copying).** Turncoat Hare, Penny Second, the Keeper and the Fetch. You can see your next opponent's last board, so you place Turncoat and Penny across their key unit. Against Bog folk, Penny takes the Mire unit's text, the enemy loses its Mire, and Penny holds the Mire instead. Against Hunters, Penny takes the Quarry text. The Keeper fires stolen Start of Combat text twice. The Fetch makes a copy of your carry for each fight, if you keep a slot empty for it.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Reading the board.** A Changeling has two texts, Tam has three and the Lady can have five or more. The UI has to show a Guise as a compact second line or a tag, or Changeling boards become unreadable. This is the biggest look-and-feel cost.
- **Borrowed economy.** A Guise of a neutral gold unit doubles that gold. The one-of-each rule caps it at one extra copy, plus the Lady. If it is still too much, gold text pays half when worn as a Guise.
- **Strength depends on the lobby.** Changelings are only as good as the other tribes and neutrals that roll. In a weak lobby the tribe has low stats and borrows little.
- **Weak identity.** Players may feel they are playing someone else's tribe. The Changeling payoffs (Grandfer, Veil-Stitcher, Hollin, Bettie) are there to keep the tribe the owner of the board.
- **Hand space.** A full hand blocks an Exchange. Players may find this annoying at Start of Turn with the Placement Officer.
- **Penny's silence** can feel bad for the player who loses their key text. It hits one unit, needs Penny to be the bigger unit, and is visible from the recorded board.
- **Stock clutter.** Moll can fill the board with weak 1/3 Stocks. They sell for 0 and make good Exchange fodder, but a board left full of Stocks loses fights.
- **Rules edges.** These need playtests: a Guise firing its "When played" on replay, fight Guises giving way to recruit Guises, Exchanged units in a frozen shop, the Lady with the Fetch.
- **The Keeper and the Lady** together are the high roll. The Keeper only doubles fight triggers, and Tier 6 text can't be copied, so it stays inside a fight.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** Bodies 4 and 4, at the median. The Kit's borrowed text is about one T1 card's text, once (target ~1). The Hob gives +2 stats a turn, about 0.5 body (target 0.4). That is slightly over, but it needs a Guise kept all turn.
- **Tier 2:** Bodies 5, below the median of 6 on purpose, because the Guise or Exchange text is worth a second card. The Clerk is worth about 2 gold once, about 1 body. The Veil-Stitcher gives 4–6 stats a turn with 2–3 Guised units (0.7–1 body, at the top of the range). Pip is a once-off copy.
- **Tier 3:** Bodies 7, at the median. Grandfer gives about 6 a turn at two Guises a turn (0.85 body). Moll gives a 1/3 Stock plus a kept text a turn (~0.8). Turncoat gives a copied enemy text for one fight (~1 body in fight). The Stand-In gives a text per sell.
- **Tier 4:** Bodies 8–9. The Matron gives 12–16 a turn across 3–4 tribes (1.4–1.9 bodies). That is slightly over because it needs breadth. The Officer is worth about 2 gold a turn. Tam's two Guises match "repeat one" (Kelp Keeper) at this tier. **Broken on purpose:** T4 has both a multiplier (Tam) and an economy piece (Officer), with one buffer, because the Exchange line needs a repeatable source before its T6 payoff.
- **Tier 5:** Bodies 12–13. Hollin gives ~32 a turn with four Changelings at Guise tier 4 (2.7 bodies, above the 2 target, under the top of 5), as the direction card. Penny is a copy plus a silence in fight (~3–4 bodies against a key unit). The Keeper is the Brann slot, narrowed to fight triggers.
- **Tier 6:** Bodies 14–15. Bettie gives ~56 a turn with 7 Exchanges and four Changelings (4 bodies, on target). The Fetch's copy of the carry is ~6 bodies in fight (on target). The Lady wears 3–5 texts: a capstone with no fixed number. **Broken on purpose:** a Guise can't copy Tier 6 text, so capstones can't stack.
- **Runaway check:** No unit's gain feeds its own source: Hollin's pay rests on a fixed tier, and Bettie's on an Exchange count. The first pass found two loops: Bettie copied through Pip, Tam and the Lady (about 800 a turn), and the Keeper doubling End of Turn buffs. Three rules closed them: one of each, no Tier 6 Guises, and fight-only doubling. The worst case left is Hollin worn three ways (~120 a turn), under T6's top. Goldens double a Guise's numbers, which stays inside ×2–4.

## 6. Design rationale

1. **Gap: a closed shop.** No tribe interacts with the shop except by buying; Fairies' Rings and Hedgerow's Pass both work only on your own board.
2. **Gap: no bridge, no counterplay.** Every tribe's keywords feed only itself. No card reads the enemy's text, although the next opponent's board is visible and "across" already exists.
3. **Guise is the core,** because one verb fills the bridge gap and is the seed's "become what you replace". It copies text and tribe but not stats, so the tribe stays small-bodied and the borrowed text is the value.
4. **Exchange is the second verb,** to fill the shop gap and put the folklore swap into the rules: a fair trade, with a Stock left in place.
5. **Exchanged units arrive in hand,** so their "When played" fires and Moll's Stock can take the empty slot.
6. **Each tier adds a new Guise source,** rather than a bigger number: shop (T1), friend (T1–2), sold unit and enemy (T3), two at once (T4), every Guise (T6). This follows the "more recipients, not bigger packets" rule.
7. **Keep or swap is the main decision:** Hob and Hollin pay for keeping a Guise, Grandfer and the Stand-In for changing it.
8. **Penny and Turncoat use the visible next board,** so placing units across the enemy becomes a planning decision. The silence needs Penny to be the bigger unit, so building Penny's stats matters.
9. **The Matron counts tribes,** so borrowing many tribes pays and the bridge is rewarded, not just allowed.
10. **The guardrails came out of the runaway pass:** one of each, no Tier 6 Guises, and the Keeper doubling fight triggers only.
11. **Names and flavour came last.**
