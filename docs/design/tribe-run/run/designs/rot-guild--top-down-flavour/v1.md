# The Rot Guild — v1

## 1. Identity

**The fantasy.** You run the woodland's funeral parlour. Every unit you lose, in a fight or by your own hand, is laid down into the soil, and the next morning the Guild's mushrooms come up fat on it. The solemn toadstools do the burying while you recruit; the puffballs burst laughing in the fight. Grief at the graveside, a feast by the next rain.

**Compost** (tribe tracker, shown under the board). Each time a friendly unit dies, in a fight or Laid to Rest while recruiting, your Compost gains its **printed** Attack and Health (card stats, doubled if golden; buffs don't count). At Start of Turn, after every Fruit has resolved, the Compost empties: mourning lasts one rain.

**Fruit** (keyword). Start of Turn: this permanently gains a quarter of your Compost. A unit can have Fruit more than once, and each one Fruits separately. Golden cards with Fruit have it twice.

**Lay to Rest** (action). Destroy a friendly unit while recruiting. It counts as dying: its "When this dies" text happens now, on the recruiting board, and it goes into the Compost.

**How it plays.** The Guild's units are thin and its fights are often lost early, but nothing is wasted. Deaths in the fight and burials in the shop fill one shared pile, and every Fruit unit drinks a quarter of it overnight. So the pile is your engine and Fruit is your carry count. The tribe's cards pull on three levers: **more dying** (Spores, Amanita, Old Fingers), **bigger or doubled entries** (Inkcap, Stinkhorn, White-Rot, Three Rains) and **more Fruit** (Sealbearer, Rootbinder, Bootlace). Because the Compost counts any friendly unit and Fruit can go on any unit, the Guild is as happy feeding another tribe's carry as its own. Placement matters twice: Amanita and Sexton care who is beside whom, and Bootlace's network needs an unbroken touching run, which your own burials keep breaking.

## 3. Builds

**The Wake (shop burials).** Undertaker, Sexton, Renderer, Ludwig, Graveside Service; Puffling and Spores as fodder. Every turn you bury one or two cheap units in the middle of your board: Sexton buffs both neighbours, Ludwig eats the body whole, and the Compost holds it for tomorrow. A Puffling buried leaves a Spore to bury next turn; Hired Mute pays for the busy graveyard. Renderer buries a pumped unit at full value, which is how you move a big buff from a dying carry onto every Fruit unit. Its payoff is Ludwig plus Mourner of Three Rains, which keeps half the pile so a burial pays out for several turns.

**The Burst (puffballs dying in the fight).** Puffling, Raincatcher, Amanita, Grandsire, Inkcap, Old Fingers, Puffwell. You want as many deaths per fight as possible: Raincatcher splits into Spores as it's hit, Amanita kills her neighbours at Start of Combat so their death text fires before a blow is struck, Old Fingers digs everything up once more, and Inkcap writes each death into the Register twice. Any one Fruit carry turns all that into permanent stats. Puffwell is the in-fight cash-out: give him Damage Reduction or put him in the back row so he dies late, when the pile is biggest.

**The Network (Fruit everywhere).** Apprentice, Sealbearer, Rootbinder, Bootlace. Fewer deaths, more mouths: Bootlace turns a full touching run into seven Fruit units. The tension is that the Wake's burials leave gaps that cut the run, so you bury from the ends of the zigzag (F1, F4) or refill the gap before Start of Turn.

**The Lodger (the Guild as another tribe's undertaker).** Rootbinder and Sealbearer give Fruit to a carry from another tribe, and Stinkhorn fills the Compost from enemy deaths, so a board that wins fights still feeds. With other tribes:
- **Oozes:** an Ooze Eating your own unit is a friendly death, so it goes into the Compost. Put an Ooze with a full Belly beside Amanita and it opens at Start of Combat. A buried Ooze opens in the shop.
- **Hedgerow:** Fruit on your rightmost unit stacks with Pass, so one unit takes both streams.
- **Fairies:** a Fruit unit on a Ring grows from both sources. Note that Shy units leave, which is not death.
- **Bog:** a Mired Raincatcher is forced to take hits, so it makes its three Spores reliably.
- **Hunters:** Pack units die beside their leader, which feeds Moss-Bier's permanent buffs.
- **Birds:** a Bird sold to Fly South doesn't count; a Bird Laid to Rest never comes home. Nest Eggs compost as 0/1.

**Off-label plays Johnny will look for:**
- Seal Fruit onto Inkcap: his Health grows every turn, so he records more deaths each fight.
- Seal Fruit onto Grandsire: his death Spores get bigger.
- Bury a pumped Grandsire in the shop to fill your board with permanent quarter-size Spores.
- Renderer a big unit, then have Amanita kill Puffwell at Start of Combat: the whole board feasts on that burial for the fight.
- Amanita beside Old Fingers' targets: they die at Start of Combat, come back at printed stats, and their death text fires twice.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Losing feeds you.** More deaths means more Compost, so a weak board snowballs into a stronger one. That is a comeback lever, and Stinkhorn rewards winning too, but the tribe may have little reason to win early fights. Watch whether early hero damage kills Guild players before the pile matters.
- **Fruit stacking.** Two Sealbearers and a Bootlace on one unit give Fruit ×4 on a single carry. That's intended as a high roll, but it may need a cap ("at most three Fruit") if it dominates.
- **Golden Mourner.** Keeping the whole pile makes it grow every turn, and every Fruit unit gains a quarter of a growing pile each turn, so growth is quadratic. It isn't self-feeding (printed stats only), but it's the likeliest runaway. The fallback is "keeps three quarters".
- **Visibility.** The Compost pile, its sources, what's printed versus buffed, and the per-death doubling from Inkcap all need clear UI or the tribe reads as noise.
- **Old Fingers with death chains.** Copies re-fire death text, so Spores from Puffling and Grandsire summon again. Six times a fight limits it, but a golden copy that keeps all its stats is very large.
- **Rootbinder is dead text on a Guild-only board.** That is intended, to pull the Guild into mixed boards, but it may feel bad in a lobby where the other tribes are weak.
- **Hired Mute triggers too easily** once Spores are around. Raise the threshold to 4 if it's a free gold.
- **Start of Combat order.** Amanita resolves in reading order, so neighbours with their own Start of Combat may or may not have fired. Players need to see that.
- **Undecided rule:** back-row access isn't settled, and Puffwell's "die late" plan leans on the back row being safe.

## 5. Tier check

Estimates are for a good mid-game board: about 5 friendly deaths a fight, printed stats about 6 each, so roughly 30 Compost a turn and about 7 per Fruit.

- **Tier 1:** Puffling is a normal two-body tempo unit (5 stats). The Apprentice gives about 3 a turn early, which is near the guide, but about 7 by mid-game, nearly 2 bodies against a 0.4 target. **Broken on purpose:** it's the seed the whole tribe grows from, its 1/3 body is small, and it's worthless without deaths. If it dominates, it fruits only while you have another Rot Guild unit.
- **Tier 2:** Moss-Bier gives 4–6 permanent stats a turn (about 1 body, inside 0.3–1). Undertaker turns a sell into a burial at the same 1 gold. Mute gives 1 gold a turn on a condition, which is in line with economy pieces.
- **Tier 3:** Sealbearer adds about 7 a turn for the rest of the game (about 1 body against 0.8). Raincatcher makes about 6 in-fight stats plus Compost (inside 0.7–1.4). Sexton gives 4–8 a turn. Amanita is negative on her own and pays only through death text; that's her job as the enabler that names the comp.
- **Tier 4:** Rootbinder gives about 14 a turn and Stinkhorn about 15 with three Fruit units (both 1.6–1.8 bodies against 1.3). Inkcap doubles fight Compost, about 18 a turn across three Fruit units. That's above target, as a T4 multiplier is allowed to be. Renderer is a one-off redistribution, not new stats.
- **Tier 5:** Ludwig gives 20–30 a turn (about 2 bodies). Mourner roughly doubles every Fruit, which makes it the tier's multiplier. Grandsire is weak at printed size (1/2 Spores) but gives 30–70 in-fight once buffed, around the 4-body target; it is meant to be fed.
- **Tier 6:** Bootlace with a full run gives seven Fruit, about 105 a turn (7.5 bodies, under the top ~10). With Inkcap, Stinkhorn and Mourner assembled it reaches about twice the top. **Broken on purpose:** it's the capstone the comp is built for, and its golden ceiling is ×2. Old Fingers gives about 60 in-fight plus re-fired death text (about 5–6 bodies). Puffwell gives 40–120 in-fight depending on when he dies.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Took "death as harvest" as the core: deaths become a shared pile of stats, because the seed's play line is "lose your own on purpose".
2. The pile counts **printed** stats only, so growth never feeds its own source: the runaway check passes by construction.
3. "Growth overnight": the pile pays out at Start of Turn and then empties, so every burial is a delayed reward.
4. "The quiet network": the pile belongs to the board, not a unit, and Fruit lets any unit drink from it, which gives Johnny cross-tribe hooks for free.
5. "The undertaker caused the death": Lay to Rest is the shop-side sacrifice, and Amanita is the fight-side one.
6. "Mourning and mirth": toadstools work in the shop (burial, registers, seals) and puffballs work in the fight (bursting into Spores), two halves that feed one pile.
7. Spores compost as 1/1 whatever their size, so Grandsire's big Spores can't loop.
8. The seed's notable figures took the high tiers by their stories: Inkcap writes deaths twice, Bootlace is the network, Old Fingers digs up, Puffwell is the feast, Ludwig the lodger eats the dead wood.
9. The "one rain or three" dispute became Mourner of Three Rains, the T5 multiplier that keeps the pile.
10. "Giving back what the wood gave": Rootbinder (mycorrhiza) gives Fruit to other tribes, so the Guild has a reason to be splashed into any lobby.
11. Stinkhorn composts enemy deaths, so the tribe isn't only rewarded for losing (the Spike guard).
12. Bootlace's run plus gaps from burials: put a spatial cost on the strongest payoff, so Johnny's boards need arranging, not just collecting.
