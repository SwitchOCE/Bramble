# The Moon Hares (v1)

## 1. Identity

### Three moments this tribe is built to produce

- **In the shop: "I sold my way to the full moon."** "The moon was one phase short of Full. I sold Old Wat for nothing on purpose, because selling him turns the moon. Full came mid-shop, my Charmer fired, and the Jack beside her borrowed her text and fired it again. Then I had to choose: turn once more and pass through to New for my Sixpence gold, or stop and fight under the Full moon. I stopped."
- **In a fight: "The moon turned halfway through."** "The fight started under a Waxing moon and I was losing. Then my Moon-Caller died, the moon turned to Full mid-fight, and everything I had left changed shape: my Witch borrowed their Ooze's Belly, and Longshanks stopped taking damage and boxed their best unit to death."
- **Across the game: "My leverets grew up."** "All game, my back row was three 1/1 leverets doing nothing while the Dusk Nurse fed them. On every full moon they borrowed whatever unit stood in front of them. Then I found the Grandam, and at the next New Moon they all came of age as real copies of my best Hares, keeping everything they'd been fed. One of those copies made a triple."

What the three have in common: **you wind a clock, and when it strikes, Hares take something that isn't theirs.** That gives the tribe one shared clock, the Moon, and one keyword, Borrow.

### The Moon

- Every player has a **moon**, shown by their hero and visible to opponents. It starts **New** on turn 1 and steps one phase at each Start of Turn: **New → Waxing → Full → Waning → New**. Without help, the moon is Full on turns 3, 7, 11 and 15.
- **Turn the moon:** step it one phase now, in the shop or in a fight. Each phase it enters fires its triggers.
- **Full Moon: / New Moon:** triggers when your moon enters that phase.
- **Moonlit:** text that works only while your moon is Full, in the shop and in fights.
- The moon stays where a fight leaves it. A moon turned in a fight is still turned next turn.

### New keyword: Borrow

**Borrow (whom):** while your moon is Full, this also has the printed text of that unit (or of whatever unit is in that position now), keywords included.
- It borrows only printed text: never the other unit's Borrow, name, tribe or stats. A golden lender lends its golden text; a golden borrower borrows golden text. Both together are still just golden.
- Borrow switches on the moment the moon becomes Full, before Full Moon triggers resolve, so a Borrower beside a Full Moon unit fires that trigger too. It switches off when the moon leaves Full, including when the Borrower's own borrowed text turns the moon.
- Borrowed "When played" text never fires. Borrowed Start of Combat text fires if the moon is Full at Start of Combat. Enemy text a Borrower takes works for your side ("your units" means yours).

### How the tribe plays

The Moon Hares are a clock you can wind. Early on they fight well under turn 3's natural full moon. From Tier 2, selling and playing Hares turns the moon, so you pick the phase you shop in and the phase you fight in, which often differ. The full moon pays permanent stats in the shop and changes your board in the fight. Borrowers copy a neighbour, the enemy across or your biggest non-Hare, which makes the Hares a doubling layer for whatever else you run. The dark of the moon is a second game: New Moons bring gold and Leverets, which grow quietly in the back row and come of age at Tier 6.

### Off-label uses

- **Moon-Gazer and Old Wat** are bought for their sale. Selling Moon-Gazer on turn 2 gives a full-moon fight a turn early. Old Wat returns each turn, so with Almanac Jill one Wat turns the moon twice a turn: once when played, once when sold.
- **Moon-Caller cuts both ways:** in a fight that starts Full, its death turns Moonlit off. Golden, it can take a Waxing fight through Full, firing Full Moon triggers permanently mid-fight, and out the other side.
- **The Charmer buffs any unit next to it**, so it can sit inside another tribe's engine. **The Three Hares** should go where the most buffs land.
- **Borrowing a turner switches the Borrower off:** a Jack borrowing Almanac Jill turns the moon past Full and loses the text. That's a built-in brake.

## 3. Builds

1. **The clock (Full every turn).** Moon-Gazer and Old Wat, then Almanac Jill, Ring Referee and the Charmer, then Waxwell and Meikle Care or the Moon-Scour. A Full moon every turn takes three turns of the moon per shop (Wat played with Almanac Jill, Wat sold, a Moon-Gazer sold); with Meikle Care it takes one. The payoff is permanent stats every turn and a Moonlit fight every round.
2. **Borrowed shapes (a doubling layer).** Two or three Hares among another tribe's best pieces: a Jack left-beside an engine, Silverpenny on your biggest non-Hare, the Witch across their key unit. It needs only a few turns of the moon, aimed at the right fights, and fits any lobby.
3. **Dark of the moon (Leverets and gold).** Sixpence Doe, Doe of the Forms, Dusk Nurse, The Three Hares, then Grandam. Turn through Full to reach New, take the gold and Leverets, and feed the back row until Grandam turns them into copies, and perhaps triples. Meikle Care is a trap here.
4. **The ring (fight first).** Spring Jill, Moon-Caller, the Witch, Longshanks. Fight on a Waxing moon and let Moon-Caller bring Full mid-fight. A Jack beside Longshanks borrows her bout.

**With other tribes in the lobby:**
- **Hedgerow folk:** a Jack beside a Pass unit passes too. Leverets extend touching runs for free.
- **Fairies:** Leverets are free bodies for Ring slots. A Borrower that takes a Shy unit's text leaves for 2 gold when you aim a spell at it.
- **Oozes:** Leverets are free food for Eat and Belly. Silverpenny on your biggest Ooze gets a Belly of her own.
- **Bog folk:** a borrowed Mire aims the enemy's hits at Longshanks or a Bristling Spring Jill.
- **Hunters:** Witch, Longshanks and Quarry all care about the enemy across. A Jack beside a Pack unit borrows Pack.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Readability.** Borrowed text has to show on the card while the moon is Full, with a clear marker of where it came from. Without that UI, Borrow is unreadable. This is the biggest risk.
- **Long shops.** The clock build may turn the moon three or four times per shop, firing triggers each time. That's fine without a timer, but it needs fast animation and a log.
- **Silverpenny copying another tribe's Tier 6 capstone** doubles it from Tier 5. Fallback: "your biggest non-Hare unit of Tier 5 or lower".
- **Grandam plus triples** is the most likely runaway. Her copies count towards triples and each triple Discovers; Once a turn, back-row space and Leveret supply cap it. Fallback: Grandam's copies don't count towards triples.
- **Stacked multipliers.** Golden Waxwell with Meikle Care and two turns of the moon per shop fires Full triggers six times a turn, and Ring Referee alone then gives +6/+6 to each Hare a turn. It needs a ceiling check in sims.
- **The Moon-Scour's counter** climbs twice as fast with Meikle Care. Once a turn limits the payouts, but not their size.
- **Waning is thin:** only the Dusk Nurse cares about it. A Waning card could fill that hole later.
- **Full every turn removes the phase planning** the seed asks for. That's deliberate at the top (Meikle Care), but if it arrives too early, move Almanac Jill to Tier 5.
- **Opponents see your moon,** so a Full fight is telegraphed. That's fair for Spike, and Moon-Caller's mid-fight turn is the surprise.
- **Hand space:** Old Wat takes one of the four hand slots each turn.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** Spring Jill is the tempo body (5 stats against 4) for turn 3's natural Full fight; Moon-Gazer is the seed, valued for its moon turn. Both fit.
- **Tier 2:** the Charmer gives 6–8 a Full, about 3–4 a turn at a Full every two turns (target 0.3–1 body). Sixpence gives about 0.5–1 gold a turn. The Jack is an enabler worth its neighbour. All fit.
- **Tier 3:** Dusk Nurse gives 6 a turn, 12 when Waning: about 1 body against ~0.8, a little high on purpose because the Leveret line needs it. Doe of the Forms and Old Wat are enablers; Moon-Caller is the combat engine.
- **Tier 4:** Ring Referee gives 10–12 a Full (target ~11 a turn, with a Full every turn). The Three Hares is the first multiplier (about ×3). **Broken on purpose:** two enablers and one buffer, because the clock and Borrow builds come together here.
- **Tier 5:** Waxwell is the multiplier, Longshanks the combat engine (one sure kill under the Full moon, about the in-fight target), Silverpenny the direction card. She can go far above the target, depending on what she borrows (flagged).
- **Tier 6:** Meikle Care gives 24 a Full, one or two a turn, and keeps Borrow on. The Moon-Scour gives about 36 a turn mid-game, rising to about 84 late (target ~56, top ~140). Grandam is the New Moon capstone. **Broken on purpose:** the two capstones conflict (Meikle deletes New), so Tier 6 is a fork.

## 6. Design rationale

1. I wrote the three moments first; their common thread, a clock that you wind and that pays out in borrowed power, gave the Moon and Borrow.
2. A four-phase moon with one phase per turn, because each phase is then exactly one turn and easy to plan around.
3. "Turn the moon" works in the shop and in fights, so the fight moment (a mid-fight Full) is possible.
4. The shop phase and the fight phase can differ, which creates the main decision: Full Moon triggers in the shop, or a Moonlit fight.
5. Borrow copies text only, never Borrow itself or stats, to keep it a doubling layer and not a loop.
6. Borrow switches on before Full Moon triggers resolve, so a Borrower beside a payoff doubles it: the tribe's main interlock.
7. The Borrow targets vary (beside, in front, across, biggest non-Hare) so that position and other tribes matter, which is the Johnny hook.
8. The turners are sale and play triggers (Moon-Gazer, Old Wat, Almanac Jill), so cheap units have uses beyond their bodies.
9. Leverets (a token that borrows from in front) give the board-growth story a home in the otherwise idle back row.
10. New Moon got its own economy and Leveret line, so the full moon isn't the only phase worth reaching.
11. Two Tier 6 capstones that conflict (Meikle deletes New, Grandam needs it) make the late game a fork.
12. Names and lore last: the jills box and keep the almanac, Old Wat keeps coming back, Silverpenny walks in another shape, Meikle Care knows the long charm, and the leverets lie in the Forms.
