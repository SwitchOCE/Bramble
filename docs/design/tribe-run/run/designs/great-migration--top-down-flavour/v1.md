# The Great Migration (v1)

## 1. Identity

**The fantasy.** You are never one hero. You are a herd in single file. Whoever is in front leads, the front keeps changing, and every lemming that falls pushes the rest a little further on. The herd also booms, which means more bodies, more copies and more triples, and it crashes. Its smallest members pick fights with the biggest things on the fell.

**Lead (rule term).** Your Lead is your first unit in reading order (F1–F4, then B1–B3). While recruiting, you choose it by where you place units. In a fight it is your first living unit. When it dies, the lead passes to the next one. A unit summoned into an earlier slot takes the lead when it arrives. The Lead can be any unit, Lemming or not.

**Takes the lead: (trigger).** Fires when this becomes your Lead in a fight. Your first unit takes the lead as the fight begins, before Start of Combat effects.

**Onward: (trigger).** When this dies: the effect. "Your Lead" in Onward text means the unit that holds the lead *after* this death, so the fallen hand their legend forward.

**How it plays.** Every Lemming card points at the front. Onward units die and feed whoever leads. Takes-the-lead units wait further back and fire when the front falls. So the order of your line is the whole game: who dies first, who inherits, who leads last. Two pulls run through the tribe. The **Front** piles the herd's gifts onto one leader at a time. The **Tide** floods the board with Pups and is paid for every death. They share cards but fight over slot F1, over whether Pups should die or survive, and over whether Pups get used or tripled. The Lead accepts any unit, so a Bog folk Mire unit, a fat Ooze or a Fairy-Ringed slot can be the thing the herd marches behind.

**Timing notes.** When the Lead dies, the lead passes first, then its Onward resolves onto the new Lead, then any summon arrives. A Pup that Midwife summons into F1 then takes the lead. Cliff-Singer's "destroy" ignores Shield. Lemenår's halving rounds down.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Front (one leader at a time).** Banner-Bearer or Gerd in F1, Herding Councillor flanked by Lemmings, Route-Map Reader, then Brother Onward so every Pilgrim's and Pup's gift sticks to the Lead. Rune-Carver pays each new Lead for the dead in front of it. Front Lemming turns this inside out: the lead now rotates after every attack, so Onward gifts spread across the line instead of piling on one unit. With Brother Onward, that becomes permanent growth on the whole board, Gerd re-shouting every lap, and Banner-Bearer growing several times a fight. *Off-label:* put a non-Lemming in F1. A Bog folk Mire unit (enemies must hit it) or a Hunter with a Quarry makes the herd march behind a tank that grows with every death.

**B. The Tide (the many over the one).** Grass-Nest Mother, Ole, Snow-Hall Midwife and Old Mother Lemen keep the board refilling, and Owl-Scold Chorus bites the biggest enemy at every death. Lemenår Herald makes the Pups themselves grow. The catch is that bigger Pups die less, so Chorus and Old Mother fire less. You choose between a boom of big Pups and a stream of dying small ones. *Off-label:* Fairy Rings. A Pup summoned into a Ringed slot gains the Ring size. Ring F1, and every Midwife Pup arrives at +4/+4 or more and takes the lead.

**C. The Cliff (sacrifice as legend).** One or two Cliff-Singers kill your own Lead at Start of Combat. That fires its Onward, passes the lead (Takes the lead, Rune-Carver) and moves its stats forward, so you only lose a body. *Off-label:* kill your own Ooze to release its Belly at once. Kill a Grass-Nest Mother to start the fight with two extra Pups. Hand a huge Lead's stats to Gerd so his next shout comes from a giant.

**D. The Census (economy and pivots).** Weeks-Old Breeder feeds Pups to hand. Three Pups make a golden Pup, which Discovers a unit from the tier above. Tally pays gold for every copy you collect, from any tribe. This shell supports any build, or another tribe's triples. The tension is that every tripled Pup is one fewer body for Tent-Striker and Onward.

**In a lobby.** The Lemmings take from other tribes more than they give: any tribe can supply the Lead, and Tally pays for any triple. Fairies (Rings under Pups) and Bog folk (a Mire tank as Lead) pair best. Oozes pair well with Cliff-Singer. Hedgerow folk's *Pass X* feeds the rightmost unit while the herd feeds the leftmost, so the two can run side by side without competing. Hunters' Pack next to a growing Lead also works. Against the Lemmings, Cleave and Swoop thin the Pups, and boards of one giant feed Gerd and Chorus.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Reading the Lead.** A title that moves (especially with Front Lemming) needs a clear marker on screen, such as a banner that hops from unit to unit. Without one, the tribe is unreadable.
- **Anti-synergies are sharp.** Midwife keeps refilling F1 with Pups, so Takes-the-lead units further back never lead. Brother Onward wastes permanent stats on a Pup Lead that vanishes after the fight. These are meant as choices, but new players will feel cheated.
- **Death-count stacking.** Ole, Midwife and Nest Mother feed Chorus, Old Mother and Rune-Carver at once. A golden Midwife (8 Pups) with Old Mother is the obvious in-fight ceiling. It is fight-only, but watch it.
- **Rune-Carver grows quadratically** in deaths per fight. The lead changes only on deaths or Front Lemming laps, which bounds it, but golden Rune-Carver with Front Lemming is the spike.
- **Economy leak.** Breeder plus Tally gives a Pup, about 1 gold and a third of a Discover each turn from Tier 4. If it outpaces other tribes' economy, cut Breeder to "every other turn".
- **Low hero damage.** Pups didn't start the fight, so a Tide win scores few survivors. Its flavour fits ("survival as fact"), but it may make the Tide feel toothless as a winner.
- **Gerd and Chorus scale off the enemy.** They are strong against one giant and weak against a wide board of small units. That is intended, but it makes them swingy.
- **Hand clutter.** Breeder Pups compete for a 4-card hand.
- **Not used:** "always moving, never arriving". Free movement while recruiting makes movement effects meaningless. Front Lemming carries it in fights instead.

## 5. Tier check

Bodies: 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14.

- **T1** (tempo + seed): Pilgrim 4 body, about 3 in fight (0.75 body, a little over 0.6, since it needs a Lead behind it). Banner-Bearer 4 body, +2 permanent a fight (0.5, about on target), more once Tier 4 relays arrive.
- **T2** (two enablers or buffers + economy): Nest Mother has a body of 4, under on purpose (paid in text), and gives about 8 in fight from two Pups plus their Onward (1.3, a bit high, but fragile). Councillor gives up to +4 permanent (0.67, in range). Breeder is economy: one Pup a turn, a third of a Discover.
- **T3** (two enablers, buffer, engine): Gerd gives about 6 against a 12-Attack giant at this stage (0.86, in range) and grows late on purpose. Ole gives about 8 in fight (1.1). Route-Map Reader gives +6 permanent (0.86). Cliff-Singer adds no stats (a pure enabler) and costs a body.
- **T4** (buffer, enabler, multiplier, economy): Tent-Striker gives about 12 permanent over 6 Lemmings (1.4). Midwife gives about 16 in fight (1.9). Front Lemming is a multiplier that roughly doubles Takes-the-lead triggers. Tally gives up to 2 gold, about two-thirds of a unit. This tier has both a multiplier and an economy piece, with one buffer, because the Census build needs its payoff here.
- **T5** (direction, engine, multiplier): Brother Onward turns about 20–30 in-fight Onward stats permanent (about 2 bodies, on target). Chorus deals about 40 damage at ~10 deaths (3.3 against ~4). Lemenår adds about 32 in-fight value a turn over ~8 Pups (2.7 bodies), over on purpose for the boom, and the crash halves it.
- **T6** (two capstones + payoff): Rune-Carver gives about 80 in fight at ~10 deaths (5.7, on target). Old Mother gives about 96 (6.9, slightly over, and the Tide's ceiling). The One Who Arrived gives about 60 permanent at 10 deaths over 6 Lemmings (4.3 against ~4). Runaway check: permanent stats make Lemmings die less, so every death payoff limits itself. None of them feeds its own source.

## 6. Design rationale

1. I started from the fantasy of a herd in single file, where the front keeps changing and the fallen carry the rest. I kept five themes: who's in front is in charge; sacrifice as legend, survival as fact; the many over the one; boom and crash; tiny folk against giants.
2. The **Lead** is a title, not a slot. "The front keeps changing" needed one rule term that can move.
3. **Onward** aims at the Lead, so a death literally passes forward, the "throw themselves forward so the rest go further" image.
4. **Takes the lead** is Onward's mirror. Together they make line order the tribe's central puzzle, which is Johnny's ground.
5. The Lead accepts any unit, so other tribes' tanks, Rings and Bellies become Lemming pieces off-label.
6. Pups are 1/1 with a tiny Onward, the "many identical henchmen". Being Created, they also feed triples, so "the many" doubles as economy.
7. I split the tribe into Front and Tide, and gave them deliberate clashes (Midwife against Takes the lead, Lemenår against death payoffs, Brother Onward against Pup Leads), so every pick is a choice.
8. Boom and crash became Lemenår's growing Pup bonus that halves on a loss. Boom and crash read better as rhythm than as a punishment.
9. The tiny-against-giants theme became effects keyed to the *biggest* enemy (Gerd, Chorus). They get better as enemy boards concentrate.
10. The doom cult became Cliff-Singer, a sacrifice outlet that keeps the stats. It is an enabler, not a cost, so it is used rather than avoided.
11. "Survival as fact" became The One Who Arrived, which turns a fight's deaths into permanent stats for the herd that comes back.
12. Tally the fox became the economy piece, paying for copies, because a thousand identical silhouettes should pay off in triples.
