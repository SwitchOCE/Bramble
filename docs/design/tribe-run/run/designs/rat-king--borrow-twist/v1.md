# The Rat King: Gossip and Blurts

## 1. Identity

**Where the core mechanic comes from:** the outbreak rule of the board game *Pandemic*. A city that gets a fourth cube spills one cube onto every neighbour, which can set off more outbreaks, and each city breaks out only once per chain. Here a spill is good news, not bad.

**Gossip** is a counter on a unit, shown as two pips. "Gossip N" gives a unit N Gossip, one at a time. Any unit can hold Gossip, whatever its tribe. Gossip stays on a unit between turns. Gossip gained or spent in a fight ends with the fight, like stats, and selling a unit loses its Gossip.

**Blurt.** When a unit gets its 3rd Gossip, it Blurts: it clears its Gossip, uses its **Blurt:** text if it has one, then gives 1 Gossip to each unit touching it. Gossip left over after a Blurt stays on the unit.
- **A chain** is everything one effect sets off. Each unit Blurts at most once per chain. A unit that has already Blurted still gets Gossip, but holds it for later.
- Blurt texts follow the normal rule. While recruiting, "gain +2/+2" lasts. In a fight, it ends with the fight unless the text says permanently.
- Units with no Blurt text still Blurt and pass the news on, so other tribes carry it without gaining from it.

**How it plays.** Touching runs along the zigzag, so the board is a seven-slot fuse and a gap cuts it. Each Blurt takes 3 Gossip and passes on 2, so news fades unless you feed it with buys, Refreshes, plays and hits. You load units to two pips, light one end, and the knot Blurts down the line, each Rat paying its own text. You build the wire, not the unit. The standing choice is spend (recruit cascades for permanent stats) or hold (pay for full pips, then a fight that goes off at once). Late cards change how news travels: jump gaps, fan out from a hub, play every text twice, spill onto the enemy.

**Token and spell**
- **Stray** (token, Rat, 1/1): Blurt: gain +1/+1. Created, so it sells for 0.
- **Point of Order** (spell, 2 gold): Choose one of your units. It Blurts now and keeps its Gossip.

**Rulings Johnny will look for**
- Thresholds are not doubled on goldens. A golden Fips still Blurts at 2.
- "Extra time" effects add up. A unit touching Echo, with the Bellringer on board, uses its text three times.
- The Thirty-Two copies only **Blurt:** texts, not thresholds; copied limits count separately.
- A Stray summoned mid-chain joins it, so it can bridge a gap.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Fuse (spend while recruiting).** Taster, Huddler and Landlady feed the line. Ilse lights every unit holding two pips at Start of Turn. Fips at one end throws the news to the far end, so a chain can run from both ends or cross a gap. The Bean-Counter goes last in the line, because it gains more for each unit that Blurted before it. Echo sits between the two best Blurt texts. Endgame: the Rat King makes one guaranteed seven-unit chain every turn, and the Thirty-Two in the middle uses everyone's text, doubled by the Bellringer. The puzzle is order: Start of Turn effects go in reading order, so Ilse before the King gives two chains, after it finds nobody at two pips.

**B. Simmer and Boil (hold, then fight).** You load every unit to two pips and don't light them. Hearth Huddle pays for each pip at End of Turn, and Old Gundel's rare Blurts pay every unit that holds two. In the fight the board goes off at once. The Piper's Start of Combat Gossip sets off all the loaded units in one chain, and each one attacks straight away. The Squeaker, hit in the front row, keeps setting off new chains, and the Tangle's Strays fill the gaps that deaths leave, so the wire stays connected. Fight Gossip resets after the fight, so the board is loaded again for free. Ilse and the King work against this build: that's the choice.

**C. Bad Omen (aim it at the enemy).** The Omen fills every enemy to two pips at Start of Combat. Its first hit sets off a chain along the enemy's own touching run, and each enemy that Blurts takes damage. Attack buffs from the Fuse build make the Omen's hits hurt. The Piper gives it more attacks, and so more sparks. An enemy that leaves gaps in its board cuts the chain short. Against another Rat player it gives their texts free uses, so read the opponent's last board first.

**D. Ratskeller Roll (economy into triples).** The Landlady beside Fips turns Refreshes into gold and news; the Taster turns buys into news; the Wall-Runner Discovers from long chains. A golden Speaker or Fips reshapes the wire.

**Mixing with other tribes in a lobby**
- **Any tribe:** non-Rats carry the news, so two or three Rats can wire up four strong outsiders. Groomer and Gundel buff any tribe.
- **Hedgerow:** shares touching runs; Pass X and Fips both aim at the far end.
- **Oozes:** Strays are free food for Eat.
- **Birds:** the Egg in a Nest carries the news, so a Bird gone south doesn't cut the wire.
- **Fairies:** a Ring on the Speaker's slot makes the hub sturdy; Shy units relay but skip Rat buffs.
- **Hunters:** Pack joins each attack the Piper sets off.
- **Bog folk:** Mire holds the front while the Squeaker waits to be hit.

## 4. Risks and holes

1. **Readability.** Pips on seven units and chains while recruiting need a clear pip display and a chain that plays out in under two seconds.
2. **The early game is thin.** Nothing happens until a unit reaches 3 Gossip, so on Tiers 1 and 2 the tribe wins fights on bodies alone. The Huddler and the Taster may need a pip of starting Gossip.
3. **The Wall-Runner may be strong economy.** A Discover each turn is about one free unit. Watch it, and if it's too much, change it to "Discover a Rat" or "every other turn".
4. **The golden ceiling.** A golden Thirty-Two with a golden Bellringer, under the King, uses every text about six times a turn. Chains are bounded, but stats aren't. This is the high roll, and it needs a check against the runaway rule.
5. **The Piper's opening burst.** With a fully loaded board, seven attacks at Start of Combat may decide fights before the other side acts. If they do, change it to "attacks after the next enemy attack" or drop its Start of Combat Gossip.
6. **Hoarding is fiddly:** holding at exactly two pips means counting every buy and Refresh. Johnny may enjoy it; others may find it a trap.
7. **The Omen in a mirror** feeds the other Rat player. Intended, but may feel bad.
8. **Edge rulings needed:** leftover Gossip after a Blurt (keep or lose), Belly units as gaps, Shy relays.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** Huddler is a 2/3 tempo body with a spark. The Taster is the seed, at about 1 Blurt a turn: +2 stats and 2 Gossip passed on, about 0.4 body. On target.
- **Tier 2:** Groomer gives 4 stats a Blurt, about 4 to 6 a turn, in the 0.3 to 1 body range. The Landlady gives 1 to 3 gold. Fips gives no stats and is an enabler. On target.
- **Tier 3:** Ilse and Speaker are enablers that name the composition. Gundel gives about 4 to 6 stats a Blurt, about 0.8 body. The Squeaker sets off several fight Blurts. On target.
- **Tier 4:** the Bean-Counter gives about 8 to 10 a Blurt from an average chain of 4, about 1.3 bodies, and **twice that at the end of a full chain, on purpose**, as the reward for building the wire. Echo doubles two neighbours, about 12 a turn. The Tangle is low on stats as an enabler. **The Wall-Runner is above a usual Tier 4 economy unit** when it fires (risk 3).
- **Tier 5:** the Huddle gives 6 units 1 to 2 pips each, 18 to 28 a turn, about 2 bodies. The Bellringer doubles the board's Blurt texts, about 25 to 40. The Omen gives about 20 to 70 in a fight, depending on its Attack, against a target of about 48. On target. The Bellringer is a Tier 5 multiplier, as Brann is.
- **Tier 6:** the King gives about 7 texts of about 6 each, around 42 to 84, about 4 bodies. The Thirty-Two gives about 36 a Blurt, around 40 to 70 a turn. The Piper gives about 80 to 100 in a fight, **slightly above the in-fight target of about 84 on purpose**, as the payoff of the hold build (risk 5).
- **Shape change at Tier 4:** a multiplier **and** an economy unit, because the wire needs both a local multiplier and a reward for long chains.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Pandemic's outbreak rule, because the seed is "what one feels all feel" and news running tail to tail, and a spill rule works while recruiting as well as in fights.
2. I kept its threshold of 3 and its "once per chain" rule, because that rule ends every chain without a cap written on each card.
3. I used the touching zigzag as the map. It's a line of seven, so a chain is a fuse, and gaps and deaths cut it, which gives placement and the fight a single shared logic.
4. Blurt has no default reward, and the reward is printed on each Rat. Any unit can carry the news, which invites other tribes in without giving them free stats.
5. Gossip stays between turns and resets after fights. Loading becomes a plan over several turns, and fights get free cascades.
6. Each Blurt takes in 3 Gossip and passes on 2, so news fades. That makes Gossip sources and threshold changers (Fips at 2, the Speaker at 1) the real enablers, not stat buffers.
7. I added two opposite payoffs, spending (Bean-Counter, King) and holding (Huddle, Gundel), and gave Ilse and the Piper a side each, so every board makes a real choice between them.
8. The multipliers follow the guide's "same job, about three times as much" rule: Echo affects two neighbours, the Bellringer affects everyone, and the Thirty-Two holds every text on one body.
9. The Omen points the mechanic at the enemy's board, an off-label use. The enemy's gaps are its counterplay.
10. I capped the economy pieces (three gold a turn, one Discover a turn, only while recruiting) so that Refresh, gold and Gossip can't make a loop.
11. Names last: each seed figure got the job its story suggests (the Taster tries new things, Fips sees the far end, the Speaker never holds the floor, the Piper finally moves them one way).
