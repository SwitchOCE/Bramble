# The Olm Sleepers (v1)

## 1. Identity

**Vigil N: effect.** The unit keeps a count that starts at N. Start of Turn: it counts down by 1. When it reaches 0, the Vigil **ends**: the effect happens and the count goes back to N. A Vigil counts only while its unit is on your board. All Vigils count down together; the ones that end resolve in reading order. A unit holds at most one Vigil.

**Deep Vigil N.** A Vigil that also counts down by 1 each time another of your Vigils ends (but not when another Deep Vigil ends).

**Dripstone.** How many of your Vigils have ended this game. It never goes down. When a Vigil ends, Dripstone rises first, then the effect happens. A repeated effect (Brother Netopir) is not an ending: it adds no Dripstone and ticks no Deep Vigil.

**How it plays.** The olms are pale, slow and weak for a long time. Each of them sits on a countdown and does one thing when it finishes. Every finish lays down a little Dripstone, and the tribe's payoffs grow with that stone: Olmlings that grow a little more with each Vigil, a "Baby Dragon" whose bite is the rumour's age, an Eldest whose rare answer lifts the whole board. The puzzle is clock work: short Vigils pay often and feed the stone and the Deep Vigils; tools that ring, knock or flood make clocks finish early; bats repeat whatever was said last; and a lamplighter hangs Vigils on units of other tribes, which can't see that they're being enlisted. Against all that, a robber-knight pays you to keep quiet, and an old sitter makes every Vigil longer and louder, so a board can be built to speak often or to speak rarely and hugely.

**Tokens and spells:** none new. Pale Olmlings from the Spring-Spilled Olm are ordinary Created copies (sell for 0, count toward triples). The lamp and the copied Vigil are text on the unit, not items.

**Small rulings.** A unit inside an Ooze's Belly or away on Fly South is off the board, so its Vigil pauses. If the Sitter leaves, counts already running stay; they reset to the normal N. Kurent, Knocker and Parkelj count down Deep Vigils too.

## 3. Builds

**The long count (Dripstone).** Lots of short Vigils: Old Polh, Hrošč's lamps, Guild Knockers, Scribe copies of Vigil 2 effects, with Parkelj doubling the rate. The stone climbs by 3–6 a turn, and you cash it in with Olmlings, "Baby Dragon", the Flood-Mark Reader, the Eldest and the Dragon Under the Earth. Spike's line: steady, readable growth.

**The clutch (goldens).** Mother Ribica's Deep Vigil 12 never finishes on its own. Feed it endings: two Knockers chain-ending each other, Spring-Spilled Olms, lamps on everything. Every clutch gives a golden copy of your best unit, and playing a golden Discovers from the tier above. The Spring-Spilled Olm makes Olmling triples on the side. Johnny's line: the high roll is a golden Brother Netopir, or Netopir repeating Ribica as the turn's last effect.

**The quiet house (pulse).** The Robber-Knight pays 3 gold for a silent turn, and the Seven-Year Sitter makes Vigils longer and twice as loud. Set all your Vigils to the same length so they end on the same turn: one loud turn, then quiet paid turns. Netopir repeats the loud turn's best effect (for example a doubled Comes-and-Goes Olm). Parkelj and Kurents break the rhythm, so this build leaves them out or times them on purpose.

**Lamps for strangers (cross-tribe).** Hrošč and the Scribe hang Vigils on another tribe's carry or on neutral units. The Comes-and-Goes Olm and the Eldest buff every unit, not just olms, so the olm core can sit behind someone else's main plan.
- *Oozes:* eat the Dragon Under the Earth for its stone-swollen stats, or Belly a big Olmling to keep it safe (its Vigil pauses).
- *Hedgerow:* a lamp on your rightmost unit stacks with Pass. The Courier Bat's "next to" growth pays for a dense touching run.
- *Bog folk:* the lake's Health turns a Mire unit into a wall. The Pilgrim makes a lamp-lit Mire unit grow with each ending.
- *Fairies:* the Dragon counts as a Fairy for Ring cards. Old Polh's gold pays for aimed spells on Shy units. (The Dragon is a Fairy, but it isn't Shy.)
- *Birds:* the Dragon counts as a Bird and so can Fly South. Its stats are real and permanent, so it comes home doubled. Vigils on a Bird pause while it is away.
- *Hunters:* the Robber-Knight's Swoop plus Quarry splits their targets. The lake's Attack turns go to Pack units.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Slow start.** Before turn 4 only the Kurent is a real body. If early losses cost too much Health, raise the Olmling to 2/2 or give the Kurent +1 Health.
- **Counter load.** Up to seven countdowns, a Dripstone total and a lake state all at once. The screen has to show each count and the next ending clearly, or the tribe becomes bookkeeping.
- **Chain resolution.** Knockers, Parkelj and Deep Vigils can set off cascades. The cascades are finite (Deep Vigils don't feed each other, repeats don't count as endings), but the order of a cascade needs one written rule and an animation that players can read.
- **Netopir on capstones.** Repeating Ribica gives two goldens, and repeating the Eldest doubles a +10/+10 board buff. It is a deliberate high roll, but if it decides lobbies, exclude Deep Vigil effects from repeats.
- **The Dragon counts as every tribe.** Every other tribe's text needs a check against it: Fly South, Shy, Ring and Eat edge cases. It may be the most rules-heavy line in the game.
- **Lamps leak power.** Lamps on neutral and other-tribe units may make the Olm Sleepers' best units splash too well into other builds. Watch whether Hrošč and the Scribe get picked up by other tribes' players.
- **The Sitter's "numbers doubled" wording** needs a definition (gold yes, counts no, "a golden copy" no).
- **Dripstone never falls.** Late-game Olmlings grow quadratically. That is the fantasy, but check round 12+ board totals against other tribes.

## 5. Tier check

Body unit as in Part C: 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14.
- **T1:** The Olmling seed gives about 0.8 body a turn at Dripstone 5, twice the guide's 0.4 by mid-game and more later. This is on purpose: it's weak early and is the tribe's "then devastating" card. The Kurent is a 5-stat tempo body with a small tick.
- **T2:** Polh averages 1 gold a turn, plus Dripstone. The Courier Bat gives 2–4 stats a turn. Hrošč's lamp gives 2 a turn, plus a Dripstone source anywhere. All sit inside 0.3–1 body.
- **T3:** The Comes-and-Goes Olm gives about 6 a turn (0.85 body). "Baby Dragon" gets +8 Attack in a fight at Dripstone 8 (1.1 body). The Knocker and Spring-Spilled Olm are enablers, priced in endings and in Olmlings for triples.
- **T4:** The Pilgrim gives 12–18 a turn (1.4–2 bodies), at the generous end. Netopir is the first multiplier. The Robber-Knight averages about 1.5 gold a turn, in a build that gives up Dripstone to get it. The Scribe doubles a Vigil once.
- **T5:** Parkelj roughly doubles the ending rate, a multiplier in place of a flat amount. The Reader gives about 50 in a fight at Dripstone 15 (4 bodies). The Sitter is the direction card, a trade more than a gain.
- **T6:** The Eldest gives about 60 a turn, rising to about 120 with Parkelj (4–8 bodies, under the "top ~10"). Ribica's golden every 2–3 turns is the capstone high roll. The Dragon gives about 70 once at Dripstone 18 (5 bodies), on a deliberately small 10-stat body, plus about 12 a turn.
- **Shape:** kept 2/3/4/4/3/3. Tier 5 has no buffer. Its buffing moved to the Tier 4 Pilgrim and the Tier 6 Eldest so that Tier 5 could hold the two clock-changers.

## 6. Design rationale

1. The Eldest "last spoke before anyone's grandmother hatched": the tribe speaks rarely, so its basic unit of play is a countdown that pays out (Vigil).
2. Dripstone "grows more slowly than an olm ages": a total of endings that never falls. It gives patience a number that payoffs can read.
3. Mother Ribica's once-a-dozen-years clutch was too slow on its own, so Deep Vigil counts down on other endings. That made the core puzzle: short clocks feed long ones.
4. The Kurent's cowbells wake sleepers: a cheap count-down tool, and the T1 tempo body the slow tribe needs.
5. Brother Netopir, "loyal to whoever asked last": he repeats the last effect, which turns the order of endings into a puzzle.
6. Hrošč's lamps "nobody in the order can see": they go only on units without a Vigil. That makes the lamplighter the cross-tribe enabler, and Parkelj, the olm who can see, can take one.
7. The Robber-Knight pays the order to keep quiet: gold for silent turns, a rival plan to piling up Dripstone.
8. The olm that "stayed in the same spot for seven years": the Seven-Year Sitter makes Vigils longer and louder, a choice of direction rather than raw power.
9. "They never correct anyone about the dragon thing": "Baby Dragon" bites by the age of the rumour, and the Dragon Under the Earth counts as every tribe.
10. The Comes-and-Goes Lake: a buff that alternates between Health and Attack, which gives players a timing choice.
11. Repeats and Deep-from-Deep endings don't count, which keeps every cascade finite.
12. Kept the default tier shape, because the guide's roles mapped cleanly onto the figures from the seed.
