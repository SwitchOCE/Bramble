# The Cuckoo Clockworks (v1)

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: the hour.** In every fight each side keeps a clock. Every attack, by either side, ticks both clocks once. On every 3rd tick your clock **strikes the hour** (1st hour, 2nd hour, ...). Two kinds of body wait for the hour: Eggs and sleepers.

**Keywords**
- **On the hour:** triggers each time your clock strikes.
- **Wind N:** Start of Combat: your clock ticks N times. It can strike during Start of Combat.
- **Lay an X/Y Egg (in a host):** marks that enemy (default: the enemy across, or a random enemy if that slot is empty). At your next hour it **hatches**: you summon an X/Y **Chick** in the empty slot nearest the host, on the host's side, and the Chick at once deals its Attack to the host (this isn't an attack). If the host's side is full, the Chick goes to your side; if both are full, it waits for the next hour. If the host dies first, the Egg is lost.
- **In the nest:** a Chick on the enemy's side attacks a random enemy next to it (any enemy if none is), and enemies can attack it as if it stood in your front row. That makes it a way into the back row.
- **Brood:** a counter for the game, starting at 0. Every Chick you summon has +Brood/+Brood.
- **Asleep (until the Nth hour):** at Start of Combat it falls asleep. It can't attack, and enemies can't attack, target or damage it; for targeting it doesn't count as being in its row, so a front row of sleepers leaves your back row open. Its text still works. It wakes on that hour, or at once when you have no awake units left. Sleepers count as your units for winning and for hero damage.

**How it plays.** The Clockworks make a fight's *timing* something you build. The early hours bring delayed bodies: Eggs laid in enemy units hatch into Chicks that turn on their hosts from inside the enemy's line-up, and dormice sleep safely until their hour. Wind, pace and reading order decide *when* the hour strikes, and each build wants a different answer. Eggs want an early hour, before the host dies. Sleepers want a late one, because a Seven-Sleeper that never has to wake keeps growing. Hour engines want as many hours as the fight can give. The tension is the point: you're always tuning one dial that three things share, and many Clockworks (Oil-Can, Vault Warden, Tower Doorkeeper) do as much for another tribe's units as for their own.

## 3. Builds

**Brood parasites (Eggs).** Pipit-Line Placer and Key-Winder early, then Egg-Painter, Wechselbalg, Rohrsang and Kasimir; the capstones are Gernegroß and the Midsummer Leaver. Your hour should come early: put a Placer *before* a Key-Winder in reading order (Placer F1, Winder F2) and add one more tick (a second Winder or Coin-Turner) so the 1st hour strikes during Start of Combat. The Eggs then hatch before their hosts swing. Chicks hatch inside the enemy's rows, so they hit the back-row engines the enemy hid. With Wechselbalg they steal Shield, Cleave or Bristle from their hosts: a Cleave Chick standing in the enemy's back row hits two hosts at once. The Midsummer Leaver turns surviving Chicks into permanent stats on their layers, so a 1-gold Pipit-Line Placer can end the game as one of your biggest units.

**Clock tower (hour engine).** Oil-Can Apprentices flanking your best carries, Jar-Fattener, Ottilie, Tower Doorkeeper, with Day-Wage Journeyman and Coin-Turner paying for it. These units buff whatever stands beside them, so this build happily keeps another tribe's carries. Coin-Turner rewards saving gold for a Tier up (which already gets cheaper as you wait); faster hours feed Day-Wage, which pays back gold. With Doorkeeper, every capped hour effect fires twice.

**The Sleeping Vault (sleepers).** Bench Dozer, Seven-Sleeper, Warden, Jar-Fattener and Old Unruh, played with a *slow* clock: no Wind, no Ottilie. Sleepers can't be hit, so they ride out the enemy's opening and wake as a second wave; Old Unruh wakes fed by every death before the 3rd hour. Seven-Sleepers are near-certain survivors on a win, so each one adds hero damage, and each one grows every turn it never had to wake. The puzzle is how much of your board can sleep before the awake part loses on its own.

**Mixing in a lobby.** The Warden puts *any* unit to sleep with its text still working: a Hedgerow unit growing on its turns-in-slot clock, a Pass engine at the back, a fragile Fairy, or your Tower Doorkeeper itself, all kept safe for two hours. Oil-Can and Ottilie's front half work on mixed boards. Eggs are best against big, sturdy boards: Reedbed Placer and Gernegroß grow with the enemy's best unit, and Wechselbalg prices hosts by their keywords. They're weak against wide boards of small units that die before the hour (Wind answers that) and against Bog folk, whose Bitter shrinks host Attack before Reedbed reads it. Hunters' Quarry and Bog's Mire look across, so they never see a Chick standing in their own row. Ooze bellies make good hosts: a heaved Ooze that dies releases into a board the Chick is already inside.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Fight length decides the payoff.** Blowouts give few hours, close fights many. Early Wind and Ottilie's pace smooth this out, but hour effects will feel swingy to Spike. The caps ("first 3 hours") are what keep long fights from running away.
- **Two new spatial rules.** A Chick fighting from the enemy's side, and a sleeper that doesn't count for its row, both need clear art: an egg on the host, a ticking clock face per side, a sleeping cap or jar on dormant units. This is the tribe's biggest look-and-feel load.
- **Feel-bad Eggs.** The host dies and the Egg is lost. That's intended pressure toward Wind, but a lost Kasimir front row reads as "my card did nothing". Possible change: a lost Egg adds Brood +1.
- **Enemy-scaled payoffs** (Reedbed, Gernegroß) are strong against the lobby leader and weak against weak boards, which helps the losing player and caps the winner. Watch for Gernegroß copying a 100-stat golden carry.
- **Midsummer Leaver snowballs on wins only.** It's rich-get-richer by design; its ceiling is the enemy's size (via Gernegroß) plus Brood, so it is not self-feeding. It still needs a sim.
- **All-sleeper boards.** If nearly the whole board sleeps, the awake units die fast and everything wakes at once. That's fine as a fallback, but check it can't become a degenerate stall against boards that can't damage sleepers.
- **Day-Wage + Coin-Turner loop:** saved gold winds the clock, the hours pay gold. It's capped at 2 gold, but check how it interacts with Hedgerow Acorns.
- **Mirror matches:** two clocks, and Eggs inside Eggs' Chicks. These need a written order for rules (each side resolves its own hour in reading order).
- **Hole:** no answer to Shield-heavy boards beyond Wechselbalg stealing it. And Brood is a second counter on top of the clock; it could fold into Egg-Painter's own text if the UI gets crowded.

## 5. Tier check

- **T1:** Pipit-Line Placer has a below-body 3 stats plus a ~2-stat Chick and a 1-point heave, about 0.6 body in a fight: on target. Bench Dozer is 6 stats (1.5 bodies), above the guide **on purpose**: it pays by missing the first hour.
- **T2:** Oil-Can gives up to 6 permanent stats a turn (1 body), at the top of the range. Key-Winder is a plain enabler on a full body. Day-Wage pays about 1 gold a turn, 2 at most: normal T2 economy.
- **T3:** Seven-Sleeper grows +4 a turn (0.6 body) but rarely fights; Egg-Painter's Brood +1 adds about 4–6 Chick stats per fight, every turn, cumulatively (~0.8 body); Warden and Wechselbalg give about 1 body in a fight each. All on target.
- **T4:** Reedbed Placer gives ~2 bodies in a fight late on and less early (it grows with the enemy). Rohrsang gives ~1.2 bodies in a fight. Jar-Fattener gives ~1.3 bodies permanent with two or three sleepers. Coin-Turner is an enabler. The shape changed: **two enablers and two buffers**, because the economy lives at T2 and Coin-Turner's leftover-gold conversion takes the economy slot.
- **T5:** Kasimir gives up to ~4 bodies in a fight when the front row is full and the Eggs hatch; Old Unruh about 3 bodies; Tower Doorkeeper is the multiplier (a Brann for hours). On target.
- **T6:** Ottilie gives ~2.5 bodies permanent plus the pace multiplier; Gernegroß gives a copy of the enemy's best unit, 4–6 bodies in a fight; the Midsummer Leaver gives 3–6 bodies permanent, on wins only. Golden Ottilie with golden Doorkeeper is the ×4 ceiling, and the hour caps stop it there.

## 6. Design rationale

1. First puzzle: an effect timed by a fight's attack count, not by turns, so the player tunes *when* things happen, not just how much. That gave the clock with a 3-attack hour.
2. Wind made the timing a dial you build, and Start of Combat's reading order gave a free positioning puzzle (lay first, then wind).
3. Second puzzle: a body that's safe now but late, a better rate that isn't what you need right now. That became sleep.
4. Seven-Sleeper grows only if it was never needed, so it fights your own Wind: one dial, three wants.
5. Third puzzle: invest in the *opponent's* units and want them to live until the hour. That became the Egg, a marker on a host, not a slot-taker, so full boards still work.
6. Chicks hatch in the enemy's row and hit their neighbours, which gives back-row access without a new targeting rule for the whole game.
7. Wechselbalg copies the shared keywords, so how strong a Chick is depends on the enemy: a different puzzle each round, and a lot of Johnny surprises.
8. Enemy-scaled Eggs (Reedbed, Gernegroß) keep Eggs relevant against strong boards, and their ceiling is someone else's board, so they can't feed themselves.
9. The Midsummer Leaver turns in-fight Chick stats into permanent stats, the tribe's one leftover conversion, gated on winning.
10. Coin-Turner turns gold you saved into Wind, so banking for a Tier up isn't dead time.
11. Sleepers keep their text, so the Warden doubles as a guard for any tribe's engine. This use outside its purpose was the one I set out to find.
12. The hour caps went onto every permanent hour effect after a check of golden Ottilie with golden Doorkeeper: without caps, growth would scale with fight length times the multipliers.
