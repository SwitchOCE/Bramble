# The Trapdoor Lodge, v1

## Three moments this tribe is built to produce

- **In the shop:** "I bought a Lodge Initiate just for its text and put my *Ooze* under a Lid in the back corner, with an empty slot on each side so nothing could knock on it. For three turns it sat out every fight, Patient, getting fed and paying me gold. Then came the one fight we were losing: my front line fell, it came up last, and it still had its Belly full."
- **In a fight:** "Their Cleave unit hit my Money Spider and both units beside it. Three deaths at once, and four Lids opened. My Wolf Mother sprang and dropped Spiderlings. Diadem made her do it twice, and the Spiderlings were Lurkers too. One died, and the Lurker behind it sprang. Their board was gone before they attacked again."
- **Across the game:** "Early on I lost fights with half my board as closed doors. By turn 10 my opponents saw three small spiders and four Lids. Nobody knew the Grand Tyler had been growing under hers for six turns because she never had to come out. The one fight she did come out, it was over."

What the three have in common: units that **wait hidden in their own slot**, a **knock** (a death next to them) that brings them up with a **sudden free strike**, and a **reward for waiting** that pulls against the reward for striking.

## 1. Identity

**Lurk** (new keyword): at Start of Combat, or when summoned during a fight, this goes under a Lid in its slot. While it is under the Lid it doesn't attack, and enemies can't attack it, damage it or pick it. It **Springs** when a unit next to it dies, or when all your other units are dead or under Lids. When it Springs it comes up and makes one attack straight away (its **Spring attack**, an extra attack outside the normal order), then fights as normal. If several Spring at once, they go in reading order. Your opponent's preview of your board shows each Lurker as a closed Lid.

**Spring:** a trigger, "Spring: effect", that resolves as this Springs, before its Spring attack.

**Patient:** a Lurker that was in your last fight and didn't Spring in it.

**Spiderling** (token): 1/1 Spider, Created.

**How it plays.** The Lodge plays with gaps and neighbours. Every Lurker asks where it should sit. Put it next to cheap units that die early (Money Spiders, Spiderlings, another tribe's Eggs and summons), and it becomes an ambush that fires on the first trade. Put it alone, with empty slots round it, and it stays down the whole fight, growing as a Patient unit and adding to the hero damage after a win. The Spring cards (chains, doubled triggers, Lids that close again) and the Patient cards (gold, growth) pull a Lurker opposite ways. The late Lodge cards let one board do both. Because Lurk is a keyword you can give to any unit, the Lodge's best tricks often use other tribes' units: a hidden Ooze, a Start of Combat unit that fires twice, an Egg that serves as a knock.

Rules notes:
- Summons from a Spring go to the nearest empty slots, as usual. The seven slots are the real limit on every Spiderling chain.
- Enemy effects can't pick a hidden Lurker. Friendly ones can: an Ooze can Eat one, a Bird can be one.
- A Lurker that never Springs and is alive at the end counts as a surviving unit for hero damage.

## 3. Builds

**The Ambush (Spring build).** Fodder in the front (Money Spiders, Spiderlings) with Lurkers behind and beside it. Heath Wolf Mother and Pardo turn each knock into more units, and the new units are knocks too. Signal-Thread Spinner turns one Spring into a row of Springs. Diadem doubles the Mother's or Pardo's summons. Dew Gallery makes each Spring attack a Cleave hit. Corkhinge closes the Lids again so the same units can be knocked a second time. Purse-Web Elder banks every Spring as permanent stats. The Worshipful Master closes out the fight by running the whole sequence again. Here's the puzzle: Diadem only works on Lurkers next to it, the Spinner only chains to neighbours, and summons need empty slots, so a good Ambush board leaves the right gaps in the right places.

**The Patient Lodge (wait build).** A strong front of shown units, often another tribe's, wins the fight on its own. The Lurkers sit in gaps where nothing can knock them, and they grow: Tapwell, Reader of the Minutes and Grit-Dresser feed them, and Ballot Clerk pays gold. Each surviving Lurker adds to the hero damage after a win. When the front loses, everything under a Lid comes up at once as a second board, already grown. Mabyn is the build's capstone. Once she's out, Ambush Lurkers count as Patient too, so the two builds can merge.

**The Open Lodge (off-label).** Lodge Initiate, Regalia Tailor (with no Lurkers yet) and the Past Master give Lurk to other tribes' units. With the Past Master, a Start of Combat unit that Lurks fires its effect at Start of Combat (from under its Lid) and again when it Springs. An Inner Guard with Lurk springs the next Lurker each time it Springs. All three builds share a T2–T3 core, so you can choose late.

**In a lobby:**
- **Birds:** an Egg on a Nest is a free 0/1 knock next to a Lurker. A sold Bird leaves a gap that isolates a Patient Lurker.
- **Oozes:** a hidden Ooze keeps its Belly safe until the front breaks. An Ooze that Eats a Spiderling feeds itself, and the Spiderling's death knocks the Lurkers next to it.
- **Bog folk:** Mire units in front make enemies attack the shown wall while the Lurkers wait. Bitter slows the enemy until the Lids open.
- **Fairies:** a Lurker standing on a Ring is a hidden stat bank. Shy units and Lurkers both reward planning the slots ahead.
- **Hunters:** a Hunter that Lurks picks its Quarry when it Springs, so it ambushes the unit across from it. Pack units beside a Lurker join its Spring attack.
- **Hedgerow:** Pass X feeds the rightmost unit, so place a Patient Lurker there and it grows unseen.
- **Against the Lodge:** Cleave and random-damage boards kill several fodder units at once and set off the whole ambush early. That's the counterplay, and it also leaves a well-built chain looking even more spectacular.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Patience rewards winning.** A Lurker stays Patient only if the shown units win without it, so the Patient build snowballs on streaks and stalls on losses. Gaps help (a Lurker no one can knock stays down whenever you win), and so does Mabyn. Watch it for Spike's sake.
- **Spring attacks are free tempo.** Each Spring attack is an extra attack out of turn. With Orbweaver, Corkhinge and Master, one board can make 15 or more extra attacks. The seven-slot limit and "once a fight for each Lurker" cap it, but it needs a sim.
- **Readability.** Simultaneous Springs, chains and repeats are a lot of animation. The fight moment depends on players being able to follow it. Proposed: one Spring at a time, in reading order, each with a visible "knock".
- **Hidden information.** If previews show Lids, opponents can't plan around the real board. That's the fantasy, but some players may feel it's unfair. Option: show each Lurker's tier on its Lid.
- **Open rules questions.** Do Lurkers count when working out which side has more units and attacks first? (Proposed: yes.) What happens to a Lurker across from a Mire, or a Quarry that is hidden? (Proposed: it can't be picked, so the effect falls back to its normal target.)
- **The all-Lurker board.** With no shown units, every Lurker Springs at Start of Combat. That's a legal, swingy opening of free attacks, which may be too strong with the Orbweaver.
- **Thin on economy and early tempo.** There's one gold card (Ballot Clerk), and it's tied to Patience. Tier 1–2 fights depend on the Money Spider.
- **Off-label power** depends on what Start of Combat units exist in other tribes. The Past Master needs a check once those exist.

## 5. Tier check

(Bodies: 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14.)
- **T1:** Money Spider is a 2/1 that leaves a 1/1 (about 1.2 bodies across the fight, a tempo piece that also knocks). Tapwell is 1 body and grows by 2 a turn while Patient, about 0.5 body a turn. Both are on the guide.
- **T2:** Initiate and Ballot Clerk are under a body on purpose, since the Lurk they enable is itself protection. Grit-Dresser grows 3 a turn, 0.5 body. Ballot Clerk pays 1 gold a turn, only when you have a Patient Lurker.
- **T3:** Wolf Mother is a little under a body (Lurk, plus a free attack). Her Spring adds about 0.6 body of Spiderlings and two knocks. Regalia Tailor gives about 1.3 bodies at once with three Lurkers. Inner Guard and Signal-Thread Spinner are enablers.
- **T4:** Reader gives +3 to each Patient Lurker, 12 with four, about 1.4 bodies a turn. Purse-Web Elder gives 8–12 a fight, 1–1.4 bodies. Diadem is the multiplier: it doubles summons. The Sentry is an enabler. On the guide.
- **T5:** The Orbweaver's double damage plus Cleave is about 40–50 in a fight, around 4 bodies. Corkhinge is the direction card and multiplier. Pardo puts 3 knocks and 3 bodies on the board in one go (the same job as the Mother, three times over, as rule 3 says).
- **T6:** Mabyn gives +6 to each of five or six Lurkers, about 2–2.5 bodies a turn. With the Patient cards she switches on, she's near the 4-body target. The Master and the Past Master are payoff and multiplier. Their value depends on the board, so they need a sim. **Broken on purpose:** the Master's ceiling with a golden Diadem plus Corkhinge can be very high. Slots cap the summons, so I let it be the high roll.

## 6. Design rationale

1. I wrote the three moments first. They share the same idea: waiting hidden, a knock, a sudden strike, and a reward for waiting. So that became the mechanic.
2. Lurkers hide in their own slot rather than off the board. That keeps the seven-slot puzzle and makes gaps and neighbours matter.
3. The knock is "a unit next to it dies". That uses Bramble's geometry, and it lets cheap units and other tribes' units become triggers.
4. The Spring attack is a free strike, because the seed's "sudden strike" has to feel like one.
5. I made Patient a keyword so the waiting moment pays off in stats and gold. Its pull against Springing gives the tribe two builds in one.
6. Lurk can be given to any unit (Initiate, Tailor, Past Master). That's for Johnny and the shop moment: the Lodge's best tricks use other tribes' cards.
7. Money Spider and the Spiderling token are the fodder. One token keeps things simple.
8. The Spring multipliers (Diadem, Spinner, Corkhinge, Master) all depend on position, so stacking them is a board-building puzzle, not just a pile-up.
9. Mabyn turns Sprang Lurkers into Patient ones, so the game-long growth moment can end on a board that also ambushes.
10. Hidden Lids in the preview make the hidden-board fantasy literal, and the asynchronous preview already exists.
11. The names and lore came last and were fitted to the roles: the Tyler waits, the Recorder repeats things, Corkhinge's lid closes again, and the Master arrives last.
