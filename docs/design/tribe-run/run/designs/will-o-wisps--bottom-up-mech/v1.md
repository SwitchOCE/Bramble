# The Will-o'-the-Wisps

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: Lights on empty slots.** Gaps never close in Bramble. The Wisps make those gaps into false targets.

- **Light.** A lamp on one of your empty slots. Enemy attacks and enemy effects can pick a lit Light as if it were a unit in that slot. A front row with only Lights in it is not empty, so it still guards the back row. Whatever picks a Light is **wasted**, the whole of it, Cleave included. The Light is then **struck** and goes dark until the fight ends. Lights are not units. They don't attack, they don't count towards who attacks first or towards hero damage, and a side with only Lights left has lost. A Light made while recruiting stays and is relit every fight. A Light made during a fight lasts only that fight.
- **Hoard.** Stats buried under a Light. **Bury +X/+X** adds to the Hoard. Buried stats are always permanent, even when they are buried during a fight.
- **Snuff.** While recruiting, a unit that enters a lit slot (played or moved there) snuffs the Light. The Light is gone for good, and the unit gains its Hoard permanently. A unit that is summoned onto a Light during a fight only covers it for that fight, and the Hoard stays.

**How it plays.** Every slot asks the same question: unit or Light? A Light soaks one enemy swing a fight, so it costs the enemy an attack. But it costs you a body, the first attack (the side with more units attacks first) and survivor damage. Early on you have spare slots, so Lights are free. Late in the game slots are scarce, and the tribe has to pay you for leaving gaps. It pays in three ways. Strikes become triggers: enemies that hit Lights take damage, feed your scalers or hurt their own side. Hoards build up under Lights that you will later snuff into a carry. And payoffs count Lights. Where you put a Light matters. Front-row Lights get struck and defend. Back-row Lights are almost never struck, so they act as savings: they make gold and grow larger Hoards. The tribe is about choosing what the enemy hits, and making it hit nothing.

**Token.** *Risen Wisp*: a 1/1 Wisp, Created by Aarnivalkea. It has no text. It counts as a unit while it lives.

**Interactions worth knowing:**
- **Hobby Lantern pulls Lights forward.** When it dodges from the front row to a back-row Light, it ends up safe at the back and the Light lands in front. Every dodge also counts as a strike, so it fires Marsh-Gas Flare, Joan and the Owl.
- **Little Phos is a way to move Lights.** Sell it where you want a Light. Spunkie also moves Lights: it steps into a Light, and its old slot lights up behind it.
- **Tiddy Mun's second line soaks enemy snipes.** An enemy Start of Combat effect that "deals 8 to a random enemy" hits a lamp instead, and that lamp still fires your strike triggers.
- **Start of Combat order is a puzzle (reading order).** If Old Will resolves before Aarnivalkea, he counts the Lights before they rise, but the Risen Wisps arrive too late for his buff. If Aarnivalkea goes first, the Wisps fight but Old Will counts fewer Lights. If Follette resolves before Old Will, he also counts her fight-only Lights.
- **Lights vs other tribes.** A Light across from a Hunter becomes its Quarry, so the Hunter wastes a swing and its Take never happens. A Cleave attack that lands on a Light is wasted entirely.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Lantern Fortress (strikes).** Two or three units sit in the back row behind a front row of Lights. Follette fills any front gaps at Start of Combat. Each enemy swing becomes Marsh-Gas Flare damage and a Barn Owl team buff, and Foxfire grows from it. Tiddy Mun doubles every Light's uses. Ignis Fatuus makes each strike a friendly-fire hit on the enemy's own board. The build is best against a few big attackers, and weak against wide boards that clear the lamps in one cycle.

**B. Hoard Mere (bury and snuff).** Keep Lights in the back, where they stay unstruck. That doubles the Sexton's Bury and pays the Map-Seller. Bury with the Lamplighter, and with Joan on any front Lights. Then choose how to cash in:
- Snuff into a carry with Spunkie hopping each turn, Gaffer Turncoat splitting each Hoard to its neighbours, and Stingy Jack paying gold for every snuff.
- Never snuff, and let Aarnivalkea raise the Hoards as fighters every fight. When a Risen Wisp dies it becomes a lamp again, and Joan grows the Hoard under it.

Lamplighter's choice is the build's core trade-off. +3/+3 now, or a larger Hoard that you can't use yet.

**C. Few and Bright (count).** Three big units and four Lights. Irrlicht feeds the biggest unit every turn, and Hobby Lantern protects it. Old Will gives +3/+3 times Lights to each unit, so the bonus is units × Lights. On a 7-slot board that product peaks at a 3/4 or 4/3 split. Every slot is a real choice, and nothing needs a cap.

**Mixing in a lobby.** The Wisps want few, strong units, which makes them a good off-tribe filler:
- **Oozes.** Their Bellies hold units without using slots, so Lights fill the gaps. When the Ooze dies, its units spill into the nearest empty slots and cover Lights for that fight.
- **Bog folk.** Mire forces the enemy across onto your Mire unit, and Lights in the other front slots take the rest of the swings.
- **Birds.** The Eggs in Birds' Nests are decoys too, so the two tribes compete for the same empty slots.
- **Fairies.** A Light on a Ring slot gives a double cash-in when a unit later snuffs it.
- **Hedgerow folk.** Pass X sends stats to your rightmost unit, which Irrlicht also likes when it is the biggest.

Off-tribe, Follette protects any back-row carry, and Tiddy Mun counters snipe-heavy lobbies.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Rules load.** Light, struck, Hoard, Bury and Snuff are five terms. The Lights also have to read clearly on screen as targets that are not units. Without a strong lamp visual this will confuse players.
- **Accidental snuffs.** Moving a unit onto a lit slot to tidy the board throws away a Light. That move needs a confirm or a clear preview.
- **Map-Seller is free early.** Back slots are empty early on anyway, so up to 1–2 gold a turn costs almost nothing. If it overperforms, make it "every 3".
- **Wide-board blind spot.** One Light soaks exactly one swing, whatever its size. A 7-unit board clears the lamps in one cycle. Tiddy Mun is the only answer above Tier 4, so the Fortress may fold to wide lobbies.
- **Hard counters.** A Light across from a Hunter blanks its Quarry, and a Light wastes a whole Cleave attack. That may feel unfair to those tribes. A gentler rule is that a Cleave attack on a Light still hits the units beside it.
- **Golden stacking.** Golden Tiddy Mun with golden Barn Owl, at 3 Lights × 3 strikes × +4/+4 × 4 units, is about 290 stats in a fight, roughly 6× Tier 5's target. It is a two-golden high roll, but watch it.
- **Hoard is hidden power.** Large Hoards are invisible strength until you snuff them. Opponents previewing your board must be able to see the Hoard numbers.
- **Undecided rules this design leans on:**
  - Can a Light be swapped with mid-fight? Hobby Lantern assumes yes.
  - What does "nearest" mean? This design assumes fewest steps along next-to links.
  - Do summons land on lit slots? This design assumes yes: they cover the Light for that fight.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** Phos is the tempo body: 2/2, plus a Light worth about a 0/1 Egg. Foxfire is the seed. It gains up to 4 stats a turn at its cap, but typically 2 (about 0.5 body). The cap is about 2.5× the target, on purpose, because it is the Fortress's first scaler. Fallback: change it to +1 Health.
- **Tier 2:** One enabler (Hobby Lantern dodges about one enemy attack a fight, about 1 body in a fight), one buffer and one economy piece, to the guide's shape. Lamplighter's choice is about 6–8 stats once, on target. Map-Seller makes about 1 gold a turn.
- **Tier 3:** Two enablers (Glim, Spunkie), one buffer and one combat engine, to the guide's shape. Joan buries 4–6 stats a turn, against a target of about 5.6. Flare deals 6–9 damage a fight, inside the 5–10 band.
- **Tier 4:** One buffer, one multiplier, one enabler and one economy piece.
  - Irrlicht gives 9–12 stats a turn at 3–4 Lights, against a target of 11.
  - Turncoat roughly doubles each Hoard. It is the tribe's first multiplier.
  - Follette is up to 4 fight-only Lights, capped by the slots you leave empty.
  - Stingy Jack makes up to 2 gold a turn.
- **Tier 5:** Sexton is the direction card. It buries 12–24 stats a turn against a target of 24, on the high side on purpose, because buried stats are locked until you give up a Light. The Owl is the combat engine, at about 48 in a fight with 3 strikes and 4 units, on target. Tiddy Mun is the multiplier, doubling strikes.
- **Tier 6:** Two capstones and one payoff.
  - Ignis Fatuus does about 60–120 damage a fight to the enemy's own side, against a target of 84.
  - Old Will tops out at about 72 at the best 3×4 split.
  - Aarnivalkea gives the total Hoard as fighters, about 90–180 late. Its top end is above target on purpose, because it is the Hoard build's only payoff that keeps the bank intact. Its growth is linear (struck Risen-Wisp lamps add Joan's Bury), so it passes the runaway check.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Started from two rules: gaps never close, and attackers pick randomly from the front row. An empty slot that can be picked makes a decoy with no body.
2. Lights are not units, so each one costs you first attack and survivor damage. Unit or Light becomes the tribe's main trade-off, and it gets sharper as the board fills.
3. Every wasted enemy swing is a "struck" trigger. That gave damage, scaling and team-buff cards a shared hook.
4. Back-row Lights are rarely struck. So unstruck Lights pay out (Map-Seller, Sexton's double Bury), and position becomes a choice between defence and savings.
5. Hoard is the conversion. Burying gives a better rate than a direct buff, but you can't use it until you give up the decoy, as Lamplighter's choice shows.
6. Snuffing is the only way to cash a Hoard in, so filling the board late is a decision, not a default.
7. Spunkie, Turncoat and Stingy Jack make snuffing a repeatable engine, each limited per turn so it can't loop.
8. Old Will scales with units × Lights. The 7-slot board limits that product, so it needs no extra cap.
9. Hobby Lantern's dodge and Tiddy Mun's pull on enemy effects let Lights take hits aimed at units too.
10. Each capstone turns one subsystem outward: strikes hurt the enemy (Ignis), Hoards fight (Aarnivalkea), and the count buffs (Old Will).
11. I kept the guide's tier shape, because every role it asks for fell out of the three subsystems.
