# The Shiny Syndicate (v1)

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: Debt.** It comes from Debt tokens in *Dominion: Empires* (a deckbuilder): you buy now with Debt, and you can't buy again until you've paid it off.

In Bramble, the lock moves from buying to **Tier up**, interest is added, and paying off Debt becomes a resource of its own.

**The rules (one panel, shown by your hero's IOU pile):**
- **Owe N:** take N Debt.
- **Paying:** while recruiting, pay 1 gold to clear 1 Debt. You can't clear Debt during the same recruit phase you took it.
- **Interest:** at Start of Turn, you Owe a fifth of your Debt, rounded up (1 to 5 Debt costs 1; 6 to 10 costs 2; and so on). Interest counts as Owing.
- **Tier lock:** while you have Debt, you can't Tier up.
- **Ledger:** the total Debt you have cleared this game, by any means. It never goes down.
- **Skim N** (new keyword): when you win a fight, clear N Debt at your next Start of Turn. Skim does nothing if you owe nothing.
- **Start of Turn order:** Skim first, then interest, then other Start of Turn effects.

**How it plays.** The Syndicate turns one pile of IOUs into three different resources, and each build reads it its own way.
- **Carry builds** want Debt to be *high* at Start of Combat: per-Debt Attack, gold for owing, the exactly-7 capstone.
- **Collection builds** want Debt *cleared*: every Debt paid, Skimmed or fenced grows units and the Ledger.
- **Skim builds** want to *win*, so that victory pays the bills for free.

Most cards borrow (Owe), some pay back (clear), and several do both, so a single action often sets off three or four triggers. You choose each turn how much to pay, when to let interest pile up, and when to clear everything so you can Tier up. Other tribes' units become Syndicate fuel through credit buys, the Fence and Silas's thefts, and Syndicate tools such as Tick-Book Magpie and Uncle Jack fit naturally into other tribes' boards.

**Notes on wording.**
- "When you clear Debt" fires once per Debt cleared. "When you Owe" fires once per Owe action, interest included.
- With Sorrow and Joy, clearing 1 Debt counts as clearing 2 Debt for every card.
- Silas's copies go to your hand as Created units, so they sell for 0 gold, but the Fence still clears their tier in Debt.

**Tokens and spells:** none. The only new screen element is the IOU pile, which shows Debt, how much of it is new this turn, next turn's interest, and the Ledger.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Long Ledger (collection).** Thought and Oast Usurer take on Debt every End of Turn, and you pay it off the next turn.
- Each payment triggers Chimney Jackdaw, Gallows Lane Collector and Memory.
- Sorrow and Joy doubles every clear, and Grandmother Corbie turns the Ledger into a fight-wide buff.
- The Johnny tension is between paying a little at a time and paying in one lump. Chimney and Collector are capped per turn, so they reward small payments every turn. Memory rewards one big payment.
- Strong play is to let interest pile up for two turns, then clear everything on the turn Memory is on the board.

**B. On Tick (carry).** Hold Debt and fight with it.
- Belfry Jackdaw pays you for owing, Orchard Heavy hits harder for every IOU, and Arne buffs the whole board.
- Uncle Jack removes the tier lock and lets you Tier up on credit. Each credit tier-up is an Owe event, so it also triggers Wassail Bowl-Bearer.
- Seven turns the build into a puzzle: you must finish recruiting with exactly 7 Debt. That means paying down old Debt precisely and letting Thought's End of Turn Owe 1 land you on 7.
- The risk is interest: a fifth compounds, so carrying 30 Debt costs 6 a turn.

**C. Protection Racket (Skim tempo).** Win early with Hawthorn Fledgling and Orchard Heavy, so that Skim clears your Debt for free.
- Gallows Lane Bagman turns Skim into gold once you're debt-free.
- Rookery Don turns every Skimmed Debt into permanent stats for the whole board.
- The clever line is to keep just enough Debt for the Don to Skim. Fresh Owes from Thought or Silas top it up.

**D. The Fair (fence and steal, cross-tribe).** The goal here is to make other tribes' units into Syndicate money.
- Tick-Book Magpie buys a unit on credit.
- Silas steals plain copies of the biggest unit from each board you beat.
- Thorn Nest Fence sells those units to clear their tier in Debt.
- Uncle Jack lets you rush tiers, so the copies Silas steals, and the units the Fence clears, keep rising in tier.

**Mixing with other tribes in a lobby:**
- **Birds:** a Bird sold through Fly South pays nothing, but the Fence still clears its tier in Debt, and the Bird comes home doubled. This is the best cross-tribe trick in the set.
- **Hedgerow folk:** Acorns are gold you can hold, so the Larder becomes a choice between saving and paying off Debt. Thought's "next to this" buff fits touching runs and Pass.
- **Oozes:** Belly can keep Silas's stolen copies safe, Eat can turn them into stats, and the Fence turns them into Debt cleared. Created units are worth nothing to anyone but the Syndicate.
- **Hunters and Bog folk:** both build boards that win fights, which keeps Skim running. A Syndicate pairing that relies on Mire boards wins steadily and Skims every turn.
- **Fairies:** Shy units leave and pay 2 gold, which can go towards Debt. Ring slots stack well with Thought's buff to the units next to it.
- **Neutral tools:** Tick-Book Magpie (an extra buy each turn), Gallows Lane Bagman (gold from wins) and Uncle Jack (tier rush) work in any comp. Other players will contest them in the shared pool.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Rules weight.** Debt brings seven new ideas: Owe, interest, the new-Debt rule, the tier lock, the Ledger, Skim, and the Start of Turn order. The IOU pile has to show all of them at a glance, or the tribe reads as bookkeeping.
- **Tier lock frustration.** Thought and Oast Usurer take on Debt whether you want it or not. A player who forgets the new-Debt rule will find they can't Tier up. The interest preview and a "pay all" button are essential.
- **Uncle Jack may be the best card in any lobby.** A credit Tier up is roughly 8 to 11 gold of tempo. If it dominates, make it "Once a game" or add +1 to the credit cost.
- **Debt is free at the end of the game.** Nothing collects on a player who dies owing. A Carry player can borrow everything on their final turns with no downside. A possible fix is that losing a fight costs +1 hero damage for every 5 Debt you have.
- **Rich players feel no pressure.** Gold carries over uncapped, so a big bank turns interest into small change. Watch whether Debt still forces choices for economy-heavy heroes.
- **High-roll stack.** Sorrow and Joy plus Rookery Don or Corbie doubles the top payoffs, and a golden Sorrow and Joy triples them. Neither payoff feeds its own source, since Skim is limited by Debt and the Ledger only buffs in fights, but this needs the two-pass runaway check.
- **Silas and triples.** Created copies count towards triples, so stealing the third copy of a unit you already hold is a real (and fun) high roll. Plain copies keep it from stealing buffed stats.
- **Fence and Discover.** Every Discover becomes worth its tier in Debt. That is fine while Debt exists, but watch whether Discover-heavy heroes make the Fence too strong.
- **Seven's exactly-7 condition** could feel fiddly. If it does, use "7 or more" with +5/+5.
- **Owe numbers assume units cost 3 gold** (credit buy, Hawthorn Fledgling). Rescale them if Bramble's economy changes.

## 5. Tier check

The bodies match the guide's 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14 within one stat. As a check on gold values: 1 gold is about a third of a unit purchase.

- **Tier 1:**
  - Hawthorn Fledgling gives about 1 body once (+4) for about 3 gold of Debt, which is on target.
  - Chimney Jackdaw grows +2 a turn (0.5 body) when you pay, slightly over the 0.4 target. I left it there on purpose: it is the seed every collection build grows from.
- **Tier 2:**
  - Gallows Lane Collector gives 4 stats a turn (0.67 body), on target.
  - Belfry Jackdaw nets about +1 gold a turn after interest, on target for economy.
  - Tick-Book Magpie is a 3-gold loan each turn, so it is an enabler and not costed as stats.
- **Tier 3:**
  - Thought gives about 6 stats a turn (0.85 body).
  - Orchard Heavy gives up to +8 Attack in a fight (1.1 bodies) plus Skim 2. That is a little over the guide; the cap of 8 keeps it in band.
  - Oast Usurer and Thorn Nest Fence are enablers and economy pieces worth about 3 gold a turn, each with a cost attached.
- **Tier 4:**
  - Memory and Wassail Bowl-Bearer each give about 12 stats a turn in a good setup (1.4 bodies), on target.
  - Gallows Lane Bagman gives 2 to 4 gold a win.
  - Uncle Jack is outside the table on purpose. He gives tempo, not stats, and he is flagged under Risks.
- **Tier 5:**
  - Arne gives 48 in a fight at 12 Debt (4 bodies), on target. The cap of 30 Debt allows 120 (10 bodies). That is broken on purpose, as the greed high roll, and interest pays for it.
  - Sorrow and Joy is the ×2 multiplier.
  - Silas is a better Discover each turn you win, paid for with Debt.
- **Tier 6:**
  - Grandmother Corbie gives 96 in a fight at Ledger 40 (6.9 bodies, against a target of 6).
  - Seven gives exactly 84, the target.
  - Rookery Don gives 84 a win, or about 56 a turn at a two-thirds win rate (4 bodies).

## 6. Design rationale

1. I took Debt from Dominion because the seed is "credit now, pay later", and Debt is a proven design for exactly that.
2. I moved the lock from buying to Tier up. Buying is the whole Bramble turn, while Tier up is the big purchase worth locking.
3. Interest is a fifth, rounded up, so small debts are cheap and large ones compound. That makes carrying limit itself without a hard cap.
4. Debt you take can't be cleared in the same recruit phase. This closes the borrow-and-repay-at-once loop that would fill the Ledger for free.
5. Interest counts as Owing, and Skim and selling count as clearing. That way every hook fires from several sources, which is where the overlapping combos come from.
6. The payoffs split three ways (carry, collect, skim), and each wants the same Debt pile handled differently. Builds pull against each other instead of stacking.
7. Paying a little each turn and paying in one lump both have homes: per-turn caps on Chimney Jackdaw and Gallows Lane Collector, a per-3 scale on Memory, and exactly 7 on Seven.
8. Skim resolves at Start of Turn, before interest. Winning lowers your interest, and every clear lands inside a recruit turn, so "this turn" is unambiguous.
9. Credit buys, the Fence, Silas, Bagman and Uncle Jack are cross-tribe hooks, so other tribes become fuel and the Syndicate's tools travel to other comps.
10. The multiplier (Sorrow and Joy) doubles clears, not Debt, so it doubles payoffs without doubling costs.
11. I capped Orchard Heavy at 8 Debt and Arne at 30. Otherwise per-Debt payoffs plus Uncle Jack could grow without limit.
12. Names came last: Thought asks (Owe) and Memory remembers (clears); Seven is the rhyme's "exactly seven"; Silas's finder's fee is the theft; Arne is the warning about greed.
