# The Olm Sleepers — v1

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: Age.** Age is a counter on a unit, shown as pips. Any unit can hold it, whatever its tribe. It stays when the unit moves and is lost when the unit is sold. A golden keeps the highest Age of its three copies. Age only changes while recruiting, never in a fight.

**New keywords and words:**
- **Still:** End of Turn: if this unit wasn't played or moved this turn, it gains 1 Age. (Still resolves before other End of Turn effects.)
- **Awaken N: effect.** This happens once, the moment the unit has N or more Age. A golden doubles the effect, not N.
- **Oldest / youngest:** your unit with the most / least Age. Ties go to the first in reading order.
- **Spend Age:** remove it from the unit. Moving Age takes it off one unit and gives it to another.

**How it plays.** An Olm board is set once and then left alone. Every turn a Still unit isn't touched it gets older, and the tribe's cards turn Age into stats, gold, damage and one-off Awakenings. For a long time the board is pale and thin, and then the thresholds start to land. The sleepers aren't the interesting part, though. The bats, dormice and odd figures around them treat Age as a resource that can be moved. You can sell an old unit and send its Age to a newcomer, hand it along a row to tip someone over an Awaken threshold, cash it in for gold, or aim it at whichever unit your other cards read as "oldest" or "youngest". Each turn, the question is which unit to leave alone and which one to feed.

**Item (from Old Polh):** **Pilgrim's Ham.** Use: gain 2 gold, or give a unit 2 Age.

No tokens and no spells.

**Interactions worth spelling out (the Johnny layer):**
- **The relay.** Brother Netopir sits between an awakened unit and an empty slot. Play a new Awaken unit into that slot and it takes all the old unit's Age, so it can awaken the same turn. The source has already awakened, so its Age had nothing left to do. Play a Dormouse Pilgrim beside it as well and the receiver gets 1 more.
- **The sell-bank.** Seven-Month Dormouse spends its own Age, so it tends to be your youngest unit, and that makes it the unit Echo Bat feeds. Selling an old Hatchling becomes gold over the next few turns.
- **Oldest and youngest as aim.** Beehive Painter feeds your youngest unit and Cloister Novice feeds your oldest. A unit from another tribe that never gains Age is always the youngest, which makes it Painter's permanent target and turns the Olms into a buffer tribe for someone else's carry. Netopir can strip a unit's Age to make it the youngest on purpose. Echo Bat makes the youngest older, which moves Painter's target.
- **Cascades.** A Clutch-Sister awakens and gives 2 Age to its neighbours, which can tip a Dripstone Sleeper or Old Polh beside it. Mother Ribica makes these effects recur, and her "once a turn each" limit stops the loops.
- **Geometry.** The Robber-Knight rewards matching your aged units to the enemy slots across from them, and you can see your next opponent's last board. But moving breaks Still, so you either pay a turn of Age or bring in Parkelj, for whom moving *is* ageing.
- **Lamplight.** Lamplighter Hrošč doubles every card that counts Age (Novice, Painter, Pressure-Sense, Robber-Knight, the Eldest, the Dragon) but not the thresholds, so it raises a stat or combat build and leaves the Awaken build alone.

## 3. Builds

**1. The Long Sleep (stats).** Gilled Hatchlings, Cloister Novice and Kurent Bellman early. You never move anything, and you finish on the Eldest, with Hrošč to double it. It's the honest version: weak until about turn 8, then every unit adds stats equal to its Age each turn. The only decision is where Bellman goes and who the Novice treats as oldest.

**2. The Relay (Awaken).** Clutch-Sisters and Dripstone Sleepers placed so their neighbours overlap, Brother Netopir and Echo Bat to pass Age around, Old Polh for Hams, and Mother Ribica to make every threshold recur. The goal is the Eldest's Awaken 20, reached by handing Age along rather than waiting for it. It's the most active Olm build: something moves every turn, just never the sleepers.

**3. The Hidden Passage (combat).** Pressure-Sense Olm, the Robber-Knight and Dragon of the Springs, with Parkelj so you can rearrange to match the opponent across from you without losing Age. It wins fights rather than growing stats, and Hrošč doubles all three cards.

**4. Patrons (half-Olm).** Cloister Warden lends Still to another tribe's units. Beehive Painter feeds your youngest, which is the carry from the other tribe. Parkelj ages units that have to move. You get 3–4 Olms as an engine, wrapped around another tribe's payoff.

**With the other tribes in a lobby:**
- **Hedgerow folk** already reward units that stay in their slot, so Still costs them nothing. *Pass X* buffs your rightmost unit, and Painter's youngest can be set up to sit there.
- **Fairies:** Rings sit on slots, and Still units never leave their slot. The two fit together naturally. A Ringed slot next to Cloister Warden can hold a non-Olm that ages.
- **Oozes:** a unit in a Belly isn't on the board, so it doesn't age. *Eat* takes stats but not Age. Echo Bat treats an eaten unit as gone, not sold, so its Age is lost. This pairing is mostly anti-synergy, which is a fair cost.
- **Birds:** *Fly South* is a sale, so Echo Bat catches the Bird's Age before it leaves. The Bird comes home with doubled stats and no Age, which makes it the youngest unit, in Painter's sights.
- **Bog folk:** Mire units sit in the front and never need to move, so they work well with Cloister Warden.
- **Hunters:** *Quarry* points across, like the Robber-Knight. Each suits a board set to match the opponent's.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Early weakness.** Only Dormouse Pilgrim is a real early body. The tribe will lose fights in rounds 3–6, which is intended but may lose too much Health. If it does, raise the Hatchling to 2/2 rather than adding tempo text.
- **"Never move" can feel passive.** The Long Sleep has few decisions after turn 6. The Relay and Patrons builds carry the Johnny play, and the Long Sleep may need one more active card.
- **UI load.** Age pips on up to 7 units, plus "who is oldest or youngest" changing during a turn. The board needs a clear marker for the current oldest and youngest, or the Painter, Novice and Echo Bat interactions can't be read.
- **Loops and runaways.** Ribica plus two golden Clutch-Sisters could loop without the "once a turn each" limit. A golden Netopir *copies* Age, so it creates Age from nothing and is the likeliest runaway. The Eldest's Awaken doubles the board once; I made it never repeat so Ribica can't make it recur. Check golden Netopir with golden Echo Bat in the two-pass runaway check.
- **Hams fill the hand.** A recurring Old Polh with Ribica gives 2 Hams a turn, and the hand holds 4. The cap is natural but might feel like a chore. A variant: Hams go straight to gold if the hand is full.
- **No interaction with the enemy.** Opponents can't touch Age. The only answers are killing the board fast early, or being bigger late.
- **Overlap with Hedgerow folk's turns-in-slot clocks.** The two differ in that Age is kept through moves, can be passed on, and is spent. If the overlap still reads as the same thing, Still could instead trigger on "didn't attack last fight", but that would break the "kept and left alone" seed.
- **Ties in oldest and youngest** follow reading order, which players will need to learn. It's a small rules tax that Johnny might enjoy exploiting.

## 5. Tier check

- **Tier 1:** Hatchling has a 3-stat body (below 4) and grows 2 stats a turn, about 0.5 body against the guide's 0.4. It goes slightly over on purpose, since it's the seed and has no other text. Pilgrim is a 5-stat tempo body with a small one-off. Both fit.
- **Tier 2:** Novice gives 4–6 stats a turn once its target is old, 0.7–1 body, which is the top of the range. The Dormouse gives about half a gold a turn on its own and more when fed. Echo Bat is pure enabler. These fit; the Dormouse is under target until it's fed, on purpose.
- **Tier 3:** Dripstone Sleeper's single buff is about 12 stats to three neighbours, roughly 1.7 bodies against about 1.4. It's over on purpose, because it comes 3 turns late. Pressure-Sense deals 8–12 damage in a fight, the top of the range. Warden and Clutch-Sister are the enablers that name the comp. These fit.
- **Tier 4:** Painter gives about 12 stats a turn (1.4 bodies, on target). Bellman is the first multiplier, adding 3–4 Age a turn. Netopir is an enabler. Polh is 4 gold or 4 Age, once, and recurs with Ribica. These fit.
- **Tier 5:** The Robber-Knight deals about 50 damage a fight against matched units (about 4 bodies, on target). Parkelj is the direction card, giving 4–6 Age a turn and freeing the board to move. Ribica is the multiplier. These fit.
- **Tier 6:** The Eldest grows the board by 40–60 stats a turn (3–4 bodies, near target). Its Awaken 20 doubles the board once, far outside the guide on purpose: it's the late chase that the Relay build is built to reach. The Dragon gives about 70 stats in a fight against a target of 84. Hrošč is a multiplier with a ×2 ceiling, ×3 when golden. The Eldest has a weak 14-stat body on purpose.

## 6. Design rationale

1. Age is a counter that persists through moves and can be moved, not a slot clock. That keeps it apart from Hedgerow folk's turns-in-slot clocks and makes Age a resource Johnny can route.
2. Still is a keyword on cards, not a tribe rule, so Cloister Warden can lend it to other tribes and Parkelj can be the one card that breaks it.
3. There are five categories of card: generate, move or convert, grow stats permanently, grow in a fight, and thresholds. Moving Age got the most thought, because the interwoven play lives there.
4. Awaken thresholds give the seed's "weak, then devastating", and a golden doubles the effect but not N, which keeps goldens worth chasing.
5. The shape follows the guide's starting shape. The only change is Parkelj as Tier 5's direction card: a rule-breaker is what changes the build.
6. Oldest and youngest act as a targeting system, so cards point at each other's results: Painter, Novice and Echo Bat.
7. Tier 2 economy spends Age, which creates tension with thresholds and gives Echo Bat a natural target.
8. Brother Netopir's relay ("whoever asked last") makes already-awakened Age useful, which is the tribe's main "outside its intended purpose" trick.
9. Mother Ribica makes Awaken recur rather than doubling it, so a few Awaken cards are enough, and "once a turn each" caps loops.
10. Hrošč multiplies the cards that read Age, not the thresholds, so each of the two Tier 6 lines has its own multiplier.
11. Damage in a fight reads Age through board geometry ("across", "lowest Health"), so a combat build exists, and moving to line up has a real cost.
12. Names and lore came last: olms sleep and awaken, bats carry Age, dormice cash it in, and the black olm who moves is the scandal.
