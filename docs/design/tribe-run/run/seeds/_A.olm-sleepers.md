## Part A: the seed — The Olm Sleepers

**Who and where.** The Olm Sleepers are pale, blind cave folk of **Hollowdeep**, the karst country of caves and lost rivers: a river vanishing into a gorge, vast echoing halls, hanging bridges over the dark, stalactite curtains, a lake that comes and goes, a castle built into a cliff mouth. Its hidden rivers surface in other lands. The olms keep the deep pools; **greater horseshoe bats** hang in the vaults above and carry their word. Its culture is Slovenian: cured ham and red wine, painted beehive panels, miners' guilds, shaggy carnival demons, dragons in the springs, mine goblins, the robber-knight of the cliff castle.

**The real animal.** The olm (*Proteus anguinus*) is a Dinaric cave salamander, Europe's only cave-dwelling vertebrate: about a foot long, eel-thin, pinkish-white, keeping feathery red gills all its life. Its eyes lie buried under skin, though the skin still senses light and shuns it; it finds its way by smell, water pressure and weak electric fields. Olms are estimated to live over a century; one went about ten years without food in a lab, and one in Herzegovina stayed in the same spot for seven years. Females seem to breed only about once every twelve years. Floods once washed olms out of springs, and locals took them for the young of a dragon under the earth, as the chronicler Valvasor recorded in 1689. Slovenes call it *človeška ribica*, "human fish", for its skin. A rare **black olm**, dark and with working eyes, lives in a few south-eastern springs. Horseshoe bats call through their noses and hang wrapped in their wings; edible dormice sleep about seven months a year.

**Society.** An underground theocracy, gently parodying a silent contemplative order: cloister, pilgrims, relics and a gift shop, all very slowly. The olms pronounce rarely and briefly; the bats carry the words, add a little, and fly for either side of any quarrel, like Aesop's bat. Surface dormice think the olms are gods of the vanishing rivers and wake from their long sleep to leave offerings. Everyone else thinks they are baby dragons. They never correct anyone about the dragon thing.

**Notable figures.** **The Eldest**, a sage in the deepest pool who last spoke before anyone's grandmother hatched; the answer is still being interpreted. **Mother Ribica**, keeper of the clutch that comes once in a dozen years. **Parkelj the Dark**, the black olm of the warm southern springs, who can see, has colour, and is a scandal. **Brother Netopir**, chief bat courier, loyal to whoever asked last. **Old Polh**, a dormouse elder who leads the ham-and-wine pilgrimage and sleeps through the answers. **Lamplighter Hrošč**, a cave-beetle verger tending lamps nobody in the order can see. **The Robber-Knight**, a lynx who threw fresh cherries to his besiegers from a secret passage, and pays the order to keep quiet about it.

**Places.** The Sinking Reach, where the river drops into the gorge; the Echo Nave, where the bats roost; the Comes-and-Goes Lake, a lake one season and a meadow the next; the Dragon Springs, where floods spill olms into daylight; the Still Pool of the Eldest.

**Objects and icons.** Feathery red gills, dripstone that grows more slowly than an olm ages, flood-marks on cave walls, clay dragons left at the springs, hams and wine jugs at the pool's edge, beehive panels painting olms as dragons, dormouse-fur caps, sheepskin carnival costumes with cowbells, bat-carried scrolls, the beetle's unneeded lamps.

**Themes.** Patience and long life, the long fast, stillness as wisdom, rarity, sensing what eyes cannot, floods bringing the deep to the surface, a flattering mistake left uncorrected, the old outlasting the hasty, messengers who can't be trusted with the message.

**The play it suggests:** pale cave creatures that live a century: weak for a long time, then devastating, growing from every round they are kept and left alone.
