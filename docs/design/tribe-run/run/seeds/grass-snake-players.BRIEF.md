# Designer Brief: The Grass Snake Players

Your task is to design a complete new Bramble tribe from this seed: **The Grass Snake Players**. This brief has three parts: the seed, how Bramble plays, and a tier guide.

---

## Part A: the seed — The Grass Snake Players

**Who they are.** A touring melodrama company of snakes, lizards and their amphibian neighbours, working the heaths and pond edges of the Moor & Bog: purple heather, sandy banks, sphagnum pools, clapper bridges and low fog. They are famous for one thing: nobody dies on stage like a grass snake, and nobody gets up quicker for the bow.

**The natural history.** The grass snake is olive-green with a yellow-and-black collar behind its head, harmless, and a strong swimmer that hunts frogs, newts and fish. Cornered, it first bluffs: it hisses and strikes with its mouth shut. If that fails it plays dead, rolling onto its back with mouth open and tongue lolling, going limp, sometimes with blood at the mouth and nostrils. Turn it right way up and it flips back over. It also lets off a foul-smelling musk. It lays eggs in compost and manure heaps for the warmth, often many females in one heap, and it sheds its skin in a single piece, eyes turning milky blue beforehand. The slow-worm is not a snake but a legless lizard: it can drop its tail, which keeps wriggling while the owner leaves, and, unlike any snake, it can blink. Male adders in spring rear up and wrestle, pushing each other down: the "dance of the adders". The adder is the one member of the cast whose bite is real.

**Society and what it parodies.** The Victorian touring stage: actor-managers, barnstormers, penny-gaff melodrama with moustached villains, drowned maidens and stage blood, plus the theatre's habits and superstitions. Roles are cast by seniority and vanity. The Green Room is a damp bank of moss. Most hatched in the compost heap behind the barn: "born in the wings". In the Baltic farmhouse tradition a grass snake under the floor is a household guardian, fed milk and never harmed; the Players remember this and expect to be treated accordingly.

**Notable figures.**
- **Sir Hesketh Hiss**, actor-manager, whose death throes lengthen every season.
- **Dame Natrix Collar**, leading lady, who has drowned, been poisoned and died of a broken heart in one week.
- **Little Anguis**, a slow-worm understudy who keeps leaving his tail on stage, which then gets better notices than he does.
- **The Brothers Berus**, adders and fight directors, who stage the spring dance as a duel and never quite pull the blow.
- **Old Crest**, a great crested newt in the prompt box under the stage, who has the whole repertoire by heart.
- **The Natterjack**, front-of-house barker, whose call carries across the heath on spring nights.
- **Mr Ardea Grey**, the heron critic. His reviews are short, and some actors do not survive them.

**Places.** The Compost Heap Theatre (warm, crowded, sold out), the Millpond Stage, the Red Barn, sandbank dressing rooms, the Heath Circuit of village greens.

**Objects and icons.** Shed skins on costume racks; phials of stage blood; the musk bottle, kept for hecklers; the prompt book; footlights of glow-worms; playbills and pinned-up reviews; wreaths; tragic masks; a saucer of milk left out by the stage door.

**The repertoire.** *Eglė, Queen of Serpents*, the Lithuanian tale: the grass snake Žilvinas marries a girl who finds him in her clothes after bathing, and her brothers kill him with scythes. In the original he stays dead; here he gets up for the bow. Also *The Drowning of the Millpond Maid*.

**Themes.** Death as performance, the curtain call, the encore, understudies stepping in, shedding a role like a skin, ham against method, bluff strikes and the one real bite, the critic who really does eat actors, the show going on.

**The play it suggests:** a melodrama troupe whose leads die spectacularly and get up for the bow, so that deaths happen, count and pay out without losing the body.

## Part B: how Bramble plays

**Words.** Bramble says *unit* (not minion), *shop* (not Tavern), *tier*, *Tier up* and *gold*. Card text is "Trigger: effect" (Start of Turn, End of Turn, When played, Start of Combat). Limits read "Once a turn" or "Twice a fight". Fight changes end with the fight; lasting ones say **permanently**. The **biggest** unit has the most Attack plus Health. Shares of stats round down.

**Format.** Eight players each pick one of three heroes (40 Health, a passive or an active Power) and play until one is left. Each round is a Recruit Phase, then an automatic fight against another player's board. Play is meant to be asynchronous, against recorded boards, with no turn timer (details not settled yet). You can see your next opponent's last board.

**The board.** Seven fixed slots in two staggered rows: front F1–F4, back B1–B3. Play a unit into any empty slot; move units freely while recruiting. Gaps never close up. **Beside:** left or right in a row. **In front of, behind:** the diagonal links. **Next to:** any of these. **Across:** the enemy slot with the same label. **Touching:** an unbroken chain along the zigzag F1, B1, F2, B2, F3, B3, F4. The hand holds 4. The board is the fight's HP: you have no health in a fight.

**Shop and pool.** Tiers run 1–6. The shop shows 4 units at Tiers 1–3, 5 at Tiers 4–5 and 6 at Tier 6, plus one spell, rolled from your tier or lower, weighted towards yours. Units come from one shared pool (15 copies of each Tier 1 unit, down to 9 at Tier 6). A lobby uses five of the six tribes, plus fifteen neutral support units (gold, Shield, Swoop, spell help). **Discover:** choose one of three units from the pool.

**Economy.** You get 3 gold on turn 1, then 1 more each turn up to 10. Unspent gold carries over, uncapped. A unit costs 3 and goes to hand. A Bought unit sells for 1; a Created one (summoned, copied, Discovered, given) for 0. Refresh costs 1 for the first 15 a turn, then 1 more every 5. Freeze is free. Tier up costs 5 / 7 / 8 / 11 / 13, 1 less each turn you wait.

**Spells and items.** The shop's spell has its own price and is used once, while recruiting. Spell and hero stat gains scale: each printed +1 is 10% of the target's stat, never less than printed. Harvest Supper raises a **tribe bonus**: standing stats on every unit of a tribe you own, now and later. Some cards give **items**, one-use hand cards. There are no trinkets, quests or anomalies.

**Triples.** Three copies across board and hand (Created ones count) become a golden unit in hand: double base stats, buffs kept, every number in its text doubled except thresholds and counters. Playing it Discovers a unit from the tier above yours.

**Combat.** The side with more units attacks first. Start of Combat effects resolve one side, then the other, in reading order (F1–F4, then B1–B3). The sides then alternate attacks, units going in reading order, once a cycle. Attackers hit a random front-row enemy; the back row is attackable only once the front row is empty. "A random enemy" ignores rows. Both units deal their Attack at once. A dying unit's own summons take its slot; others go to the nearest empty slot. Other back-row access is not settled yet.

**Hero damage.** The side with units left wins. The loser takes a round base (0 in rounds 1–3, rising to 13 by round 14) plus 1 per winning unit that started the fight and survived. A tie deals nothing.

**Shared keywords.** **Shield:** prevents the first damage each fight. **Cleave:** attacks also hit the units beside the target. **Damage Reduction N:** takes N (or N%) less from each hit. **Swoop:** attacks the enemy back row while anyone is in it. **Bristle N:** an enemy that hits this takes N back. **Hunker:** takes half damage when attacked. **Nightshade:** when attacked, first loses half its Health.

**Today's tribes** (16 units each):
- **Hedgerow folk:** **Acorns**, items worth gold (1; Stash 5; Great Hoard 16; three make the next size), whose held total is the **Larder**. **Pass X:** your rightmost other unit gains X. Turns-in-slot clocks; growth along touching runs.
- **Fairies:** **Rings** on slots: a unit there gains your Ring size (now +4/+4) per Ring, and cards grow the size. **Shy:** only an aimed spell buffs it; then it leaves for 2 gold next turn.
- **Oozes:** **Belly N:** holds N units, safe and inactive; they come out when the Ooze dies. **Eat:** destroy a unit and gain its stats permanently. **Digest:** Start of Turn: eat what's inside.
- **Birds:** **Fly South:** a sold Bird pays nothing, leaves for two turns and comes home to hand with doubled stats. Its empty slot is a **Nest**, where a 0/1 Egg fights.
- **Bog folk:** **Mire:** the enemy across must attack this while it lives. **Bitter:** an enemy attacking this first loses a quarter of its Attack; the total removed is your **Toll**. **Soak:** Bitter, banking its Mired enemy's loss as stats next turn. **Snap:** attacks the Mired enemy with most Attack.
- **Hunters:** **Quarry:** the enemy across, which this attacks whenever it can. **Take:** when the quarry dies; gains land next turn. **Pack:** joins attacks by units beside it, using up its own attack.

**Stat scale.** A typical Tier 1 unit is 1/2 to 2/3; Tier 6, 6/8 to 8/8. The tier guide's "body" (median printed Attack + Health) is 4 / 6 / 7 / 8.5 / 12 / 14 at Tiers 1–6. Much growth is written as shares and multiples of a unit's stats.

## Part C: tier guide

A short guide to what each tier is for and roughly how much a unit there gives. It was built from 310 Battlegrounds units valued by role and by stats added per turn. It's a guide, not a limit: a unit can break it if that makes the tribe more fun and you say why.

### Part 1: what each tier is for

| Tier | Job | Roles it usually holds (Battlegrounds today) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A body that wins the early fights, or a small seed a later engine grows from | tempo, combat engine; a few buffers, scalers and economy |
| 2 | The first buffers and economy; a direction starts | buffer (about 40%), economy, enabler, combat engine |
| 3 | Enablers that name your composition: they make what the tribe feeds on | enabler, economy, combat engine, buffer |
| 4 | "A strong player in a composition that sets up future things, enables, gives you some stats" | buffer, economy, enabler, plus the first multipliers |
| 5 | "Strategic direction or a lot of tempo": the card that says what this game's build is, or holds the line while you find it | buffer (about 40%), combat engine, economy, multipliers |
| 6 | "A capstone … a pivotal engine piece or a big scaling payoff": the comp is built to reach it | capstone, payoff, enabler, multiplier |

**A 19-unit tribe (2 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 3 / 3), as a starting shape:**
- **Tier 1:** one tempo body and one seed.
- **Tier 2:** two buffers or enablers and one economy piece.
- **Tier 3:** two enablers, one buffer, and one combat engine or economy piece.
- **Tier 4:** one or two buffers, one enabler, and one multiplier or economy piece.
- **Tier 5:** one direction card (a payoff or a strong buffer), one combat engine, and one tempo piece or multiplier.
- **Tier 6:** one or two capstones and one payoff.

Change the shape when the tribe's idea needs it, and say why.

**Three rules of thumb:**
1. **The tier is paid for in text, not body.** Printed stats rise only about ×1.26 a tier (median 4.5 at Tier 1, 12.5 at Tier 6).
2. **Output roughly doubles each tier from Tier 3.**
3. **The same job one tier up gives about three times as much,** through more triggers or more recipients rather than a bigger number per trigger. Examples:
   - Gatekeeper Amalgam (T6) gives the same +4/+4 packet as Lightfang (T5), four times a turn instead of once: 160 against 40.
   - Brann (T5) repeats every Battlecry, where Kelp Keeper (T4) repeats one: +72 against +24.
   - Nadina (T6) gives three shields, where Glowscale (T5) gives one: 120 against 40 a fight.

### Part 2: the curve, as ratios

Bramble's economy and stat scale will change (owner, 12 Nov), so the guide is anchored to **ratios, the curve and each tier's job**, not to Battlegrounds' gold or stat numbers. The owner: "as long as it's not like anchored to Battleground's economy numbers and more about like the ratio and the curve and the kind of like strategic parts they play."

**The unit of measure is a body:** the median printed stats (Attack + Health) of a vanilla unit at the same tier, in whatever scale Bramble uses. Every target below is in bodies, so it moves with the scale.

The targets are for a normal (non-golden) copy in a good setup, meaning the tribe's composition is mostly assembled, around two-thirds of the way through a game. **Permanent** stats stay on the board. **In fight** is stat value inside one fight, starting again each fight. **Once** is a one-off permanent buff. A unit usually gives one of the three.

| Tier | Body grows (vs T1) | Permanent / turn | Or in fight | Or once | Ceiling with goldens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0 | ~0.4 body | ~0.6 | ~1 | ×2 |
| 2 | 1.3 | 0.3–1 body | ~1 | ~1.3 | ×2 |
| 3 | 1.6 | ~0.8 body | 0.7–1.4 | ~1–1.4 | ×2 |
| 4 | 2.0 | ~1.3 bodies | ~2 | ~2 | ×2–4 |
| 5 | 2.2 | ~2 bodies (top ~5) | ~4 | ~2.5 | ×2–4 |
| 6 | 2.8 | ~4 bodies (top ~10) | ~6 | ~6 | ×2–4 |

**The curve in one line:** bodies grow about ×1.2 a tier, while what a unit adds grows about ×2 a tier from Tier 3, so by Tier 6 a unit adds about four of its own bodies a turn.

- **Goldens.** One golden key piece usually doubles its effect. Two golden key pieces, or a golden multiplier (a golden Brann triggers three times), double it again. The owner (12 Nov): "With a single golden unit, you can start doubling things, and you get multiple copies of goldens, you can start doubling things." The Battlegrounds values were measured on normal copies, so their top end is understated. Cost the normal copy against the table, and check that the golden ceiling is a high roll worth chasing, not a runaway.
- **Economy.** Price gold against what it buys at that stage: one gold is worth the share of a unit purchase it pays for (in Battlegrounds, a third of a unit). Don't use a fixed stats-per-gold rate, because Bramble's economy will change.
- **Well outside** means about twice the tier's target, or half of it, with no reason written down. Flag those and propose a change that keeps the card's core idea.
- **Runaway growth is a separate check.** This table prices growth that adds a set amount each turn. A unit whose gain feeds its own source goes through the two-pass runaway check in `bramble-design-guidance.md`.
- **Battlegrounds' own numbers,** for reference only. The bodies are 4.5 / 6 / 7 / 9 / 10 / 12.5 stats at Tiers 1–6. Median permanent growth a turn is 2 / 2 / 5.5 / 12 / 20 / 53, and in fight it is 2.5 / 6 / 4.5 / 20 / 42 / 80.
