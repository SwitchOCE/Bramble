## Part A: the seed — The Salmon Run

**Who they are.** The Salmon Run are the woodland's returning travellers: Atlantic salmon and their cousins the sea trout, born in the gravel of the forest's cold, fast rivers, who leave for the sea for years and come home to the very bend they hatched in. When the Run is in, the whole wood turns out to watch, and some of it to eat.

**The real fish.** A hen salmon cuts a nest, a *redd*, in clean gravel with sweeps of her tail. Eggs hatch into *alevins* living off a yolk sac, then *parr* with dark thumbprint marks down their sides. After a year or a few they turn silver as *smolts* and go to sea, to feed as far off as Greenland and the Faroes. A *grilse* comes back after one winter at sea; others stay several. They find their home river largely by smell. On the way up they stop eating, leap falls and weirs, and change: silver darkens to bronze and red, and the cock fish grows a hooked lower jaw, the *kype*. Some small male parr never go to sea; they ripen in the river and dart in to fertilise eggs beside the big cocks. Most salmon die after spawning; a few spent fish, *kelts*, mostly hens, drift back to sea and may return again. Their bodies feed the forest: sea-nitrogen from carcasses has been traced into bankside trees. Otters, herons, ospreys, bears and seals wait along the way.

**Society and parody.** The Run is the Camino by water, with a pilgrim passport stamped at every weir, crossed with the emigrant who left a skinny parr and comes back grand, red-faced and hook-jawed, telling everyone about the Faroes. They travel and reunite by year-class, like old school years, and fast on the road proudly. Home, they owe the gravel everything, and they pay it.

**Notable figures.**
- **Old Bradán**, the Run's oracle. In Irish myth the Salmon of Knowledge ate the hazelnuts of the Well of Segais; the poet Finnegas fished the Boyne for it seven years, and the boy Fionn mac Cumhaill, cooking it, burned his thumb, sucked it and got all its wisdom. Bradán answers everything, then forgets it did.
- **Captain Kype**, a red cock with a pothook jaw, first at every leap.
- **Mother Redd**, a hen on her third run, which almost never happens, and she mentions it.
- **Little Jack Gravel**, a parr who never went to sea yet turns up at every spawning.
- **Brother Grilse**, back after one winter, mortified among the veterans.
- **Old Kelt**, thin and dark, drifting seaward to do it all again.
- **The Bailiff of the Weir**, a heron who stamps passports and occasionally eats a pilgrim.

**Old stories.** In Welsh legend the Salmon of Llyn Llyw, oldest of animals, carried Arthur's men to free Mabon. In Norse myth Thor caught Loki, hiding as a salmon, by the tail, which is why salmon tails taper.

**Places.** The Gravel, the nursery redds; resting pools named like coaching inns; the Weir Steps and the fish pass; the Long Leap, where bears stand in the spray; the Estuary of salt, seals and farewells; the Far Feeding, the sea nobody in the woods has seen; the Hazel Well at the source.

**Objects and icons.** Stamped passports, a pebble from your own redd, hazelnuts, the kype, silver turning red, fish ladders, claw-marks on wet rock, spent bodies under alder leaves.

**Themes.** Going home, the road as the point, changing on the way, spending yourself so the next run can start, a gift back to the forest, endurance, wisdom at the source, being hunted where the river narrows.

**The play it suggests:** Fish that work their way up the board fight after fight; the ones that reach the head spawn the next run and are spent.
