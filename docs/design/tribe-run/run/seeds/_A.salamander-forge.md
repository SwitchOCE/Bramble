## Part A: the seed — The Salamander Forge

**Who they are.** Fire salamanders and newts of **Ashfell**, the volcanic uplands of grassy cones, crater lakes, basalt columns, hot springs and fumaroles. They are alchemist-smiths who walk into the fire, come out holding something better, and regrow a burnt hand by morning. Their neighbours bake bread in hot ground and age cheeses in stone huts; the Forge supplies the metal, and the burns. Safety regulations are a rumour.

**The real animals.** The fire salamander is black with yellow (sometimes orange or red) blotches, a warning to anything that might eat it: glands behind its eyes and along its back ooze a bitter toxin. It lives for decades, hunts slugs and worms on wet nights, and gives birth to larvae in cool streams rather than laying eggs. The medieval belief that salamanders live in fire came from logs: they shelter in damp rotting wood, and when a log went on the hearth they crawled out of the flames. Pliny wrote that one was cold enough to put fires out. In truth they need cool, damp woods and dry out quickly in heat. Newts and salamanders really do regrow lost limbs, tails, parts of the heart and eye, though it takes weeks, not a night. Some newts flash bright orange bellies when threatened; the great crested newt's belly is spotted in a pattern unique to each animal, and in spring the male grows a jagged crest and fans his tail at the female, who wraps each egg in a folded leaf. Both shed their skin and usually eat it.

**Society and what it parodies.** A medieval craft guild with no fear of injury: apprentices, journeymen on their wandering years, masters admitted for a masterpiece, a guild mark on everything. It borrows too from the alchemist's workshop, where every recipe is "heat it again", and from Paracelsus, who named the salamander the elemental of fire. Grinders lie on their bellies over the wheel, as the cutlers of Thiers did. A lost finger is a week off. In private, every smith retreats to the wet cellar to keep their skin damp, and nobody admits it.

**Notable figures.** **Guildmistress Ottilie Ashbelly**, head of the Forge, rebuilt piece by piece more times than she remembers; **Brother Paracel**, alchemist who believes anything can be improved if heated long enough; **Crested Konrad**, a great crested newt journeyman who wears his breeding crest to every meeting; **Smooth Tobias**, an apprentice who has regrown the same tail eleven times; **Mother Atra**, a black alpine salamander from the high passes who carries her young two years or more and calls it patience; **Warden Quench**, the safety officer, unseen since the rulebook was used to light the furnace.

**Places.** The Crater Forge, built into a live cone; the Quenching Pools, hot springs where blades and smiths are cooled together; Slag Row, the journeymen's lodgings; the Masterpiece Hall; the Wet Cellar under mossy logs; the rift parliament, which the Forge attends to complain about noise.

**Objects and icons.** Tongs, crucibles, the furnace that never goes out, quench buckets, "salamander wool" aprons (really asbestos cloth, once said to be salamander hair, cleaned by throwing it in the fire), Francis I's emblem of a salamander in flames with the motto *Nutrisco et extinguo* ("I nourish and extinguish"), burn salve, shed skins, and "eye of newt" from *Macbeth*, which the Forge regards as slander.

**Themes.** Making and remaking; the same piece heated until it is worth more than a new one; craft pride and masterpieces; injury treated as routine; coming back from damage, but not unchanged; legends they let stand because they are good for business; warning colours; cool skin, hot work.

**The play it suggests:** Fire-walking smiths who make gear for the board and keep reforging it: buy fewer bodies, and heat the same pieces until they are worth more than anything new.
