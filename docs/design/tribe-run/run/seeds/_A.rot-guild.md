## Part A: the seed — The Rot Guild

**Who they are.** The Rot Guild is the woodland's undertakers: mushrooms and toadstools who lay the fallen to rest and turn them back into soil. They live in the deadwood and leaf litter of the Elderwold, where fallen giants are left to rot, and they keep an old quarrel with tidy Beechhold, whose charcoal burners and timber rafters cart off logs the Guild regards as its congregation. The toadstools are solemn, black-gloved and slow to speak; the puffballs are round, cheerful and forever bursting into laughter. Both agree that a funeral is a harvest festival: grief at the graveside, a feast by the next rain.

**The real fungi.** A mushroom is only the fruit; the fungus itself is the mycelium, a mesh of threads (hyphae) running through soil and wood. Many species wrap tree roots in mycorrhizae ("fungus-roots"), trading minerals for sugars, and these links join trees into what the press calls the "wood wide web" (how much trees really share through it is still argued). Honey fungus spreads by black, bootlace-like cords under bark and through soil; one individual can spread across many hectares of forest, and it kills living trees as well as eating dead ones. Its mycelium glows faintly in rotting wood, one source of foxfire. Mushrooms appear overnight after rain because the fruit was built small beforehand and only needs water to swell. White-rot fungi are among the few living things that can break down lignin, the tough stuff of wood. A puffball releases its spores in a puff when a raindrop strikes it, and a giant puffball holds trillions. The shaggy ink cap dissolves into black ink, once used for writing. Stinkhorns smell of carrion so that flies carry off their spores. Stag beetle grubs live for years inside rotting wood.

**Society.** A medieval craft guild crossed with a Victorian funeral parlour and a harvest supper. Apprentices, journeymen and masters; a livery of brown and cream; hired mutes who stand silent at the gate; a Register of the Fallen; and an old dispute over whether mourning lasts one rain or three. Nothing is wasted and nobody is forgotten, though some are composted rather promptly.

**Notable figures.** **Grandmother Bootlace**, the honey fungus, the Guild's founder, whom no one has seen all of at once, and who is rumoured to attend funerals she arranged herself. **Warden Amanita**, a fly agaric in scarlet and white: solemn, poisonous and never offered a hand. **Clerk Inkcap**, who writes the Register in his own ink and grows shorter with every entry. **Bellows Puffwell**, a giant puffball, master of the harvest feast, who laughs in clouds. **Old Fingers** (dead man's fingers), the gravedigger, reaching black and patient out of stumps. **Stinkhorn**, the herald nobody wants downwind. **Ludwig**, a stag beetle grub who has lodged in the Guild Hall for five years and is not ready to leave.

**Places.** **The Long Fall**, a toppled oak hollowed into the Guild Hall; **the Undercroft**, the threadways beneath the litter where word travels root to root; **Foxfire Chapel**, a rotten log that glows on moonless nights; **the Litter Acre**, a churchyard of leaf mould where nothing stays buried long; **Stumpwick**, the puffballs' hamlet of stumps.

**Objects and icons.** Foxfire lanterns; spore prints pressed as guild seals; the Register, written in ink cap; black bootlace cords; moss biers; harvest baskets; tinder-fungus braziers; candlesnuff candles; a chain of office strung with King Alfred's cakes (black ball fungi from dead ash); spore-dusted mourning crepe.

**Themes.** Death as harvest; the quiet network under everything; long patience, then growth overnight; giving back what the wood gave you; mourning and mirth in one procession; the awkward truth that the undertaker sometimes caused the death.

**The play it suggests:** Mushroom undertakers for whom every fallen friend is compost: lose your own on purpose, and the network under the board fruits from it.
