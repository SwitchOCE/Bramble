# UI clutter, reminders and tutorials (9 Nov 2026)

The owner's words in the orchestrator session, as steering for the ideation build. Transcribed: dictation errors, filler words and repeats removed, and punctuation added. *[Editor notes in italic brackets.]*

I wanted to add some steering here about thoughts I have about the busyness of UI and UX design and reminders in our game. I know this may change a little bit of your review criteria mid-run, which may be inconvenient, but it's in the goal of steering you towards something that's going to be of a higher quality.

Currently, the game, the UI, etc., is all, in a way, judged by agents trying to get higher scores from critics who are giving potentially bad feedback. I can see these diagonals on the board and all these targeting arrows, in terms of static images that convey information. These are not the ways that players learn and understand games.

When I play chess, there isn't a sign on my knight telling me that it can jump over other pieces and that it moves two and then one in two different directions, and explaining that you can either move horizontally two and then vertically one, or vertically one and then horizontally two if you prefer. There's just a horse. And the chess player is expected to have learned the rules and know that the horse is there.

Something that might serve a player who is unfamiliar with something, because they're encountering it for the first time, is not something that you want to have in the game, because the majority of players will only play for the first time once, and then spend hundreds of games playing when they already know what things do.

We need to have far less visual clutter devoted to essential tutorialization that's taking up part of our main game space. The job of a tutorial is to tutorialize. Other than that, the player is assumed to know how to play the game when they're in the game environment.

We need to have fewer reminders, fewer static images that indicate where things are. Animations should, by player feel, capture how a game is and how it plays.
