# Owner rule: don't double-tap a nerf or a buff

Said in chat on 19 November 2026, while the first balance pass after the bot league was being planned. Read with [2026-11-19b-balance-pass-aims-and-method.md](2026-11-19b-balance-pass-aims-and-method.md).

> A big problem in game design is the double tap nerf, or a bit less commonly, the double tap buff. And what I mean by that is: let's say there are two cards that are both really strong and part of the same strategy. If you nerf both of them, because both of them look like they're overpowered, you might make them completely unviable because they actually relied on each other. And either of the two nerfs would have been enough to bring both cards in line, because their most common and best use case was with each other. You need to be really careful when you're making balance changes that you're not double-tapping. And that means finding the correct place to apply the adjustment.

The owner allowed this to be recorded "as a note or update a skill around it if you wish, if you think it's worthwhile, and it's likely to lead to better designs."

*[Editor note: in the same message the owner described, as an analogy they asked not to be quoted, a way of judging a card: give it a defence before convicting it, the way a trial does. The defence argues that another card is the real problem and this one is only enabled by it, or that everything else is too weak. The aim is to argue against our own choice before making it, and to treat every card as part of the wider system.]*
