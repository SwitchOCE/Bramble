# Owner rule: the balance pass fixes the game's balance, and weighs every kind of adjustment

Said in chat on 19 November 2026, while the first balance set after the bot league was being planned. Read with [2026-11-19a-balance-reads-bot-preferences.md](2026-11-19a-balance-reads-bot-preferences.md).

## The aim

> Utility effects across the board are too strong. Extra summons summon too many things, mostly, and the fact that you don't have to worry about missing out on them with the Hoppers is a big issue. The number of Hoppers you can get is the problem, not the Tide scaling. I'd prefer better Tide scaling and less summons. Not markedly less, but definitely at the lower tiers. Especially slot buffs are incredibly powerful with summon strategies, because you get to use them twice or more than twice in some instances, which is what's making the Pigeon so good. I had an idea for this, that we could maybe change the rules so that tokens don't benefit from slot buffs, but it does feel a bit counterproductive in terms of fun. I don't know. It's just some interesting thoughts, I guess.
>
> We also don't have any good scam units in the game. We do need a neutral pool, something similar to Battlegrounds, like the equivalent of a Black Hole or a Leeroy from Galaxy and Battlegrounds respectively. We can address some balance problems with tech units. The traditional way of countering a Cleave is a Taunted Leeroy, which in our instance would be a Guarded whatever we add to the game.
>
> Your goal is to fix the game's balance issues. Some strategies may be fixable by adding counters, some may require card redesigns. I'm not sure if Fury is a great mechanic. You're allowed to go back and look at things from first principles. You can try small changes and go, okay, are these working, or is this system just problematic in its ability to scale throughout the game in a line shape that's similar to the other tribes. Does this unit need to move up a tier because its effect is too good for tier three, but it doesn't really need a redesign because it's in a good spot? Is this tier four unit justifying its space as a four? Is this tier six justifying its spot as a six? Those are questions you should be asking.

On scam units:

> Scam refers to effects like poisonous, venomous units that have a thousand Attack or something but very little Health, so they just take out one big unit. Something that essentially allows you to use a small unit to kill a big unit. Peck cards can be scam cards. Scam is a subset of tech.

## The method

> Essentially, with every unit, come up with a numerical adjustment, an economic adjustment, i.e. moving its tier or place in the game, a replacement adjustment, a rework adjustment, multiple types of adjustments, and then figure out which one makes the most sense. I want agents to be forced to think about the different types of modifications we can make to a unit and which one actually makes the most sense at solving the problem they're trying to solve.

Added straight after:

> Additionally, in that list, you might want to consider if other cards make the card unhealthy, and it's not the card's fault, it's the support for it. Sometimes a card can be balanced; it's the structure around it that exists in the game that pushes it over the edge.

Earlier the same day, on the tribes and The Colonel:

> I tend to agree with the bots that Pigeons and Locusts are very strong and that Badgers and Mice are rather weak, as a general rule; obviously there are individual units that break the rule. I would agree with the T6 units that they found to be overpowered being overpowered. The one that turns every card in your hand into Reserve is ridiculously busted. It cannot be allowed to survive unchanged. We can either choose to go with different cards, rework cards, remove them and replace them, whatever. There are different cures for different things in terms of the balance levers that we can pull. It's best to try and do the best job we can, and figure out what the best choice is.

On Flying Ace, the case that set this off:

> The fact that Flying Ace reached those stats is more a fact that it is the best unit at using stats in the entire game, and the optimal strategies revolve around that unit. The Medal cap is not the way of balancing it. Cleave is a ridiculously powerful thing, especially when combined with Sortie and the ability to have it attack instantly at the beginning of combat. You have misdiagnosed the part of the card that is strong. It could have no ability to gain stats and its utility effects would still be absurdly good. If it does have Cleave, it probably shouldn't have Sortie [or] immediately attack at the beginning of combat. And the card that causes your [Sortie Pigeons] to immediately attack at the beginning of combat has to go. I would also probably change Hoppers in general. And I would say that tier one units are probably too strong based on the strategies the bots had in terms of their levelling curves. Most tier one units should probably get a nerf to their long-term potential. The tempo of them is fine.
