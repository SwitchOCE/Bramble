# Owner direction: clear, concise wording

Said by the owner in chat on 8 November 2026, after playing build 6 from the ideation branch.

## Build 6's text is too wordy

Okay, I played the build, and what I can say immediately is that our descriptions of mechanics are terrible. Absolutely awful. Far too long, far too wordy. I want agents to carefully inspect how mechanics are described in other games. Look at Hearthstone Battlegrounds. Look at all the mechanics and how they're worded, how simply they're explained in their wording, how keywords are used to reduce the length of paragraphs, etc. We are spending far too much text on screen on explaining stuff. I'm sure that all the effects are fine, but a lot of the explanations of them are incredibly convoluted and need to be simplified greatly. This is an absolute must happen before anything more occurs. I'll test the build after that additional iteration.

*[Editor note: the owner also asked for this to go to the session responsible for the build. It went to the ideation orchestrator, which is running the wording pass for build 7.]*

## The principle

Clear and concise wording. Make complicated things simple. That is the goal of explanations. Not to be overly specific, not to handle every use case. You do not need to explain how something that's a little bit marginal or implied by a mechanic is going to work. If it's implied, let it be implied, and the player can find out the mechanism in additional details elsewhere.

## Making it a rule

The owner approved making this a working rule for all Bramble agents, with the wording below: "That's good. I like that."

> **Clear, short wording in the game (owner, 8 Nov).** Card, keyword, tooltip and guide text says the main effect plainly, in as few words as it takes, and uses keywords to save sentences, as Battlegrounds does. It doesn't explain edge cases or anything the mechanic implies. Those go in a second layer (reminder text on hover or tap, the guide, the rulebook) or stay implied. Purpose: build 6's text was "far too long, far too wordy", and the point of an explanation is to "make complicated things simple", not "to handle every use case". Where it doesn't apply: the rulebook and engine specs, which must stay exact because they are where the edge cases live.
