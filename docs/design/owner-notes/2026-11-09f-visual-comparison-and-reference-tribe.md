# Visual comparison and a reference tribe (9 Nov 2026)

The owner's words in the orchestrator session. Transcribed: dictation errors, filler words and repeats removed, and punctuation added. *[Editor notes in italic brackets.]*

## Compare screenshots with other games

I would just really encourage, in general, comparing screenshots of our game directly with screenshots of Hearthstone, Battlegrounds, Galaxy, TFT, etc., to see where we are from a visual layout, but also a visual comparison tier, I suppose.

## A reference tribe from Battlegrounds

On a separate point, if you're struggling to get the tribe scaling stuff working, perhaps take a copy-paste of an existing Hearthstone Battlegrounds tribe into our game. Not so we can use it in our final release; we don't want to. But so you can re-power-budget it into our game with the card's identity preserved and all the effects effectively the same. You can change the numbers on the cards. Then getting a feel for how our tribes play against those would be instructive in teaching us some design lessons. Maybe a tribe that plays relatively straightforward.

Make sure you look at the Tier 7 or the Hearthstone Deck Tracker guides on how to play the tribes correctly, and maybe watch a YouTube video of G for XQN *[probably "Jeef or XQN", two top Battlegrounds streamers]* and record the strategic units they use to achieve that. As a result, you'll be able to do a strict A/B comparison against something we know, maybe not that it is perfect. We're obviously trying to improve Battlegrounds and make changes, and some of the decisions they make we want to do differently, but at least get a feel for how an endgame strategy plays out.

And also, I think, understand how the early game is played. You might not even use units of a specific tribe in the early game, and you might change into a tribe later, and you still need those flexibility games to be able to set up different outcomes. Early units should be somewhat flexible as well as enabling. There's a balance to be struck between the two, which pull at each other.
