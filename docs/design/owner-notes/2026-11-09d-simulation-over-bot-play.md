# Simulation over bot play, and the shape of the curve (9 Nov 2026)

The owner's words in the orchestrator session, sent while the fundamentals audit was running. Transcribed: dictation errors and repeats removed, and punctuation added. *[Editor notes in italic brackets.]*

## Empirical simulation over observed bot play

You should rely far more on empirical simulation data than on observational bot play and self-play data. The bots will not be good players. You can make them okay at best, but you are never going to make elite players out of bots. That would be a project harder than making the game itself. So if you can instead create empirical simulations, at various turn thresholds, with various setups of things you think might be strong, that will be far more reliable and should be weighted far more heavily than the observational data. The observational data can serve as a common-sense check every now and again.

## Don't force balance; watch the shape of the curve

When I say I want power budgets and performance to be relatively close, I don't want you to force it. There will be situations where, because these games have positive feedback loops inside them, someone gets ahead. That gives them an economy lead, which helps them get a tempo lead, which means they can greed more economy without losing health, which gets them more economy, and more tempo, and they take the best long-term greedy line and win the entire time. That happens in any game with a significant amount of RNG, and this genre is filled with it. I'm not worried about the top 1% of games having 10 times as many stats as the average game. That does not concern me. To some degree it's an inevitability: if the game is going to be interesting and fun, it will happen. I'm more concerned about the shape of the curve: the distribution of how frequently that happens. I don't want common situations where economy snowballs happen in almost every game.

Bots are also going to be very bad at executing that kind of playstyle. If you tried to get a bot to execute a Tethys, Proud Privateer, APM Undead game, it would very much struggle in a Battlegrounds scenario to execute it anywhere close to as well as a player like G4XQN, or even me. *[Named Battlegrounds builds that need many precise actions per turn; G4XQN is a top Battlegrounds player.]* I'm roughly top 200 in the world at that game. I'm good, but the gap between me and the best players is large, even though I'm well within the top 1%.

So don't let reviewers fail good work on power-budget concerns that don't matter, if the thing happens very, very rarely and it produces a good game in general.

## Distribution of stats beats the total

One thing the power budget will never capture, which is very important and which you've highlighted already: there were various metas in Battlegrounds where a unit could actually reach 2,147,000,000 Attack and Health, and it was very beatable with scam units. It takes clever play to win even when you have a stat lead. The distribution of stats matters a lot. It's way harder to beat 3 units that are 500 million / 500 million than a single 2.1 billion one. Even though these numbers are absurd, should never happen and indicate game design problems, it's still the case.
