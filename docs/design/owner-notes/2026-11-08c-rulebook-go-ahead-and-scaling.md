## 2. The third scaling factor (C)

*[A = how many sources give a buff, B = how often they trigger, C = upgrading the size of the buff itself during the game.]*

That stuff is so difficult to judge. Galaxy brought it in widely with Candylar. Before that, only Fae could do it. In the beginning of Galaxy, Fae could do it through a very small number of cards, and it was always the best comp if you could pull it off. It was obscenely better than anything else. But it was really fun. One of my favourite cards was Wink, the card that gave you the C.

The problem is that it exacerbates small balance differences and leads to boards of wildly varying strength, based on something that feels pretty arbitrary. If you give somebody a C, it almost always leads to one-sided fights, even if boards are pretty close in A and B. A single slight difference can lead to insane power variations.

I don't like it. There's a big part of me that wants to say no C factor: no Candy sweetness and no Fae buffs, to use a Galaxy analogy; no Tavern spell buffs and no Blood Gem-like systems, to use a Battlegrounds analogy. But the problem is that a lot of players expect it.

I think the issue is **repeatable** C engines, and the fact that they often double power at the first granularity breakpoint. If a C took something from 3/3 to 4/4 and didn't do it every turn, so the card does it once and it's very hard to repeat, that's not a big deal. But when a C takes a 1/1 to a 3/3 and then gives an extra +2/+2 every turn, you run it for three turns and end up with 7/7s instead of 1/1s. Your power budgeting's out the window.

I think we can try to have Cs, but we watch them very carefully. The main two scaling factors should be the number of times per turn you can apply your buffs, and how good the buffs are, rather than how quickly you can improve the buffs.

That said, I don't know. I'm so torn on this. It's very difficult. Let's say we go with Cs. Let's try Cs, be careful with them, and make sure things don't get too out of hand. I'm willing to pursue that avenue for now.

## 3. Engine output: no fixed number, but plenty of actions late

I don't necessarily know if we want to put a specific number on it. But I do think the player should get to take a good number of actions per turn that give stats, in the end game and at least the late mid game. That comes from a good combination of economy effects that provide whatever your scaling vector is.

In the menagerie example, whenever you played a Tavern spell, a unit of each type got about +3/+3, I think. So you wanted multiple units of different types, and to cast as many Tavern spells as you could. You picked your units of different types so that some gave you Tavern spells, multiplying the number of spells you could play, and some held stats really well. That was a really good way of doing things in that comp.

## 4. Stats per turn and the economy inflection point

I think there's data on the amount of stats players gain per turn in Battlegrounds. Firestone used to capture it for my own replays when I used Firestone. I wonder if it's public somewhere.

What I found in Battlegrounds is that you'd hit an inflection point very quickly and then start getting enormous amounts of stats. Once you hit an economy inflection point, you accelerated from there. So if you got lucky and hit it two turns earlier through good RNG, people could basically never catch up with you. I don't know how I feel about that. I think it's because of how aggressively the curve served as a positive feedback loop: economy lets you get more economy, which gets you more economy, and so on.

We have a native negative feedback loop built into the game with the escalating shop refreshes. Hopefully we won't design as many crazy positive-feedback mechanics, especially with units selling for 0. But I don't know. We'll figure that out. I don't know the answer yet. I'll try more builds and see how it goes.
