# Owner corrections: design reactions are directions, not decisions

Said by the owner in chat on 2 November 2026, after reading Claude's "Checkpoint read-through: notes and diff" doc, which had treated the reactions in the [02h note](../../sources/2026-11-02h-checkpoint-read-through.md) as decisions.

## Nothing in design is cut straight away

Firstly, Fast isn't cut. Nothing in design ever gets cut straight away. You think about it a bit and see what it can teach you. Fast might be a dead end, but we should still explore it.

So this is hard for you as an agent, right? It's going to be hard for every agent: in design, you have a discussion rather than saying something is a dead end. What changes?

Only when it comes to designs, never when it comes to anything else: when it comes to evaluating designs, my reaction doesn't necessarily dictate what changes. It dictates what iterates. You can push back on a design and say, "Hey, it works here, and here's an example." Or, "Okay, it doesn't work like that, but what if we change it to work like this?" A lot of my comments were about where I'd like to see future design iteration, rather than stuff necessarily being cut immediately.

I think the diff is perhaps an overreaction to a lot of my reactions. Those are just my thoughts. They are more suggestions than hard rule decisions, except with the lack of economy. I think everybody should have access to some economy, even if it's the general pool. Do we have a neutral pool? We haven't decided that yet.

## Every idea needs an example card

I think all of these things need an example unit with rules text: how it would interact, and why the player would want to run the unit. An example of a situation where the unit would be good. That's pretty much true for all of these. What it would look like on an example card is the next required step from turning these ideas into actual design.

## Economy

I would say that economy can just give gold. But it's cool to have a lot of economic effects that benefit from the strategy you're in. It's nice if not every economy effect is good for you, or at least if some economy effects are way better for the strategy you're in, even if another is, on paper, a better economy effect. I just want variation in the designs. I don't necessarily think that just giving gold is a bad thing. It's just that it's nice to have cool designs as well. We're going to need a mix of both. We're going to want to try everything.

## The two proposed rules

**Rule 1 (no rewards for inaction).** Rule 1 is way too simplistic. And it shouldn't be a rule; it should be a design principle. If a design rewards not taking actions, it should be isolated, so the player still has plenty of interesting choices, or it should be time-bounded. That can make it really interesting, because instead of it being a lack of decisions, it means that you have to try and wait for its payoff to trigger. "Don't move for 3 turns, then X": that's cool. "Don't move to get stats every single turn": not so cool. It's a delicate balance.

**Rule 2 (pitch ideas concretely).** Rule 2 is amazing. I really like that. It doesn't have to include an analogue from Battlegrounds or Galaxy; I would not put that there. But if there's an analogue of a design that I might be familiar with from another game, and it doesn't have to be Battlegrounds or Galaxy, it may be worth saying, "Okay, it's kind of similar to this." But the majority of Rule 2 is really good.
