# Owner direction: style rules and an artist mood board

Dictated by the owner on 1 November 2026, after choosing Ink + flat B and before merging the style trials.

## Before merging

Before we merge, I want to understand any risks from this merge. I want to make sure that we're keeping the rules for the art style simple but clear, not over-engineered, not too many rules about negatives and what not to do. Explain everything that's going to be material to the project.

*[Editor note: Claude then reported that the Ink B prompt has four NO rules, two of which repeat positive rules, and suggested a positive rewrite. The next section is the owner's answer.]*

## The NO rules stay

Those no rules are fine. I don't mind them. A tiny bit of repetition is okay, especially because in a couple of the images there was a little bit of shading. So even the positive and the negative rule didn't quite fully get the message through. So those are okay.

## An artist as inspiration

I think it might be good to use a specific artist as a piece of inspiration. I know we might get derivative stuff from that, but this is all concept art that's going to be redrawn by a human artist anyway. So that's okay.

So get a mood board of what you think are good images that are representative for the style guide. Put some work into it, and then when that's done and I like it, we'll merge.

## The mood board

How about you just create the mood board of images by those artists? They don't need to give them the artist's name necessarily. Or does the image tool only accept text prompts?

*[Editor notes:*
- *"Those artists" are the four Claude offered as anchors: Albert Uderzo (Asterix), Walt Kelly (Pogo), Tove Jansson (Moomins) and Luke Pearson (Hilda).*
- *"They don't need to give them the artist's name" is read as: image prompts don't have to name the artist; reference images could steer the style instead.]*
