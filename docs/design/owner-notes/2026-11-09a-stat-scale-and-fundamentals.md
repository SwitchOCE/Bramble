# Stat scale, concentration and fundamentals (9 Nov 2026)

The owner's words in the orchestrator session, after the Hedgerow case study. Transcribed: dictation errors and repeats removed, and punctuation added. *[Editor notes in italic brackets.]*

## On Alderman Dozey and the power budget

If we're looking at a card that's only getting 8/8 in stats, that's the only effect it really gets, because it's capped at 8/8 when it doubles. *[The doubling fires once; Acorn feeding adds up to +12/+12 more.]* And if that one big unit getting 8/8 is enough to overwhelm a game, I think our power budgeting across the board is very, very much too low. In most healthy Battlegrounds metas, it's pretty common for pretty much every unit on the board to end up over 100/100, at least in the end game. Even in the mid-game, you start seeing units at 150/150 in many metagames. And that's because of a lot of the compounding effects of economy. Perhaps our tier power budgeting isn't steep enough.

I think this is revealing something that you probably won't like, which is that a lot of the design needs to change. Large amounts of design need to be completely reworked, and that's okay.

## Condition and payoff

With every design, you have a condition and a payoff. The condition might be a trigger condition, or it might be a condition that basically doesn't exist, and the absence of the condition is what makes something good. Say our normal power budget for the best a tier 3 unit could be was a 5/5. That would be a very low budget. Then the condition would be "unconditional", and the payoff strength, 5/5. Whereas say you gave a different tier 3 unit 2/2, and "at the end of every turn it gains +1/+1", and our power budgeting had that at roughly the same. It would reach the same power level in 3 turns and be better every turn thereafter. The payoff would be stats and stat growth, and the condition would be end of turn. If there were end-of-turn synergies, the card would be relatively stronger. Some cards might buff end-of-turn effects or make end-of-turn effects trigger other things. That increases the payoff. Other cards might say "whenever an end-of-turn effect activates, do X". Then it's an enabler for different strategies. You need to look at a card's effects in terms of: what are the conditions, what are the payoffs, and does it enable other cards with how it works.

I don't think there's anything fundamentally too wrong with Hedgerow. In terms of design, it all looks pretty reasonable. And if it's overperforming, I think that's a big hint about where our other tribes' designs are.

## Stats are not equal: concentration

You need to look at the principle that stats are not equal in a game like this. Here is the analogy I always give people. Imagine two boards in a 5-unit game with combat rules similar to ours. On one board there are 3 units with 10 Attack and 11 Health. On the other there are 5 units with 10 Attack and 10 Health. One board has 63 stats and the other has 100. In theory the second is much stronger, and yet the board with the three 10/11s is guaranteed to win, no matter what happens. There's a phenomenon in this game, especially when you don't have what I'd call "scam" units: concentrating stats is always better than spreading them out. This applies doubly if you have units that hold stats really well, such as units that deal damage to an extra unit or take less damage when they attack.

I wonder how many effects in our game actually allow for concentrated stats on a particular unit. Suppose most of the effects buff large numbers of units by small amounts and spread out the power. Then a unit like Alderman Dozey, who gets really big individually, will value trade and kill two or three other units. That means the rest of your board only has to trade roughly 1 for 1, and you'll win fights. That's something I worry you maybe haven't considered.

If numbers that are rather small in the grand scheme of things are causing such issues, our numbers may just be too small in general. I don't necessarily want Battlegrounds' number system. But we may need to hand out more power effects and look at the general shape of these games.

## Research

It's probably worth watching some YouTube videos, or having agents extract frames at various points from YouTube videos or replay files, to understand how the average Battlegrounds or Galaxy game goes in terms of effective stats per turn at a high level, and which cards and synergies enable that. I think you'll be quite surprised at how large the stats can grow.

## Questions the owner asked

- How many fights can a player lose in our game in different situations: a weak early game, a strong early game, then losing in the mid-game before they die? How many turns can you afford to lose a fight?
- Are wins evenly distributed around exactly 50%? How does that function?
- If we're using ghosts, from bot games and so on, and the active player can preview the board, does the active player get to adapt to a ghost that can't adapt to them? That ghost may already have adapted to fight a different composition, so it may be worse at fighting the player, who gets to change their board after seeing the next opponent.
