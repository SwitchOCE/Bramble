# The owner's directions for the Pigeon, Mouse, Badger and Locust reworks, 19 Nov 2026

Said in chat and in comments on the Balance Pass One page (https://claude.ai/artifact/CTGmFFEMTnr8knaZnsAsfN), 19 Nov 2026, dictated. These are directions for design pitches, not decisions. Lightly cleaned: filler and repeats removed, punctuation added. The owner said they were tired and that the numbering of their answers slipped; the topic of each answer is given in the headings.

## Pigeons, renown and two global keywords

> The way I'm thinking about the Pigeons is, I really like them as a military and a decorated, you know, like the slot buffs. I kind of feel like the slots are the brigades, 5th brigade or fourth company or whatever, right? And the brigade gets reputation from its achievements, I guess. So any unit that joins that brigade, which is the slot in this analogy, benefits from that reputational buff, which is the power buff, right? I'm thinking about a paradigm where maybe we call it renown, actually. Maybe instead of buffing slots, we give a slot a renown, and we can change the size of the renown maybe through the game. And we rebudget Pigeons as an entire thing around the fact they give renown. And renown gives stats only to starting units or reservists. And the Pigeons' whole thing is that they can have summons that can be buffed by renown.
>
> I think Orders: we're just going to need activated abilities in the game, and that shouldn't be a Pigeon keyword. So Orders we can just have as an Active or something, because every tribe might want access to activated effects. It's much better from a design space. I think we move the Order effect to an Active or Activate or something of the sort, a keyword like that, that can have a cost or not, and that's just a global keyword any tribe can have access to, any unit can if the design space requires it.
>
> And I think we should have a keyword called Charge, another global keyword, or something similar to charge. Charge is just a set number of uses, so we don't have to consistently go "(up to N times)". Maybe a different word if you've got a better word for it. Charge three: Padre, for example, let's say Padre is Charge 8, "give a slot +1 when a unit dies in it". I've paraphrased it; I can't remember the exact wording of Padre. Essentially the idea is that you replace the "up to eight times" effects with Charge. And maybe there's a charge that resets every fight and maybe there's a charge that never resets, and we have two different words for it, I don't know. Or we change the rules of the game so the charges used in fights reset, something like that. I'm not sure; propose something of the sort with pros and cons. That's the kind of effect I'm trying to make more intuitive to players, shorten our tooltips, and also give us more design space to design resisting *[probably "resetting" or "repeating"]* effects. So back to Pigeons: obviously Orders would be removed.
>
> I like the idea of Pigeons as caring about Reserve, or air support or something, which is these reinforcements that come in, and maybe reservists get double benefit from renown or something of the sort. So the idea is that you try to make these units that have good keywords that help them fight in combat, and you really benefit from scaling your regiments, which is your slots. Your regiments, your brigades, your companies, whatever we call them. Squadron, maybe squadron's the best term. I'm not sure. Propose ideas, essentially. Come back with some ideas and a new artifact.

Earlier the same day, on the Colonel's comment: "I think we need to think a bit more deeply on how Reserve works and what its goals are. I want to pitch an entire Pigeon tribe redesign, or not full redesign, but a rework."

## Mice

On the Needle Pikeman's comment: "This card is more of a tier six. This is an incredibly strong effect. We should figure out what's required to make it a tier 6 and design it from scratch around that effect, because it's incredibly powerful; it's one of the strongest cards in the entire game. But it needs to be a tier six. And we need to move other cards down. Mice need an entire tribe redesign. Because when you move something to T6, you have to move something else out. And when you swap a piece, you have to figure out what to do with it."

In chat:

> Mice need a plan other than just free hits. It's hard. The Cousins thing is cool, but it's very fragile, right? The entire Mouse tribe really struggles if their synergy breaks, because they've really required the pile-on *[heard "pylon"]* etc. It needs to be less fragile, and stronger and more varied. We can play a little bit more with some themes on Mice, maybe give them an extra mechanic or something. Do we have extra ideas as to how to make Mice a little more interesting? Pitch something to me.

## Badgers

On Herald of the Charge's comment: "I think Badgers need to survive. I want them to stack up all the Fury. Attack doesn't really help them. They want to consistently be outnumbered. And that means having a big Health unit that fights off a lot of smaller stuff and gets Fury to get even stronger and stronger."

In chat:

> I like the idea of Badgers as these tough units, really survivable. They're really hard to kill. Maybe they take less damage. Maybe they have high starting Health. Maybe they've got good Health scaling in the tribe, but not so good Attack scaling. But Fury helps them gain all their Attack: they enter this berserk mode and get stronger and stronger as they survive hits. And maybe they become more powerful permanently with Fury sometimes; some Fury effects give them permanent Attack. The idea is you want your Badgers to become more and more powerful if you don't kill them cleanly. And they need to get bigger. Currently there's this tier 6 Badger that's ridiculously powerful. It just gets the stats of any unit that dies, and it's absurdly strong, but it can be countered by Sortie and stuff. That one might be a little too strong already. It might be one of the only good Badger units. Lord Brock or something, I think it's called *[Old Brock, Last of His Sett]*. Big Badgers would be interesting, or bigger Badgers. Pitch me some ideas around this kind of mechanic. The outnumbered thing is interesting, I guess, but it feels a bit cheesy and fragile. Let's try to find something a bit more consistent. The Badgers kind of force you to play into them a bit vertically in a way. We might need to pitch entirely new designs, look at what they do, compare them to the current ones, and think about the strengths and weaknesses.

## Locusts

On the summons-and-slot-buffs comment: "What if we instead change the Locust swarm so that instead of being Hoppers, it's a bunch of damage hits, right? Equal to your Tide. It's numerically a bunch of insects swarming, and they can just be random damage hits instead of summons, and that way they don't have to benefit from slot buffs. Obviously this would be a rework of the entire tribe, but I'd like to see how this would play out."

Earlier in chat: "Hoppers are most similar to Scallywags from Battlegrounds ... I don't mind the idea that the Locust swarm is kind of unlimited, but the swarm factor doesn't really come through for me. Maybe all of the Locusts should attack at the end after [the rest of your board] dies, or maybe whenever you try to summon a Hopper and your board's full, the Tide increases instead. ... They're too bland. There are too few downsides, but the upsides are not as interesting as they could be."

## Balance Pass One answers given at the same time (built, not pitches)

- Sentry: the numbers option, +1/+1 a turn ("One one").
- Flying Ace: to tier 4, no Sortie ("I don't think it needs Sortie. It can be given it by other methods"), and "the cleave should only hit one thing, not two".
- Squadron Leader: down to tier 3 with a nerf to compensate ("I think Ace's old combo is fine to come back ... the Squadron Leader is not bad to go to tier three, it's not too broken").
- Wing Commander: "Maybe we can keep this as is, then if it's balanced, try not changing it."
- Orchard Stripper: "Happy with this for now. This seems reasonable as an approach. We can test again later if it's still too strong."
