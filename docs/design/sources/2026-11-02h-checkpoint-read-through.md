# Owner reactions: read-through of the Stage 2 checkpoint

Dictated by the owner on 2 November 2026 while reading the "Stage 2 checkpoint: archetypes, tribes, mechanics" doc (rev 7), section by section. The owner had not yet read [mechanics-ideas.md](../mechanics-ideas.md) in full.

## Archetypes

Okay, the archetypes: good. I agree with all of those. That's kind of how these games function.

One note I would make about the economy archetype, from having played them in other games, is that they tend to just be the best at high-level play. If they have options that give them stats at an efficient level, they'll get the stats, because they've got the economy to search for it. Or they can just use their economy options to play those economy units with another tribe's scaling or archetype's scaling, and use their best scaling instead of their own. It's just something to be aware of. So I like a lot of the economy ideas of tying economy effects to specific actions that kind of reward you for being in that type of strategy.

## Mechanics shortlist

**Hunker.** This is one of the ones I mentioned. This seems cool.

**Shell.** Shell seems potentially cool. We could iterate. Maybe a flat number is problematic. Or maybe it operates like Divine Shield in Hearthstone, and the shell breaks after a couple of hits, or maybe just one hit, but it limits the damage to a certain number for the first hit, which effectively makes it somewhat interesting. If you have a shell unit that has 5 Health but a 7-strength shell, you know you need to buff it to get the power from the shell. But once you do, it acts like a divine shield, which seems cool. ## The position-text rule

On the position-text rule you asked about: I think that's fine. It just lets you choose what units you want to buff. I don't see that being a problem at all. The player's got a lot of flexibility. They can choose what they want to buff; they can make their best units. Figuring out what your best unit is is sometimes not easy in a game like this. I don't agree with the statement that "give to the units in front of the Fairy" is a bad design at all. I would actually say that's quite a good design, myself. I think it would be intriguing, because it's not reductive.

That being said, that refers to position during the Recruit Phase. Position during combat is very interesting, and that changes a lot of things, because there are factors that will influence what you want to have in combat. And in the tavern phase, the Recruit Phase: if I have two different fairies, one that buffs things behind it and one that buffs things in front of it, it is our choice where to put things and which units to put where. It can be an interesting puzzle to figure those things out.

So I'm not sure precisely what the rule I was asking about in the proposal was, but I like these kinds of designs. If that's an answer to the rule, that's the answer.
