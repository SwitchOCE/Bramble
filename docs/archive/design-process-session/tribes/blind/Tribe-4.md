# Ogres

## 1. Identity

**Core mechanic: the Chieftain.** Your **Chieftain** is your minion with the highest Attack. It can be any minion type. A crown icon marks it in both phases. The crown moves only when another minion's Attack goes *strictly* above the Chieftain's, or when the Chieftain dies or is sold. Then the highest remaining minion takes it (leftmost wins a tie). Ogre text refers to the crown in three ways:

- **Tribute (X):** Give your Chieftain +X/+X. A minion can pay Tribute to itself if it is the Chieftain.
- **Usurp:** Triggers when this minion becomes your Chieftain. A minion's Usurp can fire at most once per Recruit Phase and once per combat.
- **Crowned:** This text works only while this minion is your Chieftain.

One design rule keeps the crown stable: no Crowned effect ever changes another minion's Attack. Without it, the crown could pass back and forth forever.

**How the tribe plays.** Tribute piles stats onto one minion. Usurp pays you each time the crown changes hands. These two pull against each other, and that tension is the tribe. A Tribute player builds one huge king and protects it. A Usurp player keeps the crown moving: they swap Attack around in the Recruit Phase and let kings fall in combat, because every death of a Chieftain crowns someone new and fires their Usurp. Since the Chieftain can be any minion, Ogres can also serve another tribe's carry as hired muscle. The tools that move the crown (swapping Attack, swapping Attack with Health, passing on the Attack of a dead or sold king) also work on non-Ogres. Much of the tribe's depth comes from aiming them at things they weren't written for.

Rules notes:
- With Cho'gall, each of your Tributes goes to every Chieftain, and Usurp fires when a minion takes either crown. The "low" crown moves when another minion has strictly lower Attack.
- A Usurp that fires again because of King Gordok still counts as that minion's one Usurp for the phase.
- Mar'gok and Mog'dorg give the Chieftain at least as much as the others, so the crown never moves because of them.

**Tavern Spell**
- **Gordok Green Grog** (Tier 2, 1 Gold): Give a friendly minion just enough Attack to become your Chieftain (at most +6).

**Trinkets**
- **Crude Gordok Crown** (Lesser): Your Chieftain has Taunt.
- **Tribute Chest** (Greater): Your Tributes give an extra +2/+2.

No tokens.

## 3. Builds

**The Throne (tall king).** Pick one Ogre king, usually the Brawler (Windfury) or the Pit-Fighter (its Rally Tributes itself), and feed it with the Tax-Taker, the Stonemaul Mercenary and Maulgar. The Flatterer grows beside the king. Mog'dorg, seated next to the king, spreads the Tribute to its neighbours, and Mar'gok spreads it to the whole board, so the build isn't one minion with five weak ones behind it. Ko'ragh protects the king from Venomous and other one-shots. The Champion sends the king at the enemy's biggest threat before combat starts. This is the Spike line: steady and readable.

**Succession War (Usurp chain).** Field several minions with similar Attack and keep the crown moving. Put a Mound Bouncer as a Taunt king (or use the Crude Crown) so it dies first. Each dead king crowns the next minion: the Upstart grows, the Herald gives everyone else +2 Attack, and that can push another minion over the new king and set off its Usurp. Phemos and Pol attack the moment they're crowned, Cho'Rush grows permanently with each crown change, and Kromcrush hands each dead king's Attack to its successor. King Gordok doubles every Usurp. In the Recruit Phase the Two-Head, the Grog Slinger, the Grog and the Magus set up who gets crowned next turn, because each minion's Usurp fires once per turn there too.

**Mercenaries (Ogres as support).** Crown another tribe's carry and pay Tribute to it. The Sellsword adds +1/+1 to each Tribute, the Stonemaul Mercenary pays Gold while the king isn't an Ogre, and Maulgar turns every Ogre into a Tributer. Four or five Ogres can carry a Beast, Dragon or Undead build. The Two-Head can move a big Attack onto a better body: a Divine Shield Mech, a Venomous Murloc, a Reborn Undead.

**Cho'gall's Two Heads.** Cho'gall crowns your weakest minion as well, so Tribute lands on both ends of the board. The low crown keeps moving as the weak minion grows, and each move fires another Usurp and spreads Tribute further. Upstarts and the Twinhead become kings on purpose. The Magus can flip a minion's Attack and Health to choose exactly which minion holds each crown.

**In a five-tribe lobby.** The crown reads Attack, so every tribe affects it:
- Undead and Dragon Attack buffs move the crown on their own, which feeds Usurp for free.
- Quilboar Blood Gems and Elemental buffs decide who will be crowned next.
- A Mech king takes Magnetic upgrades and Divine Shield.
- Demons eating Tavern minions gain Health, which the Magus can turn into Attack.
- Pirate Gold pays for the Magus and the Grog.
- An Aberration Deity joining combat with high Attack takes the crown and fires its Usurp.
- In Duos, partners can pass the Grog and the Two-Head to set up each other's king.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Readability.** Players must always know who holds the crown. The crown icon, and a preview of where it will go after a buff, are required. Twinhead's "counts as double" and Cho'gall's second crown add hidden state.
- **Crowns passing back and forth.** The Usurp cap, the tie rule and the "Crowned never changes Attack" rule prevent loops. Every future card needs the same check.
- **Tall-king fragility.** Venomous and destroy effects punish the Throne build. Ko'ragh is the only real answer and sits at Tier 5.
- **Scaling.** Maulgar with the Sellsword, Mar'gok and the Tribute Chest grows very fast in a late game. Treat the numbers as starting points.
- **Kromcrush on a sold king.** It moves stats without creating them. Watch Swap followed by a sell in the same turn, which can move a big Attack onto any body for 3 Gold.
- **Pure-Ogre dead text.** The Sellsword and the Stonemaul Mercenary's Gold do nothing without a non-Ogre king. That's intended, but it lowers the build's ceiling.
- **Phemos in the Recruit Phase.** Its "attack immediately" must do nothing outside combat, and the text must make that clear.
- **Trinkets.** Two isn't enough to guarantee an Ogre trinket in every offer. At least two more of each level are needed.
- **Pool contention.** The Mercenary build takes Tier 2 and 4 Ogres away from the Ogre players in the same lobby.

## 5. Design rationale

1. The core is a crown based on Attack, not on a resource: everything on the board already has Attack, so other tribes interact with it without asking.
2. A strict-exceed tie rule, so buffs that only match the king never flicker the crown.
3. I split effects into categories: generate crown changes (4), feed the king (6), pay off on Usurp (5), Crowned (3), convert the crown into Gold or stats (2), bend the rules (4). Feeding is the most common and the most basic action.
4. Tribute and Usurp pull in opposite directions (stack on one minion versus move the crown), so building the tribe is a real choice, not a checklist.
5. Usurp fires naturally whenever a king dies in combat, so its payoffs work from Tier 2 with no setup.
6. Once-per-phase Usurp and "Crowned never changes Attack", to rule out loops.
7. The tools that move the crown (Swap, Magus, Kromcrush, Grog) work on any minion, which invites off-label use.
8. The Mercenary hook (non-Ogre king) makes Ogres a support package in other tribes' builds, which matters with five tribes per lobby.
9. Tiers 3/4/5/5/4/3, with the generators early so Usurp can be set up before its payoffs arrive.
10. The three Tier 6 cards each change the rules (two crowns, double Usurp, Tribute spreads to the board) rather than adding stats.
11. Few extras: one spell that moves the crown, two trinkets, no tokens.
12. Names came last: Gordok's court for the rules of succession, Highmaul for the arena, Maulgar and Mar'gok for rule over others, Cho'gall for the two heads that disagree about who rules.
