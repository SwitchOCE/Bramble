# Ogres

## 1. Identity

**New keyword: Tribute.** *Tribute: [effect]* means "after you sell a minion next to this, [effect]." In Duos, passing a minion next to it to your teammate also counts. Adjacency is checked at the moment of the sale. A minion sold between two Tribute Ogres triggers both, and when the board closes the gap the next minion slides in, so one Ogre can take tribute from a whole row in turn.

**Sub-theme: empty spaces.** Some Ogres scale with the empty spaces on your board. Selling for Tribute creates those spaces, so the tribe always has to choose between refilling the board and staying small.

**How it plays.** Ogres are a tribe that eats its own board. You buy cheap minions and fodder (Pit Fighter tokens), place them next to the right Ogres, and sell them. Each sale feeds whichever Ogres were beside it: stats, Gold, Grog, a Tavern discount, a copy in the Tavern, or a buff for the whole tribe. Every turn is a seating puzzle: who sits next to whom, which side, what order to sell in, and whether to refill the gaps or leave them empty for the empty-space Ogres. Late cards change the rules of Tribute itself. It can fire for every Ogre, fire in combat, copy itself to a neighbor, or be triggered by discards. The tribe also works as a toolkit for other tribes: a way to re-run Battlecries, fix minion types, break Taunts, and turn a dead minion into value.

## 3. Builds

**A. The Tribute Table (wide selling engine).** Fodder makers (Pitmaster, Chain-Boss, Beastcaller, Chain Gang) feed a row of Tribute Ogres. The standard seating is *Glutton, fodder, Bickering Magi, fodder, Debt Collector*, with Beastcaller refilling one gap each turn. Phemos and Pol sits between two strong Tribute Ogres and fires both of their effects. King Gordok removes adjacency entirely, so every sale triggers every Ogre, and the Greater Trinket turns the number of sales into stats for the whole tribe. Its weak point is time: the engine is slow to assemble, and it is thin in combat until Bickering Magi has stacked.

**B. Elbow Room (go tall).** Sell down to four or five minions and keep the gaps. Hermit and Mar'gok grow every turn from the empty spaces, Ringmaster makes your smaller board attack first, Ko'ragh turns the gaps into Divine Shields, and Mound-Matron fills them when she dies. Challenger kills the opponent's biggest threat. The empty spaces also help summon-heavy partners, because Beast and Undead Deathrattle tokens have room to land. This build is Spike's: few pieces and clear numbers.

**C. The Butcher's Pit (Tribute in combat).** The Butcher makes Glutton, Bickering Magi and Maulgar grow in combat whenever a neighbor dies. Pair them with minions that die and come back: Undead Reborn, Beast Deathrattle tokens, Mech Divine Shields. Beastcaller then summons a fresh Pit Fighter beside them in combat, which will die and trigger them again. Maulgar placed next to a Reborn minion takes its stats twice.

**D. The Fence (Ogres as a toolkit for other tribes).** Ogres used outside their intended purpose:
- **Fence and Enforcer** re-run any tribe's Battlecries. Sell a Murloc or Elemental, and a fresh, frozen copy is waiting in the Tavern to buy and Battlecry again. Elementals can also buff the copy while it sits in the Tavern, or a Demon can eat it from there.
- **Bodyguard for Hire** becomes an Undead, Dragon or Mech next to one, so it catches that tribe's buffs to all its minions. Placed next to an All-type minion, it becomes every type.
- **Pit Fighters** count as summons for Beast payoffs, and as bodies for Quilboar Blood Gems.
- **Grog and Cho'Rush** supply Tavern spells, which Dragons' Chromadrakes improve.
- **Debt Collector** next to Pirates turns their spare minions into Gold.
- **Challenger** works as a Taunt-breaker in any build.
- **Two-heads** buffs whatever sits on either side of it.

**In a five-tribe lobby.** Ogres take few cards from other tribes' pools and sell minions back into the pool, which slightly helps the other players. Their best partners:
- **Murlocs and Elementals:** Battlecry and Tavern synergies through Fence.
- **Beasts and Undead:** empty spaces for their summons, and deaths for The Butcher.
- **Aberrations:** Cho'gall turns discards into Tribute.
- **Pirates:** Gold.

**Weakest pairing:** Demons. Both tribes eat, and they fight over the same spare minions, but Demons eat from the Tavern and Ogres from the board.

## 4. Risks and holes

- **Turn time.** Many sales each turn, plus repositioning, can push against the Recruit timer. The client needs neighbor highlights and a "Tribute triggered" counter, or players will misplace minions.
- **Multiplying triggers.** Golden King Gordok, Phemos and Pol, Altar of Storms and Enforcer stack multipliers on top of each other. The once-per-turn limits on Gold, cards and fodder hold the economy, but the uncapped stat effects (Glutton, Bickering Magi) need a test for the worst case.
- **Gold loops with Pirates.** Fence plus Debt Collector plus Pirate Gold could come close to free loops. Each limit is once per turn, but the combined Gold made per turn needs simulation.
- **Venomous and go-tall.** A four-minion board is very exposed to Venomous, and Ko'ragh only partly answers it. Builds A and C refill the board, so this mostly hurts build B.
- **Cho'gall is narrow.** Without Aberrations he is a 5/7 with weak text. He should be offered only when Aberrations are in the lobby, like other dual-type minions.
- **Bodyguard plus All-type** may break type-count effects in ways no one has listed yet.
- **Ringmaster** changes a core combat rule. Two Ringmasters, or Ringmaster against other attack-order effects, need a defined tie rule (random).
- **Early tempo.** Pitmaster into Glutton gives a 4/3 plus a free trigger on turn 2. This is fine if Glutton's numbers stay small, but it needs watching.
- **Hole: no Taunt Ogre.** The tribe protects its big minions only through Divine Shield and attacking first. It may need a Taunt at Tier 2 or 3 if go-tall loses too often to cleave.
- **Hole: Pit Fighters are dull.** As tokens they exist only to be sold. That is the point, but Timmy gets nothing from them.

## 5. Design rationale

1. **Gaps found:** board size is never a choice, because every build fills seven slots, and the "more minions attacks first" rule and empty spaces are never resources. Selling is a dead end, worth 1 Gold and nothing else outside pivots.
2. **More gaps:** position matters only through Taunt and attack order; adjacency is unused as a core system. Combat targets are fully random, and no tribe can pick its fights.
3. **One keyword, Tribute (selling a neighbor),** because a single action touches all three gaps: it pays for selling, it empties slots, and it depends on seating.
4. **Ogres eat the board, not the Tavern,** to stay out of the Demons' lane.
5. **Pit Fighters sell for no Gold,** so fodder feeds Tribute without also becoming free economy.
6. **Any effect that makes Gold, cards or fodder is once per turn, and golden makes it twice;** only stat effects are uncapped. This is for Spike: the economy stays bounded.
7. **Empty spaces became a second payoff,** so the gaps Tribute leaves create a real decision between refilling and staying small.
8. **I fixed go-tall's natural weaknesses (attacking second, Venomous)** with one card each (Ringmaster, Ko'ragh) rather than making them a theme.
9. **Economy without Gold where possible** (Tavern upgrade discounts, Grog, Tavern copies) to avoid overlapping Pirates.
10. **Bridges are built in deliberately:** Beast, Pirate and Aberration dual types, Bodyguard as a type fixer, Fence for Battlecries and Elementals, Grog for Dragons, and empty spaces for summoners. This is for Johnny's off-label uses.
11. **The late game bends the rules rather than adding bigger numbers:** Phemos and Pol, King Gordok, The Butcher and Cho'gall each change how Tribute works.
12. **Names and flavor came last,** mapped to roles: Gordunni greed for economy, Highmaul's arena for fodder and fights, two heads for the left/right cards, and the Imperators and kings as rule-changers.
