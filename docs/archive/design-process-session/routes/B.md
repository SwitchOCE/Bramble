# Razorfen Gemcutter

## 1. The card

- **Tier 3 · Quilboar · 3/4**
- **Text:** Blood Gems played on this give it no stats. After 3 are played on it, your Blood Gems give +1/+1 this game. Each time after that, it needs 1 more. *(Counter: 0/3, then 0/4, 0/5...)*
- **Golden (6/8):** Same, but each Blood Gem played on this counts as 2. Extra progress carries over.

*"Every gem he cuts gets smaller. Every gem after gets better."*

## 2. Its place in a game

Bought on turns 5–7, once you make 2–4 gems a turn. It sits 6th or 7th on a gem board that later adds Moon-Bacon Jazzer and Agamaggan. Each gem asks: stats on your carry now, or bigger gems later? Early, the upgrade wins easily. From turn 11, the carry wins. You decide when to stop feeding it and when to sell it.

## 3. Interactions outside Quilboar

- **Rent and sell:** the upgrade outlasts the minion. Any comp with stray gems (hero power, trinket, a neutral Relaxer) can feed it 3, sell it, and keep +1/+1.
- **Spell counters:** Blood Gems are 0-cost tavern spells. A Naga or Elemental board counting spells can cast gems here for its triggers and bank progress too.
- **Routing:** gems played on it by other effects count, including random spreaders like Geomagus Roogug. Johnny can automate feeding; random targets make it messy.
- **Golden overflow:** counting in twos overshoots odd thresholds by 1, which carries over. Players count ahead to waste nothing.

## 4. Risks and failure cases

- **Runaway scaling:** rising thresholds mean 18 gems buy only +4/+4, but test it with Agamaggan and several Jazzers.
- **Combat feeding:** gems played on it in combat also count, giving hard-to-predict permanent upgrades. If too swingy, limit it to "in the Tavern".
- **Misclicks:** a gem dropped here by accident gives nothing. It needs a clear counter and a targeting warning.
- **Selling at 2/3** loses partial progress. Intended; it must be visible.

## 5. Design rationale

1. Start from one choice: stats now, or a better long-term return that doesn't help this fight.
2. "No stats" makes feeding a real cost and stops it becoming the carry.
3. Count gems played *on it*, not used from hand, so routing effects and spell counters plug in.
4. Rising thresholds create the leftover puzzle and the falloff Spike needs.
5. Tier 3 lands where gem income starts, so its value fades over the game.
6. "This game" lets the upgrade outlive the minion, making rent-and-sell a real line.
7. Golden counts double with carryover: faster, and the counting puzzle stays.
8. Name and flavor last.
