# Storybook Ink (#1)

- **Idea:** the decided Ink + flat B style applied straight to the UI: cream paper, one ink colour for every line and all text, flat fills with one hard cel shadow, colour kept for meaning (attack, health, gold, Nightshade, freeze, tribe).
- **For:** players who like a warm, readable picture-book table (Hearthstone Battlegrounds players, plus casual mobile players). It's the baseline the other directions are measured against.
- **What makes it distinct:** the UI is drawn the way the portraits are drawn, so frames, buttons and art sit together. Touches like the market-stall awning, the open-book menu and the stamped standings carry the storybook feel without adding ornament. The brick board shows as two offset hedge-plates, with dotted "behind" lines from each back slot to the two front slots in front of it. Attack is a round mustard badge and health a berry heart, so shape as well as colour tells them apart.
- **Godot 4 cost: low.** One Theme with three StyleBoxFlat styles (paper, card, ink: border width, corner radius and the shadow offset all cover the flat cel shadow), two fonts (Fraunces, Nunito), about 10 SVG icons, a nine-patch only for the frost edge and the awning. No shaders. Nightshade, the halving and the damage splats are Tween scale and pop on Control nodes. The attack arrow is a Line2D or a drawn curve.
- **Risks:** it can look plain or generic next to bolder directions (the art-style note flagged the same risk), so the woodland has to come from the portraits and small props. Thick ink lines eat space on a 390 px-tall phone: board names are hidden on phone, and shop text shrinks to keyword chips. Gold coin, golden frame and attack all sit in the yellow family; badge shape separates them, but this needs a colour-blind check. The portraits are stand-ins: only six Ink B trials exist, so units borrow the nearest one.
