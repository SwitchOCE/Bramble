Part I

Ideas to pick from

Still ideation: options, not a design. Each section is a menu. Take the pieces that spark something and ignore the rest.

Where the game could sit

Tone compass

Your three touchstones sit at three different corners. Everdell is cozy all the way down. Redwall keeps a warm home under real threat and real deaths. Root uses the cute look to pull you into a game about war and politics. The Penguins of Madagascar add a fourth direction: a comic ensemble that is very good at its job.

The shaded zone marks the sweet spot this brief describes: a cozy, storybook surface, stakes that matter, and a cast that is funny on purpose. Switch to the faction seeds to see where each idea from this folio would land.

    Sixteen candidate zones · real European biomes

    World map

    Each zone pairs a real European habitat with a human culture that grew up there: Dutch water boards, Alpine cantons, Slavic forest beekeepers, Hebridean island parliaments. That gives every animal culture a home and a borrowed flavor without copying any one nation. The names are working titles.

    Sixteen is too many to ship. The three groupings below show different ways to cut them down to 6–8 regions. Click a zone on the map to jump to it.

    Pick, merge or throw away

    Faction seeds

    Thirty-six starting points instead of eight finished factions. Each is one hook: a species, a place, an institution or genre it parodies, and a mechanic that comes out of the animal's real behavior. Some are one species, as in Root. Others are a mix where the mix is the joke.

    The rule used to keep them from being derivative: every seed has to have a twist that Redwall, Root or Everdell doesn't. Hares aren't soldiers, they're moon-witches. Badgers aren't warrior lords, they're faded landlords. Rats aren't a horde, they're a single knotted hive-mind.

    Star the ones you like. Stars are saved in this browser and you can filter by them.

    One animal, several futures

    Species takes

    Each species gets three or four different identities to choose between. They overlap with the seeds on purpose: a seed is one take placed in a zone. Mix a take from one species with a zone from the map to start a new seed.

    For when you're stuck

    Idea shuffler

    The Penguins of Madagascar formula is a cute animal plus a serious institution plus one odd trait. This draws one of each, plus a zone and a mechanic. Lock any slot you like and shuffle the rest. Most results are nonsense; the occasional one is a faction.

    AI concept art that humans can match

    Art direction

    The same mouse scout in 13 styles, generated with Codex. Each is scored on four things: how hard it is for human artists to keep consistent across 60–120 units, how cheaply a small team can animate it, how well it reads as a small board token, and how risky it is to hand AI concept art in that style to a human. Lower difficulty and risk are better; higher animatability and readability are better.

    A finding from making these: on the first pass, 5 of the 13 styles (ink-flat, gouache, paper-cut, spot-black ink, pixel) came back in the same detailed, painted storybook look, whatever the prompt said. They had to be regenerated with strict "no gradients, no fur, flat fills" constraints. The image model pulls toward painterly detail by default, which is exactly the drift a human artist would later have to undo.

    Researchers' common finding: AI images are good for exploring silhouette, costume and palette, and bad as final rendering targets. Before briefing freelancers, have one lead artist redraw 3–5 "golden" units by hand and build the style guide from those, not from the AI images.

    Effort versus fit

    Animation effort per unit (idle, attack, hit, ability, faint) against how well each style fits the "childlike but for adults" European storybook tone. Top left is where you want to be. Dot size shows readability at token size.

    Three hybrids worth testing

    • Root units in an Everdell world. Ink-flat units carry the volume at 4–8 hours each. Only 10–20 painterly backgrounds and key art need a senior painter. Give units a thin outline and rim light so they separate from soft backgrounds (Sable's lesson).
    • Two drawing styles as faction identity. Gouache or watercolor for wild-zone cultures, woodcut for the "civilized" ones (water boards, cantons, courts). Wolfwalkers does exactly this for town versus forest.
    • Simple tokens, rich cards. Ink-flat or clean-vector tokens with one shared print filter (paper grain, halftone, slight misregistration) applied by shader, and painterly portraits only for legendary units. The filter unifies different artists for free.

    The cheapest route that still feels handmade

    • Paper puppets. Each unit is cut into 4–8 rigid parts with no mesh bending, driven by a shared library of tweens for a few body types (small biped, bulky, flyer, swimmer).
    • 2–3 swap drawings per unit: wind-up, impact face, faint.
    • Everything stepped at 12 fps, with one object-space line-boil or paper-grain shader.
    • Shared hand-drawn FX: dust, sparks, a faint that dissolves into leaves.
    • About 1–1.5 person-days per unit, so 60–180 days for 60–120 units. Spend frame-by-frame or Live2D only on bosses and shop portraits.

    Asking AI for riggable concepts

    • One character per image, full body, in the in-game facing (side or three-quarter), on a plain background.
    • Flat, even front lighting with no cast shadows. Limbs apart, not crossing the torso.
    • A parts sheet: head, ears, torso, arms, legs, tail, cloak and weapon drawn whole, including the bits hidden behind other parts.
    • Never take texture (halftone, paper, watercolor bloom) from AI output. Apply it in a shared filter or shader so it stays consistent.
    • Easy to segment: clean-vector, ink-flat, gouache, paper-cut. Hard: painterly, watercolor, woodcut, tapestry. AI pixel art always needs a full redraw.
    Three finalists · three takes each · six species and biomes

    Style trials

    The three front-runners from the style tests, each pushed in three directions (A, B, C). Every take draws the same six subjects: four animal folk from different zones, plus an ooze and a fairy to check that the style holds for the magical creatures too. Each subject's palette comes from its biome.

    View by style to compare the A, B and C takes, or by subject to see one character across all nine takes. Click any image to enlarge it. Prompts are in tools/art/prompts/trials-*.txt.

    First read, from the contact sheets: all nine takes held their style, unlike the first style tests. Ink + flat C and gouache A drift towards textured storybook painting, and gouache A and B come out close to each other. Ink + flat B and spot-black B read best at token size. The ooze and the fairy work in every take, so the magical creatures don't need a separate style. The otter's "wide straw hat" kept coming out as an East Asian conical hat; a delta costume needs a more specific brief.

    Part II

    Reference

    The research behind Part I: what the three touchstones do, what audiences already believe about each animal, and the wider shelf of influences.

    Touchstone I · political war

    Root

    PublisherLeder Games, 2018 DesignCole Wehrle ArtKyle Ferrin ExpansionsRiverfolk · Underworld · Marauder · Homeland (2026)

    Root is a counterinsurgency wargame in disguise. Wehrle took the COIN wargame model and gave every faction a different rulebook. The cute art is a deliberate lure: it lowers players' guard so the betrayal and occupation hit harder. He has said fantasy let him drop the mess of real history and keep the core of the problem. Official lore says no faction is simply good. Even the Woodland Alliance is an unelected insurgency willing to make martyrs.

    FactionSpeciesOne verbPolitical fantasy

    How Ferrin draws it

    • Hand-inked lines with hatching and visible pen texture, colored digitally. Hard-edged solid black shadows.
    • Round bodies, tiny dot eyes, expression carried by brows, posture and props.
    • The board palette was sampled from photos of autumn leaves. Each faction gets one flat color, chosen with colorblind players in mind.
    • Childhood influences: Quentin Blake, Bill Peet, James Marshall. He also cites Redwall, Mouse Guard and Disney's Robin Hood, and calls Root a far departure from them.
    • Violence is drawn plainly but never gory, often as comic menace: a mouse holding a knife to a startled cat.

    Steal from Root

    • One verb per faction. Cats build, birds obey a decree, the Alliance spreads sympathy. Each auto-battler tribe should be one sentence.
    • Species explains the rule. When the animal didn't fit, they changed the animal: the Keepers in Iron were turtles before they were badgers.
    • Pair each species with a political archetype (empire, old aristocracy, insurgency, merchant company, cult, warlord, diaspora, peace council).
    • Hirelings are cheap two-sided neutral units, a ready model for a shop's neutral pool and upgrades.
    • Mixed clearings. Species is who you are; loyalty is political and can change.
    Touchstone II · cozy village

    Everdell

    PublisherStarling Games / Tabletop Tycoon, 2018 DesignJames A. Wilson ArtAndrew Bosley WorldPearlbrook · Bellfaire · Spirecrest · Newleaf · Mistwood · Farshore · Silverfrost · Duo · Emerland

    Everdell is a village chronicle. Critters gather under the Ever Tree to found a new city before the year ends. The history has wars, plagues and coups, but all of it happens offstage, in a 100-year timeline. On the table it's cozy. The villains are outsiders with alliterative titles: Rugwort the Rascal, a rat, and Nightweave, a spider with eight bad moods (the Nefarious, the Needy, the Nonchalant…).

    The founding myth is gentle. Corrin Evertail, a mouse orphaned in the Serpent War, crossed desert and mountains for nine months and raised nine white stones in the valley. The calendar runs Everfrost, Hopewatch, Mistrise, Puddledeep, Greensprout, Everblossom, Glimmer, Sublaze, Gatherwind, Witherleaf, Starfall, Bellsong.

    CritterSpeciesWhy it fits
    SpeciesBellfaire player power

    How Bosley paints it

    • Ex-Ubisoft concept artist. He calls his style "whimsical but realistic" and cites Redwall, Don Bluth (NIMH, An American Tail), N.C. Wyeth and railway posters.
    • Warm golden-hour light, saturated greens and autumn tones, candle-lit interiors.
    • Real fur and anatomy, upright, in simple pre-industrial clothes. Purple is kept for rank.
    • Every critter holds a prop that names its job: quill, crook, mailbag, broom. Glasses mean "scholar".
    • Buildings are cutaway storybook homes in stumps and roots, often with their critter visible inside.

    Steal from Everdell

    • Name the role, show the species. Players learn "Doctor" first and enjoy "hedgehog" as flavor.
    • Three ways to match species to job: literal biology (mole → miner), folk personality (owl → pastor, skunk → fool), lore lineage (mice rule because the founder was a mouse).
    • Species = one short power. Bellfaire gives every species one nature-based rule. That's a tribe synergy waiting to happen.
    • Big Critters (Honeypaw, Stubblehoof, Truffle the boar) as an oversized legendary tier.
    • Grow the cast by region: river, mountain, coast, snow, jungle each bring local species.
    Touchstone III · heroic peril

    Redwall

    AuthorBrian Jacques Books22 novels, 1986–2011 CoversTroy Howell (US), Pete Lyon, Fangorn, David Wyatt (UK) ScreenNelvana series 1999–2002; Netflix film and series reported cancelled April 2026

    Jacques wrote the first book to read aloud to children at a school for the blind in Liverpool, which is why the prose is all smell, taste and sound. The feasts are famous for this reason. Each species is a voice plus an appetite plus a trade, drawn from the patchwork of British regional speech. Underneath the comfort are sieges, slavery and beloved characters who die. Jacques refused to talk down to children.

    Timing note: Netflix reportedly let its option lapse in 2026 and the rights went back to Penguin Random House. A 40th-anniversary edition is due this month and a final novel, Orphans of Mossflower, is listed for 2027. Nobody is currently serving the Redwall fantasy on screen or in games.

    SpeciesRole in the booksVoice & habitKit

    The recurring furniture

    • Redwall Abbey: red sandstone, ramparts, a bell tower, Cavern Hole for dining, and the tapestry of Martin the Warrior. Roles: Abbot, Recorder, Friar, Cellarhog, Foremole, Skipper of Otters, Badger Mother, Champion.
    • Salamandastron: a hollow volcano on the coast with a badger forge and the hare barracks.
    • Two-track plots: a siege at home and a mixed-species quest party far away.
    • Martin's ghost appears in dreams and speaks in verse riddles.
    • Feasts: October Ale, deeper'n'ever pie, hotroot soup, meadowcream, candied chestnuts, damson pudding.

    Steal from Redwall

    • Species = voice + food + trade. These map straight onto unit barks, shop flavor and callings.
    • A war cry per faction. Every faction seed could carry one.
    • Bloodwrath: a badger that enrages at low health is an auto-battler trigger already written.
    • The Abbey as home base: the place you defend, upgrade and feast in between rounds.
    • Real loss, gently. A faint should have weight, even when it's a pratfall.

    Childlike, but for adults

    The Penguins rule

    The Penguins of Madagascar work because of a gap. The bodies are nearly identical flightless bullets; the attitude is an elite commando unit. Kids get the slapstick, adults get the spy-movie parody. Skipper is the overconfident leader, Kowalski the over-analyzing brains, Rico the wildcard who coughs up whatever's needed, Private the earnest heart. Craig Kellman, the lead designer on Madagascar, treated the animals as rubber bands and insisted their motion bounce.

    Minions, Rabbids and Redwall's moles use the same tricks: one simple body with many small variations, a voice that is funny without translation, and a consistent wrong logic.

    Genre neighbors

    Auto-battler lessons

    A structure that falls out of all this

    • Two tags per unit: Species × Calling. Species (Mouse, Badger, Otter…) gives the faction synergy, silhouette and voice. Calling (Guard, Slinger, Sapper, Friar, Bard, Scout, Seer, Rogue) gives role and prop. This is TFT's origin × class, dressed in Everdell's "name the job, show the animal".
    • Five of 8–10 factions per lobby, with migratory or wandering folk (storks, the corvid syndicate) as neutral glue.
    • Feasts as the between-round economy: food items as buffs (Super Auto Pets), feast-day events (Redwall).
    • Trinkets on triples: a thimble helm, a conker shield, a button buckler (Storybook Brawl's treasures, Mouse Guard's human-junk gear).
    • The Oath (Farthing Wood): predator and prey on the same board earn a pact bonus, which rewards breaking faction lines.
    • Seasons as set rotation: spring, summer, autumn and winter sets or events (Brambly Hedge, Everdell).
    Beyond the three

    The wider shelf

    Decisions to make early

    Open questions

    1 · Are some species evil?

    Redwall says yes: Jacques's own answer was that goodies are good and baddies are bad. Critics point out that the vermin speak Cockney or pirate dialect and are often scarred, which reads as class-coded. The ferret Veil, raised in the Abbey and still cruel, is the famous test case.

    Options. (A) Keep the Redwall split for clarity. (B) Root's approach: every faction has mixed motives. (C) Tie morality to the faction's creed, not the animal, and let any species defect.

    Suggestion: C. Species drives mechanics and flavor; a faction like the Shiny Syndicate is a creed of charming crooks, not "the bad animals". Turncoat heroes (a good wildcat like Gingivere, a kindly searat like Blaggut) become rare recruitable units.

    2 · What scale is the world?

    Mouse Guard: true scale. A fox is a monster and a heron a boss. Redwall: compressed. Mice to badgers is a few times larger, there are no humans, and big predators are rare. Root: everyone is roughly pawn-sized.

    Suggestion: Redwall-style compressed scale for the playable cultures, with big beasts (bison, bear, stag) as rare legends and true-scale monsters (heron, adder, owl, pike) as bosses. Size becomes a readable stat.

    3 · Is there a human world?

    Mouse Guard and Beatrix Potter keep humans just offscreen: gear made from thimbles and buttons, a farmer's garden as danger. Redwall and Root have no humans at all. Human leftovers give you charming item art; a world without them is cleaner lore.

    4 · How dark is a faint?

    Potter, NIMH and Farthing Wood keep real danger, and that earns adult respect. Super Auto Pets plays faints as bonks. A middle path: units "retire from the field" with a dignified or comic beat, and the narration carries the weight, like Tails of Iron's storybook narrator.
