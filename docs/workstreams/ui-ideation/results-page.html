03

A · The great window, refit

The shop and the 4-and-3 board stay in the middle of a three-light window in dark stone. Cards are larger and keep a bare face; a name and a short effect sit on a plaque under each. The left light holds you, a reading pane and the hand; the right light holds the eight players.

Opus, gallery test"A is clearer than Battlegrounds at a glance: names, effects, tribe and prices without a hover, and standings named." But "Battlegrounds and Glass Masquerade have more depth, light and modelling. A is flatter, a well-made 2D window."
Neon, gallery test"A clearly belongs in this gallery of beloved hits." It sits at "the gallery's solid upper-average mark rather than the very top only because its three-light Gothic symmetry is more restrained and traditional than showstoppers".
Sol, gallery test"The amber and green sections make the window feel like one object, and the fixed inspector leaves the offer comparison visible." But "The fight has a less immediate focus than Battlegrounds, and the smallest plates have less glass depth than Glass Masquerade."

Compared with: Battlegrounds (shop and fight), Once Upon a Galaxy (shop), Monster Train (shop), Glass Masquerade. Its own weakest point, in the designer's words: the fight cards are the smallest on the centre board, and the four rows of captions use room that could have gone to the art. Still open, not blocking: the hand's seven slots sit 3, 2, 2.

04

D · The glazier's bench

You are a glazier in a woodland workshop. Glass for sale stands in the window with its name and full rules on a tinted pane beneath. Your board is the panel on the light table. In the fight both panels hang in the window, and a plaque shows what a loss will cost.

Opus, gallery test"D reads best of all three: full rules in the shop, cues on the board, stakes in the fight, the clearest 'why'." But "Three-quarters of the screen is rectangular panels (shop labels, player rows, left lane, hand tray), so it looks more like a tidy board-game app than a lit object."
Neon, gallery test"After the Round 1 and Round 2 fixes, D is genuinely standout." It is "a tactile, unforgettable signature interface that is also the fastest of the three to read mid-game."
Sol, gallery test"The shop is easy to buy from because the aligned plaques explain every offer, and the warm surface reads as a workbench." But "The beige combat fields and repeated smoked controls have less depth than the shop surround or the best workshop references."

Compared with: Backpack Battles (shop), Inscryption (board), Wingspan (board), Wildfrost (shop). Its own weakest point: board cards are 216 px in recruit and 200 px in fights, against about 240 today, so the art is less of a hero. Opus on what would lift it from average: narrow the light table to the board and give the margins to the bench art. The designer added the glow but could not narrow the table, because the background picture's own lit table lies under the margins. That would take a new picture.

05

E · Glass in the forest

One stained-glass clearing at dusk. The shop hangs from a bough with price tags on the chains, your board stands on the moss, the other players are lanterns in the trees, and End turn is the next opponent's lantern. In the fight a tally shows the kills each side needs.

Opus, gallery test"E has the strongest flavour of our three and holds up against Root: the stained-glass clearing, the hanging bough shop and the lantern players make the place the interface." But "The plates are plain dark rectangles. That keeps it below Wildfrost's bespoke card plates."
Neon, gallery test"E is the visual showstopper of the run." And: "Mixed into the gallery, it is a screen players pause on immediately."
Sol, gallery test"E belongs on the strength of its recruit screen. The bough, price tags and lanterns make the setting feel essential to the layout." It found the fight more crowded, and kept E at about average after the last fixes.

Compared with: Root (board), Dorfromantik, Wildfrost (fight), Gwent (board), Once Upon a Galaxy, Glass Masquerade (map). Its own weakest point: the name plates make the clearing busier than the first version, and fight cards shrank 22% to fit two boards with plates. The council asked for 10 to 15% and accepted 22.

07

The three not kept

  • B · Honeycomb. The whole screen is one lattice of the card's own shape. Neon ranked it second at first for originality, then dropped it. Opus: "cold slate hexes with large dead regions"; the price coins look like the stat discs.
  • C · Rose window. A radial screen with the eight players on the rim. Opus: "a striking rose window", but "the cards are the smallest of the six (about 100x170) on the busiest background". Its curved board would be a rules change.
  • F · The reading book. Your side on one leaf, the shop or the opponent on the other, names in bands across the cards. Sol shortlisted it at first. The bands cover about 40% of each card's art, and Neon failed it on glass: it reads "as a flat matte web dashboard". The council named one thing worth borrowing from it: the next opponent's last board in view during recruit.
08

Things they suggested beyond the two screens

You asked that concepts suggest other things. None of these is in the game; each is a suggestion for you.

  • Explain a lost fight. All six show the damage sum (10 for turn 9 plus 3 for a heavy loss) and name the enemy units that beat you. The build keeps no fight log, so today those lines are worked out from the two boards. E and F suggest recording one.
  • A stakes plaque during the fight (D): kills so far, and what a loss costs at each threshold. The council named this as worth borrowing whichever concept goes on.
  • Show reserves during the fight (B, C, D, E).
  • The next opponent's last board during recruit (F); click a player to see the last board of theirs you saw (D).
  • Give gold and the stat discs different shapes (A, D, E): your own 17 Nov point that they look too alike.
  • One Journal for past fights and the card browser (E), or one Game menu (A, D).
  • "Watch again" after a fight (D, E): a flow change; the game has no replay today.
  • The shop's bough gains a lantern per tier (E).

Whichever direction you take, the members named these as worth carrying over:

  • D's stakes readout during the fight and its broken-glass look for a fallen slot.
  • E's reading pane, which lights the units a card works with.
  • F's view of the next opponent's last board.
09

What nobody judged, and calls made without you

Not judged. These are stills at 1920 × 1080. Nobody dragged a card, watched a fight play, waited on a hover or heard a sound. Agents never get bored or lost, and they already know every card. Whether a returning player recognises units by their art alone, which decides how much the name plates are worth, is a question only your hands can answer.
  • Not redrawn: the start screen, the result screen, pause, the card browser and the compost panel. Only recruit and the fight with its end.
  • Not built: nothing here is in Godot. Building any of it is your call.
  • The baseline game was played against the build's simple bots, because this build's ghost field was not in the checkout. The opponents' boards are plausible but weaker than league play.
  • New art: D and E each have one generated background, made from the designer's written request in the chosen glass style. The other four use only today's art. That helps D and E on the look.
