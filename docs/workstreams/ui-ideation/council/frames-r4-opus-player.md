# Frame council, round 4 re-check: the player (Opus)

**1. The owner's point.** Yes, it blends now. At 112 px on teal, lead and glass read as one border of about one width. The glass climbs the lower sides as a narrow wedge and the bottom is only a little deeper, so nothing steps where lead meets glass. Better than round 3, whose glass skirt read as a second part. **Flow** answers it better: its taper is "wider at the bottom but not jarring". Near is almost the same; its even stripe reads as a coloured edge.

**2. Gained and lost.** Gained: one border; golden is now a full gold frame instead of the lead, the best golden so far and unmissable even on yellow art; stat rings in tier glass, not poker chips; more art. Lost: **tier at a glance.** The tier is now a thin strip that vanishes on matching art. Tier 6 on the fire-art raccoon and tier 5 on sunset art are hard to place. Round 3's petal and leaf crests gave each tier a shape. Round 4's braided strip differs only by colour.

**3. Good design?** Yes. The first take that could sit beside a published frame without looking amateur. Strongest: golden, and the even border. Weakest: tier on warm or same-coloured art, and in greyscale tiers 2 to 5 still look nearly the same.

**4. Craft at 300 px.** The tier 5 and 6 strips are a twisted-rope texture tiled along the edge: machine-made, not cut glass. Lead lines cross the strip at fixed steps. The lead is still a smooth vector bevel, not cast. Tier 1 is still white marble or rope, and golden tier 1 is still gold over silver.

**5. Changes still needed.**
1. Make the tier read without help from the art: a dark lead line between strip and picture, and a cut pattern for each tier so the tier reads by shape too. This also fixes greyscale.
2. Cut real panes into the strip (pane ends, not a tiled braid), and give the lead a cast finish.
3. Tier 1 as pale clear glass; settle golden tier 1.

Glow, sheen and motion: not judged from stills.
