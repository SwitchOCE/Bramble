## 1. The fixed things

**Sol fails B, C, D and E on the hexagons (spell frames). I disagree that it is a fail, for any of them. It is a fix.**
- The fixed thing is "units and cards are tall, stretched hexagons set in lead, on today's card art and tier frames, undistorted". All six draw spells as tall hexagons in lead (B's lattice cells, C's alcove hexes, D's dark outlines, E's hanging hexes), so the shape rule holds.
- The orchestrator's fact settles the rest: no spell frame exists. Today's game draws spells as a stand-in (`glass_spell.gd`: "no spell frame yet"), and row 50 (spell shape) is marked unsure. "Use today's tier frames" cannot be met for spells by anyone.
- A and F are not cleaner than B to E here. They put spells in the unit tier-1 frame, so each Sharpen shows two empty gold stat discs (A mid at 1445 to 1560, 185 to 280; F mid, the mini hexes in the Sharpen strips). That is a unit frame showing unit parts on a card with no stats, which is worse to read than a stand-in.
- So all six concepts have the same gap, and the same fix: one agreed spell treatment (a tier frame with the stat discs masked, or a lead hexagon of the unit's proportions until an artist makes a spell frame). It is no reason to keep any concept off the list.

**Neon fails F on lead and glass. I partly agree, but it is a fix, not a fail.**
- Agree: the standings bar, the stat chips ("11 Health", "20 Gold", "3 Compost"), the shop header buttons, the Sharpen strips, the scouting rows and the charcoal name slabs are flat dark rounded rectangles. Crop `f-mid-head.png` shows chips with a 1 px border and no lead or light, so they read as app UI. That breaks the rule's "every panel, button, counter and label".
- Disagree that the concept fails: the two leaves are smoked glass with lead tracery and leaf ornament, the hinge is leaded, and every card is in its real frame. The breach is in the chrome styling and can be restyled without changing the layout.
- I apply one standard to every concept: a breach confined to one kind of element, fixable without changing the concept, is a blocking fix. By that standard D's paper labels and E's flat dark result box also need fixing. F stays off my list for its look score and the art its bands cover, not for this.

## 2. The shortlist

**I now hold A D E (it was A E D).**

What moved me:
- **D above E.** Sol and neon both put E's reading at 4 to 5.5 and call the gap structural. I checked whether my "add name plates" fix really is additive. It isn't, quite. In E mid the front row's lower tips (y ≈ 735) already overlap the back row's tops (y ≈ 715), and the shop's price row (y ≈ 380 to 420) is the only free band under the shop. Name plates need every card about 10 to 15% smaller, which is a layout change, not a decoration. D already passes reading, and its look fixes are restyling. So D ranks above E.
- **E stays third, ahead of F and B.** All three members score E's look highest (8.5, 8.5, 9). The owner's first priority is "the game's look and feel". The owner also asked for "different styles and different layouts", and E is the only concept where the woodland is the stage. F's look (sol 7.5, neon 4, mine 6) and its art cover are worse problems for that priority than E's missing names.
- **Not B.** I re-viewed B's right half (`b-mid-right.png`). Seven large cells hold only Freeze, Past Boards, Cards, Refresh, Turn and Menu, plus empty dark slate cells. It is cold and mostly empty, and its price coins match its stat discs. Neon's case is originality, which I grant, but originality is not the look criterion: "made, not assembled", carried down to the smallest counter.

Score gaps of 2 or more:
- **B look: mine 6, neon 8.** I move to 6.5, not further. The lattice is consistent and lead-edged, which I credit. But about a third of the screen is empty slate cells, the woodland appears only in the card art, and three gold cells compete for the accent.
- **D look: mine 7, neon 9.** I move to 7.5, level with sol. The painted bench surround (stone, leaves, brambles) is good, and the shop window's seven labels align cleanly. Not 9: the white frosted table and cream labels are the brightest things on screen, the cards sit about 170 px tall in a table with about 220 px of blank on each side (`d-mid-board.png`), and the white stat roundels and paper tags look like generic UI.
- **F look: mine 6; sol 7.5, neon 4.** I hold 6. It is tidy and consistent (sol's point), but its chrome breaches the glass rule (neon's point) and its bands cover about 40% of each card's art.
- **Reading:** no gaps of 2 or more. One correction to my round 1: D's board labels show keywords only, so Famine-caller, Squadron Leader and Granary Glutton carry a bare name (neon's point, verified in `d-mid-board.png`). D's board reading drops slightly, and I hold 8.5 because the shop, the main decision, needs no opens.
