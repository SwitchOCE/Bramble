# Frame council, re-check of take M at round 5: what the council said

Put together by the orchestrator (an agent) from the three voting members' answers (`frames-r5-opus-player.md`, `frames-r5-opus-craft.md`, `frames-r5-neon.md`). The assembly is clerical: an item is "agreed" when at least two of the three voters raised it. Nothing here is the orchestrator's judgement. The owner had not reacted to round 4 or round 5 when this was written; the owner's words outrank all of it.

## Verdict

Ready to be called the frame, subject to the owner's eye: **yes, three of three.**

- Player: "you can now tell the tier at a glance by shape, not only colour, and the blend held."
- Craft: "the glass is now cut panes. That was the last fault in the design itself. What remains is finish."
- Neon: "Round 5 resolves all four Round 4 council blockers while keeping the seamless single-piece blend at play size."

Good design, three of three: it would sit beside published frames (Battlegrounds, Runeterra) without looking amateur.

## The owner's point: does the border still blend?

Yes, three of three. Kept (player, craft); improved, because dark inner lead lines now separate the glass from warm art (neon). One new jar, from craft: "tier 1's near-white V is the brightest thing on the board. The width blends; the brightness doesn't."

## What round 5 gained

- The glass reads as cut, leaded panes. (all three)
- Tier: each tier has its own cut and the glass climbs higher with the tier, so tier reads by shape as well as colour. (all three)
- Stat discs with cut glass rings. (craft, neon)
- Golden tier 1 now pairs gold with pale glass, not silver. (player, neon)

## Agreed, still open

1. **Tier 1 is too loud.** Its near-white panes are the brightest frame on the board, so the cheapest card draws the eye first. Make it a paler, lower-contrast clear glass with a slight tint. (player, craft)
2. **Tiers 5 and 6 are cut too fine.** At 112 px they speckle and read as beadwork, and they still melt into warm art. Fewer, larger panes; rank through the shape of the panes, not the number of pieces. (all three)
3. **The lead.** Still a smooth rail: no solder where the band's lead lines meet it, an even surface. (player, craft; neon sees patina and mitres but "uniform straight-rail mottling") The band's lead lines are thinner than the art's, so frame and picture do not read as one window. (craft)
4. **Without colour, the middle tiers are close.** In greyscale tiers 2 to 4 (or 2 to 5) differ mainly by how high the glass climbs. (all three)
5. **Golden.** The rail is still a flat ochre copy of the lead (craft); tier 5's amber softens against the gilt, and the lead lines inside golden glass should stay dark (neon).

One voter each: every pane carries the same painted diagonal streak, which looks generated (craft); thin glass slivers poke up above the discs on tier 3 (craft); the foot is exactly mirrored left to right, and the side tips on tiers 4 to 6 pinch to sharp points (neon); tier 3 reads cyan (neon; the art director says so too); tier 5's green flecks could go (neon).

## Not judged

Glow, sheen and motion.
