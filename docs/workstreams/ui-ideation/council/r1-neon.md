## Full ranking (1st to 6th, plus today's game)

1. **D. The glazier's bench** — Best overall balance of gallery-grade diegetic craft (sunlit woodland workshop, leaded window shop, frosted light table) and effortless zero-hover shop readability, with the clearest fight/after autopsy and straightforward, scoped fixes.
2. **B. Honeycomb** — Boldest, most original glass-native architecture: one continuous `0.56` tall-hex leaded lattice with brass mullions gives unmistakable gallery presence and strong at-a-glance reading, with crisp, fixable contrast and disc-differentiation defects.
3. **A. The great window, refit** — Disciplined, calm refit of today's three-light Gothic window that solves mid-game recruit reading while preserving unobstructed card art; earns the third shortlist slot as the high-craft control once its stretched board stagger, fight naming, and stability bugs are fixed.
4. **E. Glass in the forest** — Breathtaking 9/10 stained-glass woodland clearing and hanging-lantern atmosphere that belongs near the top of the gallery on look alone, but misses the second judging criterion (zero names or rules text on any card; still requires 12–15 opens) and would need a structural redesign to solve it.
5. **C. Rose window** — Inventive radial composition and memorable golden `END TURN` sun medallion, but feels assembled rather than made because flat dark-slate rectangular HUD boxes clash with the rose window, cards shrink to `156–180 px`, and dense plaques make the screen noisy.
6. **F. The reading book** — Clean, calm typography and a genuinely smart Turn-8 scouting panel under the shop, but fails Fixed #2 (panels, chips, spells, and giant opaque black card slabs read as a flat matte web dashboard, not backlit leaded glass) and buries half of every card's art.
7. **Today's game** — Places **7th (last)** behind all six concepts: large empty quarry voids, stand-in slate buttons, zero names/rules on cards, cut-off hand, hidden lobby, and 1.6:1 unaffordable-price contrast.
