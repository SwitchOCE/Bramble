## Ranking

1. **A**: the most finished screen. Warm green and amber glass, lead seams and leaf tracery reach every control. Big cards with a name and effect under each, standings named and always shown, one End turn accent. It loses points for the zigzag board, for having no tribe on any card, and for a wrong standings table on the end screen.
2. **E**: the one that most clearly belongs in the gallery. The cards are the hero in a lit stained-glass clearing and the play stays central. But it fails the reading test as drawn: no names on cards or players, and prices look like stat discs. Those fixes add to the concept without changing it, and the owner's first priority is look and feel.
3. **D**: the best reading. Full rules on every offer, named board, all eight players, and a fight stakes plaque. The diegetic bench is a real idea. Its look is let down by pale backlit surfaces that wash out glowing cards, small board cards on a mostly empty table, and paper-like labels.
4. **C**: a striking rose window with good reading (names, crests, eight players on the rim). But the cards are the smallest of the six (about 100x170) on the busiest background (radial spokes behind every card), and the spells sit 450 px from the shop.
5. **F**: the strongest scouting (the next opponent's last board is in view) and the clearest end screen. But the name bands cover about 40% of the art, T-badges sit on the art, and the dark chips and pills read as an app dashboard more than a made glass object.
6. **B**: an original lattice, but cold slate hexes with large dead regions. Price coins are the same gold discs as the stats, name bands cross the art, and the cues are cryptic shorthand. It looks like a sci-fi hex HUD wearing card art.

**Today's game** places 7th, below all six. Its look is roughly at B's level but less finished (pill buttons, empty lattice panels), and its reading is the weakest: 12 opens and the opponents behind an unlabelled click.

## Defects per concept

### A. The great window, refit
1. **mid, board (355 to 1565, 525 to 1050):** the back row sits in the gaps of a spread front row, so the seven units read as one zigzag line. "Front row" is at top centre (958, 519) and "Back row" bottom left (432, 947); neither sits with its row. The same happens in fight on both sides: March Hare's back row (Weasel, Gorger, Dame Bodkin, y 80 to 370) interleaves with their front row (y 230 to 500).
2. **mid:** no tribe on any shop or board card. Captions give name and effect; tribe appears only in Card details after a select. Synergy reading needs it at a glance.
3. **mid (355, 356):** "10 at this price" sits in the Upgrade group under "−3 next turn", but it belongs to Refresh. A grouping error.
4. **mid, spells (1445 to 1560, 185 to 280):** two cards of about 55x95 px, a fifth of a unit card, each with two empty gold stat discs on a spell. One "Sharpen" label serves two cards.
5. **mid, left light:** the Card details pane (48 to 302, 305 to 650) has about 150 px of empty pane under four lines. The hand card Seek (55, 735) is about 55x105, too small to read its art.
6. **after, right light:** "Final standings" marks places 2 to 6 as Out and puts March Hare 1st. That is wrong. At turn 9 only Brother Bracken and you are out, and today's own result shot gives Dame Hoolet 23, Pipkin 21, Little King Wren 18, Old Mother Mousewife 18, March Hare 16, Hob Mumblecrust 16.
7. **after:** "why I lost" is the damage arithmetic plus one Gorger note in the Card details pane. No enemy unit is named for what it did.
8. **Notes:** row 20 (name and effect shown with the card) is reversed but not listed.

3. **mid, lobby:** the health badges sit on cell vertices between two portraits (31 at (179, 296), 23 at (101, 526)), so which number belongs to whom is unclear.
4. **mid:** a large dead area. About 12 empty dark cells on the right half (x 1040 to 1900), and the board sits left of centre (x ≈ 882).
5. **mid:** the cues are cryptic shorthand at about 14 px condensed ("Ahead: Sortie+3A", "Sell Locust→Eat", "Pile on 1→+2g"), and the player decides on them.
6. **first:** the FRONT and BACK slot labels (for example 620 to 676, 674 to 690) measure about 2.5:1 contrast.

## The fight and its end

- **A:** both boards are at full size and a selected enemy unit reads in the left pane. The zigzag makes the enemy front and back rows hard to tell apart. The end screen gives the damage arithmetic and March Hare's starting board, but almost no "what beat you", and its standings are wrong.
- **B:** both boards and your reserves are shown, plus a first-strike tag, but the screen is dense. The end screen is strong: survivors tagged, the 10 + 3 formula, three turning points. The prose is small.
- **C:** both boards with an opening-strike arrow, a reserve list and both players' panels, but the cards are small. The chronicle on the end screen is well structured (why 13, what decided it, standings), except that the standings miss three players.
- **D:** the stakes plaque ("Kills 0 of 4 · If you lose: −13") teaches the heavy-loss rule while the fight runs. The end plaque is clear: why −13, what beat you, both boards' totals.
- **E:** the kill tally hexes show the heavy-loss threshold. The end screen dims the harmless survivors and numbers the three that beat you, which is clear and calm. No unit is named in the fight itself.
- **F:** side-by-side named boards and a short fight log. Its end screen is the clearest of the six: 10 + 3 = 13, three "why you lost" events, and survivors 6/7 with "Briar King fell".
- **Today:** "Defeat" and a −13 splash; nothing says why.

## Blocking fix lists

### A (rank 1)
1. **Board rows** (mid 355 to 1565; fight both sides): make each row read as a row. Front 4 in a line with small gaps, back 3 half a slot offset beneath, both on one centre line. Put "Front row" and "Back row" at the left end of their own rows. Do the same for both fight boards.
2. **Tribe** (mid, all 12 cards): show it at a glance, as a crest on the frame shoulder or the tribe word in the caption.
3. **Refresh stock** (355, 356): move "10 at this price" to Refresh.
4. **Spells** (1445 to 1560, 185 to 280): enlarge to at least half a unit card, drop the empty stat discs from the spell frame, one label per spell.
5. **After standings:** show the real post-fight order (Dame Hoolet 23, Pipkin 21, Little King Wren 18, Old Mother Mousewife 18, March Hare 16, Hob Mumblecrust 16, you 7th out, Brother Bracken 8th out). Today's result shot has these.
6. **After, "why":** add a short "what beat you" list of three enemy units and what each did, in the space over your empty board.
7. **Left light:** cut the empty part of the Card details pane, and give the hand cards a readable size (Seek is about 55 px wide).
8. **Notes:** list row 20 as reversed.

### E (rank 2)
1. **Names on cards** (mid shop and board): add a lead-set name plate under each card (name plus one short cue, as A does). The face stays minimal, so it keeps its route.
2. **Names on players** (mid lights 1680 to 1870): label each light with the player's name, and raise the rank numerals to 18 px or more. Write the next opponent's name on End turn ("Fight March Hare").
3. **Price coins** (y ≈ 400): give prices a shape the stat discs don't have, such as a hanging tag with a coin, so a price can't read as a stat.
4. **Accent:** make End turn the most prominent lit object at bottom right (the lantern itself can be the button), and tone down the 20-gold medallion.
5. **Counters** (30 to 175, 790 to 850): add the words Compost and Tide.
6. **Hand** (215 to 330, 860 to 1075): lift the tray so Seek is whole and on screen, and raise the hand numbers to 18 px.
7. **Tribe glow:** drop the glow, or reduce it to the crest, so the frame alone carries tier colour.
8. **Fight:** space the enemy rows so marks don't sit on Renown plates. Show post-fight health in the lights at the end.

### D (rank 3)
1. **Spells** (1300 to 1570, 45 to 230): set them in a real frame. Today they break the "cards in their frames" rule.
2. **Backlight:** dim the shop window and the light table to a darker, coloured glass so the cards are the brightest things on screen. Make the labels tinted glass with lead edges, not paper.
3. **Board size** (340 to 1580, 440 to 900): raise board cards to at least about 240 px tall; the table has the room.
4. **One accent:** give the left-lane buttons one material and tone, so Freeze isn't a bright pale pane. Only End turn is lit.
5. **Renown against price:** move Renown off the top-left shoulder (a plate above the slot, as A does) or change its shape and colour.
6. **Dead areas:** fill or narrow the fight's empty side columns (right 1600 to 1900 by 250 to 990, left 0 to 330 by 100 to 570) and the empty hand strip in mid.
7. **After:** post-fight standings values.
8. **extra1:** dock the reading panel so it doesn't cover the table frame.

## Non-blocking notes

1. F's "next opponent's last board" in the recruit view is the best answer to "who I'm up against". Worth borrowing into any finalist. It is a new feature.
2. D's fight stakes plaque and E's kill tally teach the heavy-loss rule as it happens. Worth borrowing.
3. Keep one plain name per thing: C's "Vanguard/Inner Sanctum" and F's invented role names would make a tutorial say two names for one thing.
