# Frame Council Review (Round 2) — Gemini Neon

## Take M

1. **Frame & card feel:** At 112 px with art, it reads as a leaded stained-glass card, not a UI wire or art fighting art. The crisp bevelled lead came frames the art cleanly, though M is only shown on near-black, never on `mid.png`'s sage-green board.
2. **Art to border:** Right for a textless 112 px piece (~80% art). The slim upper rail and bottom apron match Runeterra and Battlegrounds tokens far better than wide Magic frames.
3. **Flourishes:** Bottom crest, rising side tapers, and top apex bead. Base color mass and T5–T6 side climb survive at 112 px; the ~2 px top bead becomes pixel noise, and inner petal cuts only read on hover.
4. **The join:** Built, not cut. Lead rails part into tapered leaf tips tucked cleanly into the came—especially cohesive on golden cards. T1's blunt diagonal stop is weaker.
5. **Tiers:** Far quieter than today's frame. Higher tiers stand out via hue and climbing glass height (30% to 45%). In M's greyscale check at 112 px, T2–T4 blur together; in color, T5 amber and T6 orange-lit crimson sit closest over warm art.
6. **Golden vs not golden:** Unmissable on every tier, and it reads as burnished gold metal. The dark-to-gold upper flip separates golden T5 from normal T5, but gold metal, amber glass, and yellow discs make T5 golden's bottom third muddy. Glow, sheen, and motion are not judged from stills.
7. **Craft:** Per-tier AI drift shows at 300 px: upper lead hue and width shift (grey T1, thin purple-charcoal T4, bronze T5–T6); golden rail width varies; only T4 golden has top filigree wings; T5 discs are oversized; T3 glass looks pinched; T5 side leaves are asymmetric.

*Spells:* Same family (lead ring, bottom glass arc), though still round-1 thin.

## Take N

1. **Frame & card feel:** At 112 px on the sage-green board, cards separate cleanly with legible ivory stat discs. Yet the thick, soft-shaded upper rail reads like an extruded clay or stone UI pipe, clashing with the sharp inked glass below.
2. **Art to border:** Reasonable (~78% art, near Battlegrounds tokens), though the chunkier upper rail crowds characters slightly more than M while confining glass to a shallower bottom V.
3. **Flourishes:** Bottom V crest and short side ears; plain top. Base color mass and ivory discs hold at 112 px, and the symmetrical motif steps cleanly from T1 diamond to T6 seven-petal crest, though inner cuts merge and T5's green tips vanish.
4. **The join:** Pasted, not built. The rail ends behind the discs while thin glass slivers ride its outer edge; T1 ends in a blunt diagonal cut, and T1–T3 golden show dark smudges above the glass.
5. **Tiers:** Much quieter than today's frame, with clear hue steps. Because glass height barely climbs from T2 to T6, high tiers lack extra frame presence at 112 px (no greyscale check shown). T5 amber and T6's orange-red tips sit closest.
6. **Golden vs not golden:** Unmissable by value jump on every tier, including T5 on the board, but it does not look like metal—it reads as pale matte cream/ivory plastic. That pale tone separates from T5 amber, yet leaves T1 golden washed-out beige and feels chalky beside T4/T6 jewel glass. Glow, sheen, and motion are not judged from stills.
7. **Craft:** Strong cross-tier geometry—uniform rails, identical discs, clean symmetry—undercut by a fuzzy airbrushed rail (with a bright peak smudge on 300 px hover) meeting crisp glass, T1's heavier dark disc ring, and dark golden-rail smudges.

*Spells:* Same family (dark lead ring, compact lower glass crescent).

---

## Overall Verdict

8. **Which take is closer:** **Take M** is closer to the best version of the owner's idea. It solves the hardest material and architectural problems: crisp leaded came that matches the art's linework, a join that looks built rather than masked, metallic gold that actually reads as metal, and rising side-glass height that gives high tiers real board weight beyond hue. Take M should borrow three things from Take N: (a) **shared master assembly** so lead rails, golden rails, and disc geometry are 100% identical across all six tiers; (b) **ivory/cream stat discs**, which read cleaner on every tier and stop yellow discs from colliding with golden metal and Tier 5 amber; and (c) **N's clean, symmetrical bottom-crest ladder** (plus testing on `mid.png`'s actual sage-green board, not dark charcoal).

9. **Would you put it to the owner as a proper pass?**
   - **Take M: No.** *Deciding factor:* Visible per-tier craft drift (mismatched lead widths/colors, oversized Tier 5 discs, pinched Tier 3 glass, and a golden top crest that only exists on Tier 4 golden) still reads as unfinished AI output rather than a unified card system.
   - **Take N: No.** *Deciding factor:* Material and join failure—the fuzzy clay-pipe lead rail, pale cream-plastic "gold," and pasted-on glass slivers with dark seam smudges do not meet the standard of a published leaded-glass card.

10. **The three changes that would raise Take M the most (most important first):**
    1. **Lock one unified master frame and fix per-tier craft drift:** Share one crisp lead upper came and one metallic gold came across all six tiers, standardize stat-disc size and ring thickness (fixing T5), symmetrize side leaves and un-pinch T3's upper glass, and make any golden top crest consistent across all six tiers (at ≥6 px silhouette scale) or remove it entirely.
    2. **Switch stat discs to ivory/cream and sharpen Tier 5 golden separation:** Replacing warm yellow discs with Take N's pale ivory keeps stats crisp on every tier and prevents golden T5's bottom third from melting into amber-on-gold-on-yellow; keep dark inner cames crisp inside the amber glass on golden T5, and verify outer-edge contrast on `mid.png`'s sage-green board.
    3. **Strengthen non-color tier hierarchy and cut sub-pixel noise:** Drop the ~2 px top apex bead that turns to dust at 112 px, adopt Take N's symmetrical petal-count growth at the bottom crest, and give top tiers (T5–T6) a clearer silhouette/mass step so neighbouring tiers (especially T2–T4) separate cleanly even in greyscale.
