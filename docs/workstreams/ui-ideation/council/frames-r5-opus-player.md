# Frame council re-check, round 5: player's view (Opus)

**1. The owner's point.** Kept, and better at tiers 4 to 6. The rail is one even width. The glass climbs the sides in a tapering wedge and widens into the foot, so lead becomes glass without a step. At tiers 1 to 3 the change happens under the discs, which hide the joint. Nothing jarring at 112 px on teal.

**2. Gained and lost against round 4.**
- Glass: gained. It reads as cut panes in lead now (shards at tier 3, a lattice at tier 5, beads at tier 6), not rope texture.
- Tiers: a big gain. Each tier has its own cut, and the glass climbs higher as the tier rises, so a tall, ornate foot means a high tier across a board. In greyscale, tiers 2 to 4 differ mostly by height.
- Lost: tier 1 is now the loudest frame on the board. Its big white panes are the brightest thing in the row, so the cheapest card draws the eye first. Tier 6 red still melts into warm art (the raccoon in fire), and tier 5 into sunsets.
- Golden: unchanged and unmissable. Golden tier 1 now pairs gold with white glass, not silver, which is better.
- Lead: unchanged, a smooth bevel. Stat discs: fine. Frame against art: unchanged and right.

**3. Good design?** Yes. It would sit beside published frames. Strongest: the tier ladder. Weakest: the lead on the top two-thirds, still a clean extruded rail.

**4. Craft at 300 px.** The lead is a code bevel with a highlight stripe and no solder. Tier 1's panes look like white tiles more than clear glass. Tier 6's beads are close to a repeated pattern. Nothing looks pasted.

**5. Ready?** Yes, subject to the owner's eye. What decides it: you can now tell the tier at a glance by shape, not only colour, and the blend held.

**6. Changes still needed.**
1. Calm tier 1: paler, lower-contrast glass, so its foot is the quietest, not the brightest.
2. Separate tiers 5 and 6 from warm art with a stronger dark lead line where glass meets picture.
3. Give the lead cast character: solder at the joints, an uneven surface.

Glow, sheen and motion: not judged.
