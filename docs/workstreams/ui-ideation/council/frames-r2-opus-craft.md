# Frame council, round 2: visual craft (Claude Opus)

Judged from the pictures first, at real size and at 3× nearest-neighbour crops of the 112 px cards; specs and notes read afterwards. Glow, sheen and motion: not judged from stills.

## Take M

1. **Frame, published feel.** Yes. It reads as a leaded window with a glass sill, closest to a Runeterra follower in weight. The upper lead is the weak part: a flat dark-brown came (value about 60 on a 0–255 scale) with no edge highlight, so on dark art (tier 3 badger, tier 6 bramble) the top sinks and reads close to a UI outline.
2. **Art to border.** Right. About 81% art, rim 6 px at 112 px, inside the study's board-piece range.
3. **Flourishes.** Only in the base: side glass plus a bottom crest. The crest survives at 112 px as a coloured V; its petals don't, which is fine. The top bead (2 px) and golden's leaf crest don't survive; they add nothing at board size.
4. **The join.** Made. The came's rails part, and the glass ends in a leaf tip with a solder knot. At 300 px this is the best join either take shows; at 112 px it reads as a clean taper, never a square cut.
5. **Tiers.** Far less busy than today's. Higher reads as higher, because the glass climbs from 30% to 45% of the card. Confusable: tier 5 and tier 6 on warm art (the sunset pigeon), and tier 2 on green art, where its band disappears. In greyscale the ladder is nearly flat.
6. **Golden.** Unmissable on every tier, and it looks like metal: real leaf-gold hue with a highlight. Tier 5 is the weak case: honey glass, gold rails and the gold discs every tier already has merge into one yellow mass. Golden still reads there only because the top rail changes.
7. **Craft.** The glass is mottled, leaded and varied, so it looks like real glass. Defects: tier 1's base is a brushed-steel plate with no leading, so it reads as metal, not glass. Tier 5's discs are oversized. The bead size varies.

## Take N

1. **Frame, published feel.** Yes, and the strongest of the two at the top. The came is a rounded pewter tube (7–8 px, with a lit centre), so the window holds against any art. The glass base and the cream discs feel more like a mobile game than a window.
2. **Art to border.** About 78% art. Right, slightly more frame than M, most of it in the base band.
3. **Flourishes.** Base only, with a plain top: correct. The crest reads as a shape at 112 px.
4. **The join.** Mostly made: a tapered tip tucked under the came. But the side glass starts at a different height on each tier with no ladder logic, so the starts look arbitrary.
5. **Tiers.** Quieter than today's. Tiers 5 and 6 grow more ornament and read as higher; 1 to 4 read by hue only. Tier 3 and tier 4 are confusable; so are tier 5 and golden.
6. **Golden.** Fails. The came turns ivory/champagne (highlights near white, around 250, 240, 214), so it reads as bone or silver-gilt, not gold. Golden tier 1 is pale on pale.
7. **Craft.** The glass is smooth airbrushed gradient with white sparkle specks, which looks machine-made and plasticky. Tier 1 has brushed-silver streaks. The cream discs at about 17 px look pasted on, not leaded in.

## Once

8. **Closer: M.** It is the proper pass on the owner's own concept. It fixes all three faults of the rejected cards (the 2–3 px rim, the tan golden, the square cut) and keeps the glass looking like glass. Borrow from N: its came profile (lighter pewter, round, a highlight line), so the upper lead holds against dark art. Also its plain top point. M was also shown only on a dark ground; N tested on the board's teal, and M must be too.
9. **Put it to the owner?** M: **yes.** What decides it: the golden reads as metal and the join looks made. N: **no.** What decides it: the golden reads as ivory, not gold, which fails one of the owner's own questions.
10. **Three changes to M:**
    1. Re-cut the upper lead with N's profile: lighter pewter, rounded, one highlight on the outer edge, still 6 px. Check it on the board's teal and on the darkest art.
    2. Let gold mean only golden. Give the disc faces ivory or cream instead of gold on every tier, and deepen tier 5's honey glass in value, so the gilt beats it on value as well as hue. Then check tier 5 against tier 6 again.
    3. Make tier 1 glass: leaded pearl or clear pieces at the same piece scale as the other tiers, in place of the steel plate. In the same pass, even out the disc sizes (tier 5) and drop the 2 px top bead.

Spells: both takes' round spells belong to their unit family (lead ring, glass lower arc). M's first-round ring is too thin against its own cards.
