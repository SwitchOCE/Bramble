## Ranking

1. **A.** The warm shop and green board feel like parts of one leaded window. Wide card faces, separate captions and a clearly named opponent make this the best combination of craft and quick recruit decisions.
2. **F.** The two leaves give the player’s formation a permanent home. Written tiers and roles are useful and the result lands well. The card bands and crowded scouting panel keep it behind A.
3. **D.** The left economy stack and seven aligned offer labels give the quickest comparison. The bench is distinctive, though the fight’s bright grid and smaller pieces weaken it. Strongest of the concepts that miss a fixed requirement.
4. **E.** The most convincing place to spend a game. The branches, warm lanterns and blue clearing make the glass feel native to the woodland. Nearly every offer still needs inspection.
5. **C.** The rose makes a clear arena, and the arc offers read well. Split standings, a dominant End turn medallion and small figures make the screen feel busier than its information requires.
6. **B.** The common lattice is a strong organizing idea. Giving actions and portraits full-size cells while packing shop and board pieces against the same seam makes the screen harder to scan.

Today’s game would place **seventh**. Its cards and dark window give Bramble an identity. The hand and lobby are harder to see, and the recruit view offers the least help identifying effects.

A is the closest eligible concept to **Battlegrounds** in how its controls, play surface and edge information feel like one object. E comes closest to **Root** in letting the woodland read as the place of play. **Glass Masquerade** sets a higher bar for carrying the finish into the finest leadwork and smallest controls. **Wildfrost** gives effects dedicated room without covering as much of the character as F’s bands. ## Defects

- **A.** In *mid*, the back-row captions sit on the bottom sill. Shop captions such as Bramble Runner also run to the edges with the price inside the name line. Front row is labeled at top center and Back row at bottom left, which makes the seven staggered pieces read as a zigzag before they read as two rows. “+1/+1 per Compost” can read as a bonus per unit already in the heap. In *first*, the only tutorial cue sits between empty slots and points at the gap.
- **B.** In *mid* and *first*, the two spell prices drop between the shop units below, and the unit prices sit on the seam at the board’s shoulders. It takes a beat to pair some coins with their offers. The 14 px effect bands cut across the art and compress Hayloft Aunt’s extra unit into “Hand-me-down +2/2.” In *first*, the Front slot text samples at about **2.5:1** on its pane. The sunburst on Menu suggests lighting. The large empty cells at right make the tight center more noticeable.
- **C.** In *mid* and *first*, the left Upgrade panel meets the first offer’s plaque with almost no gutter. The 14 px effect lines and small figures give names and art less room than the giant End turn medallion. Reading places requires jumping from the bottom left to the top right. In *first*, the tutorial overlays the Front row label and touches the lower edge of shop captions.
- **D.** In *mid* and *first*, the shop dedicates a full height of pale text panes to offers with one or two short lines, while the main board gets much smaller warriors. Decision text such as Sweetbriar’s effect is 16 px. In *fight* and *after*, the hard diamond grid runs across both bright tables and visually competes with the card edges. Labels sit close to the dividing lead strip.
- **E.** In *mid*, the shop branch and hand log are flat drawings against textured glass. The right-side standings leave the next foe’s identity to a gold ring on a small portrait, with undersized health digits. Tribe halos compete with tier frames, and yellow stat roundels and price coins look alike. In *fight*, the hand’s 14–17 px stats and bottom-edge marks are hard to read next to the board. In *extra1*, the inspector covers the player’s middle units just as it asks the player to consider their Pigeon synergies.
- **F.** In *mid*, the widest two-line card bands obscure much of the warrior art and repeat information such as Mouse in both Shrew Cutpurse’s type and keyword lines. Scouting Renown, scouting keywords and spell effect text are 15–17 px. The scouting panel carries about as many unit records as the player board. In *after*, March Hare is labeled “Winner” at fifth in the standings, where it reads as a match win.

## Fight and result

- **A.** The mirrored fight and large stats communicate who has what numerically, but only the inspected unit is named. The result explains 10 + 3 and calls out the starting Gorger. The labeled starting board and generic heavy-loss line leave the player to infer which threats decided the fight.
- **B.** Names and the battle seam make the two sides legible. The four hand cards and live board need more visual separation. The defeat panel ties threats to survivors well, particularly Weasel hitting a reservist, though the small shorthand makes that lesson slower to read.
- **C.** The equator and opening-strike arrow give the fight a clear direction. The result keeps survivors in place and separates damage from causes, but repeats standings and spends nearly half the screen on the panel.
- **D.** Names and the hit arrow make the exchange legible. The numbered callouts map loss causes to the opposing pieces, and the damage split is easy to find. Label any displayed starting stats as such in the result.
- **E.** The clearing gives combat a strong stage and the attack tell is clear, but the enemy pieces stay unnamed in the fight. The numbered markers tie the three reasons to specific survivors. The dimming of other visible survivors makes them look fallen.
- **F.** The left formation stays in place and the right side exposes all opponent names and stats. The result’s short event headlines and surviving formation make it easiest to connect a threat to the row it affected.
- **Today.** Sides and stats read, while enemy names require inspection. The Defeat splash and −13 give the outcome without explaining either the damage or the loss.

## Blocking fixes for A

1. **Separate the two rows and give labels clearance.** In *mid*, the Back row captions cross the bottom sill and the row labels have different anchors. Give both rows the same left anchor on their own lead bands, bring the lower caption baseline inside the pane, and reserve a fixed price column so Bramble Runner’s price and name have padding.
2. **Make effects and slots unambiguous.** In *mid*, Sweetbriar needs “+1/+1 when you Compost a unit” or an equally specific trigger. Attach Renown to the slot surround so its meaning is stable when a card moves. In *first*, aim the cue at one actual empty front slot and highlight only that destination.
3. **Name threats in the fight and show what survived.** In *fight*, the left reader identifies Greatsword Bearer alone. Carry the recruit names into compact fight captions, including Golden Gorger. In *after*, keep the damage arithmetic and final standings, mark which slots fell or survived and link one or two of the threats to short explanations on the result plaque.
4. **Record the roster reversal.** The always-open ranked list in *mid* reverses row 40’s on-demand presentation. Add the row number to the existing suggestion in the notes. ## Blocking fixes for F

1. **Put purchase reasons ahead of role descriptors.** In *mid*, Hayloft Aunt reads “Hand-me-down” and hides the Cousin that buying her adds. Shrew Cutpurse shows Pile on but hides the +2 gold. Show these purchase-relevant effects in the bands, remove repeated species and low-value role words, and reclaim art height. Raise text used to make decisions, including the spell effect lines, to 18 px.
2. **Make scouting clearly historical and easier to scan.** In *mid*, “Board from turn 8” implies a sourced view of March Hare from that turn, while the supplied state identifies Little King Wren as the player’s turn-8 opponent. Use a verified last-seen snapshot, or visibly label this an example of the proposed feature. Keep the date by the heading. Give scouting Renown and effect text 18 px where it stays visible, using the existing detail interaction for secondary role text.
3. **Keep the fight result distinct from lobby placement.** In *after*, the top bar calls fifth-place March Hare “Winner.” Use “Won fight” or “Opponent” there, label the bar as turn-9 standings and keep seventh place and the damage calculation in the result on the left.

## Non-blocking notes

- Add row 40 to B, C and E’s notes for the always-visible standings, and row 43 to B for moving spell prices. C labels the radial formation under R1. D and E should label Watch again as a flow suggestion. F already labels the fight record and scouting as suggestions. Showing combat reserves changes visibility and can be evaluated separately from eligibility to step in.
- Actual replay, a persistent fight log and access to opponents’ unobserved boards are separate feature decisions. The result and scouting mock-ups can be judged with labeled example data.
