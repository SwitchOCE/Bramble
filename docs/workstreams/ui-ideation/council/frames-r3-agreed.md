# Frame council, re-check of take M at round 3: what the council said

Put together by the orchestrator (an agent) from the three voting members' answers (`frames-r3-opus-player.md`, `frames-r3-opus-craft.md`, `frames-r3-neon.md`). The assembly is clerical: an item is "agreed" when at least two of the three voters raised it. Nothing here is the orchestrator's judgement.

The owner reacted to round 3 before this was assembled: "I think that the border needs to blend way more evenly", with a Magic card as an illustration: "the bottom is wider and bigger, but it's not jarring". Round 4 answers that (`briefs/27-frame-round4-even-width.md`). The items below are for the art director to weigh while doing so; the owner's words outrank them.

## Verdict

Put round 3 to the owner as a proper pass: **yes, with reservations**, three of three. What decided it: "It is one built system in which only the glass changes, and gold means golden" (player); "every agreed fix that the owner would see at board size is done" (craft); "the shared came and ivory discs unify all six tiers into a cohesive card system" (neon).

At 112 px on the teal board, all three: it reads as a board piece from a published game; the six tiers can be told apart by colour at a glance; gold now means only golden.

## The round-2 list

Done, three of three: ivory discs and a deeper tier 5; one stat disc; one upper lead; the top bead and golden crest removed; shown on the teal board; heavier spell rings. Partly: tiers that can be confused; tier 1. Tier 3's squeezed glass: done for two, "not done" for neon.

## Agreed, still open

1. **The lead now looks machine-drawn.** "A uniform vector stroke: same width, one flat highlight, no solder or patina. Beside the painted glass it looks machine-drawn" (craft); "reads more like a UI bevel than soldered came" (player); the straight came "degrades round 2's parted-rail join on T5–T6 into a dark flat fade" (neon). It needs the finish of cast lead.
2. **Golden lost richness.** Round 2's modelled gilt is now "a thin, flat mustard line" (player), "a muted brass-mustard" (craft). On yellow art it is not unmissable (player). It should be modelled metal, as wide as the lead.
3. **The stat discs' rings are flat.** "Pale, flat, and stamped over stained glass" (neon); "look like poker chips more than leaded roundels" (craft). They should be leaded glass in the tier's colour. The disc faces have "procedural-looking diagonal stripes" (player, craft).
4. **Tier 1.** The base still reads as stone, marble or steel, not glass, and golden tier 1 still puts gold over silver. (all three)
5. **The ladder without colour.** In greyscale tiers 2 to 4 are still close, and tiers 5 and 6 have the same glass mass. (all three)
6. **Tier 4's cyan jewels** look pasted on. (player, craft)

One voter each: tier 5's leaf glass is soft beside tier 3's crisp leading (player); tier 3's shards are still pinched, and tiers 5 and 6 have dark-capped splices where rail meets glass (neon); tier 5 keeps stray green flecks (neon).

## Not judged

Glow, sheen and motion.
