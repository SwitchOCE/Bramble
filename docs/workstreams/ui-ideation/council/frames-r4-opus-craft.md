# Frame council, round 4 re-check: craft (Opus)

**1. The owner's point.** Yes, it blends now. At 112 px on teal the border is one band of even width that turns from lead to glass and widens to a shallow V at the foot. The join is hidden under the stat discs. Round 3's jump from thin lead to a tall glass collar is gone, so round 4 is clearly better. **Flow** does this better than **near**: in flow the discs sit in the border, while in near they float over the art. At 112 px the two look almost the same.

**2. Gained and lost.** Gained: one even outline, more art, and disc rings of tier glass instead of flat chips. Lost: the glass shrank from about a third of the card to a thin trim, so tiers read more weakly. Tier 5 amber and tier 6 ruby merge into warm art. Round 3's leaf and petal motifs are now plain textures, so in greyscale tiers 2 to 4 still look alike. Golden reads at a glance but is still flat mustard. Tier 1 still looks like silver or marble.

**3. Good design?** Nearly. The outline and the balance of frame against art would hold up beside published frames. Strongest: the even band with the discs set into it. Weakest: the materials, meaning the lead and the glass.

**4. At 300 px.** The glass looks like a texture clipped to the band: tier 6's braid and tier 3's waves run under the lead lines instead of being cut into panes. The lead is a smooth bevel with one flat highlight and no solder. Small glass spikes poke up above the discs. Glow, sheen and motion: not judged.

**5. Changes still needed**
1. Build the glass band from real cut panes, each edged in lead.
2. Give the lead a cast finish: rounded, with solder at the joints and some patina. Make golden the same shape in modelled gilt.
3. Bring back about a third more glass height, and give tiers 5 and 6 colours that don't merge with warm art.
