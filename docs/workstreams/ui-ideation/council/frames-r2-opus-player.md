# Frame council, round 2: Opus, player lens

Lens: a player in their hundredth game who knows Magic, Hearthstone and Battlegrounds. Judged at 112 px with art first (cropped from the sheets at 1:1, then enlarged 3x only to check craft). Glow, sheen and motion: not judged; stills can't show them.

## Take M

1. **Frame, card feel.** Yes. It reads as a made object, closest to a Hearthstone minion's weight: dark came round the art, a tier-coloured base, stat coins. Not a UI outline, and the art still wins on the board.
2. **Art to border.** About right for the upper card (thin dark came, art near 80%). The bottom is too heavy: the discs are about 30 px at 112 px and, with the glass climbing to 45% at tier 6, they eat the bottom corners of the art.
3. **Flourishes.** In the right two places: a bead at the top point, a crest at the base. The crest survives at 112 px as a shape (bud, trefoil, fleur). The bead and golden's top crest don't, as M admits.
4. **The join.** The best part. The inner rail parts and curves away to hold the glass, ending in a leaf tip, so the glass grows out of the lead. It looks made at 300 px and clean at 112.
5. **Tiers.** Far quieter than today's frame. Higher tiers are more obvious because the glass rises and the crest grows, not by hue alone. Neighbours a player could confuse: **5 and 6**, both warm, especially over sunset art. Greyscale steps are small.
6. **Golden.** Real gilt; it reads as metal and is unmissable on the silhouette. But the normal stat discs are already gold coins, so golden changes only the rim. **Tier 5 golden is one yellow mass:** gilt rim, amber glass, gold discs. Tier 1 golden mixes metals: gold rails on silver glass.
7. **Craft.** Tier 5's discs are oversized. Tier 4 has cyan knots on violet glass. Tier 3's glass is squeezed at 300 px. The upper lead reads as brown wood more than lead.

## Take N

1. **Frame, card feel.** Partly. It reads as a Battlegrounds token or a Runeterra follower: quiet and art-led. On the board it reads as a neat outline with a coloured base, not yet as a crafted piece.
2. **Art to border.** Right for a board piece. Art is about 78%, the lead is a solid 6–7 px, and the discs are smaller (about 24 px) and stay in their corners.
3. **Flourishes.** Base only, top plain. The crest silhouettes are clear at 112 px and grow sensibly up the tiers. That's good restraint.
4. **The join.** Weak. The grey lead **fades** into the glass with a soft gradient. That is the old cut, blurred rather than built. Real came doesn't fade.
5. **Tiers.** Quieter than today's frame, but the glass band is the same height on every tier, so rank is hue plus crest only. Tiers 5 and 6 are close, and tier 1's grey glass looks like part of the lead.
6. **Golden.** Unmissable as brightness, but the rim is pale ivory-champagne. At 112 px it reads as bone or a selection highlight, not gold. Tier 5 holds up only because the rim is so pale.
7. **Craft.** The lead and discs are consistent, since they come from one source. The glass has white star glints. The side flame tips are uneven at tier 6.

**Spells:** both takes' spells (round 1) belong to their families: a lead ring with a tier-coloured lower arc. Both are still thin.

## Once

8. **Closer: M.** Its join looks made, its tiers climb by height as well as hue, and its golden is metal. What M should borrow from N: **ivory stat discs** on normal cards, so gold means golden. Also N's smaller, uniform discs (one cut, one size, one position) and its solid upper lead, used on every tier.
9. **Put to the owner as a proper pass?** M: **yes**. What decides it is that the lead visibly opens to hold the glass, which is the owner's "blending between the two zones" done properly. N: **no**. Its golden doesn't read as gold, and its join is a fade.
10. **Three changes to M:**
   1. Make gold mean golden. Normal discs ivory, ringed in tier glass. On golden, the discs and the rim turn gilt. Deepen tier 5's amber toward honey-brown so a golden tier 5 isn't one yellow mass, and fix tier 1 golden's silver and gold metal clash.
   2. Shrink the discs to one size, about 24 px at 112 px, the same on every tier, to give the bottom corners back to the art.
   3. Separate tiers 5 and 6 in value as well as hue: deeper crimson for 6, and a crest that is clearly bigger at 6. Then recheck the greyscale row.
