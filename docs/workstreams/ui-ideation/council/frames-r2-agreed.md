# Frame council, round 2: what the council said

Put together by the orchestrator (an agent) from the three voting members' answers (`frames-r2-opus-player.md`, `frames-r2-opus-craft.md`, `frames-r2-neon.md`). The assembly is clerical: an item is "agreed" when at least two of the three voters raised it, and each item names who did. Nothing here is the orchestrator's judgement. The owner's reactions outrank all of it. The checker's measurements (`frames-r2-sol-check.md`) were not in when this was written.

## What M does well (so it is kept)

- The join: the lead's rails part and the glass ends in a leaf tip with a solder knot. "The best join either take shows" (craft); "the best part" (player); "built, not cut" (neon).
- Golden reads as real metal and is unmissable on every tier (all three).
- Art is about 80% of the card with a 6 px rim, right for a board piece (all three).
- Higher tiers read as higher because the glass climbs, not by colour alone (all three).
- Far quieter than today's frame (all three).

## Agreed changes for M

1. **Gold must mean only golden.** The normal stat discs are already gold coins, so golden changes only the rim, and a golden tier 5 is "one yellow mass" of gilt, amber glass and gold discs. Make the disc faces ivory or cream on normal cards, as take N has. Deepen tier 5's amber so the gilt beats it in value as well as hue. (all three)
2. **One stat disc for every tier:** one size, one position, one ring. Tier 5's are oversized. About 24 px at 112 px gives the bottom corners back to the art. (all three)
3. **One upper lead for every tier, and a better one.** It drifts between tiers (grey on tier 1, thin purple-charcoal on tier 4, bronze on tiers 5 and 6; golden rail widths vary). It is flat dark brown with no highlight, so it "reads as brown wood more than lead" and sinks on dark art. Use one shared came on all six tiers: lighter pewter, rounded, with one highlight line, still about 6 px. Keep it crisp, not airbrushed. (all three)
4. **Tiers that can be confused.** Tiers 5 and 6 are both warm and sit close over warm art (all three). In greyscale the ladder is nearly flat, and tiers 2 to 4 blur (all three). Separate 5 and 6 in value as well as hue, and give the top tiers a clearer step in mass or outline.
5. **Drop what does not survive at 112 px.** The top bead is about 2 px. The golden top crest does not read, and only tier 4's golden has one. Remove both, or make the crest the same on all six and at least 6 px. (all three)
6. **Tier 1.** Its base is a brushed-steel plate with no leading, so it reads as metal, not glass (craft). Golden tier 1 mixes gold rails with silver glass (player). Its join is a blunt diagonal stop (neon). One point each, all on tier 1.
7. **Tier 3's glass is squeezed** at 300 px. (player, neon; the art director also lists it)
8. **Show it on the board's ground.** M was shown only on near-black. Put the seven-card board on the teal ground of `/data/ui-ideation/concepts/A/p2r5/mid.png`, and check the darkest art. (craft, neon)
9. **Spells:** same family, but the first round's ring is too thin against the cards. (all three; already reordered)

One voter each (optional): cyan knots on tier 4's violet glass (player); tier 5's side leaves are asymmetric (neon); tier 2's band disappears on green art (craft); adopt take N's bottom crest that grows by a clear count of petals (neon).

Its join is a fade or looks pasted (player, neon; craft calls it "mostly made" but arbitrary in height). Its glass is a smooth airbrushed gradient with white sparkle specks, "machine-made and plasticky" (craft, player). Its strengths are the ones listed above for M to borrow.
