## 5. Rank

1. **D (1st):** Biggest leap of the round—every agreed item fixed, dark surfaces let the glass and shop lead cleanly, and its card anatomy (stats, tinted buff/wound stones, tier, charge, renown, golden) is the clearest of the three.
2. **A (2nd):** Executed all five agreed items with razor-sharp craft and the cleanest top-point tier placement, held back only by a still-bright `End turn`, timid stat carets and a cramped hand.
3. **E (3rd):** Retains the best `mid` eye order and richest woodland atmosphere, but its foot-placed tier reads as a third stat, changed-stat rings broke on `mid` and `after`, and the hand log still leaves 7-card placement unsolved.

## 6. Is any of them close?

- **D — Yes, ready after one small polish round.** The layout, lighting hierarchy and card language all work together; fixing `Dame Farthing`'s `after` standings state, adding a visual turn cue, softening her `mid` portrait and inactive Golden glow, and nudging spell coins/shards will make it ready to show the owner as a finished direction.
- **A — Close, one focused fix round away.** Its architecture and card frame work well, though it needs slightly more than micro-polish: quieting `End turn` while gold remains, replacing tiny buff/wound carets with unmistakable stat colouring, unifying `Upgrade`, and reworking the cramped left-light hand so held cards and costs are readable.
- **E — Not yet; needs one real design pass first.** Moving/restyling tier off the stat line, redesigning changed-stat feedback so wounds don't disappear into crimson Health discs (and actually applying buff cues on `mid`), and solving where up to seven hand cards sit without hitting the back row are real card-and-layout changes, not small fixes.
