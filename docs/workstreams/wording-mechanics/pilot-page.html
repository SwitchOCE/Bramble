02

Divine Shield

Recommended · option 1

Divine Shield: The first time this takes damage each fight, it takes none.

Short formTakes no damage the first time

TodayThe first damage this takes is prevented.

Hearthstone's own frame ("the first time this takes damage") makes it one hit of any size. "The first damage" can read as one point, because Magic counts damage in points. It also drops the passive "is prevented".

OptionHover lineMisread
0 todayThe first damage this takes is prevented.0 of 6
1The first time this takes damage each fight, it takes none.0 of 6
2Breaks the first time this would take damage, and blocks all of it. Back each fight.0 of 6
3Once each fight, when this would take damage, it takes none instead.0 of 6
 No reader misread any option. Two readers on today's text had to guess whether blocked damage still counts for Venomous.
  • Hearthstone: The first time this takes damage, ignore it. "Ignore it" tells the player to do something they don't do.
  • Magic: Prevent the next 1 damage that would be dealt to any target this turn. This is the point-counting habit.
For youNo live card has Divine Shield; only two stand-in cards do. Is it in the game?

Fury

Recommended · option 1

Fury 1: Whenever this attacks or is attacked, it gains +1 Attack before damage, and +1 more for each unit you're outnumbered by.

Outnumbered: How many more units the enemy had when the fight started. It doesn't change during the fight. Back-row Squires don't count.

Short form+Attack when fighting, more if outnumbered

TodayWhenever this attacks or is attacked, gain +1 Attack, and +1 more for each unit you're outnumbered by.

Two added words ("before damage") and a past-tense Outnumbered line. Under today's text all three readers had to guess the timing. Under option 1, none did.

OptionHover lineMisread
0 todayWhenever this attacks or is attacked, gain +1 Attack, and +1 more for each unit you're outnumbered by.0 of 9
1…it gains +1 Attack before damage, and +1 more for each unit you're outnumbered by.0 of 9
2…it first gains +1 Attack, and +1 more for each unit the enemy had beyond yours when the fight started.0 of 9
3…it first gains +1 Attack, or +2 if you're outnumbered by 1, +3 if by 2, and so on.0 of 9
 Nobody misread. The difference is in what readers had to guess: timing under 0, how long the gain lasts under 2.
  • Magic's Rampage is the only mechanic in either game that grows with the enemy's numbers: +3/+3 until end of turn for each creature blocking it beyond the first.
  • Magic's Bushido has Fury's two-sided trigger: Whenever this creature blocks or becomes blocked, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn.
For youFury has no rulebook entry; its formula is a 19 Nov balance proposal. Also, the "Fury 1" label hides the Marshal's doubling. That is a display fix, not wording.

Pile on

Recommended · option 1

Pile on 1: Whenever a neighbour attacks, this hits the target first, up to this many times each fight. If that kills the target, your attacker isn't hit back.

Neighbour: Next to this in its row, or diagonally in front or behind.

Short formHits first when a neighbour attacks

TodayThe first 1 times a neighbour attacks each fight, this hits the target first.

The only option where readers guessed nothing at all. It says the one thing readers couldn't predict (a kill spares your attacker), and the code counts diagonal slots as neighbours, which no text says today. It also ends "the first 1 times".

OptionHover lineMisread
0 todayThe first 1 times a neighbour attacks each fight, this hits the target first.0 of 6
1Whenever a neighbour attacks, this hits the target first, up to this many times each fight. If that kills the target, your attacker isn't hit back.0 of 6
2The first time a neighbour attacks each fight, this hits the target before they fight.0 of 6
3Joins this many attacks a fight by units touching it, hitting the target first. A target it kills can't hit back.0 of 6
 Under 0 and 2, all three readers had to guess that a kill means no hit back.
  • Battlegrounds, Baby Krush: summon an 8/8 Devilsaur to attack the target first. This is the nearest wording.
  • Hearthstone says "adjacent", "next to" and "neighbors" on different cards. We should pick one word and keep it.

Renown

Recommended · option 1, plus a hover on the slot's Renown counter

Renown: Each Renown on a slot gives +1/+1 to the unit that starts the fight there, and to any reservist who steps in. Renown gained in a fight counts from the next fight.

Renown 3 (on the slot's counter): The unit that starts the fight here has +3/+3, and so does a reservist who steps in. Not summons or returns.

Short formSlot gives +1/+1 per point

TodayThe unit that starts the fight here, and any reservist who steps in, has +1/+1 for each. (The slot's counter has no hover at all.)

The clearest result of the test. With today's text all three readers gave a reservist the Renown the Padre had just added mid-fight. With any new option, nobody did. "Here" on a card has nothing to point at, so the slot becomes the subject.

OptionHover lineMisread
0 todayThe unit that starts the fight here, and any reservist who steps in, has +1/+1 for each.3 of 6
1Each Renown on a slot gives +1/+1 to the unit that starts the fight there… Renown gained in a fight counts from the next fight.0 of 6
2A unit gets +1/+1 for each Renown on its slot if it started the fight there or stepped in as a reservist. Summons and returns get none. Renown gained in a fight waits for the next.0 of 6
3A bonus that stays on a slot: +1/+1 per point, for the unit that starts the fight there and reservists who step in. Gained in a fight, it counts from the next.0 of 6
 A third board, about what the recruit board shows, was dropped from the scores: no wording says it, so it tested the display, not the words.
  • Neither game has a lasting bonus on a board slot. The nearest is Battlegrounds: Start of Combat: Give your left-most minion Divine Shield…, which names the moment, then the place.
  • Magic already has a keyword called Renown: a one-time +1/+1 counter for hitting a player.
Found on the wayOn cards that give Renown (Sentry, Padre…), the hover title reads "Renown 1": the amount given, not the slot's Renown. Traced in the code, not seen on screen.

Root

Recommended · option 2

Root 1: Once each fight, after this dies, spend 1 Compost to return it at full Health, without this fight's buffs or Renown.

Short formReturns once a fight for Compost

TodayThe first time this dies each fight, spend 1 Compost to return it.

Today two of three readers brought the unit back with its Renown. The new options all fixed that, but option 1's "its permanent stats" only worked because readers guessed it meant "no Renown". Option 2 says it outright. "Once each fight" also stays neutral on the open question below.

OptionHover lineMisread
0 todayThe first time this dies each fight, spend 1 Compost to return it.2 of 6
1After this dies, spend 1 Compost to bring it back with full Health and its permanent stats. Once a fight.0 of 6
2Once each fight, after this dies, spend 1 Compost to return it at full Health, without this fight's buffs or Renown.0 of 6
3Comes back after dying, once a fight, if you can pay 1 Compost. It comes back fresh: full Health, no buffs from this fight, no Renown.0 of 6
  • Hearthstone, Hollow Direhorn: After a friendly minion dies, spend 3 Corpses to gain Reborn. This is nearly Root, in one line.
  • Hearthstone Mercenaries already has a keyword Root: Rooted characters can't Attack.
For youRoot has no rulebook entry. Today's text says "the first time this dies"; the code means "the first death your Compost can pay for". Which is the rule?

Charge and Supply

Recommended · option 1

Charge 2 (1 left): Works up to this many times each fight.

Supply 6 (4 left): Works up to this many times in the whole game.

Short formsLimited uses, refilled each fight·Uses for the whole game

TodayWorks this many times, then stops. Refills each fight. · Works this many times in all, then never again.

The shortest true line. The live count, as Battlegrounds shows "(1 left!)", does the teaching. The test couldn't separate the four options: nobody misread any of them.

09

What this test can and can't show

Each reader had never seen the game. It got only the card text and hover lines a player would see, plus a small fight in words, and said what happens. Each reader saw one set of wordings only.

It showed: today's Renown and Root text gets misread, and every new option fixes it. Where nobody misread, the readers' written guesses showed which facts the text leaves out.

It can't show what you or a new player would do. These readers read every word, never skim, never skip a hover, and don't get tired. Three readers per option is too few to rank options that all scored zero. Your own read is the real test.

10

The process, and what comes next

The draft process was written before any wording, then followed to write these. What the pilot taught it so far:

  1. Find the facts a player can't guess. These are the trap facts: Renown waits a fight; Root drops Renown; a pile-on kill spares the attacker. They are what the extra words buy.
  2. Check every name against Magic, Hearthstone and our own card names. This found Root's clash, which the first research missed.
  3. One meaning per trigger word, in a fixed table. The draft table lacked "first" (before damage) and "once each fight", so it needs those rows.
  4. Readers' guesses tell more than right or wrong. Ask each reader to write what it assumed; that separated options when the scores didn't.
  5. Copies of reminder text sit on some cards' data and would go stale on any rewording. The process now lists every copy first.

Next, once you've picked: the process is revised from your picks and goes into the ux-copy skill by a pull request for you to read. Then a fresh worker, given only that process, words the other ~40 keywords, and you check a sample. That shows whether the process repeats. Your picks go into the game only by a pull request that waits for you.
