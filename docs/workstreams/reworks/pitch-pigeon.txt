PIGEON REWORK PITCH: RENOWN, RESERVE, AND TWO GLOBAL KEYWORDS (ACTIVE, CHARGE)
Designer's pitch, 19 Nov 2026. Nothing here is decided. Every number is a rough hand estimate, not simulated.

Units of measure used throughout
- A body is the median printed stats of a plain unit at that tier: 9 / 12 / 14 / 18 / 20 / 25 (Tiers 1-6).
- Tier guide targets used: permanent a turn 0.4 / 0.3-1 / 0.8 / 1.3 / 2 / 4 bodies; in a fight 0.6 / 1 / 0.7-1.4 / 2 / 4 / 6 bodies.
- 1 Renown is worth about 3 stats a fight on a Pigeon board: 2 for the unit that starts there, about 1 more for the reservists that step in after it and for never being lost when the unit is sold. On a non-Pigeon board it is worth about 2.
- 1 gold is worth about 3 stats in the middle game (a Tier 3 unit costs 4 gold for a 14-stat body plus its text).

The aim, from the owner's words: slots are the regiment's companies; a company earns Renown and lends it to whoever holds the line for it. Renown goes only to the unit that starts the fight there and to reservists, so other tribes' summons no longer double-dip. Pigeon summons are reservists, so they do get it. Orders become a global Active. Repeat limits become a global Charge. Reserve gets one clear job.


======================================================================
1. RENOWN
======================================================================

THE RULE (variant A, recommended)

- What it is: a number on one of your 7 slots. It replaces Pigeon slot buffs.
- What it gives: +1/+1 for each Renown to:
  - the unit standing in that slot when a fight starts, for as long as it stays alive;
  - each reservist that steps into that slot during the fight.
  Nothing else gets it: other summons (Hoppers, Cousins, Pips, copies from the Compost, Elder Mother's copies) and returns (Molt, Root) don't. The unit in a slot on the recruit board shows its stats with Renown included, because that is what it will start the next fight with.
- Pigeon summons are reservists. Pigeon cards never "summon" a token: they say "a Private steps in", and the Private is a reservist. So the Pigeons' own summons get Renown, and nobody else's do.
- How it is gained: cards say "Give this slot 1 Renown" or "give its slot 1 Renown". You gain it in your turn (drill) and from what happens in fights (kills, deaths, surviving), which matches the owner's "the brigade gets reputation from its achievements".
- How long it lasts: always permanent. It stays when the unit is sold, moved or dies, and it never travels with a unit. There is no fight-only Renown, so no card needs the word "permanently".
- Renown gained in a fight counts from the next fight. The real slot gains it at once, but the fight you are in doesn't change. This keeps fights readable and stops a slot growing mid-fight and then feeding a reservist.
- How it grows through the game: slots only ever gain Renown. The tribe's cards decide how fast and where: one slot (the Guard post), the slots in front of an officer, a whole row, every slot.
- Who reads it: effects that read a unit's Attack or Health read the total, Renown included (Hit, Lends, the Mice's pile-on). Effects that copy or take a unit's stats leave it out (Eat, Sword-heir, Old Brock, triples), as slot buffs do now.
- Units of any tribe standing in a slot with Renown get it. Pigeon officers still splash; the splash board just gets no extra use from its summons.

Tooltip (reminder text):
  Renown: The unit that starts the fight here, and any reservist who steps in, has +1/+1 for each Renown.

On the slot: the Renown number sits on the slot's base as a small banner (see naming, section 7), so an empty slot shows what it is worth.

HOW IT DIFFERS FROM TODAY'S SLOT BUFFS
- One number, always +N/+N. Today's buffs can be uneven (Grenadier gives +2/+4).
- Only the unit that starts there and reservists get it. Today any unit that enters the slot does, which is why Hoppers, Cousins and Compost copies doubled up on Pigeon slots (the owner, 19 Nov: "slot buffs are incredibly powerful with summon strategies, because you get to use them twice or more").
- Always permanent. Gains made in a fight count from the next fight.
- Only one multiplier, the Colonel, and it is for reservists from your hand. The Regimental Sergeant Major no longer doubles anything.
- What stays the same: it survives selling and replacing, so the tribe can still upgrade its soldiers without losing what the company has earned. That is the tribe's identity, and it is also the edge the bot league blamed. It is now paid for: every source gives less (section 6).
- The Mice's slot Attack buffs (Whetstone Vole, Grindstone Miller, Edgewright) are a separate matter for the Mouse pitch. Renown is not a slot buff, so the Edgewright does not double it.

THREE VARIANTS

A. Even (recommended). Starter and reservists get +1/+1 for each Renown. A card can double it for reservists (the Colonel, Tier 6).
  + One short rule. The smallest double-dip that still lets Pigeons have summons.
  + "Reservists get double" becomes a capstone you build toward, not a rule you pay for on every card.
  - Before Tier 6, a reservist's only reason to exist is "one more body". Reserve feels plain until the Colonel.

B. Reservists double, by rule. The starter gets +1/+1 for each Renown and every reservist (Privates included) gets +2/+2.
  + Reserve has a clear reason from turn 1: a 4/5 Territorial in your hand can beat a better unit on the board if it steps into a big slot. This is the owner's "reservists get double benefit".
  - Each slot's worth a fight rises by about 40%, so every Renown number would have to drop by about a third. At Tier 1 that can't be done: Sentry already gives the smallest amount, 1 a turn.
  - A 2/2 Private stepping into a Renown 20 slot is a 42/42. Holding cheap units in your hand beats buying good ones, and the Tier 1 long-term potential the owner wants lower goes up instead.

C. Ranks and worth. A slot's Renown is a small count (shown as stars), and each point is worth the player's Honour: a number on the player that starts at +1/+1 and that a few cards raise ("Your Honour is +1/+1 higher"). So Renown 3 at Honour 4 gives +12/+12.
  + Two dials: Renown for where (wide or tall), Honour for how much. Numbers on the slots stay small and easy to read, and "the size of renown changes through the game" becomes literal.
  - Two numbers to multiply in your head on every slot.
  - Multiplying growth is exactly the snowball the league found. It is the hardest of the three to cost.
  - It is close to the Locusts' Tide.

Recommendation: A. It fixes the cause the league found (summons double-dipping slots) with the shortest rule, and it gives the owner's "reservists double" a home on the Colonel, where it can be tuned without touching every card. If the owner wants reservists to matter earlier, the step toward B is a Tier 4 or 5 card that says "Your reservists have +50% Renown", not a change to the rule.


======================================================================
1b. RESERVE, WITH A CLEAR GOAL (it goes with Renown)
======================================================================

Goal: Reserve is how a company outlasts the enemy. It is depth instead of width: your best slots fight more than once, because a fresh soldier from your hand steps in when one falls and takes the company's Renown. The cost is hand space (the hand holds 7) and gold spent on units that don't stand on the board. The choice it gives you each turn is which units you hold back.

Why the Colonel broke it: Reserve had no limit. A full hand meant up to 7 extra bodies a fight, each taking a slot buff (league: 1 unit in hand won 0% of a fixed matchup, 2 won 84%, 3 or more won 100%).

The rule:
- Reserve: in a fight, after a friendly unit dies, a unit with Reserve in your hand steps into that slot. It is a reservist and has the slot's Renown.
- Limit: you call at most 2 reservists from your hand each fight. Cards can raise it ("You can call 1 more reservist each fight"). The limit is shown by your hand as 2 small banners that drop as reservists go in.
- Which slot first: your Guard post, then the slot with the most Renown, then reading order. The most famous company is refilled first. Leftmost in hand goes first.
- Privates step in as reservists and get Renown, but they don't count against the limit of 2. Each Private card has its own Charge.
- Order in a round of deaths (shared ruling 6) stays as today: reservists from the hand at step 3, Privates at step 5, after waiting summons. A Private only steps in if its slot is still empty.
- Unchanged: Reserve is not a summon or a return, reservists aren't counted for Outnumbered, and the hand is the same after the fight.

Tooltip:
  Reserve: When a friendly unit dies in a fight, this steps in from your hand to take its place. You call up to 2 a fight.
  Private: A 2/2 Pigeon reservist.

Rough power: a Reserve unit held in hand is about one whole extra body a fight once your board is full, plus its slot's Renown. That is about twice what the same unit is worth sitting on the board. The limit of 2 is what keeps it in budget.

What beats Reserve: fights that end fast; killing the officers in the back row (Sortie, Hit), since the Colonel and the Quartermaster are auras; damage that isn't stopped by Guard, to the units next to the post (Cleave, Gleaner); damage to all enemies once small reservists are in; and Overrun carrying through a queue of bodies.


======================================================================
2. ACTIVE (global keyword, replaces Order)
======================================================================

Rule:
- "Active (N): effect". In the Recruit Phase, pay N gold to use the effect. "Active:" with no number is free.
- Once a turn for each unit, by default. A unit with Active works like Charge 1 that refills each turn. A printed Charge replaces that: "Active (1), Charge 2:" can be used twice a turn.
- Only on the board, never from the hand. A golden copy doubles the effect, not the cost.
- The cost is gold by default. Other costs fit the same frame: the Briars' Bury already works this way, with a shop unit as the cost, and can stay its own word.

Tooltips:
  Active (1): Once a turn, pay 1 gold to use this.
  Active: Use this once a turn.

In the recruit screen:
- A unit on the board with an Active shows a round glass button on the bottom edge of its card, holding the cost as a gold coin (blank for free).
- Tap the button to use it. If the effect needs a target (a slot or a unit), the valid targets light up; tap one, or tap anywhere else to cancel.
- After use the button dims until next turn. If you can't afford it, the coin shows the cost in red and the button doesn't respond.
- No warning on End turn for unused Actives, because the main screen assumes a player who knows the rules (owner, 9 Nov).
- On hover or long press, the tooltip.
- Bots need to be taught to use Actives (the league bots never used Orders on purpose).

Rough power: price it as what it gives each turn, minus the gold at about 3 stats a gold. The old Lance Corporal gave 8 stats a turn for 1 gold (net +5), and the critics called him every tribe's best Tier 2. Actives are repeating permanent effects, so keep the net small at low tiers.

Three examples on other tribes, to show it is global (names and numbers are illustrations):

- Badger, Sett Armourer, Tier 2, 3/8: "Squire. / Active (1): Give a unit +4 Health permanently."
  Role: a gold sink that grows Health, which the owner wants Badgers to have. About 4 stats for 1 gold: net +1 a turn, about 0.1 body. Weak on purpose: it turns spare gold into survivability and is still worth it late, when gold is plentiful.
- Mouse, Tinker Shrew, Tier 2, 2/6: "Pile on. / Active (1), Charge 2: Give a Mouse +2 Attack permanently."
  Shows Active with Charge: up to 2 gold for +4 Attack a turn, net about +2. It gives the Mice a way to feed their best piler.
- Locust, Gorger (Tier 4, 5/7), rewritten: "Active (1): This eats a unit in your hand."
  Today: "The first time you sell a unit each turn, this eats it." The Active version lets you choose when and what, and drops the sale's gold, so the 1 gold cost is a real price. About the same power, more control.

The two Pigeon Actives are below: Lance Corporal (Active (1)) and Grenadier Guard (a free Active).


======================================================================
3. CHARGE (global keyword)
======================================================================

What it is for: a set number of uses, shown as pips on the card, in place of "(8 a fight)", "(up to N times)" and "the first time each fight". The pips do most of the teaching: they dim as they are used and light again when they refill.

Format: Charge goes in front of the effect it limits, like a trigger label: "Charge 4: After a friendly unit dies, give its slot 1 Renown." With Active: "Active (1), Charge 2: ...".

The options

1. One word that always refills. "Charge N" refills at the start of every fight and every turn.
  + One word and one rule. Covers every limit in the game today.
  + Effects that happen in your turn (the Gorger's once a turn) refill each turn without a second word.
  - No way to say "N times in the whole game", which is the tool for capping long-term growth on Tier 1 and 2 cards (owner, 19 Nov: "Most tier one units should probably get a nerf to their long-term potential. The tempo of them is fine.").

2. Two words. "Charge N" refills each fight and each turn. "Supply N" never refills: N uses in the whole game, then the effect stops for good. (Other names for the second word: Wick, Fuse, Rations.)
  + Both kinds of design space. Supply caps a scaler's total without touching its early tempo: a Tier 1 that builds early and then runs dry.
  + Pips make the difference visible: round pips for Charge, which relight, and small square crates for Supply, which empty and stay empty.
  - Two words to learn, and players must remember which one refills.
  - Supply must be rare (2 or 3 cards in the game), or it turns into bookkeeping.

3. One word, and a rule about where it was used. "Charge N" used in a fight refills after the fight; used in your turn, it never refills.
  + One word, and it gives both kinds.
  - The rule is hidden: two cards with the same word behave differently. The Gorger's once a turn can't be written.
  - The owner floated it; I think it fails the "more intuitive" aim.

4. One word that never refills, and the refill is printed. "Charge 8 a fight" against "Charge 6".
  + One word, nothing hidden.
  - It is barely shorter than today's "(8 a fight)", so it saves little text.

Recommendation: option 2, Charge and Supply. It meets all three of the owner's aims: limits read at a glance, tooltips get shorter, and there is new design space. Supply is what the Tier 1 long-term nerf needs. Start with Supply on one or two cards (Sentry below) and add more only if it earns its place.

A warning on the word Charge: in Hearthstone, Charge means "can attack at once", and Bramble already has "attacks immediately" effects and a Badger card called Herald of the Charge. Players coming from Battlegrounds may misread "Charge 1". It is the owner's word and it reads well as "a battery that recharges", so I'd keep it, but a playtest should check. The fallback is "Uses N".

Rules:
- Charge N: the effect works N times, then stops until the next fight or the next turn starts, when all its uses return.
- Supply N: the effect works N times in the whole game, then never again. A triple adds the copies' remaining Supply together.
- Golden: doubles the effect's numbers and leaves Charge or Supply alone, unless the card says otherwise. That avoids ×4 goldens. Where the effect has no number (a Private steps in), the golden doubles the Charge instead.
- Other cards can interact with it: "Give a unit +1 Charge this fight" or "Refill a unit's Supply" become possible designs.
- Molt and Stiff Upper Lip are already Charge 1 effects underneath, and Molt is already "counted in charges" in the shared rules. They can show pips without changing their words.

Tooltips:
  Charge 4: Works 4 times. Refills each fight and each turn.
  Supply 6: Works 6 times in all, then never again.

Today's cards rewritten (from src/content/*/units.json):
- Padre (Pigeon, Tier 3)
  Today:      "After a friendly unit dies, give its slot +1/+1 permanently (8 a fight)."
  With Charge, today's design: "Charge 8: After a friendly unit dies, give its slot +1/+1 permanently."
  In this rework: "Charge 4: After a friendly unit dies, give its slot 1 Renown." 1 Renown is worth about 3 stats and Renown is always permanent, so Charge 8 would give about 24 stats a turn, twice the Tier 3 target. Charge 4 is on target.
- Needle Pikeman (Mouse, Tier 3)
  Today:      "Pile on. / Can't attack. / After this piles on, it gains +{attack} Attack permanently (5 a fight)."
  With Charge: "Pile on. / Can't attack. / Charge 5: After this piles on, it gains +{attack} Attack permanently."
- Shrew Cutpurse (Mouse, Tier 2)
  Today:      "Pile on. / The first time this piles on each fight, gain {gold} gold."
  With Charge: "Pile on. / Charge 1: After this piles on, gain {gold} gold."
- Gorger (Locust, Tier 4), as a Charge that refills each turn, if it doesn't become an Active:
  Today:      "The first time you sell a unit each turn, this eats it."
  With Charge: "Charge 1: After you sell a unit, this eats it."
- Not a clean fit: Sett Sexton's "(2 a fight each)" counts uses for each unit in front of it, not for the card. Keep its wording, or redesign it as one shared Charge 4.
- Recruiting Sergeant (Pigeon, Tier 2): "(2 a fight)" becomes "Charge 1" in this rework (below).


======================================================================
4. THE REWORKED TRIBE (20 units and 1 token)
======================================================================

The Regiment. Three plans, down from four:
- Honours (wide): build Renown on every slot, so every starter is big. Sentry, Lance Corporal, Old Campaigner, Colour Sergeant, Lieutenant, Regimental Sergeant Major.
- The Reserve (deep): stack Renown on the Guard post and a few slots, and feed reservists and Privates through them. Territorial, Home Guard, Recruiting Sergeant, Padre, Grenadier Guard, Bugler, Quartermaster, Colour Ensign, The Colonel.
- Air support: flyers over the line, and reinforcements that fly in. Squadron Leader, Flying Ace, Wing Commander, Group Captain, Air Marshal.

The Medals plan (Old soldiers) is cut: Medals, Decorated, Stiff Upper Lip, Adjutant, Brigadier and Field Marshal go. Why: Renown is the owner's "decorated" idea, and two stat counters (one on the unit, one on the slot) do the same job twice. The Brigadier ("Decorated Pigeons attack twice") was the largest utility effect in the tribe. Tribe keywords drop from 11 to 6 (Renown, Reserve, Private, Guard post, Sortie, Cleave), plus the global Active and Charge.

Shape: 2 / 3 / 5 / 4 / 3 / 3. Tier 3 has five because the Reserve plan needs a good reservist there.

Format: Name, Tier, Attack/Health. [NEW / CHANGED / KEPT]
  Text (one effect per line).
  Golden. Role. Cost.

It no longer adds bodies: the limit of 2 (or 3 with the Quartermaster) decides how many come, and the Colonel decides how good they are. Privates are left out, which stops it stacking with the Bugler.
    Other Colonels the owner could pick:
    b) "Start of fight: Your 2 leftmost units in hand gain Reserve this fight." The old idea with a cap. It still works for any tribe, but it adds bodies, not quality: about the old board with 2 in hand, which won 84% of a fixed matchup.
    c) "You can call 2 more reservists each fight." The simplest option, and it stacks with the Quartermaster, so it is the most likely to run away.

20. Air Marshal, T6, 7/15. [NEW; takes Field Marshal's place]
    "Charge 3: After a reservist steps in, it attacks immediately."
    Golden: Charge 6. Role: the air plan's capstone. Reinforcements dive straight in.
    Cost: 3 extra attacks a fight by reservists sized about 30-60, each dealing its Attack and taking some back: about 4-5 bodies against a target of 6. This is the tribe's one extra-attack capstone; the skill puts a free attack at capstone level, one card at most. Charge 3 is the cap.

Cut: Adjutant (T4), Brigadier (T5), Field Marshal (T6), and Medals, Decorated and Stiff Upper Lip with them.

Tier check
- T1: a seed (Sentry) and a tempo body that becomes a reservist (Territorial). Fits. Sentry's long tail is capped by Supply.
- T2: an enabler (Recruiting Sergeant), a buffer (Lance Corporal) and a scaler (Old Campaigner). No economy unit, on purpose: Renown that outlives a sale is the tribe's saving. The Recruiting Sergeant runs a little hot.
- T3: two enablers (Colour Sergeant, Grenadier Guard), a buffer (Padre), a splash enabler (Squadron Leader) and a reservist body (Home Guard). The Colour Sergeant comes down from about twice its target to on target.
- T4: a combat engine (Ace), an enabler (Quartermaster), a summoner (Bugler, hot on purpose, about 1.5 times) and a payoff (Lieutenant, hot late on purpose).
- T5: three direction cards, one per plan. Group Captain is a little under.
- T6: three capstones, one per plan. The Regimental Sergeant Major is a little under on purpose. The Colonel is on target but stacks (section 6).

6. COMPARED WITH TODAY'S PIGEONS
======================================================================

Gained
- One stat layer with one short rule. Tribe keywords go from 11 (Slot buff, Order, Guard post, Reserve, Joins the fight, Medal, Decorated, Stiff Upper Lip, Sortie, Cleave, Private) to 6, plus two global ones every tribe can use.
- Other tribes' summons no longer double-dip on Pigeon slots: Hoppers, Cousins, Pips, Compost copies, Molt and Root returns.
- Reserve has a job and a cap: depth, at most 2 reservists from the hand a fight (3-4 with Quartermasters), and Privates capped by their Charges.
- A Colonel that can't snowball: he improves reservists instead of adding them.
- Tier 1's long tail is capped (Sentry Supply 6).
- Every former hot spot comes down:
  - Colour Sergeant: 24 to about 12 stats a turn.
  - Padre: 16 to about 12.
  - Lance Corporal: 8 to about 6 stats a turn, for 1 gold.
  - Recruiting Sergeant: 2 Privates a fight to 1.
  - Bugler: about 6 bodies to about 3.
  - RSM: double-and-doubling to 21 Renown a turn.
  - Colonel: up to 7 extra bodies to 0 extra bodies.
- Three plans, each with its own direction card at Tier 5 and capstone at Tier 6.

Lost
- The Medals plan (Old soldiers), Stiff Upper Lip, and the Brigadier's attack-twice.
- Adjutant's splash for other tribes (Medals on any unit).
- Locusts, Mice and Briars lose a free synergy: their summons in Pigeon slots. That is the point, but those tribes' boards with a Pigeon splash get weaker.
- The old Colonel's "any tribe's hand fights" splash (variant b keeps a capped form of it).

Strengths
- Renown outlives the soldier, so the tribe upgrades freely and never wastes a buff.
- It has the most bodies in a fight of any tribe, and they arrive where they're strongest.
- It has back-row reach (the air plan).

Weaknesses
- The wide plan is plain stats with little utility, so it loses to scaling capstones and to tech units.
- The deep plan's power sits in back-row auras (the Colonel, the Quartermaster) that Sortie and Hits can remove.
- The air plan is thin and depends on the enemy having a back row.
- Before turn 5 every plan is modest, because Renown builds up slowly.

Double-dips that remain, and other risks
1. Pigeon reservists and Privates still use a slot's Renown 2-4 times a fight. This is on purpose (the owner's "Pigeon summons buffed by Renown") and capped by the Reserve limit and Charges, but it is the first thing to measure.
2. The biggest stack: the Colonel's double, plus the Colour Ensign's copied Renown, plus reservists queued through the post (Board 2, turn 10). Dials: the Colonel's double for the first 2 reservists only, and Quartermasters not stacking.
3. Renown that survives sales is the "skill-free, never wasted" edge the league named. It is kept as the tribe's identity, with every source cut by about 30-50%. If Pigeons still lead, the next lever is the Renown amounts, not the rule.
4. The Lieutenant turns Renown into damage that takes none back, which is strong late. It is at half the Renown, and a third is the dial.
5. The combo the owner distrusts can be built: a Flying Ace given Reserve by the Quartermaster, Sortie from the Group Captain, and an instant attack from the Air Marshal, together give Cleave, Sortie and an immediate attack. It takes 4 cards from Tier 4 to Tier 6 and is capped by Charge 3. A playtest should look for it. If needed: the Air Marshal's attack skips units with Cleave, or the Group Captain gives +Attack only.
6. Territorial: a Tier 1 that becomes a 30-stat reservist late. The dial is listed on the card.
7. Bots: the league bots never stocked their hand or used Orders on purpose. A balance read on this tribe needs bots that hold reservists and use Actives, or the numbers will understate it (the same blind spot the league found for the Colonel).
8. Engine work: Renown is a slot layer with a filter (starter or reservist), in-fight gains written to the real slot only, a reservist limit for each side, Charge and Supply counters with refill moments, and Order renamed Active with an optional cost.


======================================================================
7. NAMING
======================================================================

What a slot is called in the Pigeons' world. Card text should keep saying "slot", one word for every tribe, as the wording rule asks. The name below is for the banner on the slot, the guide and the art.

- Company (recommended). The army word, plain, and the right size: one slot, one company, "4th Company". It fits the tribe's army ranks (Private, Lance Corporal, Sergeant, Lieutenant, Colonel).
- Squadron. The owner's lean. It fits the air plan and the RAF ranks (Squadron Leader, Wing Commander). Against it: a squadron is a big unit, larger than one slot, and it clashes with the Squadron Leader card, who affects two slots.
- Flight. A flight of birds, and a real RAF sub-unit. Lovely for pigeons, but "flight" also reads as fleeing or flying.
- Brigade or Regiment. Too big: the Regiment is the tribe's name, and a brigade holds several regiments. The Brigadier is gone, so "Brigade" is free, but it is still the wrong scale.
- Platoon or Section. The right size, but dull, and rarely used in English.
- The Colours, for the Renown banner itself. A regiment's battle honours are sewn onto its Colours. The slot's banner could be drawn as a small flag in lead and glass, with the Renown number on it.

For the stat: Renown (recommended; the owner's word, and clear). Other options: Honours, Glory, Repute.

Recommendation: slots are companies (banners numbered I-VII), Renown is the number on each company's Colours, and card text says "slot" and "Renown". If the owner wants the air flavour throughout, use Squadron, and rename the Squadron Leader (to Flight Lieutenant).
