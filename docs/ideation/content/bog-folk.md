# Bog folk

Toads, frogs, newts, a fire salamander, and two old things that live under the peat. **A swamp opera in which every frog claims to be a prince, and one of them is telling the truth.**

Stats and numbers were set by feel, then costed in `evidence/costing-3.md`. Nothing here has been played.

**The tribe in one paragraph.** Bog folk decide who fights whom. At the bell, the ground under the enemy across from a Bog folk unit turns to bog. Whatever stands there is stuck, and while the toad that sank it lives, every attack it makes has to go at that toad, even if the toad is in the back row. Toads are bitter to the taste, so each bite weakens the biter. Frogs flick their tongues across the water at whatever is stuck. The tribe's game is a matching puzzle against the board you are about to fight: which of their units gets glued to which of yours, and who is left free. Their giant's first swing goes into your toad, and Madame Bufo halves it on her way down. Their protected back-row carry is dragged into a duel with your back row, where the frogs reach it first. And because the answer depends on where the next opponent stands, the right layout changes every round.

**What the owner will see on screen.** At the bell a brown mud patch spreads under an enemy slot, with a reed-line from it to the Bog folk across. That enemy's attacks follow the line. That one picture is the tribe.

**Where the idea came from.** In 02h the owner mused about a Mire that "makes their backline attack into you... you could have effects for when the enemy's backline attacks, so you have some counterattack damage". In 02i he said plainly that he was exploring the idea, not asking for it. This tribe builds on that musing. It isn't something he requested.

**Why this isn't Murlocs in a bog.** There is no Battlecry, no "for each other Bog folk", and no buff that grows with tribe count. The tribe's text fires at the bell, and it reads the other player's board.

**Its lane.** Each tribe owns one relationship to the enemy across, and no card in another tribe copies it. **Bog folk own "the enemy across must attack this"**, and the patch belongs to the ground, so a unit that lands there later is stuck too. Hunters own the marked quarry, which follows the animal. Great beasts, on file, own the column.

## The signature: Mire

*Reminder: Start of Combat: the enemy across must attack this while this lives.*

**Example card.** **Madame Bufo**, tier 2, 1/4. "Mire. Bitter 2. If the enemy this Mires kills this, that enemy loses half its Attack this fight."

**How it plays, one fight.** Round 9. The scout shows the next opponent's 14/8 boar in F3. You put Madame Bufo in F3. At the bell their F3 is bogged. The boar's first swing must go at Bufo. She dies, Bitter takes it to 12, and her halving takes it to 6. The lock ends with her, and the boar now swings at random at a third of its strength. Put her in F1 instead, across their 2/2 token, and you have wasted a card. The slot is the decision.

**The reverse case.** Put a toad in your **B2**. At the bell their **B2**, a back-row carry, is bogged. When its turn comes it must attack your B2 toad, past your intact front row. Their protected carry is now duelling your wall, and Vaulting Hopkin, who attacks before your front row, gets to it first.

**Why build around it.**
- You pick the matchups. Taunt chooses who gets hit. Mire chooses **who does the hitting**, one enemy at a time. Battlegrounds and Galaxy both lack it.
- Every Mire is aimed at a slot on the other board. The position test found "across from" text to be the only kind that clearly created a decision.
- The best slot changes every round, because the next opponent's layout changes. The scouting rule (next opponent's board as it fought last round) makes it a skill and not a guess.
- It is a tribe that taxes a single huge unit, not with the lock itself but with what the lock sets up: Bufo's halving, the Prima Donna, and Hopkin's early strike.

**The price.**
- The lock alone is worth about nothing. Costing measured it like Taunt: about 0 SP on even boards and no better against a giant [m]. Its value is entirely in what it sets up, and the tribe is costed that way.
- A Mire on a small enemy is wasted, and a mirer that dies at once frees its target.
- If you guess the slot wrong, or the opponent rearranges, the mud lands on a token.
- Wide boards shrug it off, because one lock holds one attacker of seven.

**Similar to:** Magic's Provoke and Lure; Galaxy's "the enemy across from me" duels; Hearthstone's Taunt, turned inside out.

### Exact rules

**The bell**
- Start of Combat has a new first step, **the bell**. It comes after "first of all" text (Mag Pye) and before every other Start of Combat text. In it, both sides fix their marks and patches at the same time: Bog folk lay patches and Hunters mark quarries.
- Each unit with Mire puts a **Bog patch** on the enemy slot across from it (8.2.6: F2 faces F2, B1 faces B1). The patch remembers who laid it: its **maker**.
- Patches last this fight and stay on the slot. Fights run on a copy, so nothing carries over.
- **One patch per slot.** If two of your mirers would bog the same enemy slot, the earlier in reading order (F1-F4, then B1-B3) gets it. The later lays nothing there. This matters for the Prima Donna and the Natterjack Chorus, and it makes "who reads first" part of the layout.
- **The patch belongs to the ground.** An empty slot still gets its patch. It waits, and an enemy that later arrives there is Mired: a death summon, a unit out of a Belly, a Tunnel, a Wisp, a Changeling. This is the opposite of Hunters, whose quarry is the animal: a unit summoned into a Hunter's lane isn't marked unless a card says so.

**Being Mired**
- An enemy standing on one of your patches is **Mired**. "Mired enemy" in card text means this, whether or not its maker still lives.
- **The lock.** While the maker is alive and on the maker's side, every attack the Mired unit makes must target the maker. That includes extra attacks (Windfury-style). Cleave and splash pick their main target this way and spread as usual.
- When the maker dies or changes side, the lock ends. The unit is still Mired, but attacks as normal.
- A unit moved off its patch (Whirligig Beetle, Tunnel, Mole Cricket, Changeling) is no longer Mired, and whoever moves onto the patch is.
- Mire changes only **attacks**. Damage effects, "a random enemy", strikes that aren't attacks (Mother Goose) and Start of Combat shots ignore it, as random effects already ignore rows (9.5.6).
- A unit can't be locked onto two makers, because a slot holds one patch.

**Targeting precedence** for every attack in the game:
1. **Mire lock.**
2. **The attacker's own text:** Quarry, Snap, Swoop, Poacher's Path, Charge.
3. **Taunt** (must-be-attacked, 9.5.4).
4. **Random**, within back-row protection (9.5.1).

The lock beats back-row protection: the maker may be hit in the back row while the front row lives. It also makes the maker a legal target for its own Mired enemy even if something else says the maker can't be attacked.

**Lane meetings**
- **Two toads facing each other** lock onto each other: a straight duel. Bitter's floor of 1 and the fight-length cap end it.
- **A Mired Hunter** swings at the toad, not at its quarry. If its quarry *is* the toad (it was across), the two agree.
- **Pack and Pounce.** A Mired unit whose maker lives can join a Pack attack only if the leader's target is that maker, and can Pounce only at its maker. Bitter answers the leader only.
- **A charger** that is Mired charges the toad. Its trample goes behind the toad on its own side; with the toad in the back row, nothing is behind it, so the trample is lost.

**Edge cases**
- **Golden.** Mire has no number, so a golden mirer still lays one patch. Its other numbers double.
- **Triples.** Patches exist only in combat, so a triple loses nothing.
- **Gaps and Nests.** A mirer across an empty enemy slot lays a waiting patch. Against Birds' empty Nests, the 0/2 Egg is Mired and harmless, so that mirer is wasted.
- **Ghosts.** Mire works against a ghost's board like any other.
- **Recruit Phase.** Mire does nothing there. The tray previews the patches against the scouted board.
- **The engine's existing `mired` status** (Turnip Scarecrow, "skips its first attack") is renamed `stalled`, so the word means one thing.

## The other keywords

### Bitter N

*Reminder: after an enemy attacks this, it loses N Attack this fight.*

- It fires after the damage exchange, before deaths are checked, so it fires even if this dies. It answers attacks only, including Pack leaders and Pounces.
- **It can't take Attack below 1.** So Bitter plus a lock never leaves a unit on 0 Attack, and two locked toads can't stall a fight forever.
- It stacks: Bitter 2 from the card and Bitter 1 from Warty Nan is Bitter 3.
- Real toads secrete bufotoxin, and dogs that mouth one spit it out. That's the card.
- **Not Bristle.** Hedgerow's Bristle hurts the attacker; Bitter weakens it.

### Snap

*Reminder: this attacks the Mired enemy with the most Attack, wherever it stands.*

- When a Snap unit attacks and any Mired enemy is on the board, it targets the one with the highest Attack (ties at random). The tongue reaches, so it ignores Taunt and back-row protection. With no Mired enemy it attacks as usual.
- It sits at step 2 of the precedence: if the Snap unit is itself Mired, the lock wins.
- Snap reaches only what is stuck in your own mud, so it stays tied to Mire and doesn't become general back-row reach.

## The units

14 units: 3 / 3 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 1. Seven lay patches (Bridge Toad, Madame Bufo, Gideon, Jenny Greenteeth, The Prima Donna, The Peat King, and the Natterjack Chorus by loan). Four make use of them (Young Pretender, Vaulting Hopkin, Spitfire Sal, His Highness). Three are paid by them (The Peat Cutter, Tiddy Mun, Warty Nan's wall).

Tokens and created cards: **The Prince Restored** (transform, `pool: false`) and the **Peatling** (combat only).

"Beside" means the same-row neighbours (F2 is beside F1 and F3). "Across" is rule 8.2.6. A unit's **maker** is the unit that laid the patch it stands on.

### Tier 1

**Bridge Toad**. *Common Toad, Bridge-Keeper.* Tier 1, 1/3.
- Text: Mire. If the enemy this Mires starts the fight with their top Attack (ties count) and attacks this, get 1 gold next turn.
- Golden (2/6): Same, get 2 gold next turn.
- Row: across their biggest hitter, read off the scout. The answer changes every round.
- Why buy: it teaches the tribe in one card. Find their big unit, sit across from it, get paid for reading the scout right. It is a scout test from turn 2 to turn 14.
- Hook: a toad under every bridge, and a toll for every crossing.

**Young Pretender**. *Frog, Claimant to the Lily Throne.* Tier 1, 3/2.
- Text: Snap. After this kills a Mired enemy, get 1 gold next turn.
- Golden (6/4): Snap. After this kills a Mired enemy, get 2 gold next turn.
- Row: back, so it lives to attack. With a mirer in front it tongues the stuck enemy. Without one, it's a fragile 3/2.
- Why buy: the offensive half of the tribe on turn 1, and gold for finishing what the toads started.

**Warty Nan**. *Toad Wet-Nurse.* Tier 1, 1/3.
- Text: Bitter 1. Your units beside this have Bitter 1.
- Golden (2/6): Bitter 2. Your units beside this have Bitter 2.
- Row: between two mirers, in either row. In F2 or F3 she reaches two; at a row end, one.
- Why buy: she makes the units next to her worse to bite. Put her beside the toad their best unit is glued to.

### Tier 6

## Slots, position and scouting

| Card | What changes by slot |
|---|---|
| Bridge Toad, Madame Bufo, Gideon, Jenny | Each bogs the enemy across. Who's across changes with every opponent: read it off the scout. |
| The Prima Donna | F2/F3: three of their front row. F1/F4: two. B2: all three of their back row, pulled into a back-row duel. |
| The Natterjack Chorus | Its slot picks which two neighbours get Mire, so it picks two more enemy slots. |
| Reading order | Two mirers reaching one enemy slot: the earlier in F1-F4, B1-B3 gets it. Put the sturdier one first. |
| Warty Nan, Jenny | Beside the toad that will take the big hits; beside the hitter that answers for it. |
| Vaulting Hopkin | Back row: attacks first with +2. Front row: an ordinary Snap frog. |
| The Peat King | His slot decides where Peatlings rise. Leaving a gap beside him is a choice. |
| Front or back mirer | A front mirer locks their front-row hitter. A back mirer drags their back-row carry into your back row, where Snaps and Hopkins reach it first. |

**The decisions each fight.**
- Which of their units gets glued, and to whom?
- Who do you leave free, because your front row can take it?
- Do you spend your sturdiest toad on their giant, or on the carry behind it?
- Do you bog the Belly Ooze with the most inside it, rather than the biggest one?
- Did you scout right, or did they move?

The default answer, "sturdiest mirer across their biggest", is a rule of thumb. The depth comes from the conflicts: reading order, front or back, who goes free, and refills landing on patches.

**Against the position test's warnings.** Mire is "across from" text, the only kind that measured above the noise floor. "Beside" text is kept to Warty Nan, the Chorus and Jenny, and no card depends on moving in combat.

**Scouting is load-bearing.** Costing assumed the scouted enemy still stands in the same slot 65% of the time [g]. Every conditional gold line rests on that guess, and the bench should measure it with bots that hold their carries still.

## Economy

The bog pays for what it catches. Every source is tied to something the tribe already does, and nothing pays for doing nothing.

| Card | Pays for | Typical [m]/[g] |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge Toad | Reading the scout: the top hitter is across it and bites it | about 0.36 gold a fight |
| Young Pretender | Snapping a Mired enemy to death | about 0.2 a fight |
| The Peat Cutter | A Mired enemy dying, by any hand | about 0.9 a fight |
| The Prince Restored | Buying spells, after a three-fight courtship | 1 gold off a spell a turn |

- **About 1.5 gold a turn on a committed board,** near the Birds' flock target at half the slots.
- **It pays win or lose.** Mired enemies die in lost fights too, about 0.94 per front patch across all fights [m].
- **The export risk is Bridge Toad and the Peat Cutter together.** Both work with nothing else from the tribe. After the costing fixes they make about 1.3 gold a turn from two cheap slots. Watch that pair on the bench.
- **The Prince pays for an action.** His discount needs a spell bought, and he is earned by Mired deaths, not by waiting.

## The builds

### 1. The Wrestling Match (lock and grind)

```
F1 Gideon, Basso    F2 Warty Nan (golden)   F3 The Prima Donna    F4 Spitfire Sal
     B1 The Peat Cutter     B2 Madame Bufo        B3 Tiddy Mun
```

- **Reading order.** Gideon bogs their F1. Warty Nan has no Mire. The Prima Donna bogs F2, F3 and F4. Madame Bufo bogs their B2. Five of their seven are locked before a blow is struck.
- **Turn 1-4.** Bridge Toad across their biggest unit, Warty Nan beside it. Win or lose small, and take the toad's gold.
- **Turn 5-7.** Madame Bufo and the Peat Cutter. Gideon at tier 3. Each turn, read the scout and move each mirer across the unit it should hold.
- **Turn 8+.** The Prima Donna in the middle of the front row. Spitfire Sal burns 2 into every Mired enemy at the bell. Tiddy Mun grows Gideon and the Prima Donna, the walls that hold.
- **Wins by.** Every enemy attacker swinging into Bitter and shrinking, while Gideon hits back and Sal's fireworks finish the small ones.
- **Loses to.** Wide token boards: Bitter on a 1-Attack unit does nothing. Start of Combat shots across (Sergeant-at-Conkers) that kill a mirer before its lock does anything. Nightshade, which halves a wall each hit.

### 2. Tongues Across the Water (snipe the carry)

```
F1 Bridge Toad      F2 The Natterjack Chorus   F3 Old Delver (Hedgerow)   F4 The Peat King
     B1 Vaulting Hopkin     B2 Jenny Greenteeth      B3 His Highness, Allegedly
```

- **Their back row is the target.** Jenny in B2 bogs their B2 carry and gives it -2 Attack. Hopkin attacks before your front row with +2 and Snaps it. When the carry finally bites Jenny, Hopkin (her strongest neighbour) hits it again at once. His Highness Snaps whatever is left.
- **The front.** The Chorus lends Mire to Bridge Toad (who has it already) and to Old Delver, a Hunker wall now holding their F3. The Peat King bogs F4.
- **Turn 1-4.** Young Pretender and Bridge Toad. Sell the Pretender later.
- **Turn 5-8.** Hopkins, a borrowed wall, the Chorus. Jenny at tier 4: from now on, the scout's question is "where is their carry?" His Highness starts counting kisses.
- **Turn 9+.** The Peat King. Peatlings rise in the gap a fallen Hopkin leaves. The Prince arrives in B3 and lays a patch of his own.
- **Wins by.** Removing the one unit their board was built around before it acts.
- **Loses to.** Boards with no single carry, carries that take two kills (a Belly, a shield), and opponents who move their carry each round, so the scout lies.

### 3. The Borrowed Bog (other tribes' walls pick their fights)

```
F1 Old Delver (Hedgerow)   F2 The Natterjack Chorus   F3 Dr Urchin (Hedgerow)   F4 The Prince Restored
     B1 Dustwing Moth (Fairy)    B2 The Natterjack Chorus (golden)    B3 Belladonna Sprite (Fairy)
```

- **Mire on loan.** Two Choruses give Mire to Old Delver, Dr Urchin, Dustwing Moth and Belladonna. Bristle and Hunker walls now pick their attackers, so the pricks land on the carry, not a token. Belladonna in B3 bogs their B3 and Nightshades their F3 and F4, which are already glued to your walls.
- **Turn 1-5.** Hedgerow or Fairies as the base, with Bridge Toad and spells.
- **Turn 6-8.** Two Choruses and His Highness. The borrowed patches kill Mired enemies, which kiss him.
- **Turn 9+.** The Prince's spell discount feeds the Fairy spell engine (Dustwing Moth rings the slot of every spell). Tiddy Mun, if found, grows the borrowed walls, since the maker can be any tribe.
- **Wins by.** Other tribes' walls that now choose their fights, plus a cheaper spell every turn.
- **Loses to.** Tempo while the Choruses are only 3/4s. The B1 Moth with borrowed Mire is fragile: its locked enemy kills it quickly. That's the real cost of lending Mire to everyone beside the Chorus.

## The 5 strongest combos

1. **Prima Donna in B2.** Their whole back row must attack her, past your front row, each shrunk by Bitter 3 on its first bite. Hopkins and Snap frogs pick them off.
2. **Madame Bufo across their giant, Warty Nan beside her.** The giant's first swing kills her, Bitter 3 takes some, and the halving takes half of what's left.
3. **Jenny and Hopkin in the back row.** Their back carry is at -2, Snapped before it swings, and hit again at once when it bites Jenny.
4. **Spitfire Sal and the Prima Donna.** Three or more patches, 2 damage each at the bell, before any attack.
5. **The bog against Oozes.** Every unit coming out of a Belly lands on the patch, is Mired, and is locked onto the same toad. Each death pays the Peat Cutter, counts for Tiddy Mun, kisses His Highness and raises a Peatling, each within its own cap.

## With other tribes

### Hedgerow folk
- **Bog to Hedgerow.** The Natterjack Chorus gives Mire to Old Delver (Hunker) and Dr Urchin or Miss Prickett (Bristle). The prickly wall now picks its attacker.
- **Hedgerow to Bog.** Hand Along buffs land on the Line end, which can be a toad. Spill's Acorns fit a spare-gold turn.
- **Against Hedgerow.** Sergeant-at-Conkers throws 15 at the slot across. If that's your mirer, it dies at the bell and the lock goes with it, though the patch stays and keeps counting as Mired for the payoffs. The Full Parish is too wide for Mire to hold much of it. The Treasury's tall carries are what Bufo and Hopkin are for. Hedgerow summons landing on a patch are Mired.

### Fairies
- **Bog to Fairies.** A Mired enemy locked onto your wall is a fixed target for Belladonna's Nightshade and a Hex from a beside slot.
- **Fairies to Bog.** Toadstool Pixie Rings make walls sturdier. Dustwing Moth and the Prince's discount turn the spell turn into a wall turn.
- **Against Fairies.** A Changeling across your mirer swaps with it at Start of Combat. Your toad now fights for them, so it has changed side and its lock ends. The scout shows the Changeling: put a cheap unit across it, not a toad. Hex across a toad costs little, since toads don't need Attack.

### Oozes
- **Bog to Oozes.** A Chorus beside a Belly Ooze gives it Mire, and a fat pudding is a fine mirer.
- **Oozes to Bog.** Little Dollop's cheap Belly can hold a spare toad for the next mirer slot.
- **Against Oozes.** The best matchup. Each body out of a Belly lands in the bogged slot, is Mired, and is locked onto the same toad. Hasty Pudding swallowing your mirer at the bell ends its lock: the patch is laid, but the lock needs the maker on the board.

### Birds
- **Bog to Birds.** Snap is the closest Bog folk come to Swoop.
- **Birds to Bog.** One Swallow's gold pays for rolling for mirers.
- **Against Birds.** The hardest matchup to plan. The scout shows Nests but not who is away, and a Mire on a Nest bogs a 0/2 Egg. Mother Goose's strikes aren't attacks, so Mire does nothing to them. **Mag Pye** across your mirer takes its text "first of all", before the bell: she lays the patch on your slot, and your toad lays nothing. Swoop is overridden by the lock, which protects a back-row Bog folk carry.

### Hunters
- The two lane tribes ask different questions. Against Bog folk you ask "which of my units does my opponent want to pull?" Against Hunters you ask "which of mine is marked, and do I move it?"
- **Moving answers Bog folk** (a unit moved off a patch is free). **Walling answers Hunters**, and feeds Bog folk. A Mired Hunter swings at the toad, not at its quarry, and a Mired Pack unit can join only an attack on its maker.

### Neutrals and spells
- **Whirligig Beetle** swaps the enemy's rows after the bell. The patches stay, so their back row lands on your front mirers' patches, and front toads catch back-row carries.
- **Turnip Scarecrow** (`stalled`, after the rename) and Mire on the same enemy: a stalled, locked unit is stuck and idle.
- **Ichneumon Wasp:** a locked enemy attacks on a schedule you can see, so "dies after its next attack" can be planned.
- **Wormwood Charm** halves the Attack of the enemy across from a unit, and that unit can be a mirer: a hard answer to a giant.
- **Poacher's Path and Ballooning Spider** on a Mired enemy are overridden by the lock. That makes Bog folk a quiet counter to back-row reach.
- **Stand Behind Me, Elderflower Cordial:** Health on a toad is Health on a wall.

## Weak against

- **Wide boards of small units.** One Mire holds one of seven, and Bitter does nothing to a 1-Attack token.
- **Start of Combat removal aimed across.** Sergeant-at-Conkers, Choirboy Tobias and similar shots kill a mirer before its lock does anything.
- **Halving effects.** Nightshade on a toad wall halves it each hit.
- **Opponents who move their carry** to dodge your last-seen layout, and Birds, whose board changes on its own.
- **Tempo in rounds 3-6.** Walls don't win fights; they make the enemy lose them slowly. The early gold has to carry the tribe through.

## Variations of the signature

1. **First Bite.** "The enemy across's first attack must target this." Much softer, and closer to a one-turn Taunt. The fallback if the lock feels oppressive.
2. **The Sinking.** The patch deepens each time the stuck unit attacks: -1 Attack and -1 Health a swing, no Bitter needed. One keyword instead of two, and it reads well ("it's going under"). A slow kill rather than a lock.
3. **Your own Pools.** Mire as a slot effect on **your** board. Bog folk make Pools in their own slots, and the first enemy to attack a unit in a Pool is locked onto it. A trap rather than a choice: you don't pick the victim, you pick the ground.
4. **True Duel.** The maker must also attack the unit it Mired. Symmetric and readable, but it costs the frogs their targets.

Ruled out: "Deep Mire" (every Mired enemy can be attacked wherever it stands). Making a unit attackable anywhere belongs to the Hunters' quarry.

**For screens and bots, not engine rules:**

- **Scouting is required.** Without the next opponent's last board, every Mire is a guess and the tribe will measure as noise.
- **Tray preview.** When a mirer is dragged, show the scouted enemy across, the mud patch it would lay and who would claim it in reading order. Show Bitter totals from auras and the Prince's kiss count.

Avoid Nests. Opponent bots must hold their carries still between rounds, or every lane card measures as a coin-flip.

## Review calls

Where the critics agreed, their rewrite is applied: Bridge Toad's name and scout test, removing Kiss's gold-for-buffs (it copied Fairies' Shy), a Prince who pays only for an action, the `stalled` rename, and the Leapfrog rule. Costing's numbers are applied throughout. Where they split, or where a ruling forced a choice:

1. **Vaulting Hopkin.** The play critic's "Leapfrog Hopkin" still echoes Battlegrounds' Leapfrogger. The feel critic's "Vaulting Jack" is a fifth Jack. Took a word from each.
2. **His Highness.** Took the play critic's direction (kisses come from the tribe's own action, no recruit intercept on stat gains) and the feel critic's Prince (a frog, paid in spell discounts). The play critic's trigger, his own Snap kills, would take about 11 fights [m], so the kiss uses costing's suggested trigger, any Mired death, once a fight to keep it to three fights. Costing priced the feel critic's pair at -6%. With kisses no longer costing spells, it's about +14% [g], within the gold band. The bench should measure turns to the third kiss.
3. **The Prince Restored.** The feel critic's spell discount over the play critic's "gold per Mired death last fight", which would pay up to 3 gold a turn at tier 4.
4. **Jenny Greenteeth.** Her original exposure ("can be attacked wherever it stands") belongs to Hunters under the lane ruling. Took the play critic's inbound rewrite with costing's single-neighbour limit and 3/5, and the feel critic's hook.
5. **Madame Bufo.** Took the play critic's halving (the feel critic didn't object). Costing found it adds little [m], but it makes the anti-giant claim honest, and the intro now says only what is true.
6. **The Peat King.** Took the feel critic's rewrite (no human remains, no stealing enemy cards, which also stays off the Changeling's ground). The token is a Peatling, a toad of peat, at costing's 5/9. Left "needs room" as is, rather than adding a queued summon nobody has costed.
7. **Tiddy Mun.** Took the play critic's rewrite (the maker grows) with costing's twice a fight and 3/5. The maker can be any tribe, so the Borrowed Bog build gets lasting scaling.
8. **Mire reminder.** The feel critic's phone version.
