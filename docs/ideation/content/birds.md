# Birds

Swallows, geese, thieves, the post, and one robin who stays behind and resents it. **Nobody is ever all home.** Sell a bird and it doesn't leave you: it flies south, you pocket the gold, and a couple of turns later it's back in your hand, ready to do its "When played" text again.

Nothing here has been played or simulated. Stats and numbers are the designer's first guess, for a later reviewer to cost.

---

## The signature: Fly South

*Reminder: when you sell this, it isn't lost. You get the gold, and it comes back to your hand in 2 turns. Its slot holds its Nest until then.*

**Example.** One Swallow, tier 1, 2/1. "Fly South. When played next to a Bird: gain 1 gold."

| Turn | What happens | Fights? |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Buy for 3, play next to a Bird: +1 gold | Yes |
| 6 | Sell: +1 gold. F2 now holds its Nest | No |
| 7 | Away. You can stand something else on F2 | No |
| 8 | Comes home to hand at the start of the turn. Play next to a Bird: +1 gold | Yes |
| 9 | Sell again... | |

So a Swallow pays 2 gold every 3 turns and is missing for 2 fights in every 3. That is the whole bargain: **tempo for gold and repeated "When played" text.**

**Why a player builds around it.**

- Your board becomes a timetable. Instead of asking "who do I stack?", you ask "who's home for this fight, who leaves today, what stands on their Nest, and which turn do they all land together?"
- You can own more than 7 units. A late Bird player has 7 on the board and 3 or 4 in the air, and several cards count them.
- A hoped-for payoff is a set of different Birds, not one big one (see The Seven Whistlers).
- Spells cast on a Bird do nothing for the fights it spends away, and the tribe's payoffs read Nests and names, not stats. That pushes away from the one-huge-unit ending the playtesters described. **It can't fix it alone:** The Great Lump and Mr Redbreast don't fly, so they can still be stacked while stat spells stay this cheap.

**Similar to:** Magic's phasing and "exile, then return". Battlegrounds has nothing that comes back after being sold. Hearthstone players will recognise the replayed Battlecry from bounce effects, but here the delay is the cost.

**How it differs from library entry 60 (also Fly South).** Selling is the send, not a new End of Turn prompt, so no new button and no new verb. It comes back to hand, not the slot, so "When played" fires again and you choose where it lands. The free unit and +2/+2 per trip are gone, because the budget review flagged them and the replayed text is the reward. Library entry 60's name is kept and its variations are below.

### Exact rules

1. **Sold means flown.** Selling a Fly South unit fires "When sold" and pays its usual sell value (Created: 0). It leaves the board and goes away. Its pool copy stays out of the pool.
2. **It must fight first.** A unit played this turn can't fly. Selling it is a plain sale and it's gone. This stops every same-turn loop, and it is also how you get rid of a Bird you no longer want. The sell button says which it will be.
3. **Trip length.** "In 2 turns" means at the start of your turn two turns after the sale, before Start of Turn text. Some cards say 3.
4. **Coming home.** It enters your hand as it left: buffs and golden kept. If your hand is full it waits, and checks again each turn, so it is never lost.
5. **While away** it isn't on the board or in hand. It doesn't fight, can't be aimed at, and doesn't count for triples or the hand limit. Three copies combine the moment the third is home.
6. **Elimination.** Copies away return to the pool (5.3.8), as for units on the board.
7. **Pawn Ticket's sale is final.** A pawned bird is pawned. Tier-value gold on a returning unit would be too good.

### Nest

*Reminder: the slot a bird left. It does nothing itself, but some Birds care.*

- It stays until the bird comes home to hand, and it doesn't move. A different unit may stand on it.
- **A slot holds one Nest.** If a second bird leaves that slot, the new Nest replaces the old one. The older bird still comes home, but it has no Nest. So seven is the limit, and you have to spread them out.
- **This is what makes it a position mechanic.** Recruit moves are free, so most recruit-phase slots aren't commitments. A Nest is: you pick it when you sell, then can't change it for two turns, against opponents you can't see yet.

### Swoop

*Reminder: attacks the enemy back row while there's anyone in it.*

- Works from either row, unlike Ballooning Spider, who must be in the back row. A target is picked at random among the back row. With nobody there, it attacks as usual.

## The units

14 units: 4 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 1 / 2. Six fly (One Swallow, Chiffchaff, Mrs Jay, Jack Daw, Old Tern, Mother Goose). Six read the fliers (Mrs Dunnock, The Great Lump, Mr Redbreast, Mrs Stork, The Gabble Ratchet, The Seven Whistlers). Two (Tom Tit, Mag Pye) don't care and are here for theft.

"Next to" means any neighbour (8.2.3): same row beside it, or in front or behind.

### Tier 1

**One Swallow**. *Swallow, Seasonal Labourer.* Tier 1, 2/1.
- Text: Fly South. When played next to a Bird: gain 1 gold.
- Golden (4/2): Fly South. When played next to a Bird: gain 2 gold.
- Row: wherever a Bird already stands. A lone Swallow gets nothing, F2, F3 and B2 have the most neighbours, and F1 and F4 the fewest.
- Why buy: the tribe's basic money, with a slot question each time it lands.
- Hook: one Swallow doesn't make a summer. Two do, and they would like paying.

**Mrs Dunnock**. *Hedge Sparrow, Foster Mother.* Tier 1, 1/3.
- Text: End of Turn: if this stands on a Nest, that Bird comes home a turn sooner.
- Golden (2/6): Same, two turns sooner.
- Row: on the Nest you want back soonest. Move her freely, so she only needs to be there at End of Turn. Never sooner than next turn.
- Why buy: a cheap body for the empty slot, and a 2-turn trip becomes 1. Bad on Mother Goose, who wants to stay away.
- Hook: raises other people's children without comment. Has never once checked whose they are.

**Chiffchaff**. *Warbler, Town Crier.* Tier 1, 1/2.
- Text: Fly South. When played: give the units in front of this +1/+1.
- Golden (2/4): Fly South. When played: give the units in front of this +2/+2.
- Row: back. A front slot has nothing in front. B2 reaches F2 and F3; B1 reaches F1 and F2.
- Why buy: every return is another aimed buff, spread over two units, not one.
- Hook: has one announcement. It is his name. He'll be back in spring to make it again.

**Tom Tit**. *Blue Tit, Petty Thief.* Tier 1, 2/1.
- Text: Swoop. After this attacks a back-row unit: get 1 gold next turn, once a fight.
- Golden (4/2): Swoop. After this attacks a back-row unit: get 2 gold next turn, once a fight.
- Row: F1, so he swings before he dies. Scout first. If the next opponent has an empty back row, you get no gold this round.
- Why buy: early money that reads the opponent's layout, and an early poke at a carry.
- Hook: has the cream off the vicar's milk every morning. Nobody can reach him for comment.

### Tier 2

**The Great Lump**. *Cuckoo Chick.* Tier 2, 1/4.
- Text: On a Nest, this also has the Attack and Health of the Bird away.
- Golden (2/8): On a Nest, this also has twice the Attack and Health of the Bird away.
- Row: on the Nest of your biggest flier. It shrinks the moment that bird lands, so you plan the turn before.
- Why buy: it stops flying from costing you the fight. The bird is abroad and its stats are still in the slot.
- Hook: much too big for the nest. Everyone is being very polite about it.

**Mr Redbreast**. *Robin, Who Stays.* Tier 2, 2/3.
- Text: Has +1/+1 for each Nest you have.
- Golden (4/6): Has +2/+2 for each Nest you have.
- Row: front, as the wall that holds while everyone's gone. His stats fall on the turn they come home, so read the timetable.
- Why buy: a body that gets better the emptier your board looks, capped at +7/+7.
- Hook: somebody has to stay. He'd like that written down.

**Mrs Jay**. *Jay, Who Plants Oaks.* Tier 2, 2/2.
- Text: Fly South. When sold: get an Acorn.
- Golden (4/4): Fly South. When sold: get two Acorns.
- Row: any. Where she leaves matters only for her Nest.
- Why buy: an Acorn and a gold per trip. Three trips is a Stash. She's the door to Hedgerow's payoffs for a Bird player, and the reverse.
- Hook: buries thousands. Remembers most of them. The rest are woods now.

### Tier 3

**Jack Daw**. *Jackdaw, Collector of Other People's Things.* Tier 3, 2/3.
- Text: Fly South. Start of Combat: take a quarter of what the enemy across has gained on top of its card (this fight). If that took anything, get 1 gold next turn.
- Golden (4/6): Fly South. Start of Combat: take half of what the enemy across has gained on top of its card (this fight). If that took anything, get 2 gold next turn.
- Row: across from the next opponent's biggest buffed unit, in either row. "Gained" counts permanent buffs and Rings, rounded down. Against a 76/106 Alderman with a 2/5 card, he takes 18/25.
- Why buy: he makes money from the very thing the other player spent theirs on.
- Hook: he always brings it back eventually. It's just never yours.

**Old Tern**. *Arctic Tern, Who Has Seen Both Poles.* Tier 3, 2/4.
- Text: Fly South, for 3 turns. When this comes home: Discover a unit of your tier.
- Golden (4/8): Fly South, for 3 turns. When this comes home: Discover two units of your tier.
- Row: any. She's a long trip with a present at the end. It sells for 0, as anything unpaid does.
- Why buy: a free pick every 4 turns, and it's the best use of Mrs Stork's early return.
- Hook: will tell you about it. Will tell you about it again.

### Tier 4

**Mrs Stork**. *White Stork, Postmistress.* Tier 4, 3/6.
- Text: After you spend 6 gold: a Bird away of your choice comes home now.
- Golden (6/12): After you spend 6 gold: two Birds away of your choice come home now.
- Row: back. Her text works in your turn, so in the fight she's just a body worth keeping.
- Why buy: **late gold now buys something.** Every 6 gold you spend brings a bird home to hand, so it can be played this turn. It can't fly again this turn (rule 2), so a long turn ends when the sky is empty.
- Hook: every parcel arrives. Not at the address, necessarily. Sometimes it's a baby.

**Mag Pye**. *Magpie, Receiver of Stolen Goods.* Tier 4, 3/5.
- Text: Start of Combat, first of all: take the text of the enemy across for this fight. It has none, and this has it.
- Golden (6/10): Same, and also the enemy across from the next slot along.
- Row: facing their engine: Belladonna, a Hedge Witch, a Standing Committee. Scout, then move her. Against a textless or recruit-only unit she takes nothing useful.
- Why buy: it's an answer to a card, not to a stat line. Take Hunker and their wall folds. Take Hex and it now hits them.
- Hook: one for sorrow, two for joy, and this one for your Start of Combat text.

### Tier 5

**Mother Goose**. *Greylag, on a Very Fine Gander.* Tier 5, 4/7.
- Text: Fly South. While this is away: Start of Combat: each Bird with a Nest, this one too, deals its Attack to the enemy across from its Nest.
- Golden (8/14): Same, and they each do it twice.
- Row: choose her Nest as you'd choose a shot. She does nothing on the board but stand there, and she's only useful away. Each trip is 2 fights of strikes, and then she must land and stand through a fight before she can leave again.
- Why buy: your absent birds fight, and each strike lands before the first attack, with no hit back. An empty slot across means that strike is wasted.
- Hook: when she wanted to wander, she flew through the air. Nobody has ever asked her to explain the rest.

### Tier 6

**The Gabble Ratchet**. *Wild Geese, Hounds of the Night Sky.* Tier 6, 4/8.
- Text: The first time a death leaves your front row empty: your Birds with Nests land on them for this fight.
- Golden (8/16): Same, and they land with double Attack.
- Row: back, so it's alive when the moment comes. Kill it first and the geese never come. If a Nest has a unit on it, the bird takes the nearest empty slot; with no room, it stays up.
- Why buy: a second board your opponent can't fully see. Back-row Nests land safe and front-row Nests land as the new wall. You choose that shape two turns ahead.
- Hook: heard overhead on winter nights. In the north they say it's hounds. They say not to look.

**The Seven Whistlers**. *Curlews, Six Looking for the Seventh.* Tier 6, 5/5.
- Text: Start of Combat: count your different Birds, on the board or away. At seven, every enemy loses half its Attack and Health. Below seven, only the enemy across does.
- Golden (10/10): Same, but they lose three quarters.
- Row: below seven, across from their biggest. At seven, anywhere safe. Rounded down, and only one Whistlers fires per fight. The loss is gone, not taken.
- Why buy: a late goal that wants breadth. You keep a dull Tom Tit for the count, and away birds count too, so your seven don't all need slots at once.
- Hook: when the six find the seventh, the world ends. In the meantime it ends a bit for whoever's standing opposite.

### Reserves, if the roster grows

- **The Sparrowhawk**, *Gentleman of the Road.* Tier 3, 4/3. "Swoop. After this kills a unit: get 1 gold next turn." The plain back-row raider.
- **A Charm of Goldfinches.** Tier 2, 2/2. "After your first refresh each turn: a Bird in the shop costs 1 less this turn." The concrete version of the old Forage idea, and it doesn't stack across refreshes, so it can't feed a refresh loop.

## Where the economy comes from

The owner asked for several different Bird economy effects with a sentence on how each plays (02h). None of these is the same effect twice:

| Source | How it plays | Better for |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly South itself** | Every bought flier pays 1 gold per trip. Costs you the body for 2 fights. | Anyone willing to lose some tempo |
| **One Swallow** | 1 more on each landing, if placed beside a Bird. | Bird-heavy boards only: useless alone |
| **Tom Tit** | Paid for reaching the back row. Depends on who you face next. | Those who scout |
| **Jack Daw** | Paid by the opponent's stacking. Nothing against a wide board of plain units. | Anyone facing a carry |
| **Mrs Jay** | Acorns, not gold, so it builds up to a Stash. | Hedgerow splash |
| **Old Tern** | Card flow: a free pick, every 4 turns. | Late, when shops are thin |
| **Mrs Stork** | Not income but a sink: 6 gold spent, one bird home. | Late, when gold sits unspent |

Tribe verdict: steady, modest, and slow to start. A Swallow needs a full trip and a Bird beside it to earn back its price, and by then it has fought once. On the owner's warning that economy comps end up best by buying someone else's scaling (02h): the body is what you give up, so a Bird economy player is weak until the flock lands. That needs a bench run.

## Slots, position and scouting

| Card | What the slot decides |
|---|---|
| One Swallow | Next to a Bird or no gold. Move a Bird beside the gap first. |
| Chiffchaff | Its back slot picks the two front units it feeds. Nothing from the front row. |
| Mrs Dunnock, The Great Lump | Only do anything on a Nest, and the Lump's size depends on whose. |
| Mother Goose | Every Nest is a shot aimed two turns ahead. |
| The Gabble Ratchet | Nests are the shape of your second board. Front row walls, back row safe. |
| Jack Daw, Mag Pye, Seven Whistlers | Across-aimed: move every turn to face what you've scouted. |
| Tom Tit | Wants F1, so he attacks while he's alive. |

**Scouting, both ways.** You read their board as usual. They read your Nests too: the scout should show which slots hold Nests but not who's in them. A good opponent moves its carry away from across your Nests, and you can't re-aim until those birds land. Your board is also hard to read: last round's board doesn't show who lands today.

**A turn, round 9.** The next opponent has a 40/60 in B2, Belladonna in B1 and three plain units in front. Chiffchaff has just come home to your hand, and your Swallow is ready to leave.

1. Jack Daw moves to B2, across from the 40/60. Mag Pye moves to B1, so Belladonna's Nightshade is now yours for this fight.
2. Chiffchaff lands in B3, which feeds F3 and F4.
3. Now the real question: where does the Swallow leave from? Mother Goose is away, so its Nest is a shot for this fight and the next. From F2 it hits a weak unit you can see. From F3 it hits a stronger one, but your next opponent may have nothing there.
4. The Lump goes on the new Nest, and it's as big as the Swallow was.

## Three ways to build

### 1. Summer and Winter (rotation, then the world ends)

```
F1 Tom Tit       F2 The Great Lump     F3 Mr Redbreast     F4 One Swallow
      B1 Mrs Dunnock     B2 Chiffchaff       B3 The Seven Whistlers
Away: Mrs Jay, Old Tern, Jack Daw
```

- **Turns 1-3.** Swallow and Chiffchaff beside each other. Sell one on turn 3 only if you're healthy.
- **Turns 4-6.** Mr Redbreast and the Lump hold the front. Two fliers always away. Each landing, ask who's next out.
- **Turns 7-9.** Old Tern and Mrs Stork. Use gold to pull the Tern home for Discovers.
- **Turn 10+.** Whistlers. You probably have five or six different Birds already. Buy the rest with Discover, then every fight opens with the board halved.
- **Wins by.** Being alive when the Whistlers count to seven, with more gold and picks than the lobby.
- **Loses to.** Early tempo. From about round 6 a loss costs 9-15 against 30 Health. Two absent birds in round 6 may simply be too expensive at those numbers.

### 2. The Passage (few on the ground, many in the sky)

```
F1 (Nest)        F2 Old Delver or Pill Bug   F3 Mr Redbreast    F4 (Nest)
      B1 (Nest)        B2 The Gabble Ratchet       B3 Mrs Stork
Away: Mother Goose, Jack Daw x2, Old Tern (Nests on F1, F4, B1, and one under Redbreast)
```

- **Turns 1-5.** As above, with walls from other tribes. Learn to live with gaps.
- **Turns 6-8.** Mrs Stork, then Mother Goose. Sell Goose when the next opponent's board suits her. You can't see the opponent after that, so her second fight's aim is a guess.
- **Turn 9+.** At the bell every Nest strikes. Spell Attack onto fliers before they go: a Cordial in the back row gives +3 Attack, and one strike a fight is not a stack. When the wall falls, the Ratchet lands everyone for a second board.
- **Wins by.** Damage before the first attack, then a second wave. Opponents read a four-unit board and misjudge it.
- **Loses to.** Back-row reach killing the Ratchet, opponents who keep the slots across from your Nests empty, and boards with lots of small units where each strike kills one 1/1.

### 3. Highway Robbery (scam, with a Fairy or Hedgerow body)

```
F1 Tom Tit       F2 Turnip Scarecrow    F3 Fairy or Hedgerow carry    F4 Mag Pye
      B1 Jack Daw        B2 Your carry's feeder       B3 Jack Daw (golden)
```

- Only four or five Birds. Re-place every turn to face what you scouted: Jack Daw across their carry, Pye across their engine, the Scarecrow across their first swinger.
- Fly a Jack Daw only when the next opponent gives him nothing to take. The sale still pays 1 gold, so that trip costs little.
- **Wins by.** Being the best answer in the lobby to the huge-unit endings.
- **Loses to.** Wide boards of plain units: there's nothing to take. Bots currently shuffle about half their units between rounds, which blunts this build (playtest B). That needs fixing for this build to be tested fairly.

## With Hedgerow, Fairies and neutrals

**Hedgerow.**
- Mrs Jay feeds Alderman Dozey, the Treasurer's Larder and Old Mother Mast. A Stash every 9 turns is slow, so two Jays or a Sleeping Partner help.
- Fliers leave gaps. That cuts a Pass, which you can use to aim one, but it's why the Standing Committee ("if all 7 touch") is a poor fit.
- The Sleeping Partner stands on a Nest happily, and the Lump doesn't care about turns in slot. Both want a still slot, which a Nest gives you.

**Fairies.**
- Will-o'-the-Wisp and Spore Sprite love an empty Nest: a Ring on a gap you already have. Nan Toadstool rings a slot, flier or not.
- Rainbow Chaser: selling a bird fades its band. Sell the Chaser first, then the flock.
- Queen of Elfland behind a fresh Nest pays with herself. Check who's behind her before you sell.
- Hob-in-the-Hearth in front of Chiffchaff gets thanked and walks out, paying you 2. That can be the plan.
- Hedge Witch and Mag Pye are the same turn's work: move both to face the same two units.

**Neutrals.**
- Turnip Scarecrow, Pill Bug and Earwig Mother are cheap bodies to stand on Nests. Earwig's Nymphs land in the gaps behind.
- Lamp Moth behind the Lump is a neat +1 Attack on a unit that may already be large.
- Whirligig Beetle swaps enemy rows and moves what your strikes hit, so read the order. Its swap should come after Mag Pye's theft.
- Charcoal Burner competes with Mrs Stork for the same late gold. Pick one.

**Spells and heroes.**
- Spells can't reach a bird away. Cast before the sale.
- Pipkin's buy limit doesn't touch replays: he suits Birds very well. Jill Down-the-Tree goes back for more fliers at tier 1. Hob Mumblecrust's debt fits slowness badly.

## Weak against

- **Tempo.** It's the main one. Absent birds are absent; losses from round 6 are large at current numbers.
- **Wide boards of plain units.** Jack Daw takes nothing, Mag Pye takes nothing, each strike kills one small unit.
- **Back-row reach.** The Ratchet, Mrs Stork and Chiffchaff sit there. Ballooning Spider is enough to spoil the Ratchet.
- **Being read.** Nests are public and fixed. A patient opponent moves its carry off them.
- **Hex** on an empty slot waits, and hits a landing bird.
- **Thin rolls.** Six fliers from a pool shared across the lobby; a second Bird player can starve you of Swallows.

## Where it could loop or run away

| Risk | What stops it, or what to do |
|---|---|
| **Stork loop:** spend gold, bird home, play for gold, sell, repeat | Rule 2: a bird played this turn can't fly. Each bird comes home at most once a turn, so a turn's returns can't exceed the birds away. No same-turn loop exists. |
| **Sell-value tricks** | Pawn Ticket's sale is final (rule 7). Pennywort Snail doesn't fly. Check any later "sells for more" card against Fly South. |
| **Two-shift gold** | A Swallow nets 2 gold per 3 turns, or 2 per 2 with a Dunnock. Eight birds all doing that is real income. If too strong, trips pay gold only on the first sale, or Swallow's gold becomes "next turn". |
| **The Great Lump + a stacked bird** | Spell a Jack Daw to 30/30, send him, and the Lump has his stats while Mother Goose strikes for 30 as well. That's the huge-unit ending by another road. Lever: the Lump reads the away bird's card stats only. |
| **Mr Redbreast, stacked** | Capped at +7/+7 by the Nest limit, but a golden one is +14/+14 and takes spells as anyone does. Lever: Attack only. |
| **Jack Daw golden on a giant** | Takes half of a huge unit's gains for a tier 3 card. Against a 96/94 that moves about 90 stats for the fight. Lever: a quarter on golden too, with the gold doubled. |
| **Tern, Stork and Dunnock together** | A Discover every 2 turns is a lot of free units. Lever: Dunnock doesn't shorten a 3-turn trip. |

**Riskiest card: The Seven Whistlers.** Halving every enemy can decide a fight on its own, and it arrives only when you've built the wide set the tribe asks for, so it's the card most likely to feel like "I lost before the bell". In order of what I'd try:

1. Halve Attack only, at seven.
2. Count only birds that are on the board or have a Nest, so the board has to be shaped for it.
3. Don't count tier 1 Birds.
4. Last resort: halve only the front row.

**If stacking persists.** Variation to try: **Moult.** "It comes home with the stats on its card." Spells on fliers then go to waste entirely. Harsher but very clear.

**Other variations on Fly South** (the first three from library entry 60):

- **Long Haul.** "When sold, choose how long it's away: 1 to 3 turns. It pays 1 gold per turn." Now each sale asks a second question.
- **Escort.** "When sold: the unit in front of this flies with it." Lets a non-Bird travel. Good with a carry that dodges a bad matchup.
- **Winter Visitor.** "Start of your turn: if you have an empty slot, a Created copy of a random Bird flies in for this turn and the fight." Birds as the opposite problem: who's turned up?
- **A spell, Wintering Ground.** "Give a unit Fly South." Lets other tribes try it, and tests whether the mechanic works outside the tribe.

**Already data:** One Swallow's gold (`on_play`, `count` on `neighbours` with `tribe`), Chiffchaff's buff (`on_play`, `in_front`), Mrs Jay's Acorn (`on_sell`, `add_card`), Mrs Stork's count (`on_gold_spent`, `count_amount`, `every: 6`), Old Tern's `discover` once a home trigger exists.

| Need | For | What |

| **Sell intercept** | Fly South | A `fly_south` script: `on_sell` still pays and fires, but the unit moves to the away list if it wasn't played this turn. Needs a `played_this_turn` flag. |
| **`on_home` trigger** | Old Tern | Fires when a unit reaches hand from the away list. |
| **Early return** | Mrs Dunnock, Mrs Stork | An effect `shorten_trip`, and `bring_home` with a player choice. |
| **Nest slot state** | Everything | A temporary slot state, one per slot, new replaces old, gone on return. Today's slot effects last the game. `per: nests` and condition `on_nest` for Mr Redbreast and Mrs Dunnock. |
| **Reads the away bird** | The Great Lump | Aura reading the stats of the bird whose Nest this stands on. |
| **Swoop** | Tom Tit | `back_strike` with a param to work from either row. Condition: the attack's target was in the back row. |
| **Stolen stats** | Jack Daw | Selector for "stats above card" (permanent plus Rings), moved to this unit for the fight. |
| **Stolen text** | Mag Pye | Remove a unit's mechanics and grant them to another, in the fight. A Start of Combat step that runs first. |
| **Strikes from away** | Mother Goose | Fight setup needs each side's away list. Damage from no unit, so no counter and no `on_attack`. |

| **Percentage debuff** | The Seven Whistlers | Halve Attack and current Health for one fight. |
| **Ghosts** | Goose, Ratchet, Whistlers | A ghost's snapshot must hold the away list and Nests, or those cards go silent against ghosts. |

**Screens and bots.**

- **Away tray:** who's up, and on which turn each lands. A timetable is the entire appeal.
- Sell button says "Fly south" or "Sell for good". Nests are drawn on the slot, on the scout panel too.
- `get_state` keys: `away`, `nests`. `next_opponent_last_board` adds Nest slots, not who.
- **Bots** need a policy for when to sell a flier and who stands on Nests. Without it, they sell fliers as plain sales or never, and Birds look weak for the wrong reason. The bench won't measure the tribe until that exists.
