# Oozes

Puddings, jellies and possets with ludicrous high-pitched voices and no sense of what is food. **They swallow things whole, and in the old tales what is swallowed whole comes out alive:** the wolf and the seven kids, the Clay Pot Boy, the old woman who swallowed a fly. An Ooze is see-through, so you can always see the startled hedgehog floating inside it.

Designed without the power budget. Stats and numbers are a first guess for the reviewer to cost.

**The tribe in one paragraph.** Oozes hold more bodies than the board has slots. Your 3-gold unit can go inside a pudding instead of onto the board, wait there doing nothing, and step out alive when the pudding dies. So every Ooze takes two or more kills, and a board of seven slots can fight as twelve bodies. Their gold goes on units rather than spells, their late game is eating the shop's leftovers, and they are the tribe that a single huge unit hates most, because one giant swing only ever kills one pudding.

## What the first playtest asked for, and the answer here

| Playtest said | What Oozes do about it |
|---|---|
| Builds end as one huge unit stacked with spells | Nothing in the tribe grows one unit. A spell on an Ooze doesn't reach what's inside, and a huge Ooze still dies to one big enough hit, with its stats gone. Power is spread across many bodies. Bubble and Squeak even halves a fed Ooze into two. |
| Tall boards run away (Dozey 76/106) | Overkill is wasted on each body in turn, and Hasty Pudding swallows the giant at the bell. A tribe in the lobby that punishes one-big-unit is itself part of the fix. |
| Stat spells outclass units | A cheap unit now has a job a spell can't do: a second life in a full slot. |
| Tier 6 hollow, late shops full of tier 1s, gold unspent | A full board can still buy. Tier 1s are spare lives and triple pieces, Dishwater Nell eats them for gold and a better shop, and Figgy Duff and Cut-and-Come-Again turn them into goldens and tier 6 Discovers. |
| Placement felt real and fresh | Where the Ooze stands decides where its insides come out, gaps steer the rest, and the order you fed it in decides who comes first. |

## The signature: Belly

**Belly N** is a keyword on most Oozes.

*Reminder: holds N units. Drop one in from the shop, your hand or your board. It waits inside, safe and idle. When this dies, it comes out alive. Last in, first out.*

**Example card.** **Little Dollop**, tier 1, 2/2. "Belly 1. Units you buy into this cost 1 gold less."

**How it plays, one fight.** Turn 5. Your Dollop stands in F2 with a Pill Bug inside. An enemy kills the Dollop; the Pill Bug appears in F2, whole. Your front row still has no hole, so your back row stays safe, and the enemy's attack only changed which body stands there.

**Why build around it.**
- More bodies than slots, which Battlegrounds doesn't have. Magnetic loses the separate unit, Reborn brings back only the same one, and death summons make tokens. A Belly brings out a unit you picked, with its text and buffs.
- Every body takes one attack each, so giant Attack is wasted. Against a board of many small hits it's only as good as its bodies.
- Your gold keeps a use when the board is full.

**The price.** A unit inside does nothing until it's out. No text, no attack, no buffs. If your Ooze never dies, you paid 3 gold for nothing that fight. And it only comes out when something has already gone wrong for that slot.

**Why it isn't a stat merge.** The owner's wish was "amalgamating things into them, and eating each other, and combining and splitting" (02h). A Belly does all four, and stats aren't added, so it doesn't feed the tall build. Rule 4.3.5's merge clause doesn't apply: everyone keeps their own card and sale value.

**Good when:** your opponents are tall, or you're tier 4+ with a full board and spare gold. **Bad when:** they go wide with many small attacks, or it's the first few turns and you need Attack on the board.

**Variations to try.**
- **Stomach Acid.** A unit inside permanently gains +1 Attack and loses 1 Health each turn. Leave it in too long and it's gone.
- **Hiccup.** "Start of Combat: the last unit in this comes out in the nearest empty slot." A Belly that fills gaps at the bell.
- **Shared Belly.** Touching Oozes count as one stomach: any of them dying lets out the last unit in, whoever holds it.
- **True Amalgam.** Three Oozes touching at End of Turn combine into one body with all three texts, as a tier 6 one-off.

**Similar to:** the library's Ride (31) and Bud (30), and Magnetic in Battlegrounds, except the units stay themselves and come apart. Anyone who has seen the wolf's belly cut open knows how it reads.

### Exact rules

- **Getting in.** Drag a unit from the shop (you pay its price), from your hand, or from your board, onto an Ooze with room, in the Recruit Phase. This isn't playing it, so "when played" text doesn't fire and no Band is painted. Swallowing from the board leaves its slot empty, which is often the point.
- **Inside.** It has no slot, can't be aimed at or hit, takes no buffs unless a card names what's inside, and its text is off. It doesn't link a Line. Its turn-in-slot count and any Cold Frame timer pause rather than reset, and the log says so.
- **Coming out.** When the Ooze dies, everything inside comes out, last in first. The first takes the Ooze's slot (8.4.1). The rest go to the nearest empty slot (8.4.2), so a same-row gap beats a back-row gap. With no room, that unit is lost for this fight (8.4.5) and is back afterwards, since fights run on a copy.
- **It counts as a summon** for anything that reads summons, and it can attack if it lands in a slot whose turn is coming (9.4.8).
- **Start of Combat text doesn't fire** when a unit comes out late, unless Mr Syllabub says so.
- **Getting out in the Recruit Phase.** You can't just take it out. Sell the Ooze, or lose it to a Teind or a triple, and what's inside goes to your hand. With a full hand, each extra unit goes back to the pool and you gain its sale value.
- **Selling** the Ooze gets you its own sale value only. The units keep theirs.
- **Triples.** Copies inside count (extends 7.3.1). This is deliberate: a Belly is the place to keep two copies while you look for the third.
- **Nesting.** An Ooze inside an Ooze keeps what it holds. It comes out still full and opens when it dies in turn.
- **Hero damage.** Units still inside at the end of a fight don't count (10.1.1 says surviving units in both rows; inside isn't in a row). So Ooze wins hit softly.
- **Golden** doubles Belly as a number (7.3.5).

**Token.** **Drip**, Ooze, 1/1, no text. Created, so it sells for 0.

## The units

14 units, tiers 3/3/2/2/2/2. Two at tier 6 on purpose. Tiers 4-6 change a rule instead of adding lasting stats; Granny's stats last one fight.

### Tier 1

**Little Dollop**. *Custard Ooze.* Tier 1, 2/2.
- Text: Belly 1. Units you buy into this cost 1 gold less.
- Golden (4/4): Belly 2. Units you buy into this cost 2 gold less.
- Row: front, usually. In the back it is cheap storage.
- Why buy: a body now and a discounted second body next turn, in one slot.
- Hook: "ROOM FOR ONE MORE!", in a voice that sets dogs off.

**Wee Wobbly**. *Junket Ooze.* Tier 1, 1/4.
- Text: After this survives a hit, summon a 1/1 Drip in the nearest empty slot, twice a fight.
- Golden (2/8): After this survives a hit, summon two 1/1 Drips in the nearest empty slot, twice a fight.
- Row: front, next to a gap. Its Drips land in the same gaps your Bellies want, so count your room.
- Why buy: an early wall that pays you in bodies for being hit.
- Hook: bits fall off. He apologises for each one, and each bit apologises back.

**Mrs Posset**. *Hot Milk Ooze.* Tier 1, 2/1.
- Text: Belly 1. When this dies, deal 3 damage to the enemy across.
- Golden (4/2): Belly 2. When this dies, deal 6 damage to the enemy across.
- Row: facing the next opponent's weak unit, as scouted. She misses an empty slot.
- Why buy: dies at once on purpose, hurts something you chose, and hands over to what's inside. It gives rounds 1 and 2 a real question.
- Hook: curdles when contradicted, and she is contradicted a lot.

### Tier 2

**Dishwater Nell**. *Scullery Slime.* Tier 2, 2/3.
- Text: Start of your turn: eat the lowest-tier unit in your shop and gain 1 gold. A unit of your tier takes its place.
- Golden (4/6): Start of your turn: eat the two lowest-tier units in your shop and gain 2 gold. Units of your tier take their places.
- Row: back. She has no Belly and doesn't want to die.
- Why buy: plain gold every turn, and the later it gets the better your shop looks. Eaten units go back to the pool, and this isn't a refresh.
- Hook: nobody asked her to do the washing-up. Some of the washing-up was alive.

**Aunt Aspic**. *Savoury Jelly.* Tier 2, 1/4.
- Text: End of Turn: what's inside the Oozes in front of this gains +1/+1.
- Golden (2/8): End of Turn: what's inside the Oozes in front of this gains +2/+2.
- Row: back. B1 feeds the Bellies at F1 and F2, B2 at F2 and F3, B3 at F3 and F4.
- Why buy: the only ordinary way to feed a unit inside, spread over two slots and every unit in them. It grows a reserve, not a carry.
- Hook: preserves anything, including grudges.

**Stone Soup**. *Broth Ooze.* Tier 2, 2/3.
- Text: Belly 1. After you buy a unit into any Belly, units in your shop gain +1/+1 until they leave it.
- Golden (4/6): Belly 2. After you buy a unit into any Belly, units in your shop gain +2/+2 until they leave it.
- Row: either. Reads nothing by slot, so it goes where a Belly opening helps.
- Why buy: the owner's "buffing the shop". A big buying turn makes each next unit bigger, so it rewards spending on units in a run. Refreshing throws the gain away.
- Hook: has a stone in it, and only needs a little something from everyone to make it perfect.

### Tier 3

**Bubble and Squeak**. *Two Oozes, Barely.* Tier 3, 3/4.
- Text: Belly 1. Once a fight, after this survives a hit, it splits: half its Attack and Health go to a copy in the nearest empty slot. The copy's Belly is empty.
- Golden (6/8): Belly 2. The same, twice a fight.
- Row: front, with a same-row gap beside it. No room, no split.
- Why buy: the tribe's split, and the way a player who did cast spells on an Ooze goes wide. A fed 20/20 hit for 4 becomes two 10/8s, swinging twice a pass.
- Hook: they argue in two pitches, and only dogs hear the second.

**The Sixpenny Pie**. *Pudding Ooze, Very Full.* Tier 3, 1/4.
- Text: Belly 3. When this dies, gain 1 gold next turn per unit that comes out.
- Golden (2/8): Belly 6. When this dies, gain 2 gold next turn per unit that comes out.
- Row: front. It only pays if it's opened, and it's a poor wall. It wants a gap or two, since a unit with no room doesn't come out and doesn't pay.
- Why buy: economy that belongs to this strategy. You stuff it with cheap units and get paid when it dies, so losing a slot early is income.
- Hook: when the pie was opened, the voices began to sing, and nobody thanked them for it.

### Tier 4

**Frumenty**. *Porridge Ooze, Cook's Taster.* Tier 4, 3/5.
- Text: Belly 2. This has the text of what's inside it.
- Golden (6/10): Belly 4. This has the text of what's inside it.
- Row: whichever slot the text wants. Text reads from her slot, so Hedge Witch inside her faces what she faces.
- Why buy: this is the amalgam. One slot with three cards' worth of text, and when she dies the units come out and carry on. It turns a Belly from reserve into engine.
- Hook: has tried everything once and has notes.
- Rulings: she doesn't borrow "Belly". "When played" never fires. Shy works, so a buff makes her leave and pay, and what's inside goes to your hand; nobody said thanking a porridge was wise.

**Mr Syllabub**. *Whipped Ooze, Light Sleeper.* Tier 4, 3/6.
- Text: When a unit comes out of one of your Oozes, its Start of Combat text fires.
- Golden (6/12): The same, and it fires twice.
- Row: back, so he lives. Where the unit lands decides what its text aims at, not where he stands.
- Why buy: a second moment for aimed units. A Hedge Witch, Scarecrow or Sergeant-at-Conkers now goes off mid-fight at whatever faces the dead Ooze's slot.
- Hook: wakes up shrieking. So does everyone around him.

### Tier 5

**Hasty Pudding**. *Steamed Ooze.* Tier 5, 2/8.
- Text: Belly 1. Start of Combat: if this has room, swallow the enemy across. It comes out on its own side, in its own slot, when this dies.
- Golden (4/16): Belly 2. Start of Combat: swallow the enemy across and the enemy nearest it, if this has room.
- Row: facing the carry, and that's often the back row. A back-row Pudding across from a back-row carry can't be reached until your front row is gone.
- Why buy: the answer to a 96/94. Their carry is off the board, its text off, until they chew through you. If you win first, it never left the pudding and doesn't count for damage. Keeping the Belly empty is the cost: this is a real choice against your other plans.
- Hook: it was meant to be for afters.

**Figgy Duff**. *Boiled Ooze, Sees Double.* Tier 5, 4/6.
- Text: Belly 1. Two copies make a golden if one of them is inside this.
- Golden (8/12): Belly 2. The same.
- Row: back. It's storage; if it opens in a fight the copy just fights.
- Why buy: bends the triple rule. Late shops full of tier 1s become a golden and a tier 6 Discover for about 6 gold, and you still need a free slot to play the golden and claim the reward.
- Hook: swears there were two of you all along, and after a while there were.

### Tier 6

**Cut-and-Come-Again**. *The Pudding That Is Never Finished.* Tier 6, 6/6.
- Text: Belly 1. Start of your turn: get a plain copy of the last unit inside this (base stats, Created).
- Golden (12/12): Belly 2. Start of your turn: get two plain copies of the last unit inside this.
- Row: back. It's the whole late game, so don't let it open.
- Why buy: you pick what the pudding is made of this game, and that pick arrives in your hand every turn. Copies count for triples, so the original makes a golden after two turns, and then it's empty and you choose again. It wants cards, not stats.
- Hook: "HAVE A SLICE! HAVE ANOTHER! THERE'S PLENTY!" There is, and it won't stop saying so.

**Granny Stir-Up**. *Christmas Pudding, Everybody In.* Tier 6, 5/9.
- Text: Start of Combat: touching Oozes go inside this, however many, and it gains their stats. When this dies, they come out to their own slots, whole, still holding what they hold.
- Golden (10/18): The same, and it gains double their stats.
- Row: F2 or F3 in a run of Oozes. A gap cuts who joins, and only Oozes go in; other tribes in the run stay put.
- Why buy: the amalgam in one swing. One great pudding hits with everyone's Attack, then falls apart into a full board of Bellies that all still have to be killed. No stats stayed on the recruit board, so spells have nowhere to pile up.
- Hook: every member of the family must stir, and she means every member.

## How it interacts with slots, position and scouting

| Card or rule | What changes by slot |
|---|---|
| Any Belly | **Front is depth, back is storage.** A front Ooze opens early and holds the row. A back Ooze rarely opens, so it's where you keep triple pieces. |
| Order inside | Last in, first out. Put the wall in last so it takes the front slot. Put the glass hitter in first so it lands second. |
| Gaps | The second unit out goes to the nearest empty slot, same row first. A gap in F3 pulls it forward. Fill the front and leave only B2 open, and it lands safely behind. |
| Full board | The first unit out always fits, in the dead Ooze's slot. The second needs a gap, so a Belly 2 on a full board is really a Belly 1. |
| Wee Wobbly, Bubble and Squeak | They compete with your Bellies for the same gaps. |
| Aunt Aspic | Feeds only the bellies in front of her. Two front slots at a time, chosen by which back slot she's in. |
| Mrs Posset, Hasty Pudding | Across-aimed, so you re-place them each round for the next opponent. |
| Mr Syllabub's units | Aim at what's across from the dead Ooze's slot. You choose the slot, not the moment. |
| Frumenty | Everything borrowed reads her slot. Move her and you re-aim all of it. |
| Granny Stir-Up | The run decides who joins. A gap you left for a Belly also cuts the family. |

**Scouting.** The scout shows each Ooze as it fought, with what it held. That gives the opponent a real read:
- See a Pudding: put a cheap unit across from it, and your carry in a slot it isn't facing.
- See a Pie full of tier 1s: killing it pays them. Your Ballooning Spider has better targets.
- See Granny Stir-Up: a Changeling across her steals the whole family for one fight. Worth waiting for.
- As an Ooze player, your scout question is "are they tall or wide?" Against tall, stuff more Bellies. Against wide, spend on bodies with Attack.

Bots currently shuffle about half their units between rounds (playtest B), which blunts Pudding and Posset. Bots need to hold their carries still before these cards can be judged.

## Where the economy comes from

A mix, on the owner's word that "economy can just give gold" but it's nice when some of it is "way better for the strategy you're in" (02i).

| Source | Kind | Why it's interesting |
|---|---|---|
| Dishwater Nell | Plain gold each turn | Costs a slot, and doubles as a better shop late. Any tribe can use her. |
| Little Dollop | A discount | Only when you buy into the Belly, so only for this plan. |
| The Sixpenny Pie | Paid on opening | You need to stuff it and lose it. Only an Ooze player does that. |
| Stone Soup | Stats on shop units | Not gold, but buying 3 units in one turn is worth a lot more. |
| Bellies and triples | Goldens for less board space | Hold two copies off the board, so a Triple Reward comes with less tempo lost. |
| Figgy Duff, Cut-and-Come-Again | Cards for little or no gold | Late economy that buys power at tier 6, when gold has no other use. |

Archetypes 1.3 said golden chasing had no owner. Oozes take it.

## The builds

### 1. Second Helpings (depth, then the great pudding)

```
F1 Mrs Posset [Earwig Mother]   F2 Granny Stir-Up   F3 (gap)   F4 Wee Wobbly
     B1 Aunt Aspic      B2 Bubble and Squeak [Pill Bug]      B3 Little Dollop [Turnip Scarecrow]
```

- **Turns 1-3.** Little Dollop, then a cheap neutral into it for 2 gold. Mrs Posset facing something soft. You are fine with low Attack; the caps make early losses cheap.
- **Turns 4-6.** Every spare unit goes into a Belly. Choose the wall as last in. Aunt Aspic behind your two best bellies. Keep one gap.
- **Turns 7-9.** Sixpenny Pie or Frumenty up front if you're short of bodies. Stop buying spells; a 3-gold unit in a Belly is better here.
- **Turn 10+.** Granny Stir-Up in F2. Touching her through the gap at F3: Posset, Aspic, and Bubble and Squeak. At the bell she's 11/18 alone in front with F4.
- **The count.** An enemy has to kill Granny, Posset, the Earwig Mother, two Nymphs, Aspic, Bubble and Squeak and maybe its split, the Pill Bug, then Dollop, the Scarecrow and Wobbly with its Drips. That's 12 or more bodies, from seven slots. A 96/94 kills one a swing.
- **Wins by.** The enemy running out of attacks first.
- **Loses to.** Wide boards with real Attack, which take bodies down faster than you hit back; Cleave and Trample; a Changeling on Granny; and low damage on your wins, so you need to keep winning.

### 2. Waste Not (the shop eater: economy and goldens)

```
F1 Wee Wobbly        F2 Stone Soup [copy]      F3 Little Dollop [copy]     F4 best unit you find
     B1 Dishwater Nell      B2 Figgy Duff [copy]     B3 Cut-and-Come-Again [your pick]
```

- **Turns 1-4.** Little Dollop and Dishwater Nell early, in the back. Take the gold. Tier up sooner than others.
- **Turns 5-8.** Any unit you see two of goes into a Belly. Stone Soup makes a big buying turn worth having. Wobbly and bodies keep you alive; you're behind on board.
- **Turns 9+.** Figgy Duff, then two copies of a late-shop unit make a golden and a Discover. Cut-and-Come-Again holds the best card you can find, or a cheap engine piece of another tribe, and your hand fills up with it.
- **Wins by.** A board of goldens from any tribe by turn 12 or so, with the back row barely touched. This is the Oozes' "use someone else's best units" plan, which the owner warned about (02h). It's paid for with three back slots of low Attack and a poor midgame.
- **Loses to.** Tempo on turns 5 to 9. Back-row reach (a Spider or Whirligig opens your storage in a fight, though it comes back afterwards). A shared pool, when others take your copies.

### 3. Rude Awakening (surprise text, for a scouting player)

```
F1 Mrs Posset [Turnip Scarecrow]    F2 Frumenty [Hedge Witch, Whirligig Beetle]    F3 Little Dollop [Choirboy Tobias]    F4 (gap)
     B1 Mr Syllabub       B2 Hasty Pudding (empty)        B3 Stone Soup [Sergeant-at-Conkers]
```

- **Turns 1-5.** Any Ooze with a Belly, plus Hedge Witch if you see her. Frumenty is the target, so buy aimed units when they turn up.
- **Turns 6-9.** Mr Syllabub in the back. Each Ooze in front now hides a bomb: the Posset's death deals 3, then the Scarecrow lands and the enemy across skips its next attack.
- **Turn 10+.** Hasty Pudding across their carry. Each round: where's the carry, what's across each of my Bellies?
- **A puzzle to enjoy.** Frumenty carries Whirligig's text, so the enemy rows swap at the bell. When she dies Whirligig comes out, Syllabub fires its text, and the rows swap back. Great or awful, depending on what you've planned.
- **Wins by.** Removing the carry, and a mid-fight run of pings, skips and Hexes that is hard to plan around, because the opponent chooses which Ooze dies but not what faces it.
- **Loses to.** Medium boards of six 8/8s, where no one unit matters and pings don't kill. Decoys in the slots you're aiming at.

## How other tribes mix in

**Hedgerow folk.**
- **Beadle Warrenby** makes all summons Spill, and coming out is a summon. So a stuffed Pie on a full board pays Acorns for each unit that has no room. Good mix.
- **The Line.** Inside units don't link it, so swallowing cuts it. Standing Committee wants a full run; Bellies want gaps. They fight, usefully.
- **Dozey and Snug.** A sleeper inside doesn't count down, so it isn't a hiding place for his timer.
- **Mrs Sorrel** behind a Pie: fine, and her Shrewlets take your landing gaps.

**Fairies.**
- **Rings** belong to the slot. A front Ooze on a +4/+4 Ring gives that Ring to everything that comes out in that slot, one after another. This is the strongest easy pairing.
- **Hob-in-the-Hearth** is safe from buffs inside a Belly, so Mothwing can't dismiss him. Aunt Aspic can: she'll thank him right out, and you get 2 gold next turn.
- **Will-o'-the-Wisp** fills empty Rings at the bell, which takes your landing gaps. Choose one.
- **Changeling** across a stuffed Ooze: the Ooze and all its insides fight for them. A risk to accept. Inside Frumenty or with Syllabub, Changeling is one of the best things you can do.
- **Queen of Elfland** tithing an Ooze takes the Ooze's stats only; what's inside goes to your hand. Cheap Teinds.
- **Belladonna** does nothing in the front row, so don't put her in a front Belly unless she'll land in the back.

**Neutrals.**
- **Pill Bug, Earwig Mother** are the classic fillings: cheap, and they work on arrival.
- **Sexton Beetle** pays for every Ooze death: Oozes die a lot. It finally has a home.
- **Lamp Moth** is the back-row helper. **Charcoal Burner** is a poor fit, since this tribe wants its gold spent on units anyway.
- **Larder Spider** with Stone Soup: wrap a shop unit that fattens up, then buy it into a Belly.
- **Spells.** Stat spells still work on an Ooze, but its stats vanish on death and don't reach inside. Spore Cloud, which Rings the slot, is much better here. That's the point.

## What it's weak against

- **Wide boards with real Attack.** Many attacks each cycle chew bodies, and Oozes hit softly until later.
- **Cleave, Trample, splash.** Damage that carries on hurts bodies in a row.
- **Early tempo.** A unit inside isn't attacking. Oozes can drop health early and need the caps to save them.
- **Being ignored.** If the Ooze doesn't die, what's inside was wasted. A back-row Belly usually does nothing in a fight unless the enemy reaches it.
- **Hex.** A Hex stays on the slot, so every unit that comes out there has it. A big Hex across your best Belly is a real answer.
- **Changeling.** Steals the Ooze and what's in it, for one fight.
- **Soft wins.** Insides don't count for damage, so a narrow Ooze win hits lightly. It's a long game, and the current damage (9-15 from round 6) may not give it the time.

## Where it could loop or run away

**Riskiest: Figgy Duff with Cut-and-Come-Again.** Buy a unit into Cut-and-Come-Again (3 gold). Next turn its copy goes into Figgy Duff. Two copies, so a golden and a Discover, and the pudding is empty again. That's a golden and a tier 6 Discover every turn for 3 gold, and golden Cut-and-Come-Again does it faster. The playable golden still needs a free slot to claim the reward, and only the plain copy is made, so it doesn't stack buffs. It's still likely too much.

How I'd adjust, in order:
1. Created copies from Cut-and-Come-Again don't count with Figgy Duff's rule: Figgy needs two Bought copies. Keeps both cards' stories.
2. Figgy Duff works once a turn.
3. Cut-and-Come-Again gives a copy every other turn.

**Hasty Pudding.** It may be too good against the very boards the game currently produces, and it makes a loss feel unfair when their carry never appears. It's fine as a late answer if tall stays common. If it's too strong: the swallowed unit comes back after your Pudding has attacked twice, or it's tier 6. Position is its built-in counter.

**Frumenty.** Golden with Belly 4 runs four cards' text from one slot. Watch for two strong auras in one body. If needed, golden keeps Belly 2 and gains stats only.

**Dishwater Nell.** Two or three Nells rewrite your shop every turn and pay you to do it, and any tribe can run them. That's the owner's warning about economy that travels (02h). If it's the best economy card in the lobby: limit to the lowest-tier unit once a turn across all Nells, or make the gold only for eaten units two tiers below yours.

**Granny Stir-Up.** All the Health counted twice. Fine at tier 6 if fights stay short; if she's too strong, she gains only their Attack.

**Smaller loops, all bounded.**
- **Sell and replay.** Play a unit, swallow it, sell the Ooze, replay it from hand: "when played" fires again, at a net 2 gold each time for a fresh tier 1 Ooze (3 to buy, 1 back). Fine unless a card pays 2 gold or more on play.
- **Stone Soup** stacks within a turn, but it's bounded by gold and Belly room, and refreshing resets it.
- **Hiding a tall unit.** You can stack spells on a carry and then swallow it, to dodge Nightshade and Changeling. It then doesn't fight until the Ooze dies. Acceptable, and Hasty Pudding can't touch it, but it's slow.
- **Nesting** is bounded by your buys. Fights can only hold seven on the board at once.
- **Fight length.** More bodies mean longer fights. The bench should watch this.

## Engine needs beyond `engine.md`

1. **`belly` mechanic.** Per-unit ordered contents (number param, doubles on golden), kept in save and fight copies, plus a nested case. Contents are real units with buffs and paused counters.
2. **Swallow action.** A new port action and a `LegalActions` entry: from shop, hand or board onto an Ooze with room. The buy variant goes through the buy-cost query, with a `cost_into_belly` modifier for Little Dollop.
3. **Coming out in combat.** On death, after the unit's own death text. Uses 8.4.1 and 8.4.2 placement, fires `on_summon`, and goes through Spill. New trigger `on_come_out`, and a `comes_out` count in the event for The Sixpenny Pie's `per`.
