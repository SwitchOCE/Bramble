# Cross-tribe critique, pass 3

9 Nov 2026. The cross-tribe critic for Track C (`docs/ideation/plan-scale-rework.md`, step 1).

**What I read:**
- the owner's notes 09a, 09c, 09d and 08e;
- `fundamentals.md` §1 and §3, the plan, rulebook §8–10 with the BRAMBLE ideation rules and the shared scale mechanisms, and the wording guide;
- the six syntheses (`birds-`, `fairies-`, `bog-`, `hunters-`, `oozes-` and `hedgerow-pass3.md`) and the breakers where they flag cross-tribe points;
- the 13 pool neutrals and 3 tokens in `content/units/` (set `slice`, no tribe), the Track B levers in `content/levers/`, and the spells (for context only);
- `docs/ideation/evidence/reference-curves.md`.

No price list was used, and Godot was not run. I built no new model. Every number below comes from the six syntheses' own models, put on one scale (§2).

## In short

1. **The six tribes feel different.** There are six engines and six verbs, with at least four different clocks. Every tribe has a carry route and a wide route (§1).
2. **The panels sized to two different targets.** Four used the brief's typical board (about 500 at R12). Fairies and Oozes used Battlegrounds' September 2025 *mean* (759) as their typical board. On one scale, the Fairies and Oozes typical lines run at **1.7–2.2×** the others from R12. That would make the tribe pick the snowball, so it must be fixed before the harness runs (§2).
3. **Seven tribe payoffs splash** to other tribes, which is the Lollop lesson: His Highness, Tiddy Mun, Cradle Fetch, Hob, Dr Urchin, Old Isegrim and Old Brock. Each needs its tribe word (§5).
4. **Answers.** The tribes hold four any-row answers against the plan's two or three, so Belladonna goes back to "across". The bigger point is async play. The active player places against a snapshot that can't move, so "across" answers land whenever they're placed, and they should be counted as aimed. Carries, wide boards and answers form a triangle on paper. Answer density tilts it against single carries (§4).
5. **Overlaps:**
   - four tribes grow permanently from enemy stats;
   - six "every third" clocks sit in three tribes;
   - three wordings exist for half damage;
   - Bog's "the Toll" clashes with the Toll-Bard.

   Each has a call (§3).
6. **Two capstones look dominant in their own models:** Granny Stir-Up (97–98% at equal totals) and the Seven Whistlers (42% → 96% against strong boards). One pool rule makes Granny answerable without a nerf: biggest-enemy effects resolve last at Start of Combat (§4.3).
7. **Early game.** Birds have no reward moment before R5 and lose every modelled R6 fight. The Hedgerow Parish and the Fairy Ring also lag. There are two small fixes (§6).
8. **Neutrals** (§8):
   - keep 9 and change 4 whose text is dead at scale;
   - add 2: a Shield holder, and a "When played" doubler in place of Track B's End of Turn doubler;
   - add no neutral any-row answer and no neutral full-board aura;
   - retire the 38 prototype bodies.

## 3. Overlaps and the call on each

### 3.1 Four tribes grow permanently from enemy stats

| Tribe | Card | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Hunters | every Taker, the Glutton, the Lodger | half the quarry, on a kill |
| Bog folk | Soak | what Bitter took from each bite |
| Birds | Jack Daw | a quarter of the enemy across, every fight, no kill needed |
| Oozes | Hasty Pudding | a fifth of the biggest enemy it swallows |

**Call:**
- **Hunters own "a share of what you kill".**
- **Bog keeps Soak.** Its trigger is different (being bitten), and it *is* the answer identity. Both breakers already agree on that split.
- **Jack Daw becomes fight-only.** A permanent no-kill steal is a simpler, easier Take, in a tribe whose growth identity is the homecoming.
  - New line: *"Start of Combat: your biggest Bird steals a quarter of the stats of the enemy across."*
  - He is off line A's board by R12. Their dial shows a fifth costs 3% at R15, but fight-only is untested, so it's a harness check. If Birds fall below the band, keep him as he is.
- **Hasty keeps its gain.** It's one card, it is how an Ooze eats, and it's an answer that pays, which helps the variance budget.

**Systemic note.** With two parasitic tribes in a five-tribe lobby, part of the lobby tracks its leader. Against ghosts and small boards, they starve. Measure Bog and Hunters only in all-line lobbies, never against a fixed field.

### 3.2 "Every third": six clocks in three tribes

  - On the Ring line's schedule it grows twice (R9, R11) instead of three times (R9, R12, R15). That also moves Fairies toward the common target in §2.

## 4. Carries and answers

### 4.1 Is there a rock-paper-scissors?

| Formation | Who builds it | Beats | Loses to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single carry**, 45% or more | Bog court (55–68%), Dozey on R9–R11 (61–76%), the Big Pudding (48% at R12) | small boards; even boards only with holders | even boards of the same total (overkill is wasted; audit 1.6); every answer |
| **Two or three big bodies** | Fairy Ring (two giants), Birds (Lump plus Redbreast), Pudding plus the second stomach, the late Pack | even boards of the same total (audit 1.6: fewer, evenly bigger bodies win) | Cleave; answers that hit the one the others copy |
| **Wide and even**, under 25% each | Parish, Open Season, Pot Luck, the Swamp, the Court, the Flock | single carries; answers, which land on a fifth of the board | Cleave (the Lump, Granny), Sal's Toll, the omen; two or three big bodies |

**So the triangle exists on paper:**
- a single carry with holders beats a wide board;
- a wide board beats a carry without holders;
- answers beat single carries;
- two or three big bodies beat wide boards, and so does Cleave.

No shape dominates by construction. Three pressures can tilt it:

1. **Async play turns "across" into "aimed".**
   - **The mechanism:** the active player fights a recorded snapshot (rulebook 2.1.4) and sees it while placing. The snapshot was placed against someone else and can't move. So an across answer lands whenever the active player places it. The defender's only counters are formation and holders, never a dodge. This is the owner's ghost question from 09a, applied to answers.
   - **The gap:** the plan's field fights use blind layouts for both sides, which understates every across answer.
   - **Fix:** add a "scouted attacker against blind snapshot" orientation to the harness.
2. **Answer density.** After the Belladonna change there are 3 any-row, 5 reach, 9 across and 3 on-contact answers (§4.4).
   - **They multiply.** A carry facing the Whistlers and Jack Daw that then kills a splashed Bufo fights at about 3⁄16 of its Attack.
   - **Expect single carries to weaken first.** Bog's own court is the most exposed.
3. **Two capstones.**

   | Capstone | Win rate with it | Without it | Against |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Granny Stir-Up | 97–98% | 62% | boards of equal total |
   | the Seven Whistlers | 96% | 42% | strong boards |

   Both numbers come from each tribe's own model.

**Nothing in the pool stops proportional stat loss** (halving, Nightshade, a steal) except spreading your stats. Shield only stops damage. That is intended: the answer to scam is distribution, which is the owner's point in 09d.

**Why Fairies:** they keep two other answers, plus the Fetch to copy them, and their answers are places, like their carry.

**Why the other three stay:**
- Hasty is the Oozes' only real answer.
- The Lantern belongs to the answer tribe.
- The hunt is the Hunters' identity.

**Neutrals get no any-row answer.**

### 4.3 Start of Combat order (a pool rule)
- **The problem:** today a random player resolves all their Start of Combat units first (9.3.2). So whether Belladonna, Hasty, the Lantern or Isegrim see the gathered Granny, the Fetch twin, a Changeling swap or a Jenny pull is a coin flip. It happens in exactly the fights that matter.
- **Rule:** effects that pick the biggest enemy resolve after both sides' other Start of Combat effects.
- **What it gains:**
  - Granny's gather is always visible to answers. That answers her without a nerf.
  - The scout and the fight agree.
  - Upsets from answers stop being coin flips, and the plan counts those separately.
- **Ordering inside the last step:** the Lantern before Jenny, so the Bog's own ruling holds.

**Also adopt:** the Oozes' **see-through** rule pool-wide. Biggest-enemy effects see units inside enemy Bellies.

## 5. Splash check (the Lollop lesson)

Lollop was on 70 of 71 Hedgerow wins, and 422 of the 568 final boards that held him belonged to other tribes. The rule in `fundamentals.md`: tribe-only payoffs carry the tribe word. Answers may splash; engines and holders may not.

| Card | Today | How it splashes | New line |

## 9. Pool rules to adopt
1. **Tribe words.** Tribe-only payoffs carry the tribe word; answers may splash (§5).
2. **Start of Combat order.** Effects that pick the biggest enemy resolve after both sides' other Start of Combat effects (§4.3).
3. **See-through.** Biggest-enemy effects see units inside Bellies.
4. **One queue for permanent gains.** Hunters' Takes and Bog's Soak land at the next Start of Turn through one queue, shown as a pending badge in the fight.
5. **Pack joins never cleave.**
6. **Extra triggers never repeat a doubling or move a countdown,** whichever doubler causes them.
7. **One target table.** Every model and the harness use `reference-curves.md` (§2).

## 10. What the harness must test first
1. **All six on one target**, in all-line lobbies: each tribe's field win rate, and its share of boards at twice the median or more. Fairies and Oozes are the risk.
2. **The answer sim**, with a scouted attacker against a blind snapshot:
   - **Measure:** the upset share on R10–R14, aiming for 14–26%. Split it into answers, placement and coin flips.

3. **Tall carries against even boards** of the same total: Bog's court, Dozey on R9–R11, and the Fairies' two giants.
4. **Granny and the Whistlers** in field fights, with the order rule on.
5. **Bog and Hunters** in lobbies only, never against fixed fields. Include their fights against ghosts.
6. **A splash audit:**
   - field lines that may buy any card;
   - each tribe card's share of other tribes' final boards (the Lollop metric: 422 of 568).
7. **The early game:** Birds' and the Parish's R3–R7 win rates, and the Health they've lost by R8.
8. **Unspent gold** by tribe from R10.
