# Fairies

Pixies, sprites, brownies, changelings and moth folk. **They don't fight the board; they change what the board means.** A slot can hold power with nobody on it, a ring can dance down the attack line, an enemy can wake up on your side, and your gaps can be worth more than your units.

Most Fairies work on whatever you already have, so they sit happily beside other tribes. Designed without the power budget; stats and numbers are the designer's first guess for the reviewer to cost.

**Three things Battlegrounds doesn't have:**

- **Rings that move in the fight.** A Ring is power on a slot. With the Reel-Mistress, a Ring walks down the attack line, so one Ring swings with every unit, and each unit it leaves is suddenly fragile.
- **A rainbow across the seven slots.** Each slot is one colour of the rainbow. Play a unit into each slot to paint it, and the pot at the end holds the gold. You must fill your whole board in turn to reach the full pot.
- **A brownie you must not thank.** Give a Shy unit any stats and it walks out, paying you. Half the puzzle is keeping your own buffs off it, unless you want the gold.

### Tier 1

**Toadstool Pixie** — Pixie Trickster, 2/1
- **Text:** When played: Ring this slot +1/+1.
- **Golden:** When played: Ring this slot +2/+2.
- **Row:** Either, wherever you'll want power later. It can move off or be sold; the Ring stays.
- **Why buy:** Cheap permanent stats for your future carry, on a body you can throw away.

**Hob-in-the-Hearth** — Brownie Helper, 1/3
- **Text:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units in front of this +1 Health.
- **Golden:** Shy (4 gold). End of Turn: give the units in front of this +2 Health.
- **Row:** Back. It does nothing in front. B2 reaches F2 and F3; B1 reaches F1 and F2.
- **Why buy:** It feeds two front units every turn, then pays you when you finally thank it.

**Mothwing Pixie** — Moth Pixie, 2/2
- **Text:** End of Turn: give the units behind this +1 Attack.
- **Golden:** End of Turn: give the units behind this +2 Attack.
- **Row:** Front. F2 and F3 feed two units; F1 feeds only B1 and F4 only B3, so a corner keeps it off a Hob.
- **Why buy:** It turns the protected back row into hitters. It is the other half of the owner's puzzle with the Hob.

**Rainbow Chaser** — Leprechaun Cobbler, 1/2
- **Text:** Rainbow. When sold: gain 1 gold per Band (12 for all seven), then wash them.
- **Golden:** Rainbow. When sold: gain 2 gold per Band (24 for all seven), then wash them.
- **Row:** Either; it wants to sit somewhere it isn't blocking an unpainted slot.
- **Why buy:** Every unit you were going to play anyway now builds a pot. When to sell it is the decision.

**Hedge Witch** — Hag of the Hedgerow, 1/2
- **Text:** Hex 1. After you cast a spell, gain Hex +1.
- **Golden:** Hex 2. After you cast a spell, gain Hex +2.
- **Row:** Whichever slot faces their best attacker: re-placed every turn against each opponent.
- **Why buy:** Spells you'd cast anyway shrink their carry. The spell-fuelled plan starts here.

### Tier 2

**Leaf-Gold Sprite** — Sprite Swindler, 2/3
- **Text:** When played: gain 1 Fairy Gold per empty neighbouring slot.
- **Golden:** When played: gain 2 Fairy Gold per empty neighbouring slot.
- **Row:** Whichever slot has the most empty neighbours when you play it (see the table under Fairy Gold).
- **Why buy:** A big tempo turn on an open board, nearly nothing on a full one. Only good if you can spend it now.

**Crossroads Hob** — Hob Dealer, 3/2
- **Text:** Bargain: give a unit +4/+4. In 2 turns: this dies; its neighbours lose 2/2.
- **Golden:** Bargain: give a unit +8/+8. In 2 turns: this dies; its neighbours lose 4/4.
- **Row:** A corner (F1 or F4) by the due turn. It has only two neighbours, so you clear them or leave junk there.
- **Why buy:** A spike on your carry now, if you can keep space around the Hob when the debt falls due.

**Spore Sprite** — Sprite Gardener, 1/3
- **Text:** End of Turn: Ring your empty slot nearest this +1/+1.
- **Golden:** End of Turn: Ring your empty slot nearest this +2/+2.
- **Row:** Next to the gap you want grown. Move it each turn to spread Rings, or keep it still to stack one.
- **Why buy:** It pays you for gaps. Every unit you play onto its Ring gets a jump, but a full board stops the growth.

### Tier 3

**Dustwing Moth** — Moth Spellwright, 2/4
- **Text:** After you cast a spell on a unit, Ring its slot +1/+1.
- **Golden:** After you cast a spell on a unit, Ring its slot +2/+2.
- **Row:** Back, safe. Where it stands doesn't matter; where the spell lands does.
- **Why buy:** Each buff spell pays twice: once on the unit, once on the ground. Cast on a cheap unit, then move your carry in.

**Cradle Fetch** — Fetch Double, 1/1
- **Text:** Start of Combat: become a copy of the unit behind this.
- **Golden:** Start of Combat: become a copy of the unit behind this, then gain +4/+4 this combat.
- **Row:** Front. F1 copies B1, F4 copies B3. F2 and F3 have two units behind and copy the left one.
- **Why buy:** Two of your best unit, one of them hitting from the front. Worthless if nothing stands behind it.

**Cluricaun** — Leprechaun Reveller, 3/4
- **Text:** Rainbow. Your units on Bands have +1 Attack.
- **Golden:** Rainbow. Your units on Bands have +2 Attack.
- **Row:** Either. It paints and rewards; it doesn't cash.
- **Why buy:** It makes washing the Bands cost you fights: hold the rainbow for Attack or cash it for gold. It also evicts any Hob standing on a Band.

### Tier 4

**Changeling** — Changeling Babe, 2/2
- **Text:** Start of Combat: trade places with the enemy across. Each fights for its new side.
- **Golden:** Same, and the unit brought over gains +4/+4 this combat.
- **Row:** Whichever slot faces their carry, front or back. Re-placed against each opponent.
- **Why buy:** It answers a huge unit by making it yours for one fight. A cheap unit put across from it wastes it.

**Will-o'-the-Wisp** — Wisp Lantern-Bearer, 2/5
- **Text:** Start of Combat: summon a 1/1 Wisp into each of your empty Rings.
- **Golden:** Start of Combat: summon a 2/2 Wisp into each of your empty Rings.
- **Row:** Back. It is the reason to leave Rings empty on purpose.
- **Why buy:** A five-unit board becomes seven at the bell. Wisps take the Ring's stats, and every Wisp is one more attack for Nightshade.

### Tier 5

**Reel-Mistress** — Pixie Dancing-Mistress, 3/7
- **Text:** After a unit on a Ring attacks, that Ring dances to your next unit.
- **Golden:** Same, and each Ring gains +1/+1 this combat when it dances.
- **Row:** Back, where she lives long enough to keep the dance going.
- **Why buy:** It turns stationary Rings into a wave: all your Rings gather and every later attacker swings with all of them.

**Belladonna Sprite** — Sprite Poisoner, 3/7
- **Text:** Start of Combat: give Nightshade to enemies across from the slots in front of this.
- **Golden:** Same, and also the enemy across from this.
- **Row:** Back; it does nothing in front. B1 hits enemy F1 and F2, B2 hits F2 and F3, B3 hits F3 and F4.
- **Why buy:** The owner's answer to a very big unit. Place her under the giant, then bring many small attacks.

### Tier 6

**Queen of Elfland** — Fairy Queen, 7/7
- **Text:** Every 3rd End of Turn: destroy units behind this (or this). Others gain their stats.
- **Golden:** Same, but others gain double their stats.
- **Row:** Front. A corner tithes one unit, F2 or F3 tithes two. In the back row she has nothing behind her and pays with herself.
- **Why buy:** Every third turn you choose who feeds the rest. Tithe a token for a nudge, or your 20/20 for a huge jump.

## Keywords

Each has a one-line reminder for the card, the exact rules, and what it could become.

### Ring (+A/+H)

*Reminder: whoever stands on this slot has these stats.*

- A slot effect (rulebook 8.5). A slot holds one Ring; a new one adds to it. It lasts the game. It stays when units move, are sold or die, and it copies into fights.
- It does nothing while the slot is empty, unless a card says so (Will-o'-the-Wisp).
- A stolen unit standing on your Ring has it.
- **Rings leaving (Reel-Mistress only):** when a Ring leaves a unit, the unit's maximum Health falls. If its damage now equals or exceeds its Health, it dies.
- **Variations to try:** a Ring with text instead of stats ("whoever stands here attacks first"; "the first unit to die here comes back with 1 Health"). A Ring that grows when a unit dies on it. A Mound: a front slot whose unit can't be attacked while other front units live.
- **Similar to:** Galaxy's slot buffs and Paint.

### Dance (Reel-Mistress's text)

- After a unit standing on a Ring attacks, that Ring moves to your next living unit in attack order (F1, F2, F3, F4, B1, B2, B3, then back to F1), skipping empty slots.
- Rings that meet add together for the rest of the combat. Fights run on a copy, so Rings return home afterwards.
- If the unit on a Ring dies before attacking, the Ring stays on that slot until a unit standing there attacks. A summon landing there picks it up.
- One dance per attack, however many Mistresses you have.

### Rainbow, Bands and the Pot

*Reminder: while this is on your board, a unit played into an unpainted slot paints a Band.*

- Each slot has a fixed colour: F1 red, F2 orange, F3 yellow, F4 green, B1 blue, B2 indigo, B3 violet.
- Only playing a unit from hand paints. Moves and summons don't. A Rainbow unit paints its own slot as it arrives.
- Bands last until washed, even if the Rainbow unit leaves. The Pot is the count of Bands, shown beside your shop.
- **Bounded:** each Band needs a play, and each play cost a buy, so the Pot can't outrun what you've spent.
- **Variations to try:** a task for the seventh Band ("the last Band can only be painted by a tier 4 or higher"). A Pot that moves: the richest slot changes each turn. The library's pot that grows every turn with a task, as a second leprechaun.
- **Similar to:** Hearthstone's Quests, but here the task is laid out on your board.

### Hex X

*Reminder: Start of Combat: the enemy slot across from this has -X Attack.*

- A hostile slot effect, for this combat. It lands on the slot, not the unit, so whatever stands there later has it too, including death summons and returns. It stays after the Hexer dies. Hexes on one slot add up.
- Attack can't go below 0. A 0-Attack unit still attacks and still takes counter-damage.
- If the slot is empty, the Hex sits and waits.
- **Variations to try:** a Hex that also lowers Health. A Hex that spreads to the neighbours when its unit dies. Nettled: "the first time it is attacked, it loses half its current Health", the library's low-tier Nightshade.
- **Similar to:** the library's Scent variation of Mark. This is the owner's "curses that debuff enemy units" (02h), in place of the frog.

### Nightshade

*Reminder: whenever attacked, this first loses half its current Health, rounded down.*

- As in the library and `nightshade_mark`: Health loss, not damage. It lasts this combat, doesn't stack and never kills by itself (1 Health loses nothing). It halves once per attack, as the first part of the damage exchange.

### Changeling

*Reminder: Start of Combat: swap with the enemy across; each fights for its new side.*

- As in library entry 33. If the slot across is empty, nothing happens. A unit swaps at most once a combat. Each counts as its new side for all effects, and its death counts for that side. A permanent effect on it goes to its owner's recruit board.

### Shy

*Reminder: if this gains stats in the Recruit Phase, it leaves and you gain 2 gold next turn.*

- Any rise in Attack or Health counts: spells, other units' text, standing on a Ring, an aura such as Cluricaun's. The check runs when the rise happens, so walking onto a Ring dismisses it. Tripling doesn't count.
- It returns to the pool. It doesn't trigger "when sold". Gold always arrives next turn, so a mid-turn thank-you can't buy anything now, which rules out same-turn loops.
- Combat buffs don't count.
- **Variations to try:** a Shy unit that leaves behind a Ring of its own stats. A Shy unit that comes back in 2 turns. Shy on a big unit you protect from buffs with position.
- **Similar to:** the folklore brownie: give it clothes and it leaves, as Dobby does. No close match in another game.

### Fairy Gold

*Reminder: gold that turns to leaves at End of Turn if unspent.*

- It is spent before your ordinary gold, and doesn't count as gold you carry into the next turn.

Empty-neighbour slots (rulebook 8.2.3, adjacent plus in front plus behind):

| Slot | Neighbours | Most Fairy Gold |
|---|---|---|
| F1, F4 | 2 | 2 |
| B1, B3 | 3 | 3 |
| F2, F3, B2 | 4 | 4 |

- **Variations to try:** a unit that gains +1 Attack for each Fairy Gold that vanished, to pay the player who over-rolled. Fairy Gold that buys only spells.
- **Note:** the owner was lukewarm ("okay..."), so it is one card, tied to board position so it asks a question.

### Bargain

*Reminder: get this now. The price comes due later at the start of your turn.*

- The debt is yours. It stays if the unit is sold, tripled or dies. It uses the last slot the unit stood on when working out its neighbours.
- The due turn shows on the card and on your hero. Debts resolve at start of turn, before start-of-turn triggers. You can't move between End of Turn and the due moment, so you must plan the turn before.
- Stats lost can't take Attack below 0 or Health below 1.
- **Variations to try:** the library's Toll Brownie (pays you for paying debts), and Open Bargain (choose your own due day).
- **Similar to:** Magic's Pact cards.

### The Teind (Queen of Elfland's text)

- In the ballad of Tam Lin, the fairies pay a tithe to hell every seven years. Count End of Turns from the turn she is played; the counter shows on her. The first falls two turns after the one she is played in.
- Destroy each unit behind her. Your other units permanently gain the destroyed units' combined Attack and Health.
- If nothing stands behind her, she is destroyed instead, and your others gain her stats.
- Shy units leave when it pays out, which is fine: a Teind is the best thank-you there is.

- **Wisp:** 1/1 Fairy token, combat only. Golden Will summons 2/2s.
- **Spells for the spell designer to take or leave,** because Hedge Witch and Dustwing need some in the shop. All are bounded by cost and none refreshes or gains gold without a price.

## Builds that win

### 1. The Long Dance (slots, medium board)

**Final board:**

| Slot | Unit | Ring |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Mothwing Pixie | +4/+4 |
| F2 | Cradle Fetch | +2/+2 |
| F3 | Any tough unit (another tribe's) | +3/+3 |
| F4 | Toadstool Pixie | +2/+2 |
| B1 | Your big carry, fed by the Mothwing | |
| B2 | Reel-Mistress | |
| B3 | Spore Sprite | |

**Turn by turn:**

- **Turns 1-3:** Toadstool Pixie in the slot you want to keep. Mothwing Pixie in front of whatever you'll grow. Leave gaps.
- **Turns 3-6:** Spore Sprite. Each turn, decide: move it to start a second Ring, or keep stacking one. Play each new unit onto the Ring that suits it. Cradle Fetch goes in front of your carry.
- **Turns 7+:** Reel-Mistress. Now each fight's order matters most. The biggest Ring goes first, tough bodies go early, and glass goes last, because a unit that has swung loses the Ring's Health and can die.

**The wave:** without the Mistress, that +11/+11 of Rings adds 11 Attack per pass. With her, the units swing at +4, +6, +9, +11, +11, +11, +11: 63 Attack per pass.

**Loses to:**

- Anything that kills F1 before it attacks, which strands the first Ring.
- Skipped attacks (Mire, Whistle), which stall the chain.
- A Hex across your best late attacker.
- A big counter-attack on early swingers, which die once the Ring moves.

### 2. Hag and Nightshade (spell-fuelled scam)

**Final board:**

| Slot | Unit | Ring |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Empty, so a Wisp lands here | +3/+3 |
| F2 | Hedge Witch, Hex 5 or more, facing their best attacker | |
| F3 | Changeling, facing their carry | |
| F4 | Empty, so a Wisp lands here | +3/+3 |
| B1 | Belladonna Sprite | |
| B2 | Will-o'-the-Wisp | |
| B3 | Dustwing Moth | |

**Turn by turn:**

- **Turns 1-4:** Hedge Witch, and a cheap spell most turns. You'll lose some early fights on small bodies, but each spell keeps growing the Hex.
- **Turns 4-6:** Dustwing Moth. Cast buff spells on a cheap unit in F1 or F4, then move the unit off. The Ring stays, and you've built a Wisp nest.
- **Turns 6-8:** Will-o'-the-Wisp and Changeling. Now you have to read each opponent: where is their carry, and is it worth stealing?
- **Turns 8+:** Belladonna Sprite. Each turn pick: steal the giant with the Changeling, or poison it and swarm it? You rarely want both on the same unit, because once the Changeling has swapped, Belladonna would poison your own babe sitting there.

**What the math does:** a 40/70 alone in their front row, with Nightshade, against 4/4 Wisps: 70 halves to 35, minus 4 is 31; halves to 16, minus 4 is 12; halves to 6, minus 4 is 2. Three small hits take it from 70 to 2. The Witch's Hex lowers its swing meanwhile.

**Loses to:**

- MEDIUM boards of six or seven 8/8s. Halving 8 takes 4, Hex touches one unit in seven, and the Changeling steals an 8/8.
- A cheap decoy in the slot facing your Changeling.
- Boards whose power sits in back-row units, past Belladonna's reach and well defended.

### 3. Hired Help (Fairies feed someone else's carry)

**Final board:**

| Slot | Unit | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Mothwing Pixie | Its only unit behind is B1, so it never touches the Hob |
| F2 | Non-Fairy carry | Fed Health by the Hob |
| F3 | Non-Fairy carry | Fed Health by the Hob |
| F4 | Queen of Elfland | Her only unit behind is B3 |
| B1 | Back-row striker | Fed Attack by the Mothwing |
| B2 | Hob-in-the-Hearth | Feeds F2 and F3, kept away from the Mothwing |
| B3 | Tithe slot | Whatever you've chosen to feed the rest |

**Turn by turn:**

- **Turns 1-4:** Rainbow Chaser, Hob and Mothwing. Paint as you play, and find the placement where the buffs land and the Hob stays home.
- **Turns 4-7:** Sell the Chaser when the Pot pays for a tier-up and a buy on the same turn. Start buying the best units of another tribe. Play Crossroads Hob to give your carry +4/+4, provided you'll have space around the Hob in 2 turns.
- **Turns 8+:** Queen of Elfland. Each third turn you choose: a token in B3, or swap your fed B1 striker into B3 and hand its stats to everyone else. If you tithe, the Hob leaves too, which pays you 2 gold, and you need a new one.

**Loses to:**

- Changeling and Nightshade: this is a tall, front-heavy board.
- Early tempo: End of Turn buffs are slow and the first fights are small.
- The turn after a big tithe, with an empty slot and a new fragile fodder unit.

## The five strongest combos

1. **Reel-Mistress + any spread of Rings.** All Rings gather into one travelling Ring. Four +2/+2 Rings on the front row hit for +2, +4, +6, +8, +8, +8, +8.
2. **Cradle Fetch + Changeling.** Fetch in F1, Changeling in B1. At the bell, Fetch becomes a Changeling and steals their F1, then the real one steals their B1. Two units stolen, which is enough to flip most fights.
3. **Spore Sprite + Will-o'-the-Wisp.** Gaps grow. Three empty Rings at +2/+2 each make three 3/3 Wisps every fight, and a golden Will makes them 4/4. The price is leaving those slots empty in the Recruit Phase.
4. **Will-o'-the-Wisp + Belladonna Sprite.** More attacks mean more halvings, exactly the wide answer to tall boards that the owner described in 02k. Move Belladonna under the giant each turn.
5. **Changeling + a big Ring.** A stolen unit stands on your Ring. Put the Changeling on a +6/+6 Ring and their 14/14 fights for you as a 20/20.

**Smaller interactions to enjoy:**

- Hob behind a Mothwing on purpose: one last buff, then 2 gold next turn.
- Cluricaun with a Hob on a Band: the Hob walks out, so keep it on an unpainted slot or cash the Bands.
- Leaf-Gold Sprite + Rainbow: the Fairy Gold buys the next unit, and that unit paints the next Band.
- Dustwing Moth + Glamour: swap your unit's stats, and the slot keeps a Ring.
- Hedge Witch + Fool's Bargain: the spell raises the Hex, and you can pay for it later.

## Riskiest card to balance: Changeling

**Why:** it gets stronger as the enemy gets bigger, with no upper limit. On turn 10 a 2/2 can take the best unit in the lobby and leave a 2/2 in its place. It's also the worst to lose to, because your own carry kills your board.

**How I'd adjust it, in order (keep the steal if possible):**

1. **Borrowed Child.** After the stolen unit attacks once, the two swap back. It's still a surprise, and still a real hit, but the carry goes home.
2. **Tier 5** with no text change, so it comes when players are ready for it.
3. **Attack limit,** as a last resort: only steals a unit with 8 Attack or less, and the number moves up with the bench. This keeps it out of the turn 10 late game.

**Also watch:**

- **Reel-Mistress** multiplies the whole board's Rings; if it's too strong, Rings keep only their Attack when they dance, or each dances once a fight.
- **Queen of Elfland** can grow fast if players keep tithing their biggest unit; if so, only half of the stats transfer.
- **Few cards read the Fairy tag,** since they mostly bend slots and enemies. If players never commit to the tribe, let the Queen pay Fairies double.

## Engine features beyond the doc

Data already covers Toadstool Pixie (`on_play` plus `slot_effect`), Mothwing Pixie and the Hob's buff (`end_of_turn`, `in_front`, `behind`), and Dustwing Moth if `on_spell_cast` exposes the chosen unit.

| Need | For | What |
|---|---|---|
| Named, stacking slot effects | All Ring cards | One `ring` per slot that adds up, and a way to read it |
| Selectors `nearest_empty` and `empty_with: ring` | Spore Sprite, Will-o'-the-Wisp | Empty-slot targets, using the 8.4.2 order; summon `at` these slots |
| Ring moves in combat, with a Health-drop death check | Reel-Mistress | New script, plus a Health check when a Ring leaves |
| Hostile combat slot effect, with `Attack` floor 0 | Hex | New script from the `mark` precedent, and the 8.5 ruling on who owns an enemy-board effect |
| Recruit stat-gain listener, across all stat layers | Shy | New script that hears auras and slot effects as well as buffs |
| Bands | Rainbow Chaser, Cluricaun, Rainbow's End | Slot flag set on play only; player-level Pot in `get_state`; gold `per` Band; aura over banded slots |
| Tagged gold | Fairy Gold | Separate count, spent first, removed at End of Turn; `per` empty neighbours |
| Debt step | Crossroads Hob, Fool's Bargain | Timed player-level debts, resolved at start of turn before triggers; remembers a slot |
| Combat self-copy | Cradle Fetch | Become a copy mid-queue, including firing the copy's Start of Combat text once; excludes the source's Ring |
| Side switch in combat | Changeling | Combat-side attribute behind every `friendly` and `enemy` selector (library rules review R10) |
| Selector: enemies across from the slots in front of this | Belladonna Sprite | Variation of `nightshade_mark`; replaces highest-Health pick in the existing card |
| `gain_stats_of` effect, with self-fallback | Queen of Elfland | Script. `every` covers the count, and the counter needs showing |
| Next opponent's last board in the Recruit Phase | Hex, Changeling, Belladonna | Without it the across-reading cards are guesses, not decisions |
| Slot badges | Rings, Bands, Hex, debts | Every slot shows what it holds, on the phone board too |

**Rulings these cards assume, for the rulebook:**

- A 0-Attack unit still attacks.
