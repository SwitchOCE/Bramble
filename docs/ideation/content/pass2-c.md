# Birds, tier 3

## Triage

- **Symptom.** In the player check (`birds-player-check.md`), "Tier 3 is the one I'd skip": Tom Tit was weak every time, Old Tern slow, and Jack Daw did nothing. Every Bird game that got going then lost 4–6 fights in a row in rounds 7–12, from 40 to 4 Health in G2.
- **Card (kept).** All three are 2/4s at a 6.5-stat tier, against the Lodger 4/4 that grows. Tom Tit's +1/+1 per Egg adds 2–3 stats. Tern's Discover lands three turns after you fly it. Jack Daw's quarter of the enemy's buffs is about +1/+1 at rounds 5–6, when buffs are small.
- **Journey (kept).** Tier 3 is where a Bird player should pick Homecoming or Hatchery. No T3 card pointed either way, so players bought the Lodger and found the Cuckoo on their own at T4.
- **System, the slump (kept).** From round 7 the payoffs (Cuckoo, Stork, Goose) want Birds away, and an away Bird fights as an Egg at half stats. Half-Health Eggs die early. A dead Egg is one starting survivor fewer, so losses cost more just as the round base climbs to 2–9.
- **Readability (kept).** Mrs Stork's "a Bird must have missed a fight" is only in Details, so in G1 a whole turn's spending did nothing. A Nest shows `{Nest}` but not its Egg's stats.
- **Supply (partly kept).** In G3 no Bird came until shop 9. The early-rounds section's Ask for Kin addresses this. Old Tern's Discover ran out of T5–T6 Birds, and the new Tern drops the Discover.

Fix: each T3 Bird now does one job, in its window, for one route. Tom Tit makes Eggs tough (Hatchery). Old Tern brings the flock home together (Homecoming). Jack Daw is plain tempo that either route can buy.

## Tom Tit, Tier 3 Bird, 3/4 (changed)

**Text:** Your Eggs have their Bird's full Health.
**Golden 6/8:** Your Eggs have their Bird's full stats.
*Details:* Eggs keep half Attack. Redwing's full stats still apply where it reaches. Only the strongest Tom Tit counts.

- **Role and window:** Hatchery's anti-slump wall, rounds 5–10, with one to three Birds away. It doesn't fly: it's the Bird that stays home to mind the eggs.
- **Competitors: the Lodger 4/4, Gideon 2/6.**
  - With two Eggs from 4/5 Birds, each Egg goes from 2/3 to 2/5. That's 7 printed stats plus 4 Health.
  - By round 9, three Eggs from 6/8 Birds gain about 12 Health between them.
  - Eggs that live are starting survivors, so they add damage when you win, and they hold the front while Goose's Nests strike.
  - The Lodger hits harder, but it's one unit. Tom Tit's value grows with every Bird you fly.
- **Journey:** It turns Redbreast's Nests and Redwing's beside-Eggs into a front row that stands up through rounds 7–12, which is the slump window. It also makes the Cuckoo safer to run, since the Cuckoo keeps Birds away longer.
- **Great buy:** round 6, Jay and Chiffchaff both away beside Redbreast. Two 2/2 Eggs become 2/4s.
- **Bad buy:** a Homecoming board whose Birds are home. It's a 3/4.
- **Variations:**
  1. "Your Eggs have +1 Attack and their Bird's full Health." A stronger first knob if reviewers want it.
  2. "Eggs beside this have their Bird's full stats." A second Redwing, aimed at the front row.
- **Rule:** an Egg's Health is the away Bird's current Health instead of half. It's an aura that applies when Eggs are made for the fight. Both readings in pass 1b stay as they are.

## Old Tern, Tier 3 Bird, 3/4 (changed)

**Text:** Fly South. When this comes home: your other Birds away come home too.
**Golden 6/8:** Fly South, for 1 turn. When this comes home: your other Birds away come home too.
*Details:* They land in your hand at Start of Turn. Their When played text fires again when you play them.

- **Role and window:** Homecoming's flock caller, rounds 5–10. Fly it with two or more Birds, then get everyone back together.
- **Competitors: the Lodger 4/4, Brother Wolf 3/4.**
  - On the turn you buy it, it's a 3/4 body. That's level with Wolf and Lutra, and below the Lodger.
  - Its value is the cycle. Flying Chiffchaff, Swallow and Jay with the Tern means all three land on the same turn: +1/+1, 1 gold and an Acorn, replayed.
  - With Mrs Dunnock beside the Tern's Nest, the Tern comes home a turn sooner and pulls the whole flock with it. The trip becomes one turn, so the Egg board lasts one fight, not two.
- **Journey:** It's the shortest answer to the slump, because Birds spend less time as Eggs. It also sets up Stork (Stork brings the Tern home, and the Tern brings the rest), Mother Goose's homecoming buff, and the Seven Whistlers, who count homecomings this turn.
- **Great buy:** round 6, with Swallow, Chiffchaff and Dunnock on board. Fly the Tern, Swallow and Chiffchaff; Dunnock makes it a one-turn trip.
- **Bad buy:** a Hatchery board with the Cuckoo, which wants Birds away. The Tern ends their trips early.
- **Variations:**
  1. "When this comes home: your other Birds away come home too, and gain +1/+1." A bigger payoff, if the cycle proves weak.
  2. "Fly South. Birds that fly from beside this come home with it." Positional, and fits Hatchery's slot puzzles.
- **Rule:** the Tern's homecoming ends every other Bird's trip, including trips the Cuckoo lengthened. Landed Birds can't fly that turn (existing rule 12.1.4). With no hand space, they wait on the doorstep (existing).

## Jack Daw, Tier 3 Bird, 3/3 (changed)

**Text:** Fly South. Start of Combat: steal 2 Attack from the enemy across.
**Golden 6/6:** Fly South. Start of Combat: steal 4 Attack from the enemy across.
*Details:* The steal lasts the fight. With nothing across, nothing happens. An enemy can't go below 0 Attack.

- **Role and window:** a tempo thief for either route, rounds 4–7. It's also a cheap Nest for Redbreast and the Cuckoo.
- **Competitors: the Lodger 4/4, Ballooning Spider 4/3, the Natterjack Chorus 3/4.**
  - At rounds 4–6, enemy Attack is 2–5, so stealing 2 makes the Daw a 5/3 against a crippled enemy across. That's about a 4-Attack swing in the lane.
  - The Lodger grows only after it pounces. The Daw's swing happens at once.
- **Journey:** It's the Bird you buy at tier 3 when the shop has no Tern or Tom Tit. It flies like any other Bird, so it feeds both routes. Later, Mag Pye replaces it as the answer to engines.
- **Great buy:** round 5, against an opponent with one strong front hitter, such as a 6/4 Lodger. Put the Daw across from it.
- **Bad buy:** round 10, when 2 Attack off a 20-Attack carry barely matters.
- **Variations:**
  1. "...steal 1 Attack from each enemy next to the one across." Wider, and weaker against carries.
  2. "...steal 2 Attack and 2 Health." A cleaner stat swing.
- **Note:** pass 1b's buff-steal and gold go. Mag Pye keeps text theft, so the thief splash survives at T4.

## Mrs Stork, Tier 4 Bird, 3/6 (text and rule changed)

**Text:** Every 6 gold you spend: the Bird away longest comes home.
**Golden:** Every 6 gold you spend: the two Birds away longest come home.
*Details:* A tie goes to the leftmost Nest.

- **Why:** the hidden rule existed to stop a Bird flying and coming home in the same turn. Rule 12.1.4 already says a Bird "cannot fly on the turn it lands", so no loop is possible. And the Bird away longest is never the one you flew this turn, unless it's the only Bird away.
- **Swallow check:** each 6 gold spent returns one Bird. A Swallow refunds 1 gold when it's replayed and can't fly again that turn. The gold can't feed itself.
- **Role, window and competitors:** unchanged from pass 1b.

## Nests: show the Egg (rule)

- **Nest reminder:** "While its Bird is away, an Egg with half its stats fights here, unless a unit stands on it." (16 words.)
- **UI rule:** an empty Nest shows its Egg's fight stats and the turns until its Bird is home, for example `Egg 2/5 · home in 1`. These are after Tom Tit, Redwing and tribe bonuses. The scout already shows Egg numbers; this puts the same numbers on your own board.

# Fairies: Shy, +1 T3, +1 T4

## Triage

- **Symptom.** In five reports, Shy is a trap (P2 B; P3 B, C; P4 c; P5 e). Hob-in-the-Hearth walks out when the player didn't mean to thank it.
  - Rings count as buffs, including walking onto one.
  - Cluricaun's aura counts.
  - So does Harvest Supper's tribe bonus, which reaches every Fairy.
  - The round 4 warning dialog made the walkout visible, but not avoidable: a Fairy player wants all three of those effects.
- **Card (kept).** Hob is 1/3 for +1 Health to the units in front, worth about 2 stats a turn, and every Fairy payoff works against it.
- **Readability (kept).** The rule is "any stat rise in the Recruit Phase". That's broad and invisible, and players can't plan around it.
- **Competition (kept).** Fairies are already over-supplied (they rank 1 in 9 of 12 benchmark cells). The new T3 and T4 should add a route, not a ceiling. So they shouldn't be another Ring source (the brief) or another stat pile.

**Fix:** make the thank-you deliberate. Shy units ignore every buff except a spell aimed at them, and that spell sends them off with gold. Then build Shy into a three-card route, which meets the wording guide's three-card keyword bar. In the Hearth route, brownies work for you early, and you choose the turn to thank them, swapping support for gold and Rings at round 7–9.

## Shy (rule changed)

- **Reminder:** "Only a spell aimed at this buffs it. Then it leaves: get 2 gold next turn." (15 words.)
- **Rules:**
  - Rings, auras, tribe bonuses, other units' text, and spells that aren't aimed at it give a Shy unit no stats, and it doesn't leave.
  - A spell aimed at it ("Give a unit…", "Pick a unit", a Ring spell on its slot) sends it off. The spell's other effects still happen: Harvest Supper aimed at Hob still raises the Fairy bonus.
  - Gold arrives next turn, as now. A golden Shy unit pays 4. It returns to the pool, and it isn't "sold".
  - Combat buffs never count (unchanged).
  - The Queen's Teind share isn't a spell, so Shy units don't share in it. A Shy unit the Queen destroys is destroyed, not thanked, so it pays no gold.
- **What the warning dialog does now:** it warns only on an aimed spell, which the player chose. That's no longer a surprise.

## Hob-in-the-Hearth, Tier 2 Fairy, 1/4 (changed)

**Text:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units in front of this +1 Health.
**Golden 2/8:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units in front of this +2 Health.
*Golden details:* Shy pays 4 gold.

- **Role and window:** front-row support and a later cash-out, rounds 2–8, in the back row.
- **Competitors: Moss Ooze 2/4, Old Moth Annis 2/2, Mrs Jay 3/3.**
  - In B2, Hob feeds F2 and F3: +2 Health a turn, permanently.
  - By round 6 that's +8 Health on the front row, plus 2 gold whenever you thank it.
  - Moss Ooze is a bigger body with no growth. Annis is the Attack half of the same puzzle: put Annis in front and Hob behind, and both grow.
- **Journey:** It opens the Hearth route, and it's a fine support piece in any Fairy board now that Rings and Supper don't send it away.
- **Great buy:** round 2 or 3, with two front units you'll keep, such as Chiffchaff and a Wobbly.
- **Bad buy:** round 9, when +2 Health a turn is noise. Thank it instead.
- **Variations:**
  1. "+1/+1 to the unit in front" (only one).
  2. "End of Turn: give the units in front +1 Health, or +2 if they're Fairies."

## Lob-Lie-by-the-Fire, Tier 3 Fairy, 2/4 (new)

*A long, hairy hearth-lubber who does a farm's chores overnight for a bowl of cream.*

**Text:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units beside this +1 Attack.
**Golden 4/8:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units beside this +2 Attack.
*Golden details:* Shy pays 4 gold.

- **Variations:**

  2. "End of Turn: give the unit across your empty slots +1 Attack." Fits the Fairies' gaps identity.

## Silky, Tier 4 Fairy, 3/6 (new)

*A grey lady in rustling silk who keeps a great house in order at night, and leaves when she's paid.*

**Text:** Shy. After a Shy unit leaves: Ring its slot +2/+2.
**Golden 6/12:** Shy. After a Shy unit leaves: Ring its slot +4/+4.
*Details:* This counts too. The Ring cap (+4/+4) applies.

- **Role and window:** the Hearth route's payoff and pivot, rounds 7–10. It turns the early support units into mid-game Rings and gold, and frees their slots.
- **Competitors: Charcoal Burner 4/6, Will-o'-the-Wisp 3/6, the Cuckoo 3/6.**
  - On its own, it's a T4 body, level with Wisp.
  - With Hob and Lob: three Pinches of Dust (3 gold) thank Hob, Lob and Silky herself. Next turn you get 6 gold and three +2/+2 Rings, and three empty slots for bigger buys.

- **Journey:**
  - Its Rings land on emptied slots, so a Wisp fills them at Start of Combat.

# The early rounds (tally 9) and supply (tally 14)

## Triage

- **Symptom.** In six reports (P1 A, B, C; P2 A; P3 A; P4 b), rounds 1–4 are scripted, offer few decisions, and end in 0-damage ties. Supply is thin in four (P3 A, C; P4 c; P5 c): "the shop decides whether a build happens", and no Bird showed until shop 9.
- **System, stakes (kept).**
  - Damage arm B makes the round base 0 on rounds 1–3. A loss there costs only the winner's survivors, 1–3 Health.
  - So early losses barely matter, and greed (tier up on round 2, Rainy Day) is "almost always right" (P2 A).
  - Arm B fixed the snowball (tally 2), so raising early damage isn't the answer.
- **System, choice count (kept).** Tier 1 shows 3 units. On round 1, 3 gold buys exactly one of them. Three options with one obvious best body is a script.
- **Card (kept).** The neutral T1s are walls or late text. Lamp Moth's "units in front +1 Attack" does nothing on round 1.
- **Supply (kept).** Tribes now have 16 cards each, among six tribes plus neutrals. A tier 1 or 2 shop of 3–4 units shows your tribe about once a refresh, and nothing helps a committed player look.

**Fix:** give early fights a stake that isn't damage, give round 1 more to choose from, and give players a paid way to look for their tribe. These are three rules and one card. Each can be trialled alone; the order to try them is at the end.

## Rule E1: Early Spoils

**Rule (BRAMBLE ideation, 10.1.4c):** "Rounds 1–4: win a fight and get 1 gold next turn." Ties and ghost wins pay nothing.

- **Why:** tempo versus greed becomes a real trade. The best body on round 1 is worth a gold on round 2, which is a Pinch of Dust or a cheaper tier up. Greed still works, but it costs something.
- **Snowball check:** at most 4 gold in all, for the whole game. Rainy Day ("get 1 gold, or 3 if you lose") already pays the losing side, so an early loss can be planned.
- **Shown:** "+1 gold: Spoils" on the fight result, and in the next-income line beside the gold.
- **Variation:** spoils equal to your starting survivors, capped at 2.

## Rule E2: Four units at tier 1

**Rule (6.1.1, BRAMBLE ideation):** a tier 1 shop shows **4 units and 1 spell** (was 3 and 1). Other tiers are unchanged.

- **Why:** round 1's choice is then between four units, which is often two tribes and a neutral. It's also one more look per refresh at the tier where everyone shops the most. Tier 2 already shows 4, so there's no cliff.
- **Pool check:** eight players see 8 more T1 copies a round. If the T1 pool runs short, the copies per card go up by one.

## Rule E3: Ask for Kin

**Rule (4.4.8, BRAMBLE ideation):** "Ask for Kin: 2 gold, once a turn. Refresh the shop. At least two of its units share a tribe with a unit on your board."

- **Details:**
  - The two are chosen at random, by the shop's usual tier odds, from the eligible pool copies of those tribes. The rest of the shop is drawn as normal.
  - If there aren't enough eligible copies, it shows what it can.
  - It counts as a paid refresh for the escalating cost, which makes it the only refresh that costs 2.
  - Neutral units don't count as a tribe.
  - It needs a tribe unit on your board.
- **Why:** a committed player can now look for their tribe at a price, so an early lean, such as a round 1 Chiffchaff, pays off more often. The player check's 9-shop drought becomes about two Asks.
- **Discovery:** the other units are still random, and it's once a turn, so the shop still offers pivots.
- **Every turn's decision:** a 1-gold refresh or a 2-gold Ask. Early, 2 gold is a lot.
- **Risk:** strong tribes (Fairies) gain as much as weak ones. Parity work (all tribes at 16, 3/3/3/3/2/2) is what keeps that fair. Measure tribe pick rates with free-choice bots before and after.
- **Variation:** a T1 spell instead (1 gold, "Refresh the shop. At least two units share a tribe with yours."). It appears only when the spell slot rolls it, which makes it rarer and less reliable.

## Lamp Moth, Tier 1 neutral, 1/3 (changed)

**Text:** When played: your next Tier up costs 1 less.
**Golden 2/6:** When played: your next Tier up costs 2 less.
*Details:* It stacks with the per-turn discount, and can't go below 0.

- **Role and window:** the round 1 economy pick against tempo bodies. It's on the triage audit's watch list as a vanilla 1/2 on round 1.
- **The decision:**
  - Round 1: buy a 2/3 body (Chiffchaff, Field Vole), or Lamp Moth 1/3.
  - Round 2: with the Moth, Tier up costs 3, so 4 gold buys a Tier up and a 1-gold spell.
  - Without it, the Tier up takes all 4 gold.
  - With Early Spoils, the 2/3 wins more round 1 fights, worth 1 gold. So the two picks are close, and which is better depends on the shop and the hero.
- **Competitors:** Wee Wobbly 1/4, Pill Bug 1/4, Chiffchaff, Mrs Dunnock 2/3.
- **Great buy:** round 1, with tier 2 Birds or Fairies you want to reach.
- **Bad buy:** from round 3 on, 3 gold to save 1 is a loss.
- **Variations:**
  1. "When played: your next refresh this turn is free." A smaller, same-turn version.
  2. "When sold: your next Tier up costs 1 less." The decision moves to selling it later.

## What rounds 1–4 look like with these

- **R1 (3 gold):** four units. Buy the best body for 1 gold of Spoils, or Lamp Moth for a cheaper tier, or Rainy Day and carry 2.
- **R2 (4 gold, +1 if won):** tier up, or a second body and a Pinch. An Ask (2 gold) only makes sense with a tribe unit already down.
- **R3–4:** placement starts to matter. Ask for Kin decides whether to commit. Spoils still pays, and the round base begins at round 4.

Every turn has at least two plans with a cost. Before, the best line was the same every game.

## Priority and measurement

1. **E1, Early Spoils:** the smallest change and the biggest effect on decisions.
2. **E3, Ask for Kin:** the supply fix. Measure tribe pick and win rates.
3. **E2, four units at tier 1:** cheap. Check the T1 pool.
4. **Lamp Moth:** only alongside E1, since the choice depends on Spoils.

Measure with fresh players, not only bots. The tally item is "few decisions", and bot data can't show that. Ask "what were your options on round 1?" in playtest round 6.

## Calls I made

- **Tom Tit doesn't fly.** The slump fix is a Bird that stays home, and its full-Health aura reads well on Eggs.
- **Old Tern loses its Discover.** It ran the T5–T6 pool dry (player check), and its three-turn trip deepened the slump.
- **Stork's hidden rule is dropped, not explained.** The existing "can't fly on landing" rule already blocks the loop.
- **Shy is fixed by narrowing it, not deleting it.** It's the Fairies' third design pillar (`fairies.md`). The fix keeps the brownie joke, and two new Shy cards meet the three-card keyword bar.
- **Silky makes Rings.** The brief's no-Ring rule was for the T3. Silky's Rings are capped, one-off, and arrive only after giving up a unit.
- **No early damage change.** Arm B's base of 0 on rounds 1–3 stays. Spoils add the stakes in gold instead.
