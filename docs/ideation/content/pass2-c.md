# Birds, tier 3

## Triage

- **Symptom.** The player check: "Tier 3 is the one I'd skip" (Tom Tit weak every time, Old Tern slow, Jack Daw did nothing). Every Bird game that got going then lost 4–6 in a row in rounds 7–12.
- **Card (kept).** Three 2/4s at a 6.5-stat tier against a growing 4/4. Tom Tit's Egg aura adds 2–3 stats; Tern's Discover lands three turns after flying; Jack Daw steals about +1/+1 at rounds 5–6.
- **Journey (kept).** Tier 3 is where a Bird player should lean Homecoming or Hatchery. No T3 pointed either way.
- **System, the slump (kept).** From round 7 the payoffs (Cuckoo, Stork, Goose) want Birds away, and away Birds fight as half-stat Eggs. Eggs die early, so fewer starting survivors, just as the round base climbs.
- **Readability (kept).** Stork's "must have missed a fight" is only in Details; a Nest shows `{Nest}`, not its Egg.
- **Supply (partly).** No Bird until shop 9 once (see Ask for Kin); Tern's Discover ran the T5–T6 pool dry.

Fix: each T3 Bird does one job for one route. Tom Tit makes Eggs tough (Hatchery), Old Tern brings the flock home together (Homecoming), Jack Daw is tempo for either.

## Tom Tit — T3 Bird, 3/4 (changed)

**Text:** Your Eggs have their Bird's full Health.
**Golden 6/8:** Your Eggs have their Bird's full stats.
*Details:* Eggs keep half Attack. Only the strongest Tom Tit counts.

- **Role and window:** Hatchery's anti-slump wall, R5–R10, with 1–3 Birds away. It doesn't fly: it minds the eggs.
- **Competitors:** the Lodger, Gideon. With two Eggs of 4/5 Birds, each goes 2/3 → 2/5: 7 stats plus 4 Health. By R9, three Eggs of 6/8 Birds gain about 12 Health. Eggs that live are starting survivors, so they add damage and hold the front while Goose's Nests strike. The Lodger is one unit; Tom Tit scales with every Bird you fly.
- **Journey:** Redbreast's Nests and Redwing's Eggs become a front row that stands through R7–R12, and the Cuckoo's longer trips stop hurting.
- **Great buy:** R6, Jay and Chiffchaff away beside Redbreast. **Bad buy:** a Homecoming board with its Birds home: a 3/4.
- **Variations:** (1) "+1 Attack and their Bird's full Health" (first knob up). (2) "Eggs beside this have their Bird's full stats" (a second Redwing).
- **Rule:** an aura read when Eggs are made for the fight. Redwing still gives full stats where it reaches.

## Old Tern — T3 Bird, 3/4 (changed)

**Text:** Fly South. When this comes home: your other Birds away come home too.
**Golden 6/8:** Fly South, for 1 turn. When this comes home: your other Birds away come home too.
*Details:* They land in your hand. When played text fires again when you play them.

- **Role and window:** Homecoming's flock caller, R5–R10: fly it with two or more Birds and land them together.
- **Competitors:** the Lodger, Brother Wolf. The turn you buy it, a 3/4 (level with Wolf). Its value is the cycle: fly Chiffchaff, Swallow and Jay with it, and all three land the same turn for +1/+1, 1 gold and an Acorn, replayed. With Dunnock beside its Nest, the Tern comes home a turn sooner and pulls the flock, so the Egg board lasts one fight, not two.
- **Journey:** the shortest answer to the slump, since Birds spend less time as Eggs. Stork brings the Tern home and the Tern brings the rest; Goose's homecoming buff and the Whistlers' homecoming count both grow.
- **Great buy:** R6 with Swallow, Chiffchaff and Dunnock. **Bad buy:** beside the Cuckoo, which wants Birds away; the Tern cuts their trips.
- **Variations:** (1) "…come home too, and gain +1/+1." (2) "Birds that fly from beside this come home with it" (positional).
- **Rule:** ends every other Bird's trip, including Cuckoo-lengthened ones. Landed Birds can't fly that turn (rule 12.1.4); with no hand space they wait on the doorstep (existing).

## Jack Daw — T3 Bird, 3/3 (changed)

**Text:** Fly South. Start of Combat: steal 2 Attack from the enemy across.
**Golden 6/6:** Fly South. Start of Combat: steal 4 Attack from the enemy across.
*Details:* Nothing across, nothing happens.

- **Role and window:** a tempo thief for either route, R4–R7, and a cheap Nest for Redbreast and the Cuckoo.
- **Competitors:** the Lodger, Ballooning Spider, Natterjack Chorus. At R4–R6 enemy Attack is 2–5, so the Daw fights as a 5/3 against a crippled lane: a 4-Attack swing at once, where the Lodger needs a pounce.
- **Journey:** the Bird to buy at tier 3 when neither Tern nor Tom Tit shows. Mag Pye takes over as the late answer.
- **Great buy:** R5 against one strong front hitter. **Bad buy:** R10, when 2 Attack off a 20-Attack carry is noise.
- **Variations:** (1) "steal 1 Attack from each enemy next to the one across." (2) "steal 2 Attack and 2 Health."

## Mrs Stork — T4 Bird, 3/6 (text and rule changed)

**Text:** Every 6 gold you spend: the Bird away longest comes home.
**Golden:** …the two Birds away longest come home.
*Details:* A tie goes to the leftmost Nest.

The hidden rule guarded against a fly-and-return loop, but rule 12.1.4 already says a Bird "cannot fly on the turn it lands", so no loop exists. The Bird away longest is never the one flown this turn unless it's the only one. Swallow check: 6 gold spent returns one Bird, which refunds 1 gold and can't fly again that turn. Role and window as in pass 1b.

## Nests: show the Egg (rule)

- **Reminder:** "While its Bird is away, an Egg with half its stats fights here, unless a unit stands on it." (16 words)
- **UI:** an empty Nest shows its Egg's fight stats and the turns until home, e.g. `Egg 2/5 · home in 1`, after Tom Tit, Redwing and tribe bonus.

# Fairies: Shy, +1 T3, +1 T4

## Triage

- **Symptom.** Shy is a trap in five reports (P2 B, P3 B C, P4 c, P5 e): Hob walks out when the player didn't mean to thank it. Rings count, including stepping onto one; so do Cluricaun's aura and Harvest Supper's tribe bonus. The round 4 warning made walk-outs visible, not avoidable: a Fairy player wants all three.
- **Card (kept).** Hob is a 1/3 giving ~2 stats a turn, and every Fairy payoff works against it.
- **Readability (kept).** "Any stat rise while recruiting" is broad and invisible.
- **Competition (kept).** Fairies rank 1 in 9 of 12 benchmark cells. New cards should add a route, not a ceiling: not another Ring source at T3, not another stat pile.

Fix: make the thank-you deliberate, then build Shy into a three-card route (the keyword bar). **Hearth:** brownies work for you early, and you choose the turn to thank them, trading support for gold and Rings at R7–R9.

## Shy (rule changed)

**Reminder:** "Only a spell aimed at this buffs it. Then it leaves: get 2 gold next turn." (15 words)

- Rings, auras, tribe bonuses, other units' text and unaimed spells give a Shy unit nothing, and it stays.
- A spell aimed at it (including a Ring spell on its slot) sends it off. The spell's other effects still happen: Supper aimed at Hob still raises the Fairy bonus.
- Gold next turn, as now; golden pays 4. It returns to the pool and isn't "sold". Combat buffs never count.
- The Queen's Teind share isn't a spell, so Shy units don't share it; a Shy unit she destroys pays nothing.
- The warning dialog now fires only on an aimed spell, which the player chose.

## Hob-in-the-Hearth — T2 Fairy, 1/4 (changed)

**Text:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units in front of this +1 Health.
**Golden 2/8:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units in front of this +2 Health. *Golden details:* Shy pays 4.

- **Role and window:** front-row support then a cash-out, R2–R8, back row.
- **Competitors:** Moss Ooze 2/4, Old Moth Annis 2/2, Mrs Jay 3/3. In B2 it feeds F2 and F3 +2 Health a turn, permanently: +8 by R6, then 2 gold when thanked. Annis is the Attack half of the same puzzle; together both grow.
- **Journey:** opens Hearth, and now sits safely in any Fairy board with Rings and Supper.
- **Great buy:** R2–R3 with two front units you'll keep. **Bad buy:** R9; thank it instead.
- **Variations:** (1) "+1/+1 to one unit in front." (2) "+1 Health, or +2 to Fairies."

## Lob-Lie-by-the-Fire — T3 Fairy, 2/4 (new)

*A long, hairy hearth-lubber who does the farm's chores overnight for a bowl of cream.*

**Text:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units beside this +1 Attack.
**Golden 4/8:** Shy. End of Turn: give the units beside this +2 Attack. *Golden details:* Shy pays 4.

- **Role and window:** an Attack engine for its neighbours, R4–R8, either row; Hearth's middle piece.
- **Competitors:** the Lodger, Cluricaun, Gideon. Two units gain +1 Attack a turn: +6 Attack by R7. The Lodger grows only itself, after a pounce; Cluricaun needs painted slots. Lob's own body never grows, so it's behind the Lodger in its first fight and ahead from the second turn.
- **Journey:** Hob behind feeds Health, Lob beside a carry feeds Attack; at R7–R9 Silky turns both into Rings and gold.
- **Not a Ring source:** it never makes one.
- **Great buy:** R5 in F2 between two keepers. **Bad buy:** when the slot should hold a growing carry.
- **Variations:** (1) "+1/+1 to the units beside this" (first knob up). (2) "give the unit across your empty slots +1 Attack."

## Silky — T4 Fairy, 3/6 (new)

*A grey lady in rustling silk who keeps a great house in order by night, and leaves once she's paid.*

**Text:** Shy. After a Shy unit leaves: Ring its slot +2/+2.
**Golden 6/12:** Shy. After a Shy unit leaves: Ring its slot +4/+4.
*Details:* This counts too. The Ring cap applies.

- **Role and window:** Hearth's payoff and pivot, R7–R10: early support becomes gold, Rings and free slots.
- **Competitors:** Charcoal Burner, Will-o'-the-Wisp, the Cuckoo. Alone, a 3/6, level with Wisp. With Hob and Lob: three Pinches of Dust (3 gold) thank all three, and next turn you have 6 gold, three +2/+2 Rings and three empty slots. The Burner turns gold into stats; Silky turns a finished early game into gold, Rings and room.
- **Journey:** its Rings sit on emptied slots, which Wisp fills at Start of Combat, and Reel-Mistress, Monarch and the next carry use. Hearth joins the Ring route at T4, and it's why a Fairy player at T4 wants the Shy cards from T2–T3.
- **Great buy:** R7 with Hob and Lob, and a Pinch or Cordial in the shop. **Bad buy:** no Shy units; buy the Burner.
- **Variations:** (1) "After a Shy unit leaves: Discover a Fairy." (2) "After a Shy unit leaves: your Fairies gain +1 Attack."
- **Loop check:** each Shy unit leaves once, needs a spell and pays next turn. Thanking all three gives at most 6 gold and +6/+6 of Rings, once.

**Flagged, not changed:** Cradle Fetch and Belladonna Sprite fail their windows (audit §2) but aren't in Group C's list. With Lob, tier 3 no longer leans on Fetch; its first knob would be 2/3.

# The early rounds (tally 9) and supply (tally 14)

## Triage

- **Symptom.** Six reports (P1 A B C, P2 A, P3 A, P4 b): rounds 1–4 are scripted, with few decisions and 0-damage ties. Four reports (P3 A C, P4 c, P5 c): thin supply, "the shop decides whether a build happens".
- **System, stakes (kept).** Arm B's round base is 0 on R1–R3, so an early loss costs 1–3 Health and greed is "almost always right" (P2 A). Arm B fixed the snowball, so more early damage isn't the answer.
- **System, choice count (kept).** Tier 1 shows 3 units, and 3 gold buys exactly one. Three options with one obvious best is a script.
- **Card (kept).** The neutral T1s are walls or late text; Lamp Moth does nothing on R1.
- **Supply (kept).** With six tribes of 16 plus neutrals, a 3–4 unit shop shows your tribe about once a refresh, and nothing helps a committed player look.

Fix: give early fights a stake that isn't damage, give round 1 more to choose from, and give players a paid way to look for their tribe. Each can be trialled alone.

## E1. Early Spoils (rule, 10.1.4c)

"Rounds 1–4: win a fight and get 1 gold next turn." Ties and ghost wins pay nothing.

- **Why:** tempo against greed becomes a trade. The best R1 body is worth a gold on R2: a Pinch, or a cheaper tier.
- **Snowball check:** at most 4 gold all game. Rainy Day already pays the loser ("1 gold, or 3 if you lose").
- **Shown:** "+1 gold: Spoils" on the fight result and in next income.
- **Variation:** spoils equal to your starting survivors, up to 2.

## E2. Four units at tier 1 (rule, 6.1.1)

A tier 1 shop shows **4 units and 1 spell** (was 3 and 1). Round 1 then usually offers two tribes and a neutral, and the most-shopped tier gets one more look per refresh. Tier 2 already shows 4, so there's no cliff. If the T1 pool runs short, add one copy per T1 card.

## E3. Ask for Kin (rule, 4.4.8)

"Ask for Kin: 2 gold, once a turn. Refresh the shop. At least two of its units share a tribe with a unit on your board."

- **Details:** the two are drawn by the usual tier odds from eligible copies, the rest as normal; if there aren't enough, it shows what it can. It counts as a paid refresh. Neutrals don't count as a tribe; it needs a tribe unit on your board.
- **Why:** an early lean (a round 1 Chiffchaff) pays off more often; the player check's 9-shop drought becomes about two Asks. The other units stay random and it's once a turn, so pivots survive.
- **Each turn's decision:** 1-gold refresh or 2-gold Ask. Early, 2 gold is a lot.
- **Risk:** strong tribes gain as much as weak ones; parity (16 each) keeps that fair. Compare free-choice pick rates before and after.
- **Variation:** a T1 spell with the same text, rarer and less reliable.

## Lamp Moth — T1 neutral, 1/3 (changed)

**Text:** When played: your next Tier up costs 1 less.
**Golden 2/6:** When played: your next Tier up costs 2 less.
*Details:* Stacks with the turn discount; never below 0.

- **Role and window:** round 1's economy pick against tempo bodies (on the audit's watch list as a vanilla 1/2).
- **The decision:** R1, a 2/3 body or the Moth. With the Moth, R2's tier up costs 3, so 4 gold buys tier 2 and a 1-gold spell. Without it, tier 2 takes all 4. The 2/3 wins more R1 fights, worth 1 gold of Spoils, so the two are close, and the shop and hero decide.
- **Competitors:** Wee Wobbly, Pill Bug, Chiffchaff, Mrs Dunnock.
- **Great buy:** R1, with tier 2 cards you want. **Bad buy:** from R3, 3 gold to save 1.
- **Variations:** (1) "your next refresh this turn is free." (2) "When sold: your next Tier up costs 1 less."

## Rounds 1–4 with these

- **R1 (3 gold):** four units. The best body for Spoils, Lamp Moth for a cheaper tier, or Rainy Day and carry 2.
- **R2 (4, +1 if won):** tier up, or a second body and a Pinch; an Ask once a tribe unit is down.
- **R3–R4:** placement starts to matter, Ask decides whether to commit, Spoils still pays, and the base begins on R4.

Every turn now has at least two plans, each with a cost.

**Priority:** E1 first (smallest, biggest effect on decisions), then E3 (supply; measure tribe pick and win rates), then E2 (check the T1 pool), and Lamp Moth only with E1. Measure with fresh players: "few decisions" isn't something bots can report. Ask "what were your options on round 1?" in playtest round 6.

## Calls I made

- **Tom Tit stays home.** The slump fix is a Bird that doesn't fly, and full Health reads clearly on Eggs.
- **Old Tern loses its Discover.** It drained the T5–T6 pool, and its three-turn trip deepened the slump.
- **Stork's hidden rule is dropped, not explained:** "can't fly on landing" already blocks the loop.
- **Shy is narrowed, not deleted.** It's one of the Fairies' three pillars (`fairies.md`); two new Shy cards meet the keyword bar.
- **Silky makes Rings.** The no-Ring line was for the T3; hers are capped, one-off, and cost a unit.
- **No early damage change.** Arm B's 0 base on R1–R3 stays; Spoils add stakes in gold.
