## Stone Soup (T2, Oozes), changed

**2/3.** "Belly 1. After you put a unit into any Belly: this gains +1/+1 permanently. Twice a turn."
*Golden 4/6:* "Belly 2. After you put a unit into any Belly: this gains +2/+2 permanently. Twice a turn."
*Was:* 1/2, "Belly 1. After you buy into any Belly: your shop's units gain +1/+1 this turn."
*Details:* a unit put in from the shop, your hand or your board counts. Its own Belly counts.

- **Role and window:** the T2 enabler, R2–R6. It turns the tribe's core action, putting a unit in a Belly, into a front-row body that grows, so early Bellies are worth filling for tempo and not only as storage (pt4 b: "Belly felt like storage").
- **Competitors:** Moss Ooze 2/4 and Bufo 1/4 are flat; the Lodger-style growers are a tier up. Bought on R2 and fed itself on R3, it's a 3/4 for 2 cards; with a Dollop or Wobbly fed too it's 4/5 by R4 and 5/6 by R5, bigger than any T2 or T3 body except the Lodger. On an empty board it's still a 2/3, the T2 floor (Sable, Sorrel).
- **Journey:** makes every T1 Ooze better (Dollop's discount buys a Soup stack, Wobbly's spare unit grows the Soup) and makes Aspic, Elder Ooze and Granny more attractive, because their bodies are already in front. Second Helpings.
- **Great buy:** R3 with Little Dollop in hand and 3 gold: Soup, then a T1 into the Dollop for 2 gold, and the Soup is 3/4 the same turn.
- **Bad buy:** R7+ on a board with no spare Bellies and no gold to fill them: a 2/3.
- **Variations:** (1) grows Health only (+0/+2), a wall rather than a tempo body; (2) "After you put a unit into this:" only, Belly 2, so it's its own stomach and stops rewarding other Oozes.
- **Engine:** the existing buy-into-Belly hook, widened from "buy" to "put" (shop, hand, board), with a twice-a-turn counter.

## The Sixpenny Pie (T3, Oozes), changed

**2/5.** "Belly 2. When this dies: get 1 gold next turn for each unit that comes out."
*Golden 4/10:* "Belly 4. When this dies: get 1 gold next turn for each unit that comes out."
*Was:* 1/2, Belly 3, "if 2+ come out, get 1 gold next turn. Three times in all."
*Details:* a unit lost for lack of room doesn't count.

- **Role and window:** Waste Not's mid-game wall and income, R4–R8. Front row: it holds the slot, dies, and pays up to 2 gold.
- **Competitors:** Gideon 2/6 and Dr Urchin 2/5 are the T3 walls; Sexton Beetle 2/4 pays up to 2 gold on friendly deaths. The Pie matches the walls' body, pays like the Beetle, and puts two more bodies into the fight from one slot. Against the Lodger 4/4 it trades Attack for three bodies and gold.
- **Journey:** what you do with the copies Dollop and Nell bring: two copies inside are a triple waiting for Figgy Duff, and they earn while they wait. Waste Not.
- **Great buy:** R5, full board, a spare copy of your T1 in hand: the Pie replaces a 1/2 in front, swallows the copy, and the triple is one buy away.
- **Bad buy:** back row with nothing inside: a 2/5 that never dies, so never pays.
- **Variations:** (1) Belly 3, 1 gold only if all three come out, for a bigger stuffing puzzle; (2) "When this dies: the units that come out gain +1/+1 permanently", turning it into a Second Helpings oven instead of income.
- **Engine:** data only (the per-unit count already exists; the cap and "three times in all" go).

## Figgy Duff (T4, Oozes), moved from T6

**3/5.** "Belly 1. A unit inside this goes golden with just one more copy. Once a turn."
*Golden 6/10:* "Belly 2. A unit inside this goes golden with just one more copy. Twice a turn."
*Was:* T6 3/4, same effect ("Once a turn: a bought unit inside goes golden with just one more bought copy").
*Details:* both copies must be bought; Cut-and-Come-Again's base copies never count. The golden comes out to your hand.

- **Role and window:** Waste Not's golden engine, R6–R10. At T6 it came too late (P3 B): the Triple Reward Discover was capped at T6 and the board was set. At T4, a golden gives a **T5 Discover** when T5 cards are what you're reaching for.
- **Competitors:** Charcoal Burner 4/6 (gold into stats behind it), Madame Ermeline 3/6 (cheaper tier ups), Will-o'-the-Wisp 3/6. Figgy beats them for a player with a copy already stored (in a Pie, a Dollop or Figgy itself): every pair the shop shows becomes golden plus a Discover for 3 gold.
- **Journey:** the reason to keep the copies the T1–T3 Waste Not cards collect, and the step that hands Cut-and-Come-Again its best target at T6. Waste Not.
- **Great buy:** R7 with two Little Dollop copies in Bellies and Nell swapping your shop's lowest unit: the next Dollop in the shop is a golden and a T5 Discover.
- **Bad buy:** no copies stored and gold to spare only for one unit a turn: a 3/5.
- **Variations:** (1) no turn limit but Belly 1 sealed after it makes a golden until it dies in a fight; (2) "goes golden with just one more copy, bought or from your hand", widening it to Discovers.
- **Engine:** data only (tier and stats). Costing: if a T4 golden every turn is too much, "Once every other turn" is the fallback, not a smaller body.

## Elder Ooze (T5, Oozes), moved from T6 and changed

**4/8.** "Belly 1. Units coming out of your Oozes gain +2/+2."

- **Bad buy:** an Ooze splash with one Belly: a 4/8 vanilla, still a par T5 body.
- **Variations:** (1) +1/+1 and "they attack at once" (an out-of-turn swing); (2) Belly 2 and +1/+1, as its own stomach.
- **Engine:** simpler than now (no per-fight count).

# Bog folk

## The rule change: mirers wait

**Problem** (pt5 a): "My 1/3 Toad attacked Wick, died, and freed the 8/10 Annis it had mired." A mirer whose turn comes before its target's spends its life on someone else, and the lock it paid for never happens. Every Mire card depends on the target swinging into the mirer first: Bufo's halving, Gideon's counter, Jenny's strike, Bridge Toad's gold, Bitter, Tiddy Mun.

**Rule (rulebook Mire, BRAMBLE ideation).** When a unit with Mire comes to make its first attack of the fight, and the enemy on its patch is alive and hasn't attacked yet, it waits: it passes, and the next unit in its side's order attacks instead. Once a fight; after that it attacks as usual.
- A mirer across an empty slot, or whose patch holds no enemy yet, doesn't wait.
- **Two mirers facing each other** both wait once, then attack as usual, so the duel still ends.
- A side whose every unit is waiting passes that turn; the other side attacks. Nothing loops, because each unit waits once.
- A target that can't attack (0 Attack, Stalled by the Scarecrow) doesn't hold the mirer past its one wait.
- Borrowed Mire waits too: a non-Bog unit beside the Natterjack Chorus gives up its first attack. That's the cost of borrowing.

**New reminder (15 words):** "Start of Combat: the enemy across must attack this while this lives. That enemy swings first." *Details:* "This waits once a fight for it."

**Why a rule and not cards:** the failure is in the keyword; eight cards would need the same line. Taunt-like units in Battlegrounds don't need it because they don't choose their attacker; Mire does.

**Engine:** an attack-order skip in combat (`mire_wait`, once per unit a fight), and a log line, "Bufo waits for the Boar."

## Signor Ranocchio, Tenor (T4, Bog folk), new

**3/5.** "Snap. After this attacks a Mired enemy: gain +1/+1 permanently. Twice a fight."
*Golden 6/10:* "Snap. After this attacks a Mired enemy: gain +2/+2 permanently. Twice a fight."

- **Role and window:** Tongues' grower, R6–R12: the first Snap unit that gets bigger, so the route has a body that keeps up after T4. The swamp opera's tenor: the high note always lands on whoever is stuck.
- **Competitors:** His Highness 3/5 (a slow transformation), Madame Ermeline 3/6, and Charcoal Burner 4/6, which grows the back row but needs gold above 5. Ranocchio grows +2/+2 a fight from round one with any mirer on the board, and hits the enemy's best stuck unit, past Taunt and back-row protection. On a Bog board it beats the Burner from R6–R9, when gold above 5 is rare.
- **Journey:** what the T1–T3 mirers and Sal set up; at T6 the Lantern Man turns the same patches into Attack. Tongues; Wrestling players take it as their Snap.
- **Great buy:** R6 with Bufo and Peat Cutter: by R9 it's 9/11 and hitting their carry twice a fight.
- **Bad buy:** no mirers: a 3/5 that attacks at random and never grows.
- **Variations:** (1) +1 Attack only, three times a fight, a glass Snapper for Bitter boards; (2) "After a Snap unit of yours attacks a Mired enemy: gain +1/+1", a back-row choir master that grows from Pretender and Hopkin.
- **Engine:** data only (Snap, an after-attack check on the target's Mired status, permanent stats).

## Tiddy Mun (T5, Bog folk), changed

**4/6.** "After a Mired enemy attacks one of your units: that unit gains +1/+1 permanently. Three times a fight."
*Golden 8/12:* "After a Mired enemy attacks one of your units: that unit gains +2/+2 permanently. Three times a fight."
*Was:* 3/5, "After a Mired enemy dies: the unit that Mired it gains +1/+1 permanently. Twice a fight."
*Details:* the unit gains it even if the hit kills it.

- **Role and window:** the Wrestling Match's T5 payoff, R8–R13: it grows the walls that take the hits. The fen spirit who raises the water: what stands in the bog gets taller.
- **Why the old one failed:** "after a Mired enemy dies" needs the walls to kill something big, which 1–3 Attack walls rarely do (audit §2: +2/+2 a fight at best, the lowest T5 body). Being hit is what Bog walls do every fight, and with the wait rule the hits come.
- **Competitors:** Grizel 5/6 (permanent Attack on kills), Lady Belladonna 4/8, the Prima Donna 2/8. Tiddy Mun has a par T5 body and gives +3/+3 a fight that stays, spread over the walls, so Bufo, Gideon and the Prima Donna survive the R10+ carries they now die to.
- **Journey:** makes every T1–T4 mirer a long-term investment, and gives the Peat King walls that live long enough to raise Peatlings. Wrestling Match.
- **Great buy:** R8 with Prima Donna miring three slots: three hits on walls, +3/+3 that fight.
- **Bad buy:** one mirer, or a Snap board whose mirers are tokens: a 4/6.
- **Variations:** (1) the hit unit gains +0/+2 five times a fight, pure walls; (2) "the unit that Mired it gains +1 Attack", feeding Gideon's counter and Bitter.
- **Engine:** small: an after-attacked trigger filtered on a Mired attacker, with a fight counter.

## The Lantern Man (T6, Bog folk), new

**4/8.** "Snap. Has +3 Attack for each Mired enemy."
*Golden 8/16:* "Snap. Has +6 Attack for each Mired enemy."
*Details:* counted as it changes: a Mired enemy dying lowers it, a unit landing on a patch raises it.

- **Role and window:** the second T6, the finisher for Tongues and the reason a Wrestling board wants a reach unit, R10+. The light over the fen that comes for whoever is stuck: from the back row it hits the biggest Mired enemy, wherever it stands.
- **Competitors:** Peat King 5/9, Whirligig 6/8, Queen of Elfland 8/8. With four Mired enemies (a Prima Donna and two mirers) it's 16/8, and its swings go at the enemy carry, not a token. Its Attack is fight-only and falls as the bog empties, so it ends fights rather than becoming a 40/40 pile (tally 3).
- **Journey:** cashes in every patch the tribe lays, from Bridge Toad up. Tongues; Wrestling players with three or more mirers take it over a second Peat King piece.
- **Great buy:** R11, Prima Donna and Jenny on board, Ranocchio and Sal in the back: the Lantern Man hits a Bitter-shrunk carry for 13–16 twice.
- **Bad buy:** one mirer, against a wide board: a 7/8 at T6.
- **Variations:** (1) +2/+2 per Mired enemy, a sturdier back-row unit; (2) "Start of Combat: deal 2 damage to each Mired enemy for each Mired enemy", a Spitfire Sal finisher with no attack.
- **Engine:** small: a live stat aura counting Mired enemies.

## Engine work

- **Rule:** `mire_wait` (attack-order skip, once per unit a fight), with tests for two facing mirers, an all-waiting side and a Stalled target; the reminder and log text.
- **Small:** Stone Soup's "put into any Belly" hook and turn counter; Tiddy Mun's after-attacked-by-Mired trigger; the Lantern Man's live count of Mired enemies.
- **Data only:** Pie, Figgy Duff, Elder Ooze (simpler than now), Signor Ranocchio, Cut-and-Come-Again if its body changes.
- **Parity:** both tribes at 16, 3/3/3/3/2/2.
- **Bots:** value the Soup's growth when choosing to swallow; place mirers across the biggest enemy (they already try).

## Calls for the orchestrator

- **Figgy Duff at T4** is the riskiest number: a golden with a T5 Discover for one extra copy, once a turn. Fallback: once every other turn.
- **Ranocchio and Tiddy Mun** both grow permanently; together about +5/+5 a fight on a Bog board. If the costing reviewer sees a pile, Ranocchio goes to once a fight first.
- **The Mire wait** makes borrowed Mire cost a carry its first attack. If Borrowed Bog disappears in play, exempt non-Bog folk units.
- **Peat Cutter** stays as pass 1 left it; with Bufo, it's the T2 Mire. Re-check it after the wait rule, which makes its gold fire more often.
