# Great beasts (on file)

**Status: on file, not to be built in this step** (tribes 3, 7 Nov 2026). Bog folk and Hunters were picked; why is in `../evidence/design-review-3.md`. Below is the designer's draft, unchanged. Stats were set by feel and are costed lightly in `../evidence/costing-3.md`. Nothing has been played.

## Rulings to apply when it's built

Both critics agreed on these. They go in before the first build.

- **Lanes.** Great beasts own **the column**: a charge goes through the slot across into whatever stands behind it. They don't force attacks onto themselves (Bog folk own that) and don't mark units (Hunters own that). So Big Ursula loses her forced attack: "Hide 8. When her Hide breaks, deal the damage it stopped to the unit that hit her."
- **Rename Charge to Headlong.** Hearthstone's Charge means "can attack at once." Headlong should read the beast's *own* board too, so it doesn't blur with Hunters' Quarry: "Its first attack from a front slot goes at the enemy across, or through the gap behind it, and tramples. Needs an empty slot behind this." The designer's Run-up variation is the starting point.
- **Fewer keywords.** Keep Headlong, Trample, Hide and Room. Rut, Trophy and Trodden become printed text on one or two cards each. Leave Hunker to Hedgerow: Mother Wisent becomes "Hide 5. Start of Combat: units next to this gain Hide 5."
- **Old Grazer must pay for an action, not for waiting** (owner on Fast, 02h): "Hide 4. Once a turn, after you sell a unit next to this, gain 1 gold."
- **Trample is close to Battlegrounds' retired Overkill line, and Hide is a cousin of Divine Shield.** Headlong's own-board condition and Room are what make the tribe its own, so test them first.
- **Costing outliers:** Old Tusker, the Bison Elders, the Last Aurochs and Big Ursula; the fixes are in `costing-3.md`. The play critic's other rewrites (Old Steinbock, Old Tusker, Bison Elders) are in the review record.

Bears, wild boar, wisent, red deer stags, elk, ibex and the last aurochs. **The old kings of the forest: few, huge and slow, and they charge.** A Great beast doesn't pick a target. It picks a lane, lowers its head and goes straight at whatever stands across from it. If nothing stands there, it runs through the gap into the back row. If it kills what it hits, the rest of the charge carries on into the units behind.

Designed without the power budget. Stats and numbers are a first guess, for a later reviewer to cost.

**The tribe in one paragraph.** Great beasts are a tribe about lanes. Each front slot faces one enemy slot, and a beast's first attack goes down that lane: across, through the gap if there is one, and on into the units behind. So the question every turn is "which lane does each beast take against this opponent?", and the answer changes with every board you scout. Your beasts are hard to kill (Hide, the owner's Shell idea, caps the first hit; Hunker halves), they need room to graze and to grow, and they earn a little from the things they already do: charging into open ground, keeping room, and killing. Their payoffs reward several beasts charging in several lanes, not one beast charging very hard, and their best defensive cards are the lobby's answer to the single huge unit.

**How the flavour connects to the play** (the owner's question, 02h: "I don't really see how it connects to the gameplay"):

| A great beast in the wild | On the board |
|---|---|
| Charges straight ahead and doesn't turn | **Charge** aims its first attack at the slot across, not a random enemy |
| Breaks a line where it is thin | An empty enemy slot lets the charge **run through the gap** to the back row |
| Bowls through what it hits | **Trample:** damage past the kill carries on to the units behind |
| Thick hide, a boar's shoulder shield | **Hide N:** the first hit deals at most N |
| Needs space; a herd grazes open ground | **Room:** some beasts grow or earn for the empty slots next to them |
| Stags lock antlers in the rut | **Rut:** a Start of Combat duel with the enemy across |
| A head of antlers counts its seasons | **Trophy:** a beast that kills sells for more |
| A herd builds momentum | Charges speed up the beasts behind them in the attack order |

## What the first playtest asked for, and the answer here

| Playtest said | What Great beasts do about it |
|---|---|
| Builds end as one huge unit stacked with spells | Charge and its trample happen once a combat, on one lane the defender can see coming. The payoffs (Old Tusker, The Last Aurochs, The Bison Elders) count charges, so four chargers beat one. No Great beast grows itself permanently. Hide and Big Ursula are the lobby's answer to someone else's giant: Hide caps a 90-Attack hit at 5, and Ursula sends the rest back. |
| Tall boards run away (Dozey 76/106) | Trample and the run through the gap reach the back-row carry, and Old Steinbock drags it into the front. A tall carry in B1 or B3 is never safe from a corner charge. |
| Stat spells outclass units | Hide is worth nothing until the unit's Health is above N, so a Health spell on a Hide beast is a different decision from one on a carry. Charge rewards Attack spread across four lanes rather than piled on one. |
| Placement felt real and fresh | Every Great beast's value depends on the enemy slot across from it, which is the only kind of text the position test found creating a real, changing decision (+6.5 to +7.1 adaptive, against a floor of about +5). |
| The searching placer barely beats a row hint | There is no fixed best slot for a charger. The best lane is wherever the next opponent is thin, or has a carry behind something you can kill, and that moves every round. |

## The signature: Charge

*Reminder: its first attack goes at the enemy across. If that slot is empty, it runs through the gap at the unit behind. If it kills, the rest tramples on.*

**Example card.** **The Yearling**, tier 1, 2/1. "Charge. Trophy 1."

**How it plays, one fight.** Turn 4. You scouted your next opponent: a 3/2 in F1, a 2/6 wall in F2, a Hedge Witch in B1 and F3 empty. The Yearling in F1 would hit their 3/2 and trade. In F2 it would bounce off the wall. In F3, the slot across is empty, so it runs through the gap at the unit behind F3: their B2 is empty and B3 holds nothing, so it attacks normally. You put it in F1 instead and buy a Truffle for F3 next turn, when they will probably have filled B2.

**The geometry.** The board is a brick (8.2): each enemy front slot has one or two back slots behind it.

| Your charger in | Charges at | If that slot is empty, runs through to | Its trample lands on |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | enemy F1 | enemy B1 | enemy B1 (all of it) |
| F2 | enemy F2 | enemy B1 or B2 | enemy B1 and B2 (split) |
| F3 | enemy F3 | enemy B2 or B3 | enemy B2 and B3 (split) |
| F4 | enemy F4 | enemy B3 | enemy B3 (all of it) |

So corner lanes focus the trample and the middle lanes spread it. A carry in B1 or B3 can be reached by a full, unsplit trample from a corner. A carry in B2 can only ever take half. That gives the defender a real placement question too: B2 is the safe slot against Great beasts, and every tribe wants B2 for something.

**Why build around it.**
- A decision every turn that a rule of thumb can't make. The charger's best slot depends on the opponent's board, which the scouting rule now shows.
- Reach without Swoop. Battlegrounds' back row is reached by Cleave-like splash or random damage. Here the reach comes from gaps and kills, so it grows during the fight: as enemy front units die, their slots open, and a charger that attacks later finds a lane through.
- It answers WIDE. A killing charge on a token tramples into the units behind it.

**The price.** One charge a combat. After it, a beast is a big body that attacks like anyone else. A charge into a Hide, a Shell or a Hunker wall does nothing special. And the lane is fixed: if you misread the opponent, the charge goes exactly where you sent it.

**Good when:** opponents leave gaps (Birds away, Fairies keeping room, early boards), or keep carries in B1 or B3. **Bad when:** they fill every front slot with tough bodies and keep the carry in B2.

### Exact rules

**Charge**
1. Charge applies to this unit's **first attack made from a front slot** each combat. A Charge unit in the back row attacks normally, and its Charge waits. If it later attacks from a front slot (a Tunnel, Mole Cricket, a summon), that attack is the Charge.
2. **Target, in order:**
   - (a) the enemy in the slot across (8.2.6: same label);
   - (b) if that slot is empty, the enemy back-row unit behind that slot (table above). With two, the one with more Attack; ties go left. **Back-row protection (9.5.1) doesn't apply to this attack.** This is "running through the gap";
   - (c) if those slots are empty too, the normal target. It still counts as the Charge and still tramples.
3. Charge **ignores Taunt.** The lane is the lane. A card that forces a target (Big Ursula) wins over Charge.
4. It is an attack in every way: "when this attacks", "when attacked", Hunker on the defender, Bristle and the exchange (9.6.1) all apply. The charger takes the target's Attack back as usual.
5. **"After this charges"** fires after the exchange, the trample and the death check.
6. **Windfury-style extra attacks:** only the first is the Charge.
7. **Goldens:** Charge has no number. A golden charger has doubled stats only.
8. **Combat runs on a copy.** Charge is used up only in that fight. A unit given Charge in combat (The Last Aurochs) has it for that fight. A unit summoned into a front slot charges on its first attack; one summoned into the attacker's slot attacks next (9.4.8) and charges.
9. **Moved mid-combat into the other side** (a Fairy Changeling): it charges for its new side, across from its new slot.
10. **Ghost boards** charge normally.
11. **The front row is empty on both sides?** Rule 2 still works: across from your F2 is an empty enemy F2, so the charge runs through to B1 or B2.

**Trample** (part of every Charge; a few cards also give it to every attack)
1. When a trampling attack kills its target, the **overkill** carries on. Overkill = the damage the target actually took from the attack (after Hunker, Hide, Shell and any other modifier) minus the Health it had before the hit. Zero or less: nothing carries.
2. The overkill goes to the enemy units **behind the target** (the diagonals, 8.2). With two, it **splits evenly, odd point to the left.** With one, that unit takes all of it. A target in the back row has nobody behind it: the overkill is lost.
3. Trample damage is a **hit but not an attack.** Hide and Shell cap it (each half is its own hit, so it breaks a Hide). A hit-blocking layer (the Divine Shield stand-in) blocks it. Hunker doesn't halve it (Hunker is "while being attacked"). Bristle doesn't prick. "When attacked" doesn't fire; "when damaged" does.
4. **No chain.** A unit killed by trample doesn't trample on.
5. Trample lands in the **same damage step** as the exchange, before deaths are checked (9.6.3), so the target and anyone it trampled die together, and their death text fires in reading order.
6. If the attack is blocked or capped so the target survives, nothing tramples.

### Hide N (the owner's Shell, as he described it)

*Reminder: the first hit on this each combat deals at most N. Then the Hide is gone.*

- The owner's version (02h): "it limits the damage to a certain number for the first hit … then breaks, like a divine shield." A 5-Health beast with Hide 7 gains nothing until its Health is above 7. "You know you need to buff it to get the power from the shell."
- A **hit** is one instance of damage: an attack, half a trample, a Rut, a Start of Combat ping, one Peck. So multi-hit effects break Hide cheaply, as the owner wanted for Peck.
- It breaks after the first hit that deals 1 or more, even if that hit was under N.
- Order (library damage-modifier order): halve (Hunker) first, then cap (Hide).
- **Golden:** N is a threshold and keeps its value (7.3.5). A golden Hide lasts **two hits** instead.
- **One Hide per unit.** Gaining Hide again replaces the old one if the new N is higher, or if the old one has broken. Mother Wisent gives Hide at Start of Combat, so in practice she gives it to units without it, or raises a smaller N.
- Printed Hide is whole at the start of every combat (combat runs on a copy).

### Room

*Reminder: Room is how many empty slots are next to this.*

- "Next to" is 8.2.3: beside it in its row, and the units in front of or behind it. Maximum Room: F1 and F4 have 2, B1 and B3 have 3, F2 and B2 have 4.
- Read when the text fires: Start of Combat (locked in for the fight) or start of turn (the board as you left it).
- **Room is paid for in bodies.** Keeping a slot empty is a unit you didn't play. The position test warned that gap text is blank on full boards. That is the trade on purpose: Room cards ask whether this is a five-unit board or a seven-unit board.

### Rut

*Reminder: Start of Combat: this and the enemy across lock antlers and deal their Attack to each other.*

- Both hits land at once, before any attack. They are hits, not attacks, so Hide and Shell cap them and Hunker doesn't halve them.
- If the slot across is empty, nothing happens. It works from the back row too: a back-row stag ruts with the enemy back unit across.
- Start of Combat order is reading order (1.2.3), both sides interleaved as the rulebook sets. A stag in F1 ruts before a Mother Wisent in B2 gives out Hide.

### Trophy N

*Reminder: after this kills an enemy, it sells for N more gold. Up to 3 kills count.*

- A kill is an enemy that dies from damage this dealt: its attack, its trample or its Rut. The increase is permanent and goes back to the recruit board after the fight. The card shows it as tines on the antlers, up to three.
- The cap is a threshold: a golden earns 2 per kill, still up to 3 kills (+6).
- **Triples:** a golden from a triple starts with no tines. So a three-tine copy is a real choice: sell it now for 4, or keep it for the golden.
- A Created unit with Trophy sells for 0 plus its Trophy.

### Trodden (The Last Aurochs' text)

*Reminder: nothing can be summoned into this slot for the rest of the fight.*

- An enemy slot where a Charge killed. It blocks summons, units coming out of a Belly and Birds landing mid-fight. It doesn't block moves (Tunnel, Mole Cricket). A summon aimed there goes to the nearest empty slot (8.4.2), or fails if none.
- **A failed summon still Spills.** Against Hedgerow, Trodden turns their death summons into Acorns. That is a real cost of the card in that matchup, and intended.

### Token

**Squeaker**. *Wild Boar Piglet.* 1/1. Charge. Created, so it sells for 0.

## How it interacts with where units stand

The tribe is built on the position test's one clear finding: **text that reads the enemy's slots creates decisions that no rule of thumb solves.**

| Card | What changes by slot |
|---|---|
| Every Charge | Its lane: the enemy across, or the gap and the back unit behind it. Different against every opponent. |
| Truffle | Pays only if the slot across is empty. Against a full board she must find the one gap. |
| The Yearling | Needs a kill across to grow a tine. Aim at their smallest front unit. |
| The Roaring Hart | Duels the enemy across at the bell. Front for their weak front unit, back for their support. |
| Old Steinbock | Wants a sturdy target with a carry behind it. Corners pick for certain, middles pick the strongest. |
| Big Ursula | Must face their biggest front attacker. |
| Ibex Kid, The Bog Elk, Old Grazer | Room: which slots you leave empty. A gap next to the Elk is a gap in your wall. |
| Mother Wisent | Picks which four neighbours get Hide; worth it only on units with Health over 5. |
| Old Tusker | Attack order: who charges first and who goes last to collect the bonus. |
| The Last Aurochs | Which units are in the front row at the bell. A back-row carry moved forward charges. |
| Mother Mudwallow | Her Squeakers charge from her slot and the nearest gap, after the fight has opened lanes. |

**It also changes the opponent's slots.** Against Great beasts, B2 is the only back slot a trample never takes in full (until the Elders), a gap in your front row is a road into your back row, and the unit across from each enemy charger matters more than the unit with the most Health. So the tribe gives the rest of the lobby placement decisions too.

**Scouting.** Everything here assumes the scouting rule the position test recommended (`next_opponent_last_board`): you see the next opponent's board as it fought last round. Without it, Charge is still good (gaps and kills happen anyway), but its lane choice becomes a guess. The bots need it to play the tribe at all.

**Why the best slot moves.** Three things change it every round: the next opponent (each has different gaps and carries), the same opponent rebuilding (a bot that shuffled half its units, as playtest reviewer B saw), and your own Room plan (which slots you are keeping empty this turn). No fixed slot is right for Truffle, the Yearling, the Hart, Steinbock or Ursula.

## Where the economy comes from

Poor to middling on purpose: the owner wants every tribe to have "some economy ability … They can have poor economy" (02h). Each source pays for something the tribe already does.

| Card | Pays for | Roughly | Who it's better for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Truffle | Charging into an empty enemy slot | 1 gold a fight early, less late | Anyone, but only a scouting player keeps it going late |
| The Yearling (Trophy) | Killing with a Charge | up to +3 on its sale | Great beasts: Charge gives it the kill it needs |
| Old Grazer | Keeping two empty slots next to it | 1 gold a turn | The Room build, which keeps gaps anyway |
| Honeypaw | Killing in combat | up to 2 gold a fight | A front-line beast that wins exchanges |

- **Tied to actions, as the owner asked** (02h: "tying economy effects to specific actions that kind of reward you for being in that type of strategy"). Truffle pays a player who aims; the Grazer pays a player who keeps room; Trophy and Honeypaw pay a beast that kills.
- **Portable, but weaker elsewhere.** Old Grazer is the only gold anyone can use without thinking. Spending it on another tribe's scaling means giving up board slots, the trade the owner described for economy players.
- **Not in the bounding fallback's danger zone.** No source is per refresh, per gold spent or per Created unit. The loudest one, Honeypaw, is capped at twice a fight.

### 1. Stampede (four lanes, one combat-long charge)

```
F1 Old Steinbock   F2 Old Tusker   F3 The Yearling (golden)   F4 The Last Aurochs
     B1 Lamp Moth (neutral)    B2 The Bison Elders    B3 Mother Wisent
```

- **Turn 1-3.** Truffle and the Yearling across from enemy gaps. Sell the Yearling at three tines if no copy is coming.
- **Turn 4-6.** Tier 3. Old Steinbock for the walls, Mother Mudwallow in front for late chargers. Every round, re-aim every charger at the scouted board.
- **Turn 7-9.** Old Tusker. Order the front so the biggest attacker goes last. Mother Wisent at B3 covers F3, F4 and B2.
- **Turn 10+.** The Last Aurochs gives Charge and +3 to the whole front row. The Elders make every middle trample full. By F4's turn the Aurochs has +6 from three earlier charges plus its own +3. Lamp Moth in B1 adds Attack to F1 and F2.
- **Wins by.** One cycle that kills four front units and tramples into the back row, while the Trodden slots stop refills.
- **Loses to.** Full front rows of Hide, Shell or Hunker units (each charge stops dead), a carry in B2 behind a wall, and Fairy Changelings, which steal the charger across. If the first cycle doesn't break them, the beasts are only medium-sized.

### 2. The Circle of Horns (sticky wall, the answer to giants)

```
F1 The Roaring Hart   F2 Big Ursula   F3 Honeypaw (golden)   F4 Old Grazer
     B1 The Roaring Hart    B2 Mother Wisent    B3 Lamp Moth (neutral)
```

- **Turn 1-4.** Old Grazer early for gold; Harts across from what you want gone.
- **Turn 5-7.** Honeypaw and Mother Wisent. Health spells go on the Hide units, because Hide only works above its cap.
- **Turn 8+.** Big Ursula across from their giant. The back-row Hart ruts with their B1. Neutral Lamp Moth in B3 gives F3 and F4 Attack.
- **Wins by.** Making big hits cheap. Their giant hits Ursula for 8 and takes the rest back, every first hit on your front row is capped at 5, and the Harts and Honeypaw pick off their small units for gold.
- **Loses to.** WIDE boards and multi-hit (Peck): Hide breaks on the first small hit. Birds' Swoop into Mother Wisent. BURST damage that breaks Hide before attacks. Its own damage is slow, so a stall can end in a draw.

### 3. Open Country (five big beasts and room to graze)

```
F1 (gap)          F2 The Bog Elk (golden)    F3 Big Ursula    F4 Old Steinbock
     B1 Old Grazer     B2 (gap)     B3 Ibex Kid (golden)
```

- Five units, two gaps, and both gaps work twice. The Elk at F2 has F1 and B2 empty: Room 2, so +8/+8 golden. The Grazer at B1 has F1 and B2 empty: Room 2, so it pays. The Kid at B3 has B2 empty: Room 1. Ursula in F3 faces their giant.
- **Turn 1-4.** Ibex Kid and Truffle with Room on a small board. Old Grazer the first time you can keep two slots free.
- **Turn 5-7.** The Elk. Don't fill the gaps around it. Each round, decide if a sixth unit is worth Room.
- **Turn 8+.** Ursula and Steinbock. Move the Grazer to wherever it has two gaps.
- **Wins by.** A huge combat-only Elk trampling through whatever it hits, with gold from the Grazer, and Ursula holding the line.
- **Loses to.** WIDE boards with real Attack (five bodies run out), enemy Great beasts, whose charges run straight through your front gaps, and low hero damage when it wins with few survivors.

## The 5 strongest combos

1. **Tusker's stampede.** Four front chargers with Old Tusker: each charge gives the units still to attack +2. With The Last Aurochs, everyone is a charger.
2. **Corner charge on a corner carry.** A charger in F1 that kills tramples all of the overkill into B1. Against a Dozey in B1 behind a small unit, that is most of a carry gone before it acts.
3. **Steinbock and a following charger.** Steinbock pulls their back carry into the front; Old Tusker or the Elk, attacking next, kills it.
4. **Ursula and a Turnip Scarecrow (neutral).** Ursula forces their giant to hit her; she stops all but 8 and returns it. Or let the Scarecrow skip it, with Ursula across from their second-biggest.
5. **Mudwallow and the Aurochs' Trodden.** Your Charges block their refills while her Squeakers charge through the gaps that leaves.

## With other tribes, both ways

**Hedgerow folk.**
- *They help us:* Hand Along feeds a charger at the end of a Line. Old Delver's Tunnel and any Tunnel unit move a back-row beast into a front slot, where its waiting Charge fires. Hunker walls hold while beasts charge.
- *They hurt us:* Bristle walls prick every charge. Their Kits and Shrewlets fill the gaps Truffle wants.
- *We hurt them:* deaths cut the Line, and their back-row carries (Dozey, the Treasurer) sit in reach of corner tramples. **Trodden feeds them:** blocked summons Spill into Acorns.

**Fairies.**
- *They help us:* Rings on our charge slots are permanent stats on a lane. Leaf-Gold Sprite wants empty neighbours, as Room does: one gap pays both. Hex on the enemy across makes our Charge kill and trample.
- *They hurt us:* a Changeling across from our charger takes it, and it charges for them. Hex on our charger stops the kill.
- *We hurt them:* Fairies leave gaps and keep their Hob and Witch in the back row; Truffle and the run through the gap find them.

**Oozes.**
- *They help us:* Frumenty with a Great beast inside gains Charge or Hide. Hide on a big Ooze makes it take two kills and one capped hit.
- *They hurt us:* a dying Ooze refills its slot, so our through-charge finds a body. Overkill into a Pudding is wasted (each body takes its own kill).
- *We hurt them:* trample hits the units behind before the Belly opens, and **Trodden stops Bellies opening into their slots.** The Aurochs is the hardest Ooze counter in the set.

**Birds.**
- *They help us:* a bird flown south leaves an empty slot, which is Room for the Elk, the Kid and the Grazer. Bird plus beast is a real pair: sell your Swallow, and your Elk grows.
- *They hurt us:* Swoop reaches our back row, where Mother Wisent and the Elders sit with no Hide of their own.
- *We hurt them:* Bird boards have gaps by design. Our charges run through their Nests into the back row.

**Neutrals.**
- **Mole Cricket**: the unit behind steps forward and attacks. A Charge beast behind it charges from the new slot.
- **Whirligig Beetle**: swaps their rows at the bell, so their back carries stand across from our chargers.
- **Turnip Scarecrow**: pairs with Ursula (combo 4).
- **Earwig Mother, Pill Bug, Lamp Moth**: back-row support. A beast board is short of good back-row units, so these fit.
- **Ichneumon Wasp**: beats Hide. Hide caps damage but doesn't block it, so a 1-damage wasp hit still marks Ursula.
- **Ballooning Spider**: another way at the back row, for the turns no lane is open.
- **Stat spells**: Health on Hide units and Attack spread across chargers. A spell piled on one charger buys one big trample once a fight.

## Weak against

- **Full front rows of tough bodies**, with the carry in B2. No gaps, no trample in full, no kills.
- **Hide, Shell and Hunker on the enemy.** Each caps or halves the charge, and a capped charge can't trample. The mirror is a slow grind.
- **Multi-hit and BURST.** Peck, random pings and Start of Combat damage break Hide for little.
- **Steal and curse.** Fairy Changeling across from the best charger; Hex on it.
- **Wide boards with Attack**, against the Room build.
- **Misreading.** Every charge goes where you aimed it. Without scouting, or against a player who rearranges every turn, the tribe loses a lot of its edge.

## Where it could loop or run away

- **The stacked charger.** A 60/60 with Charge kills the unit across and tramples 50 into B1. With the Elders, a middle charge kills three units in one attack. It is still one attack a fight, on a lane they can see coming, and any Hide or Shell across stops it cold. **Watch first.** If it dominates, try in order: trample stops at the target's own Health ("it carries on what it broke"); Winded (variation 2); the Elders lose "doesn't split".
- **Old Tusker's chain.** +2 per charge for the units still to attack, combat only. Seven chargers (Aurochs, Squeakers) give the last unit about +12. Bounded by the board size, never permanent.
- **Ursula's return.** Stopped damage can be huge, by design, against giants. It can't loop: returned damage can't be stopped or returned (two Ursulas facing each other end after one return).
- **Win-more economy.** Honeypaw and Trophy pay for kills, so a winning board earns more. Capped (twice a fight, three tines) and small. Truffle and the Grazer pay regardless of results.
- **Old Grazer and "doing nothing".** It pays for keeping slots empty, which risks the owner's dislike of rewards for inaction. The defence: it isn't free, since the empty slots are bodies you didn't play, and it competes with Room for the Elk and Kid. If it plays as passive income, tie it to an action: "Start of your turn: if you sold a unit last turn and this has 2 or more Room…".
- **Trodden plus Spill.** Against Hedgerow, Trodden turns their failed summons into Acorns. That's a gift to them, not a loop, and capped by their own Spill limits.

## Variations of the signature

1. **Lane Charge (no run).** Charge only goes across. If the slot is empty, it's a normal attack. Simpler, keeps the back row safer, and moves all the reach to trample.
2. **Winded.** After a unit charges, its Attack is halved for the rest of the combat. A stronger counterweight to the stacked charger: the charge is the beast's big moment, and four chargers beat one.
3. **Run-up.** A unit can only Charge if a back slot behind it is empty. Beasts need room behind them to start, so each charger costs a back slot. This joins Charge and Room into one rule, and makes the size of Great beasts cost board space.
4. **Gore (front-row miss).** Charge goes straight at the back-row unit behind the slot across, even if that slot holds someone, and doesn't trample. A sniper version for the ibex.

**Similar to:** Magic's trample (overkill carries, here to the units behind rather than the player). The run through a broken line is the cavalry charge of tactics games such as Total War. Hide is the owner's Shell, and a cousin of Hearthstone's Divine Shield that caps damage instead of blocking it. Rut is a duel like Galaxy's "the enemy across from me" effects. Room is the "loner" text of Magic's Exalted or of the library's Solitary, paid in slots.

## Engine needs beyond `engine.md`

**Already there:** `across` selector, `per: empty_adjacent` (Room), `start_of_combat` and `start_of_turn` triggers, combat `grant` with `duration: combat`, the `relocate` combat event (from Changeling and `row_swap`), `hunker`, `gold` with `when: next_turn`, `limit` per fight, Created sell value 0.

**New, in rough order of need:**

1. **`charge` mechanic.** A target override on the first attack declared from a front slot: across, else the higher-Attack back unit behind that slot (ignoring row protection and Taunt), else normal. A per-unit `charged` flag on the combat copy. An `after_charge` trigger with a field for "the slot across was empty".
2. **Trample.** A damage step that computes overkill after modifiers and splits it between the target's diagonals, odd point left, in the same damage step as the exchange, before the death check. A rule flag for "doesn't split" (the Elders) and for extra charge damage.
3. **Hide N.** A damage-cap step after halving in the damage-modifier pipeline (library E89), breaking on the first hit of 1 or more, with a hit count (golden: 2). One Hide per unit; replace rule. An `on_hide_lost` trigger carrying the stopped amount (Ursula).
4. **On kill.** An `on_kill` trigger (this unit's damage killed an enemy), covering attack, trample and Rut. Honeypaw and Trophy need it.
5. **Trophy N.** A sell-value modifier on the recruit unit, raised from combat (a new carry-back op like permanent `buff`), capped at 3 kills, reset on triple. Show tines.
6. **Rut.** A Start of Combat two-way damage between this and `across`, both as hits, landing together. Needs damage equal to a unit's Attack, both ways.
7. **Room as a condition.** `start_of_turn` with a condition on empty neighbours (`per: empty_adjacent` exists; a threshold condition doesn't).
8. **Selector `not_attacked_yet`** for Old Tusker, and an `attacked` flag per combat unit.
9. **Steinbock's swap.** Combat swap of two enemy units (front target with its highest-Attack diagonal behind), then damage to both. `relocate` covers the event; the swap action is new on the enemy side.
10. **Forced target.** "Must attack this with its next attack" as a status on the enemy across, honoured by targeting unless illegal (row protection), and overriding Charge.
11. **Stopped-damage return** that can't be stopped or returned (a `reflect` damage kind, like thorn damage never triggering thorns).
12. **Trodden.** A per-fight slot state on the enemy side that blocks summons and Belly exits but not moves. A summon aimed there redirects by 8.4.2 or fails, and a fail fires Spill.

**For screens and bots, not engine rules:**

- **Lane preview.** While dragging a charger, draw its lane on the scouted board: who it hits, where it runs through, and where the trample lands, with "split" or "full".
- **The scout must show stats by slot** (`next_opponent_last_board`), including Hide and Shell values. Without it, every Great beast is a guess.
- **Bots.** A Hard bot needs a lane search: for each charger, score each front slot against the scouted board (kill across? gap? carry behind?). The board search in `src/core/bots/board_search.gd` can do this if it fights against the scouted board. Defensive bots facing Great beasts should learn "carry in B2, no front gaps". Without both, this tribe will test badly for the wrong reason.
