# Birds pass 1: triage review

8 Nov 2026, fresh cloud reviewer. Method: triage skill steps 3–4. Competition: every slice-pool unit at the tier. Boards are hand estimates against `benchmark-boards.md`; not simmed, as pass-1 isn't in the engine.

**Verdict: partly.** The owner's first question ("why would you play the early-game Birds") is answered: every T1 and T2 Bird is now a par body or better, and the fliers make Mr Redbreast a real buy. "The entire journey" isn't answered yet. Both routes break at or after T4, and the R12 low roll still comes 6th.

## 1. Role check, T1–T2 (R1–R4)

| Card | Buy on the turn you see it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One Swallow 1/2, +1 gold | **Yes, as economy** | The only unconditional T1 gold. Weak body. |
| Chiffchaff 2/3 on play | **Yes** | Level with Wobbly, Pill Bug and Pretender, and grows each trip. |
| Mrs Dunnock 2/3 | **Yes, as a body** | Par. Her text works against the Nest payoffs (ask 1). |
| Mr Redbreast 2/3 +1/+1 per Nest | **Yes, if a flier is away** | At 3/4–4/5, the biggest T2 body; par alone. |
| Redwing 2/4 | **Yes** | A Moss Ooze body; the Egg text is small at T2. |
| Mrs Jay 3/3, Acorn on sale | **Yes** | Most stats at T2; each flight pays gold and an Acorn. |

**Do early cards make later Birds more attractive? Mostly yes.** Fliers on R2 give Redbreast a Nest by R3, and Jay and Redwing set up Tom Tit. The exception is Dunnock: shorter trips and an eaten Egg weaken Redbreast, Tom Tit, Goose and the Ratchet.

T3 is fine. Tom Tit is the best T3 with two Eggs, Jack Daw is a step below The Lodger, and Old Tern's free Bird pays for its 2/4.

## 2. Skips

- **T4 is a skip for both routes: the flagged gap, confirmed and wider.** Barn Owl (4/5) and Mag Pye (3/5) are situational answers below Burner, Wisp, Ermeline and Syllabub. Stork is the only route piece, and she's Homecoming's: Hatchery has nothing at T4, and Stork brings Birds home while Goose, the Lump and the Ratchet want Nests.
- **Homecoming has no T5–T6 payoff.** Goose and the Ratchet read Nests, which means Birds away; Whistlers fits any board. Its replays end around a 6/7 Chiffchaff and some gold. It's an economy lane into Hatchery, not a second route.
- **Hatchery can't "keep Birds away".** No card lengthens a trip, so each flier gives an Egg for 2 fights in 3, with a re-sell every cycle and fliers staggered by hand.

## 3. Sub-varieties

One real route (Nests and Eggs), with Homecoming as its engine and thieves as a splash (Jack Daw, Mag Pye, Barn Owl, Whistlers: 4 of 16 cards with no route). pt3-a placed 3rd on thieves, so that's a variant, but only from T3. "Two routes" doesn't hold past T4.

## 4. Benchmark boards (estimates, method as in `benchmark-boards.md`)

**R6 high** (T4, golden Redbreast): golden Redbreast 8/10, Jay Egg 5/5 (Redwing and Tom Tit), Chiffchaff Egg 5/6, Redwing 2/4, Tom Tit 3/4, Stork 3/6, Jack Daw 3/4. About 70: **rank 3** (Oozes 92, Fairies 79).

**R6 low** (T3, no Redwing or Tom Tit): Chiffchaff 3/4, Dunnock 2/3, Redbreast 3/4, Jay Egg 2/2, Pill Bug 1/4, Sable 2/3, Moss Ooze 2/4. About 37: **rank 1–2** (Fairies 43, Oozes 32). The early game no longer collapses.

**R12 high:** the designer's "average" board, checked: 130 on view, plus Goose's 9 damage and about 40 Ratchet stats. **Rank 1–2.** It needs Goose, the Lump, the Ratchet, Redwing and a golden Redbreast all by R12, from 2 Birds at T5 and 2 at T6, so it's a high roll.

**R12 low** (T5; no Goose, Ratchet, Redwing or Supper): Lump 7/9 on Jay's Nest, Redbreast 4/5, Chiffchaff Egg 4/5, Barn Owl 4/5, Burner 4/6, Tom Tit 3/4, Stork 3/6. About 75: **rank 6**, below Fairies' 89 (the old low roll was 81).

**The low roll collapses at R9–R12:** the T4 skip, then everything hangs on the T5–T6 trio. Without Redwing, Eggs are half-size; without Goose, the Lump's Nest is small; without the Ratchet, there's no finisher. From T5, it only works on a high roll.

## 5. New-player confusion

- **One away Bird, four readings:** the Egg is half its current stats (full with Redwing, plus Tom Tit), the Lump and Goose read base stats, Goose's own Egg is 0/2, and the Ratchet lands the Bird itself.
- **Dunnock eats the Egg,** which could be 5/5 or more, and her card doesn't say so.
- **The Ratchet's landing is unstated:** whether the Bird stays after the fight, and whether its trip ends.
- **Stork** reads as pure upside, but each homecoming removes a Nest that other Birds count.

## Items

**Cut (must fix before build)**
1. T4 has no Hatchery piece, and Owl and Pye are under par for T4. Add a T4 Nest or Egg piece, ideally one that lengthens trips ("Birds whose Nests are beside this stay away a turn longer"), or turn one of the two thieves into it.
2. Homecoming has no T5–T6 payoff, so "two routes" isn't true. Give it a late payoff that rewards homecomings, or drop the claim and design the second sub-variety (thieves?) to have its own way through T4–T6.

**Ask (orchestrator's call)**
1. Dunnock works against every Nest payoff. Either let her bring home a Bird *beside* her without eating its Egg, or accept the conflict as the "shops decide the lean" split.
2. Unify the away-Bird readings. Use current stats everywhere (the Lump and Goose included), or make Egg values visible on the card.
3. Write the Ratchet's landing rules on the card or in its details.
4. Relabel the R12 board as a high roll and add a low-roll target. The T4 fix should lift the R12 low roll to about 90 or more.
5. Costing should check Swallow plus Stork: about +2 gold a turn per Swallow, more with a golden Swallow.

**Fine:** the six T1–T2 buys, Tom Tit, Jack Daw, Old Tern, and the 3/3/3/3/2/2 spread.
