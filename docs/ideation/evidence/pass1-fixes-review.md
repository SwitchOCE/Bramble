# Review: pass 1 fixes (8 Nov 2026)

A fresh cloud reviewer, on `ideation/c-pass1-fixes`. Target: `content/pass1-fixes.md`. Method: buy-moment costing (case study change A, `power-budget.md` 2.2 and 4.1) and triage step 3. Competition is the slice pool (`set: "slice"`, pool not false), read from `content/units`. I priced by hand and ran no sims. Nothing in the engine was changed or run.

**Counts:** 15 items. **4 cut, 6 ask, 5 fine.** It moves the original problems: the Chaser carry, the Ring piles, Hex 8, the gold-only bodies and Mousewife's no-room failures are all addressed. But two of the gold fixes overshoot into dominance, and Bracken's fix repeats the old Mousewife pattern.

## Cut (fix before build)

1. **Rainbow Chaser: over; dominant (the sale, not the body).** The body is now fair: 1/2 on R1, 2/3 to 3/4 by R3 (the benchmark R3 Chasers fall from 4/5 and 5/6 to 3/4), and 4/5 at most. The sale is still 12–13 gold by R5–6. Every play into an empty slot paints, so seven slots come without trying. 12 gold from one T1 slot is about 24 SP at the 2.0 sink. That is six times the T1 one-off cap of 2 gold per trigger. It makes Chaser the best R1 buy for *any* tribe: buy it, fill the board, sell it at R5. The golden version sells for 24. The designer's fallback (+5 → +3) still pays 10. **Change:** "When sold: wipe the paint and get 1 gold per 2 painted slots, +2 for all seven" (5 gold, golden 10). That keeps the cash-out decision players praised (pt5 a, e) at about the Pennywort Snail's level (sells for 4), plus a better body and Rainbow synergy. Also, the golden top is 8/10, not 8/9 (2/4 base + 6/6).
2. **Money Spider: over; dominant.** "Start of Turn: get 1 gold" is unconditional recurring gold at T2. Budget 4.1 forbids that: 1 gold a turn at T2 beats a plain unit on a 0.5 body, and the cap is 0 at T1–2. The golden version pays 2–4 a turn from R3, where +2 isn't allowed before T5. Bought R2, the plain version is about +9 gold by R12 for a 1/3 slot. That is the snowball the cap guards against (tally 2). **Change:** move it to T3 (the cap there is 1 gold a turn, priced at 5.1 SP; 1/3 + 5.1 = 9.1 against V(3) = 8, inside ±25%), drop the carry-over clause from the plain text, and give the golden its own line: "1 gold, 2 if you carried over 3 or more". Steady income that never asks you to save stays the core.
3. **The Toll-Bard: over; dominant at T2.** Its quarry nearly always attacks, so "twice a fight" is near-recurring income of up to 2 gold a turn from R2, on a 2/4 body (V(2) = 7) that also takes 2 Attack off the enemy's best early hitter. It beats Sable 2/3, Mrs Jay 3/3 and Moss Ooze 2/4 in most states, and with Money Spider it gives a Hunter +3 gold a turn by R3. **Change:** gold once a fight, Attack loss still twice. Wording: "it loses 1 Attack" reads as the Bard. Write "the quarry loses 1 Attack".
4. **Brother Bracken: over early, a trap late.** Every board at R1–5 has three or more empty slots, so every unit gets +2/+2 for free. A 3-unit R3 board goes from about 13 to 25 stats. That is the early-tempo snowball Mousewife was cut for (pt4 a). Late, the claim "six at +1/+1 roughly match a seventh unit" fails. Budget 6.4 says a gap costs 21–23 points of win rate. +6/+6 spread thin doesn't match a 15–20-stat R9–12 unit, and five at +2/+2 (+20 SP) is worth less than the two units they replace (30–40 SP). So it fails its role (the late hermit) in its window. **Change:** tie it to tier. "Start of Combat: if you have 5 or fewer units, they gain +1/+1 per tier you've reached, up to +5/+5." That gives +1/+1 on R1 and +5/+5 on five units at T5–6 (+25 SP). The hermit idea stays.

## Ask (orchestrator's call)

5. **Hob Mumblecrust: fair to over.** He's no longer a trap: a free power (cost 0) that nets +1 gold every third turn. The risk is R1: 6 gold buys two units or Tier 2 on turn 1, which starts the streak problem (tally 2). Either block the loan on turn 1, or repay 3 over 3 turns, which leaves timing only.
6. **Pennywort Snail: under; borderline trap on R1.** It's a 1/2 on the turn you'd buy it, which is the Chiffchaff failure. As a wall it trails Wobbly and Pill Bug (1/4) until R4, when you sell it. Start it at 1/3 (top 1/6, sells for 4).
7. **Cricket Busker: fair to over, watch.** Refunding the first spell each turn makes Pinch of Dust free: +2 SP a turn, above the T2 growth cap of 1.3, plus Witch and Moss Ooze synergy. It's conditional on a spell in the shop. Bench it, with "1 gold next turn" as the fallback.
8. **Hedge Witch, golden and the stored count.** Plain: fair. Hex 1 against a T1 enemy of 2–3 Attack is real on an empty R1 board, and Hex 6 at T6 is a fair late niche. The golden version reaches Hex 8 at tier 4, which brings back the original complaint at R7. A cap of "your tier + 2" would hold it. Banking spells past the cap is invisible: show "Hex 2 (5 stored)" or drop the banking.
9. **Ring cap readability.** Overflow is lost silently (Spore Cloud on +3 gives +1). The aim preview should show "+1/+1, 1 lost". Reel-Mistress stacks past the cap in a fight. That is right, since a full-board cap would kill her, but it contradicts the reminder "up to +4/+4". Add "past the cap" to her details. Also, the note says "six cards make Rings". I count four in the slice pool (Nan, Sally, Spore Cloud, Boundary Stone), plus Monarch, Wisp and Reel-Mistress, which read or move Rings.
10. **Old Lutra wording.** "Back-row Take" is ambiguous. The old text meant the quarry's row, but the design note ("rewards placing it safely") means Lutra's row. Pick one and say it.

## Fine

- **Ring cap at +4/+4: keep.** Fairies were rank 1 in 9 of 12 benchmark cells. On the benchmarks the cap costs average Fairy boards about 1/1 at R9. It costs the pile builds about 5/5 (R12 high: Rings +10/+10 → +4/+4, +1 Monarch). It costs the Annis low roll about 1–3/3. It removes the generic pile that any tribe inherits (tally 3, 5) and kills no Fairy payoff: Monarch goes over the cap, Wisp rewards spread Rings, and Reel-Mistress stacks in the fight. Keep +5/+5 as the fallback if the per-tribe bar (C) drops Fairies below its floor.
- **Little Nip 2/2: fair.** It's slightly over by formula (+1/+1 per Take at T1 is above the 1.0 combat cap), but conditional, and Hunters are flat early (tally 8). It's a real R1 pick against Wick 2/2 and Young Pretender 3/2.
- **Old Lutra 3/4: fair.** Lodger 4/4 is the better body, and Lutra grows and reaches.
- **The Peat Cutter 2/4: fair.** It's level with Bufo 1/4 as an early Mire, and it fills the audit's T2 Mire gap.
- **Old Mother Mousewife: fair.** Two t/t Mice a fight, which is 4 SP at T1 and 24 at T6. The "in its place" summon always has room.

## Calls for the orchestrator

- **Ring cap +4/+4?** Yes, as above. +5/+5 only on a measured Fairy shortfall.
- **Chaser's sale trim?** Trimming +5 to +3 isn't enough. Chaser stays the best T1 for every tribe at 10–11 gold. Halve the per-slot rate as well (cut 1). With that, it's worth a T1 slot for Rainbow and Fairy players without being the default R1 buy.
