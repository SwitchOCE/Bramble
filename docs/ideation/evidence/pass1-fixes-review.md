# Review: pass 1 fixes (8 Nov 2026)

A fresh cloud reviewer, on `ideation/c-pass1-fixes`. Target: `content/pass1-fixes.md`. Method: buy-moment costing (case study A, `power-budget.md` 2.2 and 4.1), and triage step 3 against the slice pool in `content/units`. Hand-priced; no sims.

**Counts:** 15 items: **4 cut, 6 ask, 5 fine.** It moves the original problems (the Chaser carry, Ring piles, Hex 8, gold-only bodies, Mousewife's room failures), but two gold fixes overshoot into dominance, and Bracken repeats the old Mousewife pattern.

## Cut (fix before build)

1. **Rainbow Chaser: over; dominant (the sale, not the body).** The body is fair now: 1/2 on R1, 3/4 by R3 (the benchmark R3 Chasers drop from 4/5 and 5/6), and 4/5 at most. But the sale is still 12–13 gold by R5–6. Any play into an empty slot paints, so all seven slots come free. That's about 24 SP from a T1 slot, six times the T1 cap of 2 gold per trigger, and it makes Chaser the best R1 buy for *every* tribe. The golden sells for 24. Trimming +5 to +3 still pays 10. **Change:** "get 1 gold per 2 painted slots, +2 for all seven" (5 gold; 10 golden). Also, the golden top is 8/10, not 8/9.
2. **Money Spider: over; dominant.** It's unconditional recurring gold at T2, which 4.1 forbids (the cap is 0 at T1–2; it beats a plain unit on a 0.5 body). The golden pays 2–4 a turn from R3, and +2 isn't allowed before T5. That's about +9 gold by R12 from a 1/3 slot: the snowball guard broken (tally 2). **Change:** move it to T3 (1/3 + 5.1 SP = 9.1 against V(3) = 8, inside ±25%). Drop the carry clause from the plain text, and give the golden its own line: "1 gold, 2 if you carried over 3 or more".
3. **The Toll-Bard: over; dominant at T2.** The quarry nearly always attacks, so "twice a fight" is near-recurring income of 2 gold a turn from R2. It's on a 2/4 body that also takes 2 Attack off the enemy. With Spider, a Hunter makes +3 gold a turn by R3. **Change:** gold once a fight, Attack loss still twice. And "it loses 1 Attack" reads as the Bard: write "the quarry loses 1 Attack".
4. **Brother Bracken: over early, a trap late.** At R1–5 every board has 3+ empty slots, so every unit gets a free +2/+2: a 3-unit R3 board goes from about 13 to 25 stats. The early snowball again (pt4 a). Late, five units at +2/+2 (+20 SP) is worth less than the two 15–20-stat units they replace (budget 6.4: a gap costs 21–23 points of win rate), so it fails its hermit role in its window. **Change:** "Start of Combat: if you have 5 or fewer units, they gain +1/+1 per tier you've reached, up to +5/+5."

## Ask (orchestrator's call)

5. **Hob Mumblecrust: fair to over.** He's no longer a trap: free, and +1 gold net every third turn. But on R1, 6 gold buys two units or Tier 2, which feeds the streak problem. Either no loan on turn 1, or repay 3 over 3 turns (timing only).
6. **Pennywort Snail: under; borderline trap on R1.** It's a 1/2 on the turn you buy it (the Chiffchaff failure), and it trails Wobbly and Pill Bug (1/4) until you sell it at R4. Start it at 1/3.
7. **Cricket Busker: fair to over, watch.** It makes Pinch of Dust free each turn: +2 SP a turn, over the T2 growth cap of 1.3, and it feeds the Witch and Moss Ooze. Bench it; the fallback is "1 gold next turn".
8. **Hedge Witch: the golden and the stored count.** The plain card is fair: Hex 1 matters against 2–3-Attack R1 enemies, and Hex 6 at T6 is a fair niche. The golden reaches Hex 8 at tier 4, which brings back the original complaint at R7: cap it at "your tier + 2". Stored spells past the cap are invisible: show "Hex 2 (5 stored)" or drop the storing.
9. **Ring cap readability.** Overflow is lost silently: the aim preview should show "+1/+1, 1 lost". Reel-Mistress stacks past the cap in fights, which is right (a fight-time cap would kill her), but it contradicts the reminder text, so add "past the cap" to her details. The note's "six cards make Rings" is four in the slice pool: Nan, Sally, Spore Cloud, Boundary Stone.
10. **Old Lutra wording.** "Back-row Take": the old text meant the quarry's row, and the note ("placing it safely") means Lutra's. Pick one.

## Fine

- **Ring cap at +4/+4: keep.** Fairies ranked 1st in 9 of 12 benchmark cells. On those boards the cap costs average Fairies about 1/1 at R9, the pile builds about 5/5 (R12 high: +10/+10 → +4/+4, +1 with Monarch) and the Annis low roll 1–3/3. It removes the generic pile any tribe inherits (tally 3, 5) and kills no Fairy payoff: Monarch goes past the cap, Wisp rewards spreading Rings, Reel-Mistress stacks in fights.
- **Little Nip 2/2: fair.** Its Take growth is over the 1.0 combat cap by formula, but it's conditional, and Hunters are flat early (tally 8). It's a real R1 pick against Wick and Young Pretender.
- **Old Lutra 3/4: fair.** Under Lodger's 4/4, but it grows and it reaches.
- **The Peat Cutter 2/4: fair.** Level with Bufo, and it fills the T2 Mire gap.
- **Old Mother Mousewife: fair.** Two t/t Mice a fight: 4 SP at T1, 24 at T6. "In its place" never fails for room.

## Calls for the orchestrator

- **Ring cap +4/+4?** Yes. Move to +5/+5 only if the per-tribe bar (C) measures Fairies below the floor.
- **Chaser trim?** +5 → +3 isn't enough: at 10–11 gold it stays the best T1 for every tribe. Halve the per-slot rate too (cut 1). Then it earns a T1 slot for Rainbow and Fairy players without being the default R1 buy.
