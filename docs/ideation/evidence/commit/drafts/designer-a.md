# Designer A: tribe-count payoffs on the top tiers

## 1. The idea in three sentences

Each tribe gets one **count that grows out of its own mechanic**: Hedgerow counts its unbroken run on the Line, Fairies count Fairies wearing Rings, Oozes count the bodies that have come out of a Belly this fight, Birds count the flock (home and away) and their Nests, Bog folk count Mired enemies, and Hunters count Takes. The weak tier 4-6 cards of each tribe are rewritten so their payoff grows **faster than the count** (per unit beyond the second or third, or count x count), so the 4th to 7th unit is worth more than the 1st to 3rd. Most counts are also positional (a run, the units behind, the slots across, a Nest), so a 3-tribe splash can't get them just by owning three bodies.

## 2. Why it fixes "committing doesn't pay"

- **Measure section 2:** one card in 143 counts a tribe and nothing rewards the 5th to 7th unit. This package adds about ten cards that do, all at tier 4-6, where a committed player is shopping late.
- **Measure section 1:** a 7-of-a-tribe board places worst (4.94), because the 6th and 7th units add only their printed stats. Under this package they add the most: a Standing Committee run of 7 gives 35 Attack, against 3 for a run of 3.
- **Measure sections 3-4:** tier-6 bodies (18 stats at round 15) lose to buffed tier 1s (28), because buffs land early and never move, and tier-6 text adds no stats. The new tier-6 texts add tens of stats a fight to a whole tribe, not to one body, so a late tier 6 matters when it arrives. Most of it lasts one combat, so it doesn't stack into a carry.
- **Splash test.** At the counts a mixed board reaches (2-3 of a tribe), each payoff is 0-20% of its value at 7 (table below). A splash still takes a tribe's cheap tools. It just doesn't get the late payoff.

| Card | Count | At 3 | At 5 | At 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standing Committee | Hedgerow run | +3 Attack | +15 | +35 |
| Elder Ooze | comings-out this fight | +6/+6 | +15/+15 | +28/+28 |
| Seven Whistlers | flock, slots covered | -9/-9 | -25/-25 | -49/-49 |
| The Peat King | Mired enemies | -9 Attack | -25 | -49 |

(Combat stats spread over the tribe, or stats taken off the enemy. Numbers are first guesses for the costing reviewer.)

## 3. Card changes and new cards

Thresholds ("beyond the third") never double. Counts include the card itself unless the text says "other".

### Hedgerow folk

**One term, no keyword. A unit's run** is the part of its Line (F1-B1-F2-B2-F3-B3-F4) that is unbroken Hedgerow folk and includes it. A gap or a non-Hedgerow unit ends the run. It's the parish line of hands, but kept in the family.

**The Standing Committee** (rewrite, CORE). Tier 5, 4/6.
- Text: Start of Combat: each Hedgerow folk in this unit's run gains +1 Attack this combat for each Hedgerow folk in the run beyond the second.
- Golden: ...+2 Attack for each...
- Plays: the old "if all 7 touch" goal, now graded. A run of 5 gives +3 to five units. You can't use it with a Bird on F2 or a Fairy in B1, so where you put a splash unit decides the payoff.

**Old Mother Mast** (rewrite, CORE). Tier 6, 6/8.
- Text: After your first unit buy each turn: get an Acorn, plus one more for each Hedgerow folk in this unit's run beyond the third.
- Golden: two Acorns, plus two more for each...
- Plays: a run of 7 gives 5 Acorns a turn, a Stash and change. It's economy tied to the strategy (02h): the Acorns only come if the board is all Hedgerow, so it can't pay for another tribe's scaling.

### Fairies

**Fairy Monarch** (rewrite of a vanilla 6/8, CORE). Tier 6, 5/7.
- Text: Rings under your Fairies give double their stats. End of Turn: for each of your Fairies on a Ring beyond the second, a random one of your Rings grows +1/+1.
- Golden: Rings under your Fairies give triple. ...grows +2/+2.
- Plays: Rings are 11-12% of all board stats and worn by anyone (measure section 3). The Monarch makes the Fairy player want them under Fairies, and a Hedgerow carry on a Ring gets no double. It's a one-card answer to "Rings are generic", read entirely from where your units stand.

**Moth Fairy** (rewrite of a vanilla 4/6, optional). Tier 4, 3/6.
- Text: Start of Combat: the units behind this gain +1 Attack this combat for each of your Fairies on a Ring.
- Golden: +2 Attack.
- Plays: a front-row moth that turns ringed Fairies into hitting power for the back row. It's the Recruit Phase puzzle the owner liked in 02h: who goes behind whom.

### Oozes

**Elder Ooze** (rewrite of a vanilla 7/7, CORE). Tier 6, 5/7, Belly 2.
- Text: Belly 2. Whenever a unit comes out of one of your Oozes, it gains +1/+1 this combat for each unit that has come out of your Oozes this fight, itself included.
- Golden: +2/+2 for each...
- Plays: the first body out is +1/+1, the sixth +6/+6. The second wave comes out angrier the longer the fight goes, which suits a tribe whose power is spread across many bodies. One Dollop in a Hedgerow board gets +1/+1 once.

**Figgy Duff** (rewrite, CORE; replaces the triple engine, which Cut-and-Come-Again keeps alive). Tier 6, 4/7, Belly 1.
- Text: Belly 1. End of Turn: what's inside this and inside each Ooze next to this gains +1/+1 for each Ooze next to this.
- Golden: +2/+2 for each...
- Plays: put it in B2 with Oozes on F2, F3, B1 and B3 and five held units each grow +4/+4 a turn. The stats sit inside, out of reach of spells and theft, and arrive only when a pudding dies. It's the "pudding club" shape: a positional cluster only an Ooze board can make.

**Bog Ooze** (rewrite of a vanilla 5/5, optional). Tier 4, 3/5, Belly 1.
- Text: Belly 1. A unit you put into one of your Oozes gains +1/+1 permanently for each Ooze you have beyond the second.
- Golden: +2/+2.
- Plays: gives a 5-Ooze board a reason to keep feeding mid-game, and gives the 4th and 5th Ooze a price.

### Birds (see section 4)

**The Seven Whistlers** (rewrite, CORE). Tier 6, 4/6.
- Text: Start of Combat: each enemy across from one of your Birds or Nests loses 1 Attack and 1 Health for each Bird in your flock (your Birds on the board and away).
- Golden: loses 2 and 2 for each...
- Plays: no more all-or-nothing at seven. The size of the flock sets how much, and where Birds and Nests stand sets who gets hit. A flock of 5 covering 5 slots takes 25/25 off the enemy before the first swing. The Nest you chose two turns ago is now a choice of target.

**Old Crane** (rewrite of a vanilla 8/6, CORE). Tier 6, 6/6.
- Text: Fly South. Start of your turn: each of your Birds away gains +1/+1 permanently for each Nest you have (this one too, while away).
- Golden: +2/+2 for each...
- Plays: flying now pays you in stats, not only in gold. Three Birds away on three Nests each come home +6/+6 after a 2-turn trip. Migrants come back fatter.

**Buzzard** (rewrite of a vanilla 6/4, CORE). Tier 4, 4/5.
- Text: Your units standing on Nests have +1/+1 for each Bird away.
- Golden: +2/+2.
- Plays: whoever minds the Nest grows while the flock is gone, so a board that is half away no longer fights half-sized. Stand-ins can be any tribe, but the count is Birds away, so only a Bird player gets much from it.

### Bog folk

**The Peat King** (rewrite, CORE; drops the Peatlings). Tier 6, 5/9.
- Text: Mire. Start of Combat, after the bell: each Mired enemy loses 1 Attack for each Mired enemy (to no less than 1).
- Golden: 2 Attack for each...
- Plays: one Mire is a duel, six are a swamp. The count is the tribe's own puzzle: each patch needs a Bog folk across from an occupied enemy slot, so scouting and placement set the number. A 2-Mire splash takes 2 Attack off each of two enemies.

**Crested Newt** (rewrite of a vanilla 6/4, optional). Tier 4, 4/5.
- Text: Start of Combat: the units behind this gain +1 Attack this combat for each Mired enemy.
- Golden: +2 Attack.
- Plays: Newt in F2 and frogs in B1 and B2. The more of the enemy board is stuck, the harder your back row hits what's stuck.

### Hunters

**The Laird of the Sett** (rewrite, CORE). Tier 6, 4/7.
- Text: At the bell, all your units with no quarry take the enemy across from them as quarry. After each Take this fight, your Hunters gain +1/+1 this combat.
- Golden: +2/+2.
- Plays: the count is what the tribe did this fight. Each Take grows every Hunter, so the 4th Take hits harder than the 1st. Lent quarries let a splash unit aim, but only Hunters make Takes (existing rule) and only Hunters grow.

**Stoat Captain** (rewrite of a vanilla 5/5, optional). Tier 4, 3/5.
- Text: Quarry. Start of Combat: the units behind this gain +1/+1 this combat for each of your Hunters with a quarry.
- Golden: +2/+2.
- Plays: a front-row officer whose back row grows with how well you lined up your marks. An empty slot across a Hunter costs you here too.

### Neutrals and spells

**Hiring Fair** (change, CORE). Tier 5 spell, cost 6.
- Text: Discover a tier 6 unit. If 4 or more of your units share a tribe, all three choices are of that tribe.
- Plays: the 6 gold now finds the payoff your board was built for. That turns "6 gold wasted" (playtest) into the moment a committed player cashes in. A mixed board still gets a random tier 6.

## 4. Birds

**The game-side problem.** Every Bird that flies takes stats off the board (pinned Bird boards are only a third Birds), Nests give nothing, a Bird comes back as it left, and the one payoff (Whistlers) is a 3/3 that needs seven different Birds.

**The direction: away is where Birds grow, and Nests are where the fight feels it.** That keeps Fly South's bargain (tempo now for value later) and Nests' idea (a slot chosen two turns ahead), and makes the bargain pay:

1. **Old Crane:** Birds away grow each turn by your Nest count, and come home bigger.
2. **Buzzard:** the stand-ins on Nests grow by the number of Birds away. A half-empty board fights at full size.
3. **Seven Whistlers:** each Nest and Bird is an aimed debuff whose size is the flock.

**"Returning Birds need free slots"** (optional rule, small): *a Bird coming home may be played onto its own Nest even if a unit stands there; that unit goes to your hand.* It's one swap button. The Nest is the Bird's reserved seat, and the rotation stops costing a sale.

Mrs Dunnock, Mr Redbreast, The Great Lump and Mrs Stork stay as they are: they already read Nests and the away list, and now sit inside a payoff.

## 5. Why Oozes and Birds are almost never picked

- **Oozes.** Mostly the bot's prior (measure section 6: pinned Oozes place about average). Game-side, a Belly works for any tribe: one Little Dollop holding a Hedgerow unit gets most of what Bellies offer, and no Ooze text reads Oozes, so the 4th Ooze adds nothing. Elder Ooze, Figgy Duff and Bog Ooze count what only an Ooze board has: several puddings next to each other, and many bodies coming out. **The bot needs `strategy_fit` weight and `power` for these cards, or it will skip them again for the wrong reason.**
- **Birds.** Weak as a game: flying costs board stats and nothing gives them back. Old Crane and Buzzard pay stats for exactly the thing Birds do (being away, minding Nests), and the Whistlers pay for the flock's size and spread. The bot also needs a flier policy (when to sell, who stands on Nests). Without one, the bench will still show Birds as weak.

## 6. How a game plays

**Committed (Hedgerow, rounds 9-14).** Takes Standing Committee at tier 5 with a run of 4 (+2 each). Re-orders so F1-B1-F2-B2-F3 is all Hedgerow. Sells the Ringed Pixie off B3 for a Hoglet Mother, so the run is 6 (+4 each, 24 Attack). Casts Hiring Fair with 6 Hedgerow on board: three Hedgerow tier 6s, takes Old Mother Mast. The run of 7 gives 5 Acorns a turn, which pay for a Stand Behind Me each turn and a Committee triple. Every new Hedgerow unit is now worth its card plus 7-12 Attack a fight.

**Mixed (Hedgerow 3, Fairy 2, Hunter 2, same rounds).** Same Committee: the run is 3 (+1 each, 3 Attack). Hiring Fair gives a random tier 6. Their Laird makes Takes but grows only two Hunters, and their Monarch doubles only two Rings. They still have Stand Behind Me and Rings, so they don't fold. But at round 14 they're missing the 25-40 combat stats the committed board gets from one card, and both boards spend the same gold.

**Why the committed one wins more:** the generic stat sources are equal for both. The count payoffs are superlinear and positional, so only the committed board collects them, and they come from tier 5-6 cards that arrive when the generic sources stop scaling.

## 7. New rules or engine needs

Rules: **the run** (a defined term, Hedgerow only), and optionally the **homecoming swap**. No new keywords.

Engine (most fit `per` with a selector or a counter):
- `per: run`, a selector for the contiguous same-tribe segment of the Line.
- `per` over `mired` enemies, `nests`, `away` Birds, and a flock count (board plus away list).
- Fight counters: "units come out of Bellies this fight" and "Takes this fight".
- Buffing units on the away list at `start_of_turn` (Old Crane).
- An aura onto units standing on Nest slots (Buzzard).
- A condition for Hiring Fair: the largest tribe count on board, which also names the Discover tribe.
- Bot: `strategy_fit` entries for every count card.

## 8. Risks

- **It doesn't touch the generic 40%.** Stand Behind Me and Rings still pile stats onto the first bodies bought. This package gives commitment an upside, but someone still has to cut the generic sources, or both players simply add the same spells.
- **Superlinear means swingy.** A run of 7 or a 7-Mire swamp may decide a fight at the bell ("I lost before the first swing"). Levers: cap the per-count number, or use "beyond the third" everywhere.
- **One-tribe shops.** A tribe-count payoff makes the shared pool matter more: two Hedgerow players starve each other. Hiring Fair's tribe Discover softens it.
- **Figgy Duff and Old Crane are permanent growth.** They could become the new stacked giant: hidden inside a Belly, or a Bird that comes home 30/30. Levers: a cap per unit, or Crane only while the Bird has a Nest.
- **Monarch's doubled Rings** on a Ringed Fairy carry could bring the Queen-of-Elfland stack back by another road. Watch the biggest-unit share (measure section 5).
- **Bots can't value counts** until `strategy_fit` knows them, so the first bench numbers will understate the package.
