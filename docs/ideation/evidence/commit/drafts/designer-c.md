# Designer C: tribe capstones

*Approach: one new top-tier card per tribe that turns the tribe's own mechanic into a late engine only a committed board can run. Six in, six weak top-tier cards out. Numbers by feel, for the costing reviewer.*

## 1. The idea in three sentences

Each tribe gets one tier-5 or tier-6 card that counts the tribe and only works through the tribe's own mechanic, so the fifth, sixth and seventh unit of a tribe finally pay. Each capstone answers the one-huge-unit board in the tribe's own way: the parish turns out and stones it, the bog drowns it in a chain of toads, the Ooze swallows it, the rooks pass judgment on it, the fairies elf-shoot it, the fox steals it. The bigger the enemy's stat pile, the more each capstone takes, so piling generic stats on one body becomes a risk and holding your tribe becomes the way to win.

## 2. Why it fixes "committing doesn't pay"

- **"No card anywhere rewards the fifth, sixth or seventh unit of a tribe"** (measure §2). Each capstone scales with, or thresholds on, its tribe count; three peak at seven of the tribe, today the worst number (4.94).
- **Tribe-to-tribe buffing is 1-2% of stats, while spells and Rings are 40-42%** (§3). The capstones don't outbuff Stand Behind Me; they tax the stack, reading the enemy with the most Attack or Health. A mixed board whose stats sit on one carry loses the most.
- **A tier-6 body is smaller than a round-10 tier 1** (§4). A capstone's value is its text, not its body, so it matters bought in round 11, and a Hiring Fair that finds one isn't wasted.
- **Why a splash can't take them.** Each capstone needs both a count of the tribe *and* the tribe's own keyword to work. Alone, each is a weak tech card.

## 3. Card changes and new cards

**Core: the six capstones.** Optional: the Hiring Fair change under Neutrals.

### Hedgerow folk: The Bound-Beater (replaces The Standing Committee)

**The Bound-Beater.** *Hedgehog, Beating the Parish Bounds.* Tier 5, 3/6, Hedgerow folk.
- **Text:** Start of Combat: each Hedgerow folk in this unit's Line, this included, throws a conker at the enemy with the most Health, for 1 damage per Hedgerow folk in the Line.
- **Golden (6/12):** Same, for 2 damage per Hedgerow folk in the Line.
- **How it plays.** The Line is the touching zigzag F1-B1-F2-B2-F3-B3-F4 that Pass already uses. Three in the Line throw 3 conkers of 3 (9 damage); five throw 25; seven throw 49. Each conker is a separate hit, and the target is re-picked per throw, so conkers move on once the giant shrinks.
- **Asks of the board:** 4 to 7 Hedgerow folk touching, with no gap. Tokens count. Spill wants gaps, so the player has to choose: a gap for the Acorn, or one more hand in the Line?
- **Why a splash can't:** damage grows with the square of the count. Two Hedgerow folk in a Line deal 4.
- **Replaces The Standing Committee.** Its +2 Attack aura helps any tribe.

### Fairies: The Elf-Shooter (replaces the tier-6 Belladonna Sprite)

**The Elf-Shooter.** *Fairy Archer of the Rade.* Tier 6, 4/6, Fairy.
- **Text:** Start of Combat: each of your Fairies standing on a Ring, this included, shoots elf-shot at the enemy with the most Attack: it loses that Ring's Attack and Health this combat, but not below 1 Health.
- **Golden (8/12):** Same, but it loses twice that Ring's Attack and Health.
- **How it plays.** Four Fairies on +3/+3 Rings take 12/12 off the biggest enemy before the first attack. Each shot picks its target again. Rings (11-12% of all board stats today, worn by anyone) now pay twice when a Fairy wears them: as stats and as a shot.
- **Asks of the board:** 3 or more Fairies standing on Rings. Shy Fairies walk off Rings, so Shy and this card pull against each other.
- **Why a splash can't:** a Fairy splash makes Rings for its other-tribe carry, and that carry makes no shot. One Fairy on a Ring is one shot.
- **Replaces the tier-6 Belladonna Sprite 4/5.** It shares the tier-5 card's name, and its one tribe-free Nightshade does the same job worse.

### Oozes: The Bottomless Pot (replaces Figgy Duff)

**The Bottomless Pot.** *Pudding Cloth, Never Emptied.* Tier 6, 4/9, Ooze.
- **Text:** Belly 1. Start of Combat: if this has room, swallow the enemy with the most Attack, wherever it stands. It goes back to its side, with half its Attack and Health, when this dies or when you have no other Ooze on the board.
- **Golden (8/18):** Belly 2. Same, but swallow the two enemies with the most Attack.
- **How it plays.** The giant leaves the fight for as long as your Ooze line stands. The opponent chews through Bellies and their contents before the giant returns at half size. A unit holding others can't be swallowed (no nesting), so the Pot takes the next-biggest.
- **Asks of the board:** 3 or more other Oozes, ideally with full Bellies, standing in front of the Pot; the Pot's own Belly kept empty.
- **Why a splash can't:** with no other Ooze the giant comes straight back. With one other Ooze it comes back after that Ooze's first death.
- **Replaces Figgy Duff 3/4,** named by playtesters as a wasted tier-6 offer.

### Birds: The Parliament of Rooks (replaces The Seven Whistlers)

**The Parliament of Rooks.** *Rookery, in Session.* Tier 6, 4/7, Bird.
- **Text:** Start of Combat: count your Birds, on the board or away. The enemy with the most Attack loses an eighth of its Attack and Health for each, up to seven eighths, this combat. Rounded down.
- **Golden (8/14):** Same, for each of the two enemies with the most Attack.
- **How it plays.** Rooks are said to hold courts and mob the guilty one. Four Birds halve the giant; seven leave a 38/42 Queen at 4/5. Birds away count, so a Bird player who owns ten Birds, seven on the board and three in the air, still gets the full count, and flying no longer costs the payoff.
- **Asks of the board:** 5 to 7 Birds owned. Up to 3 of them can be away, so the board can still hold 3 or 4 walls from another tribe.
- **Why a splash can't:** two Birds take a quarter.
- **Replaces The Seven Whistlers 3/3.** It counted the same thing but did almost nothing below seven. Playtesters named it as a wasted tier-6 offer.

### Bog folk: Peg Powler (replaces Bitter Toad)

**Peg Powler.** *River Hag of the Tees.* Tier 5, 2/8, Bog folk.
- **Text:** Mire, on the enemy with the most Attack wherever it stands, not the one across; this lays its patch before your other mirers. Bitter 2. While you have another Bog folk, a lock from this doesn't end when its holder dies: it passes to your living Bog folk nearest the slot where the holder died, and that Bog folk becomes the lock's maker.
- **Golden (4/16):** Same, with Bitter 4.
- **How it plays.** The giant must swing at Peg. When she dies, it must swing at the nearest toad, then the next, and Bitter fires on every link. A passed lock makes its new holder the maker, so Madame Bufo next in line halves the giant when it kills her, and Jenny Greenteeth's hook fires. Snap units (Young Pretender, Vaulting Hopkin) go for the Mired enemy with the most Attack wherever it stands, so they all pile onto it. The layout of the chain is the puzzle.
- **Asks of the board:** 4 or more Bog folk, Bitter ones near Peg. Her own slot doesn't matter for aim, only for the order of the chain.
- **Why a splash can't:** alone, Peg pulls the giant onto a 2/8 and dies after one swing, taking 2 Attack with her. That is the "Mire on a big enemy wastes the mirer" problem, unsolved.
- **Replaces Bitter Toad 3/7.** Its anti-giant text (Nightshade on damage) works for any tribe and never touches Mire.

### Hunters: Reynard (replaces The Mountain Glutton)

**Reynard.** *The Old Fox, Still at Large.* Tier 5, 3/6, Hunter.
- **Text:** While you have 4 or more Hunters, your Hunters reach their quarry wherever it stands. After one of your Hunters makes a Take, it steals: it gains permanently a quarter of what its quarry had gained on top of its card, but at least +1/+1 per Hunter you have.
- **Golden (6/12):** Same, but half, and at least +2/+2 per Hunter. The threshold stays at 4.
- **How it plays.** The scouted back-row carry is now huntable: your B2 Hunter marks their B2 giant at the bell and goes straight for it, with Pack joining and Pounce piling on. Kill it and the Hunter keeps a quarter of its stack. On ordinary Takes the floor grows a five-Hunter board +5/+5 a Take: the owner's "medium units" comp, earned by aiming.
- **Asks of the board:** 4 or more Hunters, placed across the scouted lanes you want. At least one in the back row if the giant hides there.
- **Why a splash can't:** below 4 Hunters there's no reach, and two Hunters get +2/+2 per Take.
- **Replaces The Mountain Glutton 4/5.** Its gain lasts only the fight, and playtesters found the Hunter top end flat.

### Neutrals and spells (optional)

**Hiring Fair** (tier 5, cost 6). **New text:** Discover a tier-6 unit. If you have 4 or more units of one tribe, one of the three choices is of that tribe.
- Plays: this is how a committed player who reaches tier 5 goes looking for the capstone. It ties the most expensive spell to commitment without a new rule. The Hedgerow, Bog folk and Hunter capstones are tier 5, so a tribe with no tier-6 left in the pool gets a normal offer.

## 4. Birds

**Why they fail:** flown Birds leave 0/2 Eggs, so pinned Birds boards are a third Birds and place about 5.3; a trip costs two fights of a body when a loss costs 9-15 Health; the top end was a 3/3.

**The direction: away Birds still count, and the Nest keeps the Bird's place.**

1. **Core: The Parliament of Rooks** (above). It counts Birds away, so owning more Birds than you have slots, which is the tribe's real difference, becomes the win condition.
2. **Core rule change, the Nest Egg.** Today: "A Nest holds a 0/2 Egg in fights." **New:** "While a Bird is away, the Egg on its Nest fights with that Bird's Attack and Health, buffs included, and no text." Fly South then costs only the Bird's text and its slot for two fights, not its body. Spells on Birds keep paying while they fly, and Eggs stop being free kills. The Great Lump stays the better Nest-sitter (its own stats plus the Bird's card stats).
3. **Optional:** Mrs Stork's threshold falls from 6 gold to 5. Not needed if (2) lands.

Fly South's idea stays whole; the Nest becomes something on the board.

## 5. Why Oozes and Birds are almost never picked

**Oozes.** Mostly the bot's hand-set priors (no `power`, +3-4 a unit). Pinned, Oozes place about average. On the game side, a Belly is defence only: what's inside takes no buffs, adds no hero damage and does nothing until the Ooze dies. Its top end wins nothing alone (Figgy Duff 3/4). **What changes:** the Bottomless Pot gives Oozes the clearest late goal in the game ("their best unit isn't in this fight"). Each extra Ooze keeps the giant out longer, so depth becomes the win. The bot prior needs a `power` value too (engine, not design).

**Birds.** Weak as a game (section 4). **What changes:** the Egg keeps the body on the board, the Parliament pays for Birds away, and both reward the tenth Bird rather than the third.

## 6. How a game plays with this

**Committed: Bog folk, all game.** Round 6, tier 3: Madame Bufo, Warty Nan, Peatling, Young Pretender, Natterjack Chorus, plus a Pill Bug wall. Knowing Peg Powler wants toads to pass to, they skip a tempting Fairy. Round 10: Peg is found. The next opponent's scout shows a Queen of Elfland at 30/34 in B2, stacked with Stand Behind Me. Peg goes in F1, Bufo in F2, Peatling and Nan behind. At the bell the Queen is Mired onto Peg, past their front row. Peg dies; Bitter takes the Queen to 28. The lock passes to Bufo: she dies, Bitter takes the Queen to 26 and Bufo's halving to 13. Young Pretender Snaps at her and the Peatling finishes her. Two cheap toads for their whole build.

**Mixed: Hedgerow walls, Fairy Rings, spells on one carry.** Round 10, a 30/34 carry on a Ring among 6/8s. Against the Elf-Shooter it loses 12/12 at the bell; against six Birds' Parliament it fights at 8/9; against the Pot it sits out until the Oozes fall; Reynard's Hunters hunt it in the back row and keep a quarter. It is still the lobby's biggest unit, but every committed opponent now holds a card built for it, and the mixed player's splash can run none of them.

**Why the committed player now wins more:** a reason to hold the fifth to seventh unit now exists, and it lands on the giant shape mixed spell piles make. Wide boards of medium units remain the counter-shape.

## 7. New rules and engine needs

No new keywords. One rule change (the Birds' Egg). Engine work, most of it extending what exists:

- A "most Attack / most Health enemy" selector, re-picked per hit (the tier-6 Belladonna's pick is the model).
- Tribe counts: on the board; on the board or away (as Whistlers counts); in the Line (Pass's walk).
- Ring stats read per slot; Health loss with a floor of 1 (as Nightshade).
- Swallow at range, release on "no other Ooze on the board", return at half (Hasty Pudding's model).
- Mire lock reassignment: a patch's `maker` changes on death, nearest by slot distance.
- Reach granted by a count (Old Lutra's reach); a permanent gain on Take reading "gained on top of its card" (Jack Daw reads it).
- Egg stats from its Bird (The Great Lump reads it).
- **Bots** need a `power` and a count bonus on `strategy_fit` for each capstone, or Hard bots will splash past them.

## 8. Risks

- **Six anti-giant cards may over-punish tall.** The owner names "one or two really big units" as a real comp. Each capstone hits one or two enemies; costing should check tall still wins sometimes.
- **Hard swings.** The Bottomless Pot is near-removal, and the Parliament at seven reaches seven eighths. Possible caps: four eighths; the Pot's giant back after 3 of your deaths, as Hasty Pudding.
- **Peg plus Bufo is a cheap two-card combo.** A splash with two Bog folk gets two links and a halving. Watch it.
- **Reynard snowballs.** The floor of +1/+1 per Hunter per Take could outgrow Stand Behind Me on its own.
- **The Bound-Beater's square** may be too strong for the tribe already picked most (38%). A cap of 36 damage keeps the commitment shape.
- **Readability.** Five capstones fire at the bell. Replays must draw each shot, line or lock.
- **The Egg change** may make Fly South too free. If so, give the Egg the Bird's card stats only (no buffs).
