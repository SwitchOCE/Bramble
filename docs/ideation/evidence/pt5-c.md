# Playtest pt5-c: one game per tribe (build 7, b2c3f01)

Fresh tester, `tools/play/play.sh`, full-strength bots. Seeds 101, 202–206.

**Places:** Hedgerow 6th · Bog folk **1st** · Oozes 7th · Fairies **2nd** · Birds 7th · Hunters 7th.

**Verdict: okay.** Not yet "very good": two tribes carry the game and two feel like traps.

## What's best
- **Rainbow (Fairies):** painting slots turns the board into a puzzle. To swap a unit, sell it and play the new one into the same slot to repaint it. 12–0 start.
- **Bog folk Mire into Prince:** three kisses turned golden His Highness into a 12/14 Prince Restored. I came back from 1 Health and won from there.
- **Changeling:** it swaps places with the enemy unit across, and Rings stay with their slot. It is a real, readable answer to one huge unit.

## Top 5 problems
1. **Tribe power is uneven.** Hedgerow, Oozes and Hunters units are 1–3 Attack bodies that don't grow until tier 5+. Committing to them lost 4–6 mid-game fights per game. Rainbow Chaser was the strongest unit in every lobby: 34/38 on a bot. *Alone stops very good.*
2. **Birds are a trap.** I saw one Bird in five shops from round 4 to 6. Most Birds are vanilla bodies, and a Fly South return gives little (Chiffchaff: +1 Attack). *Alone stops very good.*
3. **Last-four swings are huge.** In game 4 I went from 40 Health to out in four fights (14, 19, 22, 18), after a 12–0 start.
4. **Text is wrong or hidden** (see W).
5. **Things fail silently when there's no room.** The Mousewife hero and Wee Wobbly do nothing when the slot is taken, and full boards make that common.

## Builds that felt different
Rainbow painting · Mire lanes with Snap · Changeling and Hasty Pudding as lane answers · banking gold for Charcoal Burner, then Beltane Bonfire.

## Lens
- **Hedgerow:** a grab-bag of Pass, Spill, Acorns and Bristle with no early identity. The real payoff comes late: stacked Standing Committees and a 46/52 golden Dozey, both on a bot.
- **Bog folk:** clearest identity, and a real payoff. It starts slowly, because before tier 3 only Bridge Toad has Mire.
- **Oozes:** Belly is fiddly, and Aunt Aspic grows slowly. Hasty Pudding is great.
- **Fairies:** strongest identity and payoff, probably too strong.
- **Hunters:** the quarry lines are readable and Takes pay gold, but the bodies stay small.
- **Thinnest:** Birds. **Trap:** Birds, then Hunters.

**(P)** Placement won three fights: Changeling at B1 across a 34/38 Chaser on a +9/+9 Ring (G2 final), Changeling across a 46/52 Dozey (G4, +16), and Pudding across a 9/3 Burdock (G3). Placement lost two: Sable's quarry was a Hex-8 Hedge Witch, which cut it to 0 Attack (G6), and Ichneumon Wasp across a Chaser was useless, because normal attacks pick a random target.

**(B)** I repainted slots by selling and replaying, moved Changeling to face the biggest Ringed unit, put Mrs Dunnock on the Nest, and saved up 30+ gold for Bonfire or Last Orders.

**(W)**
- *Queen of Elfland:* "Your other Fairies split their stats" is wrong. They share the *destroyed* units' stats. Her Teind also destroyed my new Fairy Monarch, and only the hidden counter warned me.
- *Hasty Pudding:* swallowed enemies go back to their side, but only DETAILS says so.
- *The Mountain Glutton:* doesn't say whether its +2/+2 is permanent.
- *Fight log:* says "bogs the enemy's F3" where the cards say Mire.
- Hedge Witch reaching Hex 8 from a tier-1 card surprised me.
