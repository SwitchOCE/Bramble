# Critic: feel and the owner's notes (tribes 3)

Paper critique. Nothing here has been played. I read all three designs, every owner note, archetypes.md, direction-summary.md, the four built tribes and neutrals, playtest-1 and design-review-2. I don't cost anything here; the costing reviewer does that.

## The shared problem: three tribes, one lane

"Across" is no longer a tribe signature. It's the game's grammar. Built content already aims across in nine places: Sergeant-at-Conkers, Choirboy Tobias, Hedge Witch, Changeling, Belladonna, Hasty Pudding, Mother Goose, Mag Pye, Turnip Scarecrow, plus Wormwood Charm and Mother Snare. All three candidates build on it again, and they overlap with each other more than with anything built:

| Pattern | Hunters | Bog folk | Great beasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **My attack goes across** | Quarry (every attack) | (Snap aims at Mired enemies, not lanes) | Charge (first attack) |
| **Their unit across must attack me, even in my back row** | **Bait** | **Mire** | **Big Ursula** |
| Counterattack when attacked | Ambush | Gideon's attack-back | Ursula's return |

Bait and Mire are the same mechanic with two names. Each designer read the owner's Mire musing (02h) the same way: "it makes their backline attack into you". Ursula is a one-card copy of both. Quarry and Charge are near-twins for the first attack of the fight.

**So each tribe needs to own one direction, and lose the others:**
- **Hunters: I choose my prey.** Your attacks go across, and deaths across pay you. That's offence, read from their board.
- **Bog folk: I choose their attacker.** Their attacks come to you. That's defence and attrition, also read from their board.
- **Great beasts: I need room to run.** The charge goes through gaps and into what's behind. When they come back, their read should be *your own* empty space (Room, Run-up), not their lanes.

Built together, Hunters and Bog folk are complements: one pulls and one hunts. Hunters with Great beasts would be two "aim across" tribes in one lobby. Bog folk with Great beasts is clean too, but it leaves the most familiar cast and two of the owner's warmest reactions on file (see below).

**A note on citations.** Both the Bog folk and Hunters docs say the owner *asked* for the backline-attacks-into-you idea. He didn't: "I was just kind of exploring the idea... If it seems like I'm not asking you to do anything with it, I'm not" (02i). Building on it is fine, because reactions are directions. But the docs should say "built on the owner's musing", not "which the owner asked about".

## Bog folk: **strong**

**Feel.** This answers "I don't really understand what this design is" with one picture: a mud patch under their unit, and a reed-line to your toad. The flavour drives the play at every step:
- The bog sticks a unit in place (Mire).
- Toads taste bitter, so biting one weakens the attacker (Bitter is bufotoxin).
- Frogs flick their tongues at whatever is stuck (Snap).
- Leapfrog goes over the back of the unit in front.

A player can picture all of it. The tone is right too: cozy-spooky swamp opera with Tiddy Mun, Jenny Greenteeth and a frog who may really be a prince. It's no Murloc re-skin: there's no Battlecry and no tribe-count buffs. Battlegrounds has nothing like "choose who hits whom". It answers the playtest's huge-carry ending with a counter instead of a cap: their giant wrestles your toad and shrinks.

**Fun to play against?** Mostly yes. The lock ends when the toad dies, Bitter stops at 1 Attack, and the mud and reed-line make it readable ("my boar spent the fight chewing toads"). The real feel-bad is the Prima Donna in B2 holding their whole back row. The designer's fallback is right (Bitter on first attack only).

**Fun to play?** The risk is that "almost nothing happens in the Recruit Phase". Placement against the scout and the Prince are the recruit-phase toys, so keep both prominent.

**Problems:**
1. **Kiss copies Fairies' Shy.** Shy: "give it stats and it leaves, paying you". Kiss: "a buff gives gold instead". That's the same hook in two tribes, and Shy is in the slice's signature list.
2. **The Prince Restored is a human, and "Start of your turn: gain 2 gold" pays for doing nothing.** The setting is animal folk, fairies and oozes, with no humans. And gold every turn whatever you do breaks "economy tied to actions" (02h). A one-time quest first doesn't change that.
3. **The Peat King and the Bog-Bodies come from real human bog bodies.** The hook says "Kings were found in Irish peat". Those are real sacrificed people, which is darker than cozy-spooky. Raising a dead enemy on your side also treads on Fairies' line "an enemy can wake up on your side" (Changeling).
4. **Toll Toad is passive by mid-game.** The designer notes that most enemies have 4+ Attack by then. Its name also clashes with Hunters' Toll.
5. **Leapfrog Jack** is a frog whose name is close to Battlegrounds' frog **Leapfrogger** (card library).
6. Jenny Greenteeth's hook ("pulls children under") is the one line that's horror rather than cozy-spooky.
7. Phone length: His Highness and the Peat King run three lines or more.

**Rulings and rewrites:**
- **His Highness, Allegedly** (T4 3/5): "Snap. Spells give this no stats; each is a kiss. On the third kiss, he becomes the Prince Restored." Golden: the same, becoming a golden Prince. *No gold back, so it's no longer Shy. Still anti-stack.*
- **The Prince Restored** (6/7). *Frog, Rightful Prince of the Bog* (the crown finally fits; no human). "Mire. Your first spell each turn costs 1 less." Golden: 2 less. *The gold now comes from buying spells, the same thing that woke him.*
- **Bridge Toad** (was Toll Toad; T1 1/4): "Mire. Start of Combat: if the enemy this Mires is their highest-Attack unit, get 1 gold next turn." Golden 2. *Pays only for reading the scout right.*
- **The Peat King** (T6 6/10). *Toad, Old as the Moss*: "Mire. Twice a fight, after a Mired enemy dies, a 4/4 Peat-Man with Bitter 2 rises in your empty slot nearest this." Golden: 8/8, Bitter 4. *No stealing, no human remains. The bog makes its own.* Art: a vast peat-brown toad half sunk in moss, a reed crown.
- **Vaulting Jack** (was Leapfrog Jack; T2 3/2): "Snap. In your back row, attacks before your front row, with +2 Attack."
- **Jenny Greenteeth** hook: "Stay off the duckweed, dears. She's only lonely."
- **Mire reminder** (phone): "Start of Combat: the enemy across must attack this while this lives."
- **Spitfire Sal, Gideon, Bufo, Peat Cutter, Pretender, Chorus, Tiddy Mun:** text fine for feel. Leave Tiddy Mun's runaway check to costing.

**Overlaps with built signatures:** Kiss and Shy (fixed above); Peat King and Changeling (fixed above). Mire doesn't overlap Belly or Fly South. Renaming the Scarecrow's existing `mired` status to `stalled` is right.

## Hunters: **workable as written, strong after a trim**

**Feel.** It's the best fit to the setting: fox, stoat, badger, otter and wolf, the other half of the Wind-in-the-Willows and Reynard cast (archetypes ranked it #4). The flavour drives the play:
- A hunter picks its prey (Quarry).
- "A fox claims every kill in its lane" (Take) is the best line of flavour-as-rule in all three docs.
- Wolves hunt as a pack.
- The fox steals from the larder (Poach).

It hits two of the owner's warmest reactions head-on. Swarm ("really, really interesting to me") becomes Pack. "I don't know what 'foxes take a shop card' means" becomes Poach, with a worked example. The glutton loses its cap, as the owner suggested. Pack's split hits answer Shell, as the owner wanted from Peck. Every economy card pays for a Take or a lane, so it's tied to actions. It's not a Battlegrounds Beasts re-skin, because aimed-by-slot attacks don't exist there.

**Fun to play?** Yes. "Who do I aim at whom?" is a new question every round, and the opponent can see your lanes and dodge, which makes a guessing game with real information.

**Fun to play against?** Mostly. Poach takes from the pool, not their board: interactive, not cruel. The feel-bad card is **The Laird**, which rewrites the *opponent's* targeting too. Light-Fingered Nan (gold theft) is rightly kept in reserve.

**Problems:**
1. **Seven keywords** (Quarry, Take, Bait, Ambush, Pack, Poach, Toll) on 14 cards. That's too much to read on a phone, and the tribe's one-sentence identity gets lost.
2. **Bait is Bog folk's Mire.** The Sett build leans on it.
3. **"Tod Scrapling"**: Mr Tod is Beatrix Potter's fox, and Tod is Disney's fox in The Fox and the Hound. That's another work's character in this exact setting.
4. **Sable's hook "Winter is coming"** is Game of Thrones' line.
5. Tone: the intro says "nobody dies, they faint", then the wolverine "came for the funeral".
6. Phone length: Brother Wolf, Ermeline and Grizel run long.
7. The economy is win-more: rich when ahead, poor when behind. The Toll-Bard is the one counterweight, so it needs to be in the core set.

**Rulings and rewrites:**
- **Drop Bait as a keyword; it belongs to Bog folk as Mire.** Give the badgers the badger's own verb, digging, which pulls their backline *out* instead of pulling attacks *in*:
  - **Old Brock** (T4 3/7): "Start of Combat: dig out the enemy across: move it to their nearest empty front slot. Your units next to this have Ambush." Golden: also +2 Attack to those units.
  - **Young Brock** (T1 1/4): "After the enemy across attacks, your units beside this gain +1 Attack this fight." Golden +2. *He watches his lane and needs no forced targeting.*
- **Toll and Poach become printed text, not keywords.** That leaves four keywords: Quarry, Take, Pack, Ambush.
  - **The Toll-Bard**: "After the enemy across attacks, get 1 gold next turn (3 times a fight)."
  - **Mistress Russet**: "Quarry. Take: next turn your shop has a copy of that unit, 1 gold cheaper."
- **The Laird of the Sett** (T6 5/9): "Start of Combat: your units gain Quarry this fight. Those with an enemy across gain +2/+2 this fight." *The opponent's board stays theirs.*
- **Brother Wolf** (T3 4/5): "Pack. While it has a neighbour, it never leads, but joins every neighbour's attack."
- **Madame Ermeline** (T4 3/6): "After each fight, your next upgrade costs 1 less per Take (up to 3). At tier 6, gold instead."
- **Grizel** (T5 5/6): "Pack. After a Pack attack kills, the leader and its joiners gain +1/+1 permanently."
- **Little Nip** (was Tod Scrapling). *Fox Cub, Errand-Runner*, same text.
- Sable hook: "The Court's summer assassins dress in brown. They are counting the days to ermine."
- Intro: the wolverine "came for a christening and stayed for the buffet."
- Low-Water Jack, Wick, The Lodger, Glutton: fine for feel.

**Overlaps with built signatures:** none with Belly or Fly South. Poach adds a shop slot, which no built tribe owns. Take and Hasty Pudding interact without overlapping.

## Great beasts: **workable; keep on file**

**Feel.** The flavour-to-play table is the most direct answer any doc gives the owner's "I don't really see how it connects to the gameplay". Each line is drawn:
- Charges straight ahead, so the attack goes across.
- Breaks a thin line, so it runs through the gap.
- Bowls through, so it tramples.
- Thick hide, so Hide caps the first hit.
- Needs space, so Room.
- Ruts, so Rut.

Hide is the owner's Shell exactly as he dictated it. Trample is his "overkill splashing through" (10-27b), and he called it cool (02h). The tribe now has an economy, as he insisted (02h, 02i). Not a re-skin.

**Fun to play?** The charge is a good moment. The Room build ("is this a five-unit or seven-unit board?") is a real choice.

**Fun to play against?** Fine. Hide and Ursula are cheap answers to giants. Trodden blocking refills is a hard counter to Oozes' Belly, though not a lock.

**Problems:**
1. **Seven keywords:** Charge, Trample, Hide, Room, Rut, Trophy and Trodden, plus Hunker borrowed from Hedgerow on two cards. The identity sprawls.
2. **"Charge" is Hearthstone's keyword for "can attack at once".** Every Battlegrounds player will misread it.
3. **Its first attack goes across, like Hunters' Quarry.** Ursula's forced attack is Mire again.
4. **Old Grazer pays gold each turn for slots left empty.** That's closest to the owner's "Fast": "rewards you for not playing the game". The designer saw it.
5. It's a TALL tribe in a game whose loudest playtest complaint is one stacked unit. The mitigations are good (charge payoffs count chargers; the Elk's size lasts only the fight), but it carries the most risk of feeding the problem.

**Rulings and rewrites (for when it's built):**
- **Rename Charge to Headlong, and make it read your own board:** "Headlong: its first attack from a front slot goes at the enemy across, or through the gap behind it, and tramples. Needs an empty slot behind this." *This is the designer's Run-up, and it's the identity split. Hunters read their board; beasts need room on yours. Few, huge, with space to run.*
- **Old Grazer** (T2 2/5): "Hide 4. Once a turn, after you sell a unit next to this, gain 1 gold." *Opening the pasture pays: an action, not a wait.*
- **Big Ursula** (T5 6/10): "Hide 8. When her Hide breaks, deal the damage it stopped to the unit that hit her." *She loses the forced attack, which is Bog folk's.*
- **The Bison Elders** (T5 5/9): "Hide 6. Your Headlong attacks deal +3 damage, and their trample doesn't split." **Mother Wisent** (T4 3/8): "Hide 5. Start of Combat: units next to this gain Hide 5." *They keep Hide and leave Hunker to Hedgerow.*
- **Old Steinbock** (T3 3/5): "Headlong. If its target survives, swap it with the strongest enemy behind it, and deal 2 to both."
- **Old Tusker** (T4 5/5): "Headlong. After a friendly unit charges, your units yet to attack gain +2 Attack this fight."
- **Rut, Trophy and Trodden become printed text,** each on one or two cards. That leaves Headlong, Trample, Hide and Room as the keywords.

## The two I'd build: Bog folk and Hunters

- **They're complements, not rivals.** One pulls their attacks into you; the other sends yours into them. Both read the scout, from opposite sides of the same lane. With Bait moved to Bog folk, neither has the other's verb.
- **Together they carry the owner's warmest reactions and best asks.** Hunters bring Swarm (as Pack), the foxes' shop-card theft, and the glutton without a cap. Bog folk bring the clearest answer to "I don't understand this design" and the frog-prince hook.
- **Setting.** Hunters are the familiar cast, which the woodland needs alongside two magic tribes. Bog folk bring the spooky half of cozy-spooky, which no built tribe has.
- **Great beasts wait** because of identity, not quality. Next to Hunters, Charge blurs with Quarry. Its fix (Headlong reading your own empty space) should be playtested against Room first. It's the obvious next tribe after these two.

### Bog folk: first 8 (with the rewrites above)
1. **Bridge Toad** (T1): Mire and scouting gold. Teaches the tribe.
2. **Young Pretender** (T1): Snap, and gold for finishing a stuck enemy.
3. **Madame Bufo** (T2): Mire and Bitter 2. The core lock.
4. **Vaulting Jack** (T2): Snap from the back, before their carry swings.
5. **The Peat Cutter** (T2): gold when Mired enemies die.
6. **Gideon, Basso Profundo** (T3): Mire with an attack-back. The counterattack picture.
7. **Jenny Greenteeth** (T4): drags the protected carry into reach.
8. **His Highness, Allegedly** (T4): Kiss into the Prince Restored. The tribe's tagline made playable.

### Hunters: first 8 (with the rewrites above)
1. **Little Nip** (T1): Quarry, and gold per Take. The first lesson in reading the scout.
2. **Wick** (T1): Pack, growing per join.
3. **Sable** (T2): Quarry, best against their biggest.
4. **Mistress Russet** (T2): Quarry and the shop-card theft.
5. **The Toll-Bard** (T2): paid by the enemy you *don't* kill. The counterweight economy, competing for lanes.
6. **Brother Wolf** (T3): the pack's anchor.
7. **The Lodger** (T3): Ambush, the counterattack.
8. **Grizel** (T5): the lasting, spread-out scaling.

Young Brock, Old Brock, Low-Water Jack, Ermeline, the Glutton and the Laird follow in the next batch.
