One line per call that still holds. Newest calls go at the bottom of their group, with a short entry under "Entries" only if the line can't carry the reason. ## Phase rules (owner, 10 Nov: notes 10j and the plan)

- This phase is UI, UX and look and feel only. Rules, numbers, card behaviour, bots, sims and content are out, and the gate's scope-fence check enforces it. (`PLAN.md`, "Scope fence")
- Where the UI can't support a rule well, the rule changes: "If we can't make the UI work well, we can change game mechanics and systems" (owner, 11 Nov, note `2026-11-11c`). The orchestrator makes each such change itself, as its own small commit on `ideation/main` with a rulebook line, so the fence still keeps workers out of `src/core/` and `content/`. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- A loop ends when the owner has played its build, or 3 days after its day-1 gallery went out, whichever comes first. (`PLAN.md`, "Done for a loop")
- At most two or three focus items per loop, picked by the owner from the day-1 gallery.
- Each step: the gate plus one scope review. No numeric scores for looks; reviewers answer plain questions beside reference shots. No new bars or measurement plans unless the owner asks.
- Defaults until the owner says otherwise: sound out; tutorial and Rules reference in; PC landscape first, portrait later.
- Reference frames live outside git in `/data/bramble-reference/<game>/`, shared by every worktree and the gallery; the manifest and re-fetch README are in `docs/ideation/ui/reference/`. (orchestrator, 10 Nov: an in-repo folder isn't shared across worktrees)
- Step 0b's capture, backdrops and layout options are one step: one scope review over their branches together. (orchestrator, 10 Nov)
- The scope fence also covers `tests/sim/` and, once step 0 has merged, `scripts/check.sh`, so one edit can't switch the fence off. (orchestrator, 10 Nov, step 0 review ask)
- Loop 1 ends when the owner plays the build with the journey lab's layout (A3 and A4 below merged, then the build checks), not on 13 Nov: shipping Bazaar Stack would test a layout the owner has moved past. There is no merge freeze date. (orchestrator, 11 Nov, after note `2026-11-11c`: "make the choice")
- The layout is the owner's journey lab, Clean Arena layout B (`docs/design-lab/journey/HANDOFF.md` on `design-lab/journey`; review in `ui/loop1/journey-lab/`). Its 12 rules are strong direction, not pixel specs ("These aren't hard rules", owner, 11 Nov): your board never moves between recruit, scouting and fight; one unit size everywhere; the shop holds its gold and buttons; the standings are a portrait strip on the right; the hand sits left of the board and the hero and End turn right of it; rounded hex units whose back row tucks in. A2's framed surfaces and quiet empty slots carry over; the lab's slot labels ("B1") and two-turn travel schedule don't. It replaces Bazaar Stack (A). Focus items now: B (recruit feel), A3 (the lab's arrangement) and A4 (hex units). (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- The hand holds 4: at one unit size, a 2×2 tray left of the board is what fits at 16:9 beside a market wide enough for every offer. "If 4's what fits, let's do 4" (owner, note `2026-11-11c`). `game_config.gd` and rulebook 7.1.1. Cards that hand out several cards now do less, because fewer fit (Mast, Madame Ermeline, Mother Goose, Thorned Trailblazer, Crater Miner, One Swallow's Homecoming, Cut-and-Come-Again); their tests pin a 10-card hand so they still check the card's own logic, and fitting the cards to 4 is content work for later. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- The orchestrator has full authority over the look: cards, in-game assets and the art style itself, "even using different art if you choose. I want to see this brought to its conclusion." (owner, 11 Nov, note `2026-11-11a`). The portrait question is no longer the owner's. Loop 1 explores four unit-art styles on 8 units (scene-kept Ink-B, a colour grade of today's art, a painted storybook style, ink with wash and glow), picks one with an advisor check-in, then repaints every unit, hero and card to it. Never a mixed set in a merged build. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- Unit art becomes "ink with wash and glow": today's ink contour, simpler and larger watercolour wash shapes, one cream key light from the upper right with a rim light, and a background hue family per tribe. It beat painted storybook (softer edges at phone size, detail creep), scene-kept Ink-B and a colour grade (both still read as flat stickers on the painted world). Rollout: one frozen header and two style anchors for every batch; samples of spells, heroes, an item and a tribe image first; one contact sheet of all 155 at 150 px reviewed by the orchestrator. Go/no-go at midday 12 Nov; the fallback merges whole classes (all units and heroes), never a mix within a class. The card frame gets only a light pass after Bazaar Stack merges, and is cut first if time runs short. (orchestrator, 11 Nov; advisor check-in)
- Cards and backgrounds meet in the middle: "The cards are too low fidelity, too cartoony, and the background is too beautiful cel-shaded animation, and they need to come closer together" (owner, 11 Nov, note `2026-11-11b`). Ink with wash and glow becomes one recipe for both: the backdrops are repainted in it (ink contour, simpler and larger shapes, less light-ray polish), and the units go one notch more painted only if the first in-layout render still reads cartoony beside the new backdrop. That first render comes on 11 Nov, ahead of the go/no-go. The portrait grade shader goes, and the card frame gets only an outline-weight pass with no paper wash. (orchestrator, 11 Nov; advisor check-in)
- The card is paper lit by the world: warm paper face, a thin olive-bark edge, a cream highlight on the upper-right edge and a soft lower-left contact shadow, with the outline matched to the portraits' ink. It came from the first unit-art study and stays. The world art (two backdrops, foreground, stump) is one class, like the portraits. (orchestrator, 11 Nov, art step review ask)
- The art step's review found the world came down a long way but the units barely moved: same drawings, muted colour, soft light. Before the go/no-go, a 12-image calibration tries "one notch more painted" (form modelling, painted depth, slightly less exaggerated proportions, dusk backgrounds for glow units, varied Neutral hues), judged in the game on the new backdrop. If it wins, every portrait is repainted to it as one set by midday 12 Nov; if not, the merged set stands. (orchestrator, 11 Nov, art step review ask)
- The notch won. In the game it shows turned forms, painted depth, glow units that finally glow, and cards that stand out more from the lighter world, with the same drawings and ink. Every portrait and tribe image is being repainted to it in three parallel slices; it merges only as a complete set, after the gate, the UI run and one review. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- The notch set is merged (all 162 images). Its review: the rendering moved clearly toward the world, but the drawings' cartoon proportions barely changed. Changing proportions is a redraw of every character, not a repaint, so it waits for the owner's playtest reaction (loop 2). Portraits use `header-notch.txt`; world art keeps `header.txt` with its environment adaptation (recipe README). (orchestrator, 11 Nov, notch review ask)
- B merges first, once its failing test is fixed in the code. A3 and A4 then run in parallel on split files (A3: the screens, standings, hand and reader placement; A4: the unit face, slots, board packing and hit regions); the second to merge rebases. A3 and A4 may rewrite old layout tests in `tests/ui/`, listed in their step notes. No setting pair ships this loop. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)
- Units are rounded hexes (0.78:1, points a fifth deep, the lab's `--hex-v`), with the back row tucked 29/226 into the front row and input inside the hex. Golden is a heavy gilt rim with warmer paper; selection is a cream-white rim; the triple warning is violet; hover is a lift and a deeper shadow with no ring, so none can be mistaken for another. Empty slots stay quiet at rest and light up whenever the player is placing something, by drag or by tap. Mire's standing ground bars are gone (they crossed the tucked row); Mired units keep their face marks. (orchestrator, 12 Nov, hex step review ask)
- The arrangement is built (arena step): one fixed board rectangle, identical in recruit, scouting and fight; the top zone swaps between market, scouted board and opponent; one unit size everywhere, grown until the band between market and board closed ("Vertical space goes to the boards", the lab's rule): 201×258 at 1920×1080, 145×186 at 1688×780. The lab's 820 px board and 176×226 units were its agent's numbers, not the owner's. Fight tools and the gear sit on a slim dark strip with cream icons and phone-sized tap areas. (orchestrator, 12 Nov, arena step review asks)
- The design-critique skill isn't used on the game's screens: it's a product-UI checklist ("is the purpose immediately clear?", "are interactive elements obvious?") that pushes toward labels and hints, against the declutter rule, and has nothing on motion, weight or sense of place. Reviewers answer the plan's plain questions beside reference shots instead. The owner (11 Nov): "If the agents are going to give bad results because I've recommended a skill, let me know." (owner and orchestrator, 11 Nov; advisor check-in)
- The layout mockups' tile-size pass/fail (150 px phone, 270 px PC shop) is dropped: it was a one-off brief direction, and the owner said no new bars. (orchestrator, 10 Nov, step 0b review ask)
- Look clips keep the GIF and contact sheet and delete their raw frames unless `--keep-frames` is given; look sessions older than 7 days are pruned. (orchestrator, 10 Nov, step 0 review ask)

- On recruit, the player's Health lives on their hero, right of the board; their standings chip keeps its place without repeating the number. (orchestrator, 11 Nov, step A review ask; moved by the arena step, 12 Nov)
- Bazaar Stack drops resting sockets and dashed empty pockets from every board, so day-1 problem 7's "empty slots shout" is already handled when loop 2 takes up fights (item C). (orchestrator, 11 Nov, step A review)
- The background frames play and needn't show everywhere: play sits central on its own surfaces, which may cover the backdrop's centre as The Bazaar's do, and the shop, board, hand and bar, lobby rail and fight board each read as their own area: "There needs to be visual breakup between different areas of the screen" (owner, 11 Nov, note `2026-11-11b`). The fight places both heroes to match. Built as step A2 on top of A. (orchestrator, 11 Nov)

## Look

- On `ideation/*`, units, heroes, spells, items, tribe images and the world are painted in "ink with wash and glow" (recipe: `tools/art/prompts/inkglow/`). `main` keeps "Ink + flat B" (owner, 1 Nov: `docs/design/sources/2026-11-01-art-style-decision.md`). (orchestrator, 11 Nov, art step review ask)
- Every pool unit has its own portrait in that style; no fallback names on tiles. (archive, 10 Nov, "Declutter batch 2 WIP")

## Screen and declutter

- The main screen assumes a player who knows the rules: no tutorial text, rule reminders or static pointers on it; fights read through motion, not labels, arrows or a log. (owner 09e; archive, 9 Nov, "The owner's declutter direction")
- Every element passes the spec's three tests: would a hundredth-game player use it now; does it show changing state rather than restate a rule; is it an event (make it motion) or a standing image. (`ui-declutter-spec.md` §1; archive, 9 Nov, "The declutter spec")
- Opponent tiers show as numerals, not pips. (archive, 10 Nov, "opponent tiers come back as numerals")
- The Ring frame shows presence on the board; exact counts and totals live in the one-tap reader. (archive, 10 Nov, "the Ring count stays in the reader")
- Scouting puts the scouted player's last board in the market's place above your board, which stays put. The market's offers, refresh and freeze, and the standings hide until you return, so buying waits until then. Any seat can be scouted from the standings in recruit, as Battlegrounds' leaderboard shows each player's last board; in fights the standings only show, with pre-fight Health. (orchestrator, 12 Nov, arena step review asks)
- The layout and label-budget tests are regression guards, not goals. A focus worker may rewrite or delete `tests/ui/` tests that assert the old layout, listed in its step note; that needs no line here.
