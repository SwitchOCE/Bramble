Some cards have a sentence that is just a keyword, like "Hunker." or "Peck 2." In the playable game, that sentence now moves out of the rules text into a small chip sitting above the card's name. That frees up room, so the rest of the text fits. Cards didn't have chips before; this is new.

{image:1}

**What we need from you:** keep the chip?

**Why it matters:** it's the first step in fitting long text onto small cards, and it changes how every card with a keyword looks.

We'd suggest keeping it. All four shortlisted looks in the design lab ended up with keyword chips of their own, so it fits wherever the look goes.

## Detail

- **What it does:** a sentence that is only a keyword ("Hunker.", "Peck 2.") becomes a chip above the name. It's the first step of the overflow order in question 9.
- **Design lab:** keyword chips appear in all four round-2 directions (Folk Print's red chips, Woodcraft's chips on the portrait, Field Journal's and Storybook Ink's labels on the art), each with the full text on tap. This is from the round-2 screenshots and notes.
- **The picture:** in the Folk Print screenshot from the build, the Still-Water Heron's card shows "Fast (refresh, 2)" as a chip and only "Feast: gain +2/+2 permanently." as text. In the Plain look the same card has room, so the sentence stays in the text (`proto/main:docs/prototype/screens/recruit_1688x780.jpg`). Our reading of the screenshots.
- The suggestion is ours.

**The original question (prototype question 13):**
> "New keyword chip. A sentence that is only a keyword ("Hunker.", "Peck 2.") moves to a small chip above the card's name, so the rest of the rules text fits. Cards had no chip before. Keep it?"
