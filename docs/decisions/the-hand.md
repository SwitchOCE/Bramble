Early on, we'd assumed that a unit you buy might go straight onto your board. The rulebook keeps Battlegrounds' way instead: a bought unit goes into your hand, and you play it onto the board from there, into whichever empty slot you like. Your hand holds up to 10 cards.

The playable game works this way, so you can feel it: the hand is the strip along the bottom of the shop screen.

{image:1}

**What we need from you:** keep the hand, or have bought units go straight onto the board?

**Why it matters:** a lot of familiar card text needs the hand. "When you play this" effects need a moment where you play the card. Triples, Discover and units that merge into another unit work through it too. Without a hand, all of those would need a different design.

We'd suggest keeping it. It's what Battlegrounds players expect, and it keeps all of that design space open.

## Detail

- **The rule:** rulebook 4.2.1 and 7.2 keep HSBG's hand. 7.2.1 [BRAMBLE]: playing a unit from hand puts it into any empty slot the player chooses, front or back. 7.1.1: the hand holds up to 10 cards.
- **Why the rulebook kept it (7.2, note):** "the direction summary's open question 5 assumed bought units go straight to the board. This rulebook keeps HSBG's hand (4.2.1), because "when played" effects, merges, Discover and triples all depend on it."
- **In the prototype:** bought units go to the hand; you drag them onto a slot to play them.
- **"What Battlegrounds players expect"** is our reasoning for the suggestion.
