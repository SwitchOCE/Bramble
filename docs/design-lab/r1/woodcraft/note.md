# Hedgerow Woodcraft (#3)

- **Idea:** a tabletop diorama you could carve. A walnut table, an oak tray with seven carved pockets, painted wooden tiles for units, acorns for gold, leaf-shaped buttons, and a cribbage-style peg board for the lobby.
- **For:** players who like a tactile, board-game table (Battlegrounds players who enjoy the "pieces on a table" feel, plus cozy and board-game players on mobile).
- **What makes it distinct:** the 4–3 brick is the tray's own shape. The back row is a narrower block stepped in by half a slot, so the stagger shows in the silhouette before you see any slot labels. Tiles have a visible edge and a hard bottom shadow. Tokens are pale pine with a dashed edge, golden units are gold leaf, frozen units sit under a frost glaze. The attacker's thread is red yarn. Nightshade halving is shown as a purple plaque, 26 → ½ → 13 → −1 → 12. Grain is drawn in ink and never rendered, so the wood sits with the Ink + flat B portraits. Attack is a hex nut and Health a round rosehip, so shape tells them apart as well as colour.
- **Godot 4 cost: low to medium.** One Theme. StyleBoxTexture nine-patches for the oak plaque, the pocket recess and the tile, each with a tiling grain texture. Leaf buttons are TextureButtons with three or four leaf shapes. The tray is two PanelContainers, or a single nine-patch per row. Four fonts (Grenze Gotisch, Grenze, Alegreya Sans, Bitter): drop Grenze Gotisch to save one. About 10 SVG icons. No shaders, except an optional frost overlay. Animations are Tween lifts, drops and wobbles ("place a piece in a slot"), plus a Line2D for the yarn.
- **Risks:** (1) Brown on brown: the oak tray, the carved pockets and the walnut table sit close in value. Here the ink outlines carry the separation, which is a risk on dim phone screens. (2) Wood grain on every surface can feel busy next to flat portraits, so keep the grain sparse. (3) Leaf buttons waste space at their tips and need wide hit areas; on a 390 px tall phone the text area is smaller than the leaf. (4) Board names are tiny on phone (labels burned under each pocket), so the keyword chip and the stats carry the board. (5) Brass pegs and golden-tile gilt share a colour. (6) Only six Ink B portraits exist, so units borrow the nearest one (the woodpeckers and the robin use the squirrel, for example).
