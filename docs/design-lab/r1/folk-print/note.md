# Folk Print (#5)

- **Idea:** Bramble as a European folk print: three inks (lamp-black, vermilion, ochre) on unbleached paper, plus Łowicz-style papercut layers (green, moss, cobalt, plum) that only mark tribes and statuses.
- **For:** players who want a game with a strong look of its own that still reads cleanly. It suits a European woodland setting and stands apart from both Hearthstone's gilt and casual mobile candy.
- **What makes it distinct:** every unit is an arched folk niche. Its outer papercut plate has punched dots in the tribe's colour, and the tribe also gets a carved motif (moth, bird, leaf, drop, paw, arrow, hexagon, sun), so colour is never the only cue. Attack is a black shield and Health a vermilion folk heart, so the two differ in shape, colour and corner. The board is a black linocut plate with gouge marks. Its seven arched windows make the 4–3 brick, with Front/Back ribbons, slot labels and ochre stitches from each back slot to the two front slots in front of it. Other uses of the print idea: tokens get a cut-along-the-line dashed border, frozen offers get a cobalt overprint and stamp, and Nightshade gets a plum berry tag and a ½ seal. The menu uses mirrored tree-of-life cuts.
- **Godot 4 cost: low to medium.** It needs one Theme with StyleBoxFlat panels (border, corner radius, a shadow offset for the misregistered block shadow) and two fonts (Alfa Slab One, and Alegreya with its Sans SC cut). The arched card frame and the dotted tribe plate are one nine-patch or StyleBoxTexture per tribe, or a small shader that tints one white frame. Icons and motifs are about 20 SVGs. Ornament (trees, rosettes, borders) is static textures. The paper grain and gouge marks are tiled textures, not shaders. Animation is Tween pops and stamps on Control nodes; the attack quill is a Line2D.
- **Risks:** the ornament is dense, and on a busy board the dotted plates and gouge texture could compete with the portraits. A judge should check board-size reading at arm's length. On the phone (stage scale ~0.54), names on board cards shrink or wrap, and shop text is clamped to 3 lines. Two tribe pairs share a darkish ink (Hunters/ink and Bog folk/moss next to green), so motif shape does real work. The Ink B portraits are full colour, not two-ink, so they sit inside the print frame rather than matching it. A later art pass could test a limited-palette take. Only six portraits exist, so units borrow and re-crop the nearest one.
