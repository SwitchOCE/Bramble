## The strongest combinations

**1. Mice behind Knights. Fun, and probably too strong.**
A Gate Warden or Hedge Knight in front; a Needle Pikeman and Needle Pages behind. The mice stab first every time the Knight attacks, and the Knight has the Health no mouse ever gets. A Herald adds two free attacks at the start that the needles join. The Mouse designer's own check had this beating every all-mouse board. Two costs hold it back a little: each mouse lowers the Badgers' count by 1, and a kill by a pile-on isn't the Knight's. It starts at Tier 1, so it will show up in most games. First dials: Needle Page's 4 Attack, the Pikeman's five gains a fight.

**2. Edgewright and Lieutenant on a drilled row. **
A Lieutenant reads half each slot’s Renown, rounded down. Edgewright doubles the Attack of a Mouse slot buff and leaves the volley unchanged.

**3. War-drummer in front of a Crab-apple. Fun; the Compost runs away at Tier 6.**
Each time the drum attacks, the Crab-apple seeds (one more unit in the Compost) and drops a Pip, which seeds again when it dies. That is five or more units a fight where a Mulch board adds three a turn. Every one gives each Sweetbriar +1/+1 for good, and Grow and Thorns count them next fight. A Dirge-singer doubles all of it. Dials: Sweetbriar counts the first few each fight, or a unit seeds once a fight.

**4. Hazel Coppicer on a Molt board. Strong and fun.**
A Molt board with a Skin-shedder has five or more returns a fight; a Briar board has three. Each one gives the whole board +1/+1 for good, on top of Molt's own +2/+2. The Briar designer already flagged it. Dial: the first three returns a fight.

**Also worth a look: Knights in the Colonel's hand.** The Badgers' count is fixed when the fight starts and units in hand aren't counted. One Knight on the board with spare Knights in hand: each time the Knight falls, the next steps into the same slot at the full count, with the same Squires behind it. It fits the tribe; at Tier 6 the Colonel doubles Renown for up to two hand reservists, plus any extra calls from Quartermasters.

## One-dimensional or doubled up

**The same card five times.** Each tribe has a cheap unit that gains about +2/+2 a fight for free: Brock Cub (Grudge), Old Campaigner (slot Renown), Husk Sentry (Molt), Blackthorn Sapling (Root), Gate Warden. The conditions differ (be outnumbered, live, die, die and pay, be attacked); the result on the board is the same number going up.

**Three plans that win before the lines meet.** The Badgers' Charge, the Pigeons' Air wing and the Mice's Siege crew all open the fight with free damage. They aim differently (the biggest unit, the back row, the weakest unit).

**Two full-house plans.** Dormouse Cook (seven Mice) and Colour Sergeant (a row of Pigeons) are the same idea: fill it with your own tribe and it grows each turn. Both shut out other tribes' units.

**Three 2/2 tokens.** Private, Cousin and Hopper have the same stats. They behave differently when there is no room (a Private isn't summoned, a Cousin is lost, a Hopper waits), which players won't see on the card.

**Gold.** Three tribes have a Tier 2 that pays about 1 gold a turn (Relic Seeker, Shrew Cutpurse, Hedge Layer). Pigeons and Locusts have none.

**Everyone wants the back row.** In all five tribes the supports stand in the three back slots and work on "the two units in front". It makes mixed boards easy to read. It also means one answer hurts everybody: a Sortie, the Gleaner, or the Mice's engines picking off the weakest unit.

**Badgers have one weakness in all four plans:** a few big enemies. No crowd, no Fury. A board that starts short and fills up during the fight (Regrowth, a hand in Reserve) switches them off the same way.

**Mice have one weakness in all four plans:** anything that hits several small bodies at once (Overrun, Cleave, Thorns, Orchard Stripper). Their four plans are two pairs, and their best use is behind another tribe.

**Nobody answers one enormous unit.** Badgers, the Locusts' Feast and the Briars' Overgrowth can each build one. The only cards aimed at it are the Gleaner and the Tourney Champion. No tribe has a "destroy" effect.

**Some splash pieces have one customer.** War-drummer and Dirge-singer only repeat death effects, and only Locusts and Briars have any. Thorns exist only on Briars.

**Names.** Three of the five are soldiers (knights, the Thimble Guard, the Regiment), and names collide:
- Dame of the Old Sett and Dame Bodkin.
- Sentry and Husk Sentry. Gate Warden and Hawthorn Warden.
- Kettle-drummer and War-drummer.
- Brood Mother, Elder Mother, Old Mother Midden, Mother of Thousands.
- Holly Knight is a Briar, but "Knight" is a word Badger cards read. Grenadier Guard doesn't have Guard.
- "The Order" is a Badger plan and "Order" is a Pigeon keyword.

None of these were renamed.

## Three calls made for you to check

1. **The two slot doublers stack** (Edgewright in the fight, Sergeant Major when the buff is given). Both designers had said they don't. That left the Mice's slot card dead next to the Pigeons' best one.
2. **A unit that takes another's stats takes only what that unit held itself:** not its slot's buff, what Squires lent it, or the Locusts' Tide. So Old Brock on a Locust board gains 2/2 for a Hopper, and the Badgers' Last Oath plan is a bit weaker than its designer wrote it.
3. **Being outnumbered is settled when the fight starts and never changes.** It is the Badger designer's rule, now applied to every other tribe's arrivals, and it is what makes "Knights in the Colonel's hand" work.
