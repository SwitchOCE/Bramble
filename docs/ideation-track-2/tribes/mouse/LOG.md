# Mice: design log

Newest round last. The numbers quoted come from a rough fight sketch (the rulebook's attack order, rows and summon placement; made-up enemy boards). It was a scratch tool and is not in the repo, so treat every figure as a guess.

## Before round 1: what the fight sketch showed

- **Pile on as picked ("hits the same enemy too") loses every fight.** A mouse with 3 to 8 Health dies whenever it attacks and whenever it is attacked, so mice lost two bodies for each enemy body. Seven mice killed about four of seven plain enemy units of ordinary size.
- **Changed: the crew hits first.** Pile-on hits land before the attack. If the enemy is dead, the attacker isn't hit back. The rulebook already ends an attack whose target is gone (9.6.1), so it needs no new timing rule. Mice now lose a body only when the enemy attacks, and their Attack is what protects them. This is why the tribe needs "a lot of attack scaling".
- **Neighbours, not only the mice beside it.** Pile on answers the units beside, in front of and behind. The back row is then the home for needles and the front row gives them attacks to join. It also works behind another tribe's attacker.
- **Tried and dropped before any unit list:**
  - *A hit counter* ("Tally (10): …", the mice's Avenge). Mouse fights last about ten hits. It rarely fired.
  - *Pins* (each hit makes later hits on that enemy bigger). Too slow for the same reason: anything that ramps inside a fight is over before it starts.
  - *A Thimble that blocks the first damage.* It would help, but mice are meant to be weak, and the Badgers and Locusts have Guard. Mice borrow that.
  - *Mice that stab whoever attacks their neighbour.* With a Guard in front it made a board nothing could attack.
  - *A bugler* ("the units in front of this attack at the start of the fight"). The Badgers wrote the same card (Herald of the Charge), so mice borrow it.
- **Slot wording** follows the Pigeons ("give this slot +3 Attack"). A "Sharpen" keyword was dropped.

## Round 1: five plans

**Tried.** Five places to keep Attack: on the mice (the line), on the slots (sharpened ground), on the Cousins (the muster: a Dormouse Cook gave "your Cousins have +2 Attack this game"), on one mouse (the needle knight) and on back-row engines (the siege crew). Needle Standard-bearer was "your other Mice have Pile on". Kettle-drummer gave any front-row unit two attacks. Shrew Cutpurse paid 2 gold a fight.

**The critic found** (reading only the unit list, then the plans):

- Four plans without help: line, ground, muster, siege. It did not find the knight, and said why: **the knight is the line.** Both are a Pikeman behind a Harrier.
- **The muster was the weakest and the least mouse-like.** Its board had two gaps, so the third Cousin had nowhere to land. A token plus an Attack number kept on the player is the Locusts' brood and Tide with a different trigger.
- **Pile-ons in reading order starved the scalers.** Front-row Pages killed the target before the back-row Pikeman ever hit, so it stopped growing. The line's own example was wrong for this reason.
- **Needle Standard-bearer did almost nothing:** nearly every mouse in that plan already had Pile on.
- **Kettle-drummer was every tribe's best Tier 6,** and the Cutpurse every tribe's best Tier 2 at twice the usual gold.
- Siege never touches Pile on and reads as a package any tribe could take.

**Changed.**

- **Four plans, not five.** The knight is folded into the first plan, now "the needles".
- **The muster became the parish: Attack from numbers.** The Standard-bearer is now "your Mice have +2 Attack for each Mouse you have", and the Cook is "whenever you play a Mouse, give your Mice +1 Attack". The plan no longer needs gaps: it wants a full board of Mice, and the Captains and King Speck exist to keep it full. The number on the player is gone, which was the Locust overlap.
- **Pile-on hits land together,** so every unit that piles on counts. Dame Bodkin became "pile-on hits deal double damage" to match.
- **Cutpurse:** 1 gold, once a fight. **Kettle-drummer:** front-row Mice only.
- **Master of Engines is the bridge to siege:** a Pikeman in the back row Hits for the Master too. In the sketch that pairing was the strongest opening in the tribe.
- Small numbers: Fencing Master stays +2, Miller down to +2 (Ground with the Edgewright was winning every fight), Catapult up to 12 Attack.

**Kept.** All 19 names but one, the three rules (Pile on, Hit, Cousin), and the ground and siege plans as they were.

## Round 2: four plans

**Tried.** Needles, parish, ground, siege. The parish's Cook was "whenever you play a Mouse, give your Mice +1 Attack". Kettle-drummer was "your front-row Mice attack twice". Fencing Master gave +2 on every pile-on.

**The second critic found** (a new reader, same blind test):

- Needles and siege without help. It read the parish as support, not a plan: before Tier 5 it was a Piper and a Cook that "nobody buys" (+1 a play, and a Tier 1 mouse bought and sold turns 1 gold into +1 Attack on every Mouse).
- **Ground and siege are the closest pair,** sharing all three slot cards. It would fold ground into siege.
- **Every board fills seven slots, so the summoners start as blank text.** It asked for a fifth plan that starts with gaps and pays for them.
- **Pikeman with Fencing Master ran away:** +5 permanent Attack on every pile-on.
- Kettle-drummer did nothing for Harriers, which already attack twice, and with Dame Bodkin made four times the pile-on damage. Siege still had no Tier 6.

**Changed.**

- **The Cook is the parish's own engine now:** "at the end of your turn, if your board is full of Mice, give them +3 Attack". It pays only for seven Mice, so it can't be splashed, can't leave a gap, and has no buy-and-sell loop.
- **Fencing Master is capped:** each Mouse grows once a fight (+3). The Pikeman keeps its own +3 with no limit; it is the tribe's one carry.
- **Kettle-drummer moved to the siege crew:** "your back-row units attack twice", so Catapults fire twice a turn. Siege has its Tier 6 and the stack with Dame Bodkin is mostly gone.
- **Ground kept as a plan, with the summoners as its payoff:** two Captains behind sharp front slots, so each Cousin arrives as strong as the mouse it replaces. The README says plainly that ground and siege share the slot cards.

**Tried and dropped.**

- **The gap plan** (a Tier 4 "your Cousins have +6 Attack for each empty slot you started with"). In the sketch a board with three gaps never won: the side with fewer units attacks second, its one or two front-row mice die before they attack, and the back row is open. A gap plan needs its Cousins at the start of the fight, and that card is the Locusts' Sky-darkener and the Pigeons' Recruiting Sergeant.
- **Cousins that copy the Captain's Attack.** They would count the Standard-bearer and the slot twice.

**Not fixed.** The summoners are refills, not a plan of their own. The plans are not level (README, "Not sure of"). Mice behind another tribe's front row beat every pure mouse board in the sketch.

## Round 3: last check

**Tried.** The same four plans with round 2's changes.

**The third critic found** (a new reader, same blind test):

- **All four plans from the unit list alone,** and the splash pieces: Page and Pikeman behind Badgers, the slot cards for Pigeons, Master of Engines and Kettle-drummer for any strong back row. It added one the README had missed: Locusts want the Captain, because a dying Cousin pays their death effects.
- It counted needles and parish as one plan, and ground and siege as "three and a half plans". Same finding as round 2 from the other side.
- The Pikeman still had no cap. The Needle Page is over a Tier 1's worth on any board. The Miller is under half a Tier 4's.
- Gaps an engine programmer would hit: what happens after a pile-on kill, who gets the kill, whether two Standard-bearers add up.
- The README said too little, too late, about mice being strongest as another tribe's back row.

**Changed** (small fixes only):

- Pikeman: at most 5 gains a fight.
- Rules notes: deaths and "after this attacks" still resolve after a pile-on kill; the attacker gets no credit for it; two Standard-bearers and two Fencing Masters add up; Thorns that kill the attacker cancel the pile-on.
- README: "three things to know first" at the top; one board's arithmetic explained; the Locust splash added.
- Miller left at +2 with the reason written down (at +3 ground won every sketch fight).

- Needle Squire is now Needle Pikeman, here and in the rounds above: Squire became a Badger keyword.

**Left for the owner.**

- Whether two pairs of close plans count as four. If not, the cheapest fifth is a real summoning plan, and it needs one card that summons at the start of the fight, which the Locusts and Pigeons already have.
- Needle Page at Tier 1: every tribe's pick, or the point of the tribe?
- Whether Pile on should hit first at all. Without it mice lose every fight; with it, each attack is free or fatal and the numbers will be touchy.

## 17 Nov 2026: made one game with the other four tribes

Not a design round. One pass over all five tribes wrote [../SHARED-RULES.md](../SHARED-RULES.md), the glossary and the rulings where tribes meet, and brought this tribe's files into line with it. No stats, tiers or costs changed.

- **Edgewright stacks with the Pigeons' Regimental Sergeant Major** (shared ruling 13). Before: "two multipliers don't multiply each other, the bigger applies", which this log had left to the Pigeons and which made the Edgewright dead beside him. Now: he doubles a buff when it is given, the Edgewright doubles what slots give in a fight, and one of each stack. Why: the other rule needed the engine to remember which part of a slot's buff had been doubled. It is the first thing to turn back down if slot boards run away.
- **Edgewright resolves in a set-up pass** at Start of fight, before every effect that reads a slot, wherever it stands. Before: "it resolves before other Start of fight effects that read slot buffs", with no rule behind it.
- **Dame Bodkin's type line** is Dormouse Duellist, not Dormouse Knight. Why: Knight is a type word Badger cards read ("your Knights").
- **Pile on reminder:** "Whenever a neighbour attacks" (was "When"), to match Thimble Captain and the other tribes' triggers.
- **Capital after the colon** on Harvest Piper, Master of Engines and Edgewright ("Start of fight: Your …"), as every tribe now writes it.
- **Spoon Catapult:** only its own turn is replaced by the Hit; told to attack out of turn (the Badgers' Herald) it makes an ordinary attack. New line, not a change.
- **Needle Pikeman behind another tribe** has two costs written down: behind a Badger it is a counted unit, and a kill its hit makes is not the attacker's (no Medal for a Flying Ace, no extra attack for The Oakfeller).
- **Kettle-drummer's note** says it does nothing for a Locust War-drummer (which drums only from the front row); the Locust README had claimed it did.
- **units.json fields.** Same fields as the other four tribes: `tribe` and `type` on every unit, a `golden` field, `splash_for` on every unit (empty unless it is a splash piece), tribe rule blocks under `rules`, and `note` renamed `implementer` and rewritten to say how the card works under the shared rules.
