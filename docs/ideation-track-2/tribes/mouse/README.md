# Mice (The Thimble Guard)

Ideation track 2. Nothing here is decided. The 19 units are in [units.json](units.json); what was tried and changed is in [LOG.md](LOG.md). Keywords shared with the other four tribes, and the rulings where tribes meet, are in [../SHARED-RULES.md](../SHARED-RULES.md); where this file and that one differ, that one wins.

The owner (16 Nov): "they're going to need a way of scaling attack. So, but I don't want them to scale HP, so they are weak, but it'd be good for the Mice as a tribe to have a lot of attack scaling in some ways. They could have some slot attack scaling too. They could have a few units that summon stuff."

**The tribe in one line:** a mouse's Attack is its only armour. Before a neighbour attacks, a mouse with an unused Ambush strikes first; if the enemy is dead before the blow lands, nobody gets hurt. Nothing in the tribe ever gains Health, so the four plans are four places to keep Attack for that opening and the ordinary trades that follow.

**Three things to know first.**

- **Four plans, in two pairs.** Needles and parish are both boards of Mice that pile on; ground and siege share the three slot cards. The last reader shown only the unit list found all four, and all three readers said the plans in a pair were close.
- **Mice are at their best behind another tribe.** A Needle Page is a Tier 1 that adds damage to any board, and a Needle Pikeman behind a sturdy front row beat every all-mouse board in the rough check. That is what the owner asked for ("pieces for other tribes"), and it may be too much of it.
- **The summoners refill; they are not a plan.** A board that starts with gaps and fills them with Cousins was tried twice and loses.

**Against the locusts.** Locusts are paid for dying. Mice never are: no mouse has a death effect or comes back, there is no number kept on the player, and the few Cousins they summon arrive when a mouse attacks, not when one falls. Mice Ambush once per unit, then trade normally.

## Three things the engine and the other tribes build on

- **Pile on.** When a unit attacks, each neighbour with Pile on and an unused Ambush Ambushes the same enemy first. Neighbours are the units next to it, in front of it and behind it, of any tribe.
  - The pile-on Ambushes land together, at the Attack each unit had when it joined, and the piling units take no damage back. Pile-on rewards follow the requests, so they do not strengthen the strikes that earned them.
  - If the enemy is dead after the pile-on, the attack ends: the attacker deals nothing and takes nothing.
  - So back-row mice with a lot of Attack can protect the front row on their one opening, and the front row gives them attacks to join.
- **Ambush.** Deal half this unit's current Attack, rounded up, and take nothing back. Pile on, Ballista, Catapult and every Master share one Ambush per unit per fight. Returns keep a spent use; golden copies, windfury, repeated neighbour attacks and several Masters give no extra uses. Zero Attack does not spend it. Bodkin makes only pile-on Ambushes deal full Attack, normal or golden. Ballista Ambushes at the start of the fight; Catapult Ambushes its ordinary defender before its first ordinary attack. Master gives only the leftmost back-row unit +4 Attack this fight (+8 golden), then asks it to Ambush the weakest enemy; a spent unit is not replaced by another shooter.
- **Cousin.** The tribe's token: a 2/2 Mouse with Pile on. Only summoned in fights. A Cousin with no empty slot to land in is lost.

Mice also put Attack on slots. That uses the Pigeons' slot buffs as they wrote them, with Attack only.

## The four plans

Each plan keeps its Attack somewhere different. These original rough board examples are for turn 9 at shop tier 5, written front row F1–F4, then back row B1–B3, with stats as they stand in the fight. Their listed stats and old rough results are historical examples, not frozen power-check ratings; the Ambush arithmetic below uses those listed stats.

Two more were tried and didn't hold up ([LOG.md](LOG.md)): "one giant needle" was plan 1 with one card swapped, and a board that starts with gaps and fills them with Cousins loses its only front-row mouse before it attacks.

### 1. The needles: Attack earned in fights, kept on a few mice

- **The plan:** two or three needles in the back row grow after their one pile-on each fight, and the front row exists to give them attacks to join.
- **Key units:** Needle Page (T1), Needle Pikeman (T3), Shrew Harrier (T3), Fencing Master (T4), Master of Engines (T5), Dame Bodkin (T6).
- **Why choose it:** the highest damage in the tribe on the fewest units, and the best late game: one Pikeman is lethal against units far too big for the other plans. Only the back row has to be Mice, so the front can be another tribe's attackers.
- **Turn-9 board:** this original repeated-Pile-on example had the Pikeman pile on since turn 5 (+60 and the Master's +6); the Fencing Master arrived on turn 7 (+6 on each needle); one Piper was played after the golden Page (+2). The stats below preserve that example; current Pikeman growth is +3 per fight (+6 golden), after its one pile-on Ambush.
  - F: Needle Page (golden) 16/6, Shrew Harrier 5/5, Shrew Harrier 5/5, Hedgerow Scout 2/5.
  - B: Needle Page 10/3, Needle Pikeman 70/5, Fencing Master 11/6.
  - The Harrier in F2 attacks: if their Ambushes are unused, both Pages and the Pikeman Ambush first for 48. If that kills the enemy, the Harrier is untouched and attacks again. The second attack gives those units no further Ambush.
- **What beats it:** anything that reaches the back row (a Sortie, damage to a random enemy): the Pikeman has 5 Health and can't be replaced. Overrun or Cleave on the front row. A wide board, once the Harriers are dead: the Pikeman has only one Ambush per fight. At turn 9 it is the weakest of the four against an ordinary board.

### 2. The parish: Attack from a full house

- **The plan:** seven Mice, any seven, and keep it seven. A full board grows every turn and each mouse makes the others stronger in the fight.
- **Key units:** Harvest Piper (T2), Thimble Captain (T3), Dormouse Cook (T4), Parish Standard-bearer (T5), King Speck (T6). Needle Pages and Hedgerow Scouts are the cheap seats.
- **Why choose it:** every body is dangerous, so it trades evenly when it is attacked, where the needles lose a mouse for nothing. It grows in the shop, so no fight has to be won and no single mouse has to live. The Captain and King Speck put back what dies.
- **Turn-9 board:** two Pipers played early; the Cook has fed a full board three times, this turn's included (+9). Seven Mice, so the Standard-bearer gives +14 each.
  - F: Hedgerow Scout (golden) 31/10, Needle Page (golden) 35/6, Needle Page 27/3, Needle Page 27/3.
  - B: Thimble Captain 26/7, Parish Standard-bearer 23/8, Dormouse Cook 25/9.
  - Each mouse that dies costs the rest 2 Attack. The next attack by a neighbour of the Captain puts a Cousin in the gap at 16/2 and the Attack comes back.
- **What beats it:** Overrun, Cleave and damage to all enemies (the Locusts' Orchard Stripper): one swing kills three mice and takes 6 Attack off the rest. Killing the Standard-bearer. It has to be all Mice and can't leave a gap, so it can't take another tribe's best unit.

### 3. Sharpened ground: Attack on the slots

- **The plan:** lay Attack on the front slots, turn after turn, and keep bodies arriving on them.
- **Key units:** Whetstone Vole (T2), Thimble Captain (T3), Shrew Harrier (T3), Grindstone Miller (T4), Edgewright (T5), King Speck (T6).
- **Why choose it:** the Attack can't be killed or sold. A Cousin that lands on a sharp slot attacks normally as hard as the mouse that died there, so this is the plan where the summoners matter most. Units can be swapped for better ones at no cost, and any tribe's unit can stand on the slot.
- **Turn-9 board:** the Vole has sharpened F2 and F3 since turn 3; the Miller has stood in B2 for three turns, this one included. Slots: F2 and F3 +15, back row +6, doubled in the fight by the Edgewright.
  - F: Hedgerow Scout (golden) 4/10, Needle Page 34/3, Shrew Harrier 35/5, Edgewright 4/9.
  - B: Thimble Captain 15/7, Grindstone Miller 15/7, Thimble Captain 15/7.
  - Whoever stands in F2 or F3 attacks normally for over 30; a fresh 32-Attack Cousin Ambushes once for 16. When one falls, the next attack by a neighbour of a Captain puts a Cousin in the nearest empty slot, which is that one, at 32/2.
- **What beats it:** it is slow. Before the Miller has had three turns the slots are small, and a fast board wins those fights. F1 and F4 stay blunt. The Edgewright only doubles what is there.

### 4. The siege crew: one opening, then ordinary attacks

- **The plan:** Ballista and one reinforced back-row unit Ambush before the lines meet; a Catapult with an unused Ambush uses it before its first ordinary attack. Ordinary attacks and retaliation follow.
- **Key units:** Toothpick Ballista (T3), Spoon Catapult (T4), Master of Engines (T5), Kettle-drummer (T6). The Attack under the engines comes from the slot cards, or from a Needle Pikeman between two Catapults, which also fires for the Master.
- **Why choose it:** Ballista and the Master's selected unit aim their one Ambush at the lowest Health, so they can finish wounded units and pick supports out of the enemy back row. Catapult uses its ordinary attack target.
- **Turn-9 board:** B1 and B3 have +9, B2 and the middle front slots +6, doubled by the Edgewright.
  - F: Hedgerow Scout 2/5, Master of Engines 17/8, Toothpick Ballista 20/3, Edgewright 4/9.
  - B: Spoon Catapult 30/4, Grindstone Miller 15/7, Spoon Catapult 30/4.
  - Before anyone attacks, the Master gives B1 +4 Attack this fight: its 34 Attack produces a 17-damage Ambush. Ballista Ambushes for 10, for 27 between them. B1 has spent its use; B3 can Ambush its first ordinary defender for 15 before attacking normally. Neither Catapult repeats its Ambush on later attacks.
- **What beats it:** big Health. Opening engines can't gang up the way Pile on does, and each unit has only one Ambush before ordinary trades. Units that come back (Molt, Root). Its front row is four soft bodies, and once they are gone the engines are next.

Ground and siege are the closest pair: they share the three slot cards. The difference is what stands on the slots. Ground puts attackers on sharp front slots and replaces them as they fall; siege puts engines on sharp back slots and hides them.

## Units other tribes want

**Mice as somebody else's back row.** The strongest board in the rough check was not a mouse plan: four ordinary 20/40 units of another tribe in front, and behind them a golden Needle Page, a Needle Pikeman at 70 and a Shrew Cutpurse. The front row has the Health mice never get, the first eligible attack is joined from behind, and the Pikeman grows once. Badgers get the most from it (Herald of the Charge, Hedge Lancer and The Oakfeller all attack out of turn, and each neighbour can answer only once per fight), then the Pigeons' air wing. It has two costs there. A mouse in a Badger back row is a counted unit, not a Squire, so each one lowers the outnumbered count by 1. And a kill made by a pile-on is not the attacker's, so The Oakfeller gets no extra attack for it and a Flying Ace no Medal.

Who buys the other splash pieces:

- **Whetstone Vole, Grindstone Miller, Edgewright:** Pigeons. Their Lieutenant commands its full row to make ordinary attacks against the enemies across at the start of the fight, taking retaliation; slot Attack strengthens those attacks, and the Edgewright doubles that slot Attack. The Regimental Sergeant Major doubles the Vole's and the Miller's buffs as they are given, and that stacks with the Edgewright. A lone Badger Knight's slot, or an Armsbearer's, which lends the slot's Attack to both units in front.
- **Thimble Captain:** the Pigeons' "hold the post": a Cousin lands in the buffed slot. Locusts too: a Cousin that dies pays their Gleaner and The Eighth Plague like any friendly death.
- **Master of Engines:** any back row with Attack on it, such as a Badger Armsbearer or Briars that Grow.
- **Kettle-drummer:** Pigeons that fly Sorties from the back row.

What mice take from the others:

- **Guard** (Badgers' Hedge Knight and Gate Warden, Locusts' Husk Sentry, the Briars' Hawthorn Warden, the Pigeons' Guard post): one unit the enemy must attack first keeps the Harriers and Scouts alive.
- **Herald of the Charge** (Badgers): "the units in front of this attack immediately" can trigger the unused Ambushes of needles standing behind them.
- **Health from behind** (Badgers' Sett Shield-bearer lends its Health to the units in front; Pigeons' Lance Corporal gives the slots in front +2/+2): the only Health a mouse will ever see.

## Tier check

- **Tier 1:** a Pile on body that is plain alone and tempo next to any attacker, and the first summoner. Both under the usual body.
- **Tier 2:** two buffers (Piper once, Vole each turn) and the economy piece, 1 gold a turn like the other tribes'. The Vole gives about a quarter of a body a turn, at the low end, because slot Attack can't be lost.
- **Tier 3:** two enablers (Captain, Pikeman) and two combat pieces (Ballista, Harrier). No buffer, on purpose: the buffers sit at 2 and 4. The Pikeman gains at most +3 a fight after its one pile-on Ambush: it is the tribe's one carry and it has 5 Health. The Needle Page's utility is now one 2-damage Ambush, with nothing taken back, on any tribe's board; the old repeated-Pile-on tier estimate no longer applies.
- **Tier 4:** three buffers and a combat engine, no multiplier. **Under the guide on purpose:** the Miller gives about 10 Attack a turn over five slots, less than half the target. Slot Attack can't be lost and the Edgewright doubles it; at +3 the ground board won every fight in the rough check. Fencing Master: at most 21 a turn, on target. Cook: 21 a turn on a full board of Mice and nothing otherwise, on target.
- **Tier 5:** a direction card (Standard-bearer, 98 Attack on a full board, five bodies, falling as mice die), a combat engine (Master of Engines, +4 Attack this fight and one shared Ambush from the leftmost back-row unit) and a multiplier (Edgewright).
- **Tier 6:** one capstone that makes bodies (King Speck) and two multipliers (Dame Bodkin makes pile-on Ambushes deal full Attack; the Kettle-drummer doubles the back row's turns). Extra back-row attacks can trigger unused neighbouring Ambushes, but never give another use.
- **Bodies:** every mouse is under the tier's usual body, and the gap is all Health. No card in the tribe gives Health.

## Rules it needs

1. **Pile on.** In rulebook 9.6.1 step 2, after the attacker's own "whenever this attacks" effects, each neighbour (8.2.3) with Pile on and an unused Ambush Ambushes the target, all at the same moment. If the target is dead afterwards, the attack ends with no damage exchange; deaths and "after this attacks" effects still resolve, and the attacker gets no credit for the kill. A unit summoned in that step can pile on. If the attacker is already gone (Thorns), nobody does.
2. **Ambush.** A unit deals half its current Attack as damage, rounded up, and takes none back. Not an attack. One shared use per unit per fight across all sources; returns preserve it. Golden copies, windfury and several Masters give no extra uses. Bodkin changes only pile-on Ambush to full Attack, normal or golden.
3. **"Whenever a neighbour attacks"** and **"after this piles on"** as triggers, with a count for each unit each fight (Pikeman 1, Fencing Master 1).
4. **"Can't attack"** (skipped in the attack order). Catapult instead keeps its ordinary attack, Ambushing that defender first if its use is ready; ordinary retaliation follows if the defender survives.
5. **"The enemy with the least Health":** both rows, current Health, ties at random.
6. **Slot buffs** as the Pigeons define them, and one effect that doubles the Attack they give for a fight. It is applied when a slot is read, so the stored buff doesn't change.
7. **Cousin token:** a 2/2 Mouse with Pile on that lasts the fight. It never waits for a slot.
8. **Gold earned in a fight** is added at the start of the next turn.
9. **An aura that gives "attacks twice"** to a row (9.4.11 covers the attacks), and one that makes pile-on Ambushes deal full Attack.
10. **An aura that counts a tribe:** "+2 Attack for each Mouse you have", recounted as units arrive and die. And an end-of-turn check for "your board is full of Mice".
11. **"Fill your empty slots":** one summon for each empty slot, placed by 8.4.2.

## Not sure of

- **Ambushing first is all or nothing for that opening.** Once the unused Ambushes around an attacker add up to the enemy's Health, that attack costs nothing. A little under the mark and the ordinary trade follows. Each unit can do this only once per fight, but it will make the numbers touchy.
- **The plans are not level yet.** Checked only with a rough fight sketch against made-up enemy boards. Against an ordinary turn-9 board (seven 20/22s), Ground won about four in five and the Parish three in four; Siege and the Needles won less than half, with many draws. Against a strong board (seven 30/34s) only Ground won half. Late on, with Dame Bodkin, the Needles were the best of the three tried (Ground was not).
- **The summoners.** On a full board the Scout and the Captain do nothing until something dies. They earn their place on sharpened ground and in the parish; elsewhere they are bodies, and the Scout is close to a card nobody buys after turn 5.
- **One giant unit.** The picked option promised "lots of small hits bring down one big target". A lone 160/260 still beats most turn-9 mouse boards, because an attack that doesn't kill costs the attacker. Dame Bodkin strengthens the one pile-on Ambush to full Attack at Tier 6; it gives no extra uses.
- **Every plan loses to Overrun and Cleave, and to Thorns.** A swing that carries on through 3-Health bodies clears a row, and Thorns kill a 3-Health attacker before anyone piles on. Meant, since the owner wants the mice weak, but with Badgers and Briars both in the pool it may be too hard a counter. The rough check used Cleave; Overrun was not tried.
- **The Edgewright beside the Pigeons' Regimental Sergeant Major.** Settled in the shared rules (ruling 13, 17 Nov): they stack, once each. He doubles a buff when it is given; the Edgewright doubles what the slots give in a fight. Both drafts had said the bigger one applies, which left the Edgewright dead beside him. It is the first thing to turn back down if slot boards run away.
