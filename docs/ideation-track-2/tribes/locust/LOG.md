# Locusts: design log

What was tried, what a fresh critic found, what changed. The tribe as it stands is in [README.md](README.md) and [units.json](units.json). Nothing here is decided.

## Where it started

The owner turned down all three first options (the tipping point at five Locusts, Brood, Hunger) and wrote: "they kind of like swarm and, you know, leave devastation in their wake and breed more locusts. So I thought of them more of like a summoning tribe, more similar Battlegrounds Undead".

So the tribe was built from Undead's parts, one plan for each thing in that sentence:

- **Swarm:** the Tide, Attack that sits on the player like "your Undead have +1 Attack this game".
- **Breed:** Hoppers, and a plan that makes them bigger all game (Battlegrounds' Beetles are the nearest thing).
- **Devastation in their wake:** deaths that deal damage. Undead has nothing like it.
- **Won't stay dead:** Molt, which is Undead's Reborn.
- **Feeding on its own:** eating, which stands in for Undead killing its own units in the shop.

None of the three rejected options is in it. Nothing counts to five, nothing splits into halves, and nothing eats the shop.

Three calls are mine and worth the owner's eye:

1. **Hoppers wait.** On a seven-slot board a summoning tribe loses most of its summons to a full board. A Hopper with no room waits and lands when a slot empties. It makes "summon 3" always mean 3, and it gives the swarm a number on screen. Without the rule the cards still work; extra Hoppers would be lost as in Battlegrounds.
2. **The Tide is a named number.** It keeps the cards short ("Tide +2") and gives the player one figure to watch.
3. **Molt grows the unit.** Each return is +2/+2 permanently. This came in during round 2; the reason is there.

## Round 1

**Tried:** the five plans above. Prophet of the Dust was "When played: Tide +2". Molt was plain Reborn. Skin-shedder gave Molt to its neighbours when it died. The eaters took the sold unit's stats as they stood on the board. Dirge-singer was Tier 5 and The Eighth Plague Tier 6.

**The critic,** reading only the unit list, found all five plans. Then:

- **Prophet was a loop:** buy, play, sell, 2 gold for Tide +2, as often as the gold lasts. Every plan drifted towards it.
- **Eating copied the Tide.** The eater took the Tide off its meal and then had the Tide on top, so a free Hopper a turn became +22 Attack.
- **Molt was thin.** Its whole payoff was one Tier 5 card, and returned units were 1-Health husks.
- **Devastation shared a front row with the Tide plan** and its flat 4 damage never grew before Tier 6.
- **Feast was the weakest board.**
- Skin-shedder was called too fiddly to buy. Husk Sentry was dead weight after turn 6. Brood Mother's text read as a fixed +1/+1.

**Changed:**

- Prophet became "At the end of your turn, Tide +1". It has to hold a slot to pay.
- Eating takes the unit's own stats only, not the Tide, the Hopper bonus or a slot's buff.
- **Molt now gives +2/+2 permanently on each return.** This is what made Molt a plan: it grows from turn 1, the Sentry stays a real Guard late, and "die, come back, get stronger for dying" is on the keyword itself.
- Skin-shedder became a Start of fight effect on its neighbours.
- Gleaner aims at the enemy's biggest unit, so the damage plan has a way to kill a carry. The Eighth Plague moved down to Tier 5 and Dirge-singer up to Tier 6. The Devastation board was rebuilt around cheap bodies so it no longer looks like the Tide board.
- Swarm-lord counts deaths in twos. Brood Mother was reworded.

## Round 2

**The critic** (a new one) again found all five plans and the splash pieces from the unit list alone. Then:

- **Granary Glutton was the best stat pump in the set for every tribe:** 1 gold for about 9 permanent stats with no limit.
- **The Feast board didn't work as written:** all seven slots were full, so Horde-runner summoned nothing and an eaten Recruit had no room for its Hoppers.
- **The Tide plan was the strongest and Devastation the weakest.**
- Sky-darkener wasn't a real splash piece: three plain 2/2s. Molt's wording was unclear about the 1 Health. Skin-shedder's "give Molt" clashed with "the first time each fight".
- The Tide board is close to the mouse brief (see "Not sure of").

**Changed:**

- Granary Glutton eats Locusts only. Feast is a Locust plan; other tribes get the Gorger's one meal a turn.
- Horde-runner gives the Hopper to the hand, so it works on a full board.
- Gleaner and Orchard Stripper went from 4 to 5 damage. Sky-darkener is marked tribe-only.

## Round 3 (a last check of text, rules and sums)

**Found:**

- **The gold didn't add up for Tier 5.** Going up a tier takes nearly the whole turn, so Tier 5 units arrive on turn 9, not turn 8. The Glutton, then Tier 5, had one turn to eat and Feast came to about half the other boards.
- Players would expect a Molted unit to come back at its full size. The Veteran's buff read as permanent. The Eighth Plague read as many 1-damage hits. Two eaters on one board had no order. A Briar Old Stump eaten in the shop had no defined result.

**Changed:**

- **Granary Glutton moved to Tier 4 and War-drummer to Tier 5.** Feast now starts on turn 7 and can stay on Tier 4, which is a real choice the other plans don't have. The drum arriving later also takes the edge off the Tide and Brood boards, which both critics had as the strongest.
- All five turn-9 boards were redone on the corrected timeline (Tier 2 units on turn 3, Tier 3 on 5, Tier 4 on 7, Tier 5 on 9). They now come out within about half as much again of each other.
- Molt's reminder says "as a plain copy". The Veteran says "this fight". The Plague is one hit. The Gorger eats the first sale, the Glutton the rest. Death effects that need a fight do nothing in the shop.

## Dropped, and why

- **Stripping Attack from the enemy** (Orchard Stripper took 6 Attack off their biggest unit). Too small to matter against a big unit and it added a second kind of devastation. Damage does the job; the Gleaner aiming at the biggest unit is the answer to a carry.
- **Damage that scales with the Tide.** It would have fixed Devastation's growth in one line, and it would have made every plan the Tide plan.
- **A third number on the player** for death damage. Two (the Tide and the Hopper bonus) is already a lot to track.
- **Hoppers that sell for gold.** With an eater it makes gold from nothing.
- **Killing your own units in the shop** as its own action (Undead's Dead Bellringer). Eating on sale gives the same play (death effects go off in the shop) with an action the player already knows.

## Not sure of

1. **Rising Tide against the mice.** Both are small bodies with a lot of Attack. They differ in how (mice hit together and are never paid for dying; Locusts are paid for dying and the Attack is on the player), but on the table the two boards may look alike. If it reads as the same tribe twice, the Tide is the thing to change, not the mice.
2. **Hoppers waiting.** It is a new rule and it makes Locust fronts very hard to clear. If it is too much, the cut is clean: drop the rule and the cards stay as they are.
3. **Molt giving +2/+2 for free.** It is on target for each card, but a board of seven Molt units grows about 28 stats a turn from Tier 1 to 3 cards. It may want to be +1/+1, or only on units with printed Molt.
4. **The runaway corners,** all at Tier 6: Mother of Thousands with The Eighth Plague or Swarm-lord, and Dirge-singer with a drummed Famine-caller. They are listed in the tier check as the first things to try in the build.
5. **Devastation** is honest about being the weakest in a plain fight. Whether "best against wide boards and hidden carries, worst against big ones" makes it a plan people pick or a counter people tech into, only play will say.
6. **Prophet of the Dust and Horde-runner** pay in the shop phase. If the owner's idea of units moving freely between hand and board goes in, both need its lock keyword.
7. All numbers are from sums on paper. Nothing was simulated.
